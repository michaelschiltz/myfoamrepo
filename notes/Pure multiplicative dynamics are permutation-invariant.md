# Pure multiplicative dynamics are permutation-invariant

Under bare multiplicative growth the order of events does not matter, and this negative result is load-bearing rather than preliminary.

Wealth after $N$ periods is

$$W_N = W_0 \prod_{i=1}^{N} r_i$$

Multiplication commutes, so $W_N$ is invariant under any permutation of the returns. A run of $+30\%$, $-20\%$, $+10\%$ lands exactly where $-20\%$, $+10\%$, $+30\%$ lands. The same holds for the [[Time-average]] growth rate $g = \frac{1}{N}\sum_i \log r_i$, which is a sum and therefore equally order-blind.

The consequence for historical argument is severe. Most assertions that "sequence mattered" are made about dynamics that are, as specified, multiplicative — and are therefore empty as stated. Something beyond multiplicative growth must be doing the work: flows in and out ([[Flows make the dynamic affine and affine maps do not commute]]), an absorbing barrier ([[Terminal wealth is permutation-invariant but survival is not]]), or state-dependent reinforcement ([[Reinforcement selects an attractor the ensemble average never occupies]]). Those three exhaust the standard mechanisms.

This is why the invariance result is the opening move and not a throat-clearing preliminary: it converts a vague appeal to history into a demand for a named operator. See [[Path dependence requires naming the non-commuting operation]].
