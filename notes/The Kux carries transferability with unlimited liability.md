# The Kux carries transferability with unlimited liability

**The German mining share is a better-evidenced instance of the transferable-share form than the Swedish one, and it carries a configuration the joint-stock literature usually treats as impossible.**

## The form

From the fifteenth century in the Saxon Erzgebirge and the Harz, a mining enterprise — a *Gewerkschaft*, a body of mining law rather than of merchant custom — was divided into **128 *Kuxe***, themselves further divisible into fractions.

- **Freely divisible, mortgageable and saleable, without the consent of the other holders.**
- **Personal liability for the enterprise's losses**, not limited to the sum subscribed.
- ***Zubusse***: periodic capital calls when the mine ran at a loss. **A holder who could not meet a call forfeited the share to the others.**
- Traded at the **Leipzig fairs from 1472** and at Frankfurt, through specialised brokers (*Kuxkränzler*), with insider dealing and manipulation reported as ordinary.

## The finding, stated plainly

**A transferable share and unlimited liability coexisted, in a market, for centuries.**

The standard account runs the other way: transferable claims are held to *require* limited liability, because no one will buy from a stranger an instrument carrying unbounded obligations, and the pricing problem is held to be insoluble. **The Kux is a standing counter-example** — and the market did not merely tolerate the problem but priced and contracted around it, with clauses obliging the seller to meet calls on the buyer's behalf for a period after sale.

**That bears directly on [[Transferable claims reconcile lock-in with member exit]].** The Kux resolves the same tension by the opposite route: not by capping the member's exposure so the claim can move, but by **making the claim move while leaving the exposure uncapped** — and then using forfeiture as the enforcement that a liability rule would otherwise have provided.

**And the *Zubusse* is capital lock-in inverted.** The census's `capital-lock-in` asks whether contributed capital can be withdrawn at the member's will. Here it cannot — *and the member can be compelled to contribute more*, on pain of losing what he has. That is a stronger condition than lock-in, and the census has no value for it.

## Why it is better evidenced than Stora

⚠️ On present knowledge, and the comparison is the point of this note.

| | Kux | Stora |
|---|---|---|
| Legal form | *bergrechtliche Gewerkschaft*, with stated rules | not established |
| Transferability | general, without consent | one conveyance, uncle to nephew |
| Liability | personal, with calls and forfeiture | not established |
| Market | Leipzig from 1472, brokers, reported abuses | not established |
| Secondary literature | Graulau 2019 (**held**), and others | thin; a heritage narrative |
