# Pooling pays only where ventures grow on average

The gain from pooling under a barrier depends on the sign of the drift, and the three cases Vanhoyweghen, Verbeken and Ginis distinguish line up with the three cases of the Galton–Watson process. That turns the ruin route to cooperation into a differential prediction about where pooling institutions should and should not be found.

| Regime (Vanhoyweghen et al.) | Galton–Watson | Effect of pooling $N$ independent members |
|---|---|---|
| I. Escape possible: positive drift | supercritical, $q < 1$ | ruin probability $p \to p^N$; large |
| II. Absorption certain, finite expected time: negative drift | subcritical | none on expected time to ruin in the additive case ($s/\lvert\mu\rvert$ is independent of $\sigma$); about $\log k$ for lineages |
| III. Absorption certain, infinite expected time: zero drift | critical | buys time: the chance of ruin within a fixed horizon falls with $\sigma/\sqrt{N}$ |

In plain numbers, for five partners: in a trade that pays on average, a 40 per cent chance of ruin alone becomes about 1 per cent pooled; in a trade that loses on average, the pool goes under on the same schedule as each man would have alone; in a trade that roughly breaks even, a one-in-two chance of ruin within ten years becomes about one in eight.

## The prediction

Pooling institutions should cluster where ventures were **profitable on average, volatile, exposed to an absorbing loss, and struck by perils that fell separately**. They should be rare, short-lived or merely palliative where the trade was in secular decline, and they should postpone rather than prevent ruin where it was stagnant. A mutual fund among the members of a dying trade can smooth the decline; it cannot change where it ends.

This is the regime-before-institution order that Part 1, Objective 1 commits to, given a sign: record the drift of the venture class before coding the institution, then check whether pooling forms track the sign.

## Weak points

- **Drift is inferred.** For historical ventures the average return is estimated from the same accounts the institution produced, the problem flagged at [[Jensen supplies the gap but only the dynamic privileges the logarithm]]. Where a long price or freight series exists, the drift can be estimated independently of the contracts.
- **The regimes are idealised.** Real trades change regime — a route opens, peaks and declines — and the prediction is about the timing of pooling forms relative to those phases, not about a fixed classification.
- **Regime II has a rival reading.** Mutual aid in declining guilds is well attested and is better read as the pool distributing an unavoidable loss than as a failed attempt to avoid it. That reading is consistent with this note, but it means the presence of a fund in a declining trade is not evidence against the prediction; its *growth* would be.
