# Source editions — Ottoman and Islamic law

Printed and online editions of Ottoman court registers, waqf registers, estate inventories, sultanic law, fatwas, and Islamic partnership law in translation. **Already recorded elsewhere**: [[Istanbul Kadi Sicilleri - the published transliterations]], [[Tereke defterleri - the Ottoman estate inventory series]], [[Gurema - the insolvent estate distributed among creditors]].

## Waqf registers — the cash-waqf evidence

- **Barkan & Ayverdi (eds), *İstanbul Vakıfları Tahrîr Defteri 953 (1546) Târîhli*** (İstanbul: İstanbul Fetih Cemiyeti, 1970). **About 2,500 Istanbul waqfs, including 845 cash waqfs** — capital, rates of return, trustees. **The single best printed body of cash waqfs**, and directly relevant to the parked `cash_waqf` row. ⚠️ Pagination and series number unverified.
- **Mehmet Canatar (ed.), *İstanbul Vakıfları Tahrîr Defteri 1009 (1600)*** (İstanbul Fetih Cemiyeti, 2004), lxxvi + 824 pp. **3,265 waqfs; cash waqfs largely drop out** — apparently a registration choice. **A 54-year panel with a survivorship trap built in.**
- **Lejla Gazić (tr.), *Vakufname iz Bosne i Hercegovine (XV i XVI vijek)*** (Sarajevo: Orijentalni institut, 1985; Monumenta Turcica 5, ser. III, 1) — Balkan waqf deeds in translation.

## Court registers beyond the capital

- **İSAM's series covers more than Istanbul proper** — Galata and Eyüb registers are in it (e.g. *Galata Mahkemesi 90 Numaralı Sicil*, 2012; *Eyüb Mahkemesi 49*, 2011); free search at **kadisicilleri.org**. ⚠️ Volume count not established.
- **Konya**: municipal series, e.g. *Konya Kadı Sicili 50 (1726–27)* (2014) and *Defter 150–151 (1898–1907)* (2012).
- **Bursa**: the municipality is digitising **1,059 registers, 268,322 pages, 1455 onward**; 33 registers summarised by January 2024 (Digital City Archive). Print: İnalcık's selections, *Belgeler* XIII/17 (1988).
- **Other provinces**: mostly edited as MA/PhD theses — the Turkish thesis database is the practical route.
- **Jerusalem**: Amnon Cohen, *A World Within* (Philadelphia: Center for Judaic Studies, 1994; texts + facsimiles); Cohen & Simon-Pikali, *Jews in the Moslem Religious Court* (Hebrew, Yad Ben-Zvi, 1993); Cohen, *The Guilds of Ottoman Jerusalem* (Brill, 2001; appendix of 19 register entries).
- **Damascus**: Marino & Okawara (eds), *Dalīl sijillāt al-maḥākim al-sharʿiyya* (IFEAD, 1999) — a finding aid; Okawara is a Japanese Ottomanist.
- **Harvard's Ottoman Court Records Project** (Istanbul register 121, 1816–17, transliteration and English translation planned). ⚠️ Publication status unknown.

## Estates and guilds

- **Barkan, "Edirne Askerî Kassamı'na Âit Tereke Defterleri (1545–1659)"**, *Belgeler* III/5–6 (1966), 1–479 — **free PDF on DergiPark**. The classic printed estate-inventory corpus, with debts owed to and by estates.
- **Ahmet Kal'a (ed.), *İstanbul Esnaf Tarihi***, 2 vols (İstanbul Araştırmaları Merkezi, 1997) — guild orders from the Istanbul *ahkâm* registers. ⚠️ Retailer listing only.

## Sultanic law and central orders

- **Ahmed Akgündüz, *Osmanlı Kanunnâmeleri ve Hukukî Tahlilleri***, **11 vols** (İstanbul: FEY Vakfı/OSAV, 1990–2016) — not 9.
- **Barkan, *Kanunlar*** (İstanbul, 1943) — 107 kanunnames; the second volume never appeared.
- **Mühimme registers, State Archives edition series** (Ankara, 1993–2000s): nos 3, 5, 6, 7, 12, 82, 83 confirmed — **free PDFs** on the archive's publications page.

## Fatwas and Islamic partnership law

- **M. Ertuğrul Düzdağ, *Şeyhülislâm Ebussuûd Efendi Fetvaları Işığında 16. Asır Türk Hayatı*** (İstanbul: Enderun, 1972) — Ebussuud on partnership, *mudârebe*, debt and cash waqfs.
- **Yenişehirli Abdullah Efendi, *Behcetü'l-Fetâvâ***, ed. Kaya & Demiray (İstanbul: Klasik, 2011) — the standard eighteenth-century collection, for comparison with Ebussuud.
- **Tyser et al. (tr.), *The Mejelle*** (Nicosia 1901; Lahore reprints) — Book X on partnership; archive.org lending.
- **Udovitch, *Partnership and Profit in Medieval Islam*** (Princeton, 1970) — the English route into classical partnership law. **Ramli 2020** translates only the first chapter of al-Sarakhsī's *Kitāb al-Muḍāraba*; **no full English translation of Sarakhsī's partnership books was found.**

## After 1850: the commercial code and the commercial courts (added 2026-09-24)

**The Ottoman boedelkamer in the strict sense** — a procedure for the failure of a going concern — begins with the commercial code of 1850. What is in print:

- **The code itself.** *Kanunname-i Ticaret* (1850), 315 articles; **bankruptcy (*iflas*) is arts. 148–315, the concordat (*konkordato*) arts. 211–248** — more than half the code. Ottoman text in *Düstur*, 1st series, vol. 1, pp. 375–445; modern edition by Gürzumar (1962). The article structure is from **Çınar, *İstanbul Hukuk Mecmuası* 82 (2024), doi:10.26650/mecmua.2024.82.1.001** (free). ⚠️ Gürzumar's full title not recorded.
- **The French versions.** **George Young, *Corps de droit ottoman***, 7 vols (Oxford: Clarendon, 1905–06) — **vol. VII: Code de commerce pp. 55–102, Code maritime pp. 103–154, Procédure commerciale pp. 155–170.** Free on archive.org. **Grégoire Aristarchi, *Législation ottomane***, 7 parts (Constantinople, 1873–88), free on Gallica. ⚠️ Which part carries the Code de commerce was not established.
- **The registers of a commercial court.** **Damascus: 156 volumes, 1883–1929**, at the Markaz al-Wathāʾiq al-Tārīkhiyya — identified by **Mahmoud Ade, *Quaderni Storici* 51/3 (2016), doi:10.1408/86399**. **The only run of commercial-court registers located; unedited.**
- **The studies that read court files**:
  - **Demirci (2019)**, Ankara PhD on the provincial commercial courts — free PDF at APİKAM; points to State Archives series including T.d and T.MAT. ⚠️ First name and title not recorded.
  - **Masheva (2022)**, Böhlau — the commercial-court records of the Bulgarian lands. ⚠️ Title not recorded.
  - **Karsandık Yazıcı (2015)** — Adana court records, **including bankruptcy cases**.
  - **Alkan (2023)** — the *Ceride-i Mehâkim*, the court gazette that printed judgments.
  - **Ağır & Artunç, *Business History Review* (2021)**; **Kenanoğlu (2005)** — the institutional history of the *ticaret mahkemeleri*.

**What this settles for the failure record**: the procedure is in print in two languages and is dominated by bankruptcy; the case files survive at least in Damascus and are read province by province.
