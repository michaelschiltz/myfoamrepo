# Endogenous games rewrite the rules but never the player

**"Game theory treats the game as fixed" describes the textbook, not the field, and a referee will say so. Since about 2007 payoffs, environments and interaction structures have all been endogenised. What none of these models lets change is the identity of the bearer of the payoff. A transition in individuality is precisely the appearance of a new bearer; the one game-theoretic model that represents it loses individual fitness as a defined quantity. The defensible claim is therefore narrower and stronger: fixed-player formalisms can rewrite the regulative rules of a game but cannot constitute a new player. In transition theory the hierarchy "moves from being part of the explanans to being part of the explanandum" (Okasha 2022, 6).**

## The literature that endogenises the game

- *Payoffs.* Worden and Levin 2007, *J. Theor. Biol.* 245: 411–22 (mutation out of the prisoner's dilemma; abstract only); Akçay and Roughgarden 2011, *Proc. R. Soc. B* 278: 2198–2206 (players pay one another to change incentives; abstract only); Stewart and Plotkin 2014, *PNAS* 111: 17558–63 (payoffs and strategies coevolve and cooperation collapses as its rewards are maximised).
- *Environments.* Weitz et al. 2016, *PNAS* 113: E7518–25 (oscillating tragedy of the commons); † Tilman, Plotkin and Akçay 2020, *Nature Communications* 11: 915 (strategy–environment–payoff feedback; cycles and tipping points depend on timescale separation).
- *Game transitions.* † Su et al. 2019, *PNAS* 116: 25398–404 (actions select the next game; transitions lower the cooperation threshold).
- *Structures.* Powers, Penn and Watson 2011, *Evolution* 65: 1527–43; † Ryan, Powers and Watson 2016, *Biology & Philosophy* 31: 59–79 (social niche construction moves the *effective game* across the payoff plane toward harmony, possibly into a transition).
- *Programme and philosophy.* Akçay 2020, *Am. Nat.* 195: 315–30 (abstract only); Barrett and Skyrms 2017, *BJPS* 68: 329–53 (self-assembling games; abstract only).
- *Institutions.* Greif and Laitin 2004, *APSR* 98: 633–52 — quasi-parameters and self-reinforcement give endogenous institutional change inside repeated-game theory. The item that matters most here, since Greif is one of the four names the proposal inverts.

## Three things they still hold fixed

1. **The player set.** Every model above moves payoffs or structures; the bearer is the same lower-level individual throughout. Ryan, Powers and Watson's plane has the individual's payoffs on its axes. Clejan, Congleton and Lerch 2022 (*Evolution*, doi:10.1111/evo.14549; abstract only) do produce a group fitness inside evolutionary game theory, but only when invaders' outcomes become "entangled" and individual fitness ceases to be definable. The formalism records emergence as its own breakdown.
2. **The rules of rewriting.** Su et al.'s transition rule, Tilman et al.'s environmental law and Akçay and Roughgarden's incentive technology are exogenous. Endogenising the game creates a fixed game over games.
3. **Additive, history-independent payoffs.** † Fant et al. 2023 (*Phys. Rev. E* 108: L012401, at 5) note that standard payoffs neither compound multiplicatively nor depend on history, and recover a game from pooling under multiplicative growth only by treating the *entire trajectory* as one iteration. See [[More than the sum requires a non-additive aggregation rule]].

## Regulative and constitutive rules

The proposal's phrase — rewriting the rules of the game and the game itself — is the distinction between regulative rules, which govern an existing activity, and constitutive rules, which create the activity and its positions ("X counts as Y in C"). Hédoin 2015 (*J. Econ. Methodol.* 22: 439–61; abstract only) argues that game theory accommodates constitutive rules only by abandoning pure methodological individualism. The endogenous-game literature endogenises regulative rules. The partnership, the endowment and the corporation are constitutive: each creates a position that counts as a bearer of interests. That also places the project exactly against North's rules of the game: the question is when a new kind of player is constituted.

## The strongest opposing case

West et al. 2015 (*PNAS* 112: 10112–19) and Gardner and Grafen 2009 (*JEB* 22: 659–71) hold inclusive-fitness theory adequate to the transitions: the group becomes a maximising agent once within-group conflict is negligible. West et al. concede that satisfying Hamilton's rule is not sufficient for a transition (10118). The reply is that the alignment conditions are the explanandum, and that under multiplicative growth alignment can come from the dynamics rather than from relatedness ([[Pooling raises the rate and individuation draws the boundary]]).
