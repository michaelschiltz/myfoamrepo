# Diversification runs across bottoms not across partners

Five partners in one ship are one fortune. If the hull goes down, all five lose together, and the probability that the ship is lost is not changed by how many people own it. Pooling within a single venture spreads the loss across purses, so that no partner is ruined by it, but it does not reduce the chance of the loss. The gain of [[Under absorption pooling raises the probability of ruin to the power of the pool]] arrives only when the members' perils strike separately, and in maritime trade that independence was bought **across hulls, routes and seasons**.

## How merchants bought it

- **Slices of many ships.** Venice divided ships into *carati*, English owners held shares in sixty-fourths, the Dutch *partenrederij* and the Hanseatic *Schiffspart* divided hulls among owners who held parts of several [verify standard references for each before citing]. A merchant with a twelfth in each of twelve ships holds the same tonnage as the owner of one, at a fraction of the correlation.
- **Consignments split across ships.** Antonio's practice, and the Geniza merchants', who are reported to have divided shipments among several vessels [verify against Goitein, *A Mediterranean Society* I, before citing].
- **Spreading across years.** A merchant whose whole estate rides on one season has made the year his basket.

## Antonio's three lines are three axes

> My ventures are not in one bottom trusted,
> Nor to one place; nor is my whole estate
> Upon the fortune of this present year.
>
> — Shakespeare, *The Merchant of Venice* I.i, about lines 42–44 (numbering varies by edition)

*Not in one bottom*: independence across hulls. *Nor to one place*: independence across routes and the perils peculiar to them. *Nor … this present year*: independence across time, the axis permanent capital later institutionalised ([[Permanent capital pools across voyages as well as across partners]]). The play then runs the failure case: in Act III every venture is reported lost at once — the correlated catastrophe the speech assumed away — and Antonio stands at a barrier written into his own contract, the forfeit of Shylock's bond. In Act V three argosies turn out to have come safely home. The plot needs both the correlation and the barrier to make diversification fail; neither alone would do it.

## The convoy trades one correlation for another

Sailing in convoy, as the Spanish *flotas* did, reduced perils that struck ships separately — corsairs, single-ship wrecks on unfamiliar coasts — and concentrated those that strike a fleet together: a hurricane, a squadron, a war. Whether the trade-off lowered or raised the merchant's ruin probability depends on which peril dominated the route and season. **For the coding: record for each deed whether the venture sailed alone or in a fleet**, because the same contract form carries a different ρ in the two cases.

## The counter-proverb

Mark Twain inverted the rule in *Pudd'nhead Wilson* (1894): "Put all your eggs in the one basket and—WATCH THAT BASKET." Concentration with close monitoring is a real alternative, and it is the one [[Institutions substitute state-verification for outcome-inference as ruin exposure rises]] predicts where agents can be watched: it lowers the variance of the single venture instead of averaging it with others. The two strategies should sort on whether the peril is controllable by conduct (watch the basket) or not (spread the baskets).
