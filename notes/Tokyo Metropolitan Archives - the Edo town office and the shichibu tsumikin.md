# Tokyo Metropolitan Archives — the Edo town office and the *shichibu tsumikin*

東京都公文書館. <https://www.soumu.metro.tokyo.lg.jp/01soumu-archives/>

**The records of the Edo *machi kaisho* passed to Tokyo and are here.** The archive's own study, ***Toshi kiyō* 7: *Shichibu tsumikin*** (January 1960), traces the institution from its creation through its Meiji successors.

## The fund

**Created in 1791 under Matsudaira Sadanobu's Kansei reform.** The town expenses (*machi nyūyō*) borne by Edo's landholders were cut, and **seven-tenths of the saving was paid into the Edo town office (*machi kaisho*)** at Mukōyanagiwara, with a granary, for famine and emergency relief and rice purchase. After the Restoration the fund passed through the Eizen Kaigisho and the Tokyo Kaigisho, became the **common fund (*kyōyūkin*) of Tokyo Prefecture**, and financed public works and welfare.

## Why it is in this survey

**It is a compulsory, collective, funded institution against a hazard, and its records are in one public archive.** That combination is a cell the census has marked as hard to fill: the Croatian pass counted three compulsory collective-good institutions **with no contributory fund** — `averia_pool`, the Venetian *muda*, the Ragusan quarantine. **The *shichibu tsumikin* is compulsory, collective, and has a fund.** Parked as a candidate in `HistorEE_codebooks/logbook/2. data inclusion and exclusion.md`, 2026-09-11 (vi).

**Successor custody by the successor polity**: the fund and its papers passed from the shogunal town office to the prefecture that inherited the city. **The institution outlived its creator and its records went with the money.**

⚠️ Series, extent, imaging and access terms for the *machi kaisho* records not established; only the 1960 study was read, and that through a summary.
