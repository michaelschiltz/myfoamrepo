# Silk Road documents — one site's archive in twelve institutions

**The structural fact: the Inner Asian documentary record was removed by archaeological expeditions between 1900 and 1914 and divided among the expeditions' home institutions, so a single deposit — the Dunhuang library cave — is now catalogued in London, Paris, Beijing, St Petersburg, Kyoto and half a dozen other cities.**

## The division, by expedition

- **Aurel Stein**, for Britain and British India: **over 45,000 manuscripts and printed items** now at the [[British Library - the Stein collection]], within almost 50,000 Eastern Silk Road items, **in more than twenty languages and scripts**; antiquities divided with the British Museum and with India.
- **Paul Pelliot**, for France: **some 14,000 items** at the Bibliothèque nationale de France. ⚠️ Figure from IDP's summary, not from the BnF.
- **The four German Turfan expeditions**, 1902–1914: **about 40,000 fragments** — [[Berlin - the Turfan collection]].
- **Sergey Oldenburg and Pyotr Kozlov**, for Russia: **20,000 or more fragments** from Dunhuang, and the Khara-Khoto material — [[Institute of Oriental Manuscripts - St Petersburg]].
- **Ōtani Kōzui**, for no state at all: a Kyoto abbot's private expeditions, whose finds scattered further than anyone's — [[The Otani dispersal - Kyoto, Tokyo, Lushun, Seoul]].
- **What stayed**: **about 16,000 items at the National Library of China**, and the cave site itself — [[National Library of China and the Dunhuang Academy]].

## What it does to the evidence

**1. Provenance is an expedition, not a fonds.** A shelfmark records who bought or took the item, not where it sat in the cave or the house. **The order of the deposit is gone**, exactly as at the Geniza — see [[Cairo Geniza - the deposit and its survival rule]] — and for the same reason: the material left as portable objects bought or carried away, in Ōtani's case **369 manuscript scrolls purchased from Wang Yuanlu for 400 taels**.

**2. The counting unit is the fragment, and fragments of one document are in different countries.** Any statement of the form "there are N documents of type X" is a statement about a catalogue, not about a corpus.

**3. Reunification happened digitally and early.** [[IDP - the digital reunification of a dispersed archive]] was founded in 1994 to put the dispersed collections in one database. **Two of this survey's dispersed deposits have been digitally reunified — the Geniza and this one — and both were reunified because they were dispersed.**

**4. The dispersal is also a political claim.** The material is in the institutions of the states whose nationals removed it, and the sites are in China, Tajikistan, Uzbekistan and Afghanistan. **Nothing in this survey is more exposed to restitution argument**, and the collaborative digitisation described in the notes below is partly an answer to it. ⚠️ An observation; no restitution literature was read.
