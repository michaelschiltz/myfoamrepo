# National Archives of Ireland, PRONI, and the Four Courts fire

**The fifth catastrophic loss in the survey, and with Cologne one of the two that are being actively repaired.**

## What was lost

In **June 1922**, during the Civil War, the Record Treasury of the **Public Record Office of Ireland** at the Four Courts was destroyed by fire and explosion — centuries of Irish administrative, legal and ecclesiastical record, consumed together.

## The reconstruction

See [[The Virtual Record Treasury and reconstruction as method]], which is where the interesting part is.

## The two surviving systems

The **National Archives of Ireland** in Dublin and the **Public Record Office of Northern Ireland** hold what there is, and PRONI's holdings were largely unaffected by 1922 — a partition of custody that predates and outlasts the fire.

⚠️ What survives for this project's questions — Irish merchant, shipping and partnership records — is unestablished, and the presumption must be that it is thin.

## The point that generalises

Ireland's commercial record for the early modern period is **additionally** in London, because the administration was. A researcher who treats the fire as having destroyed the Irish record will be wrong twice: some of it was never in Dublin, and some of what was is recoverable. **Both errors point the same way — toward under-counting Ireland**, which matters because under-counting looks like evidence.
