# Where kō rules name a bearer beyond the drawer's security, it follows the drafting regime

Across thirty-six blind-coded rulebooks of 1915–1936, most stop at the drawer's own guarantors or pledge. **The minority that name a further bearer cluster by who drafted them**, not by any feature the census codes: a regional device in one prefecture, a licensing template in two others.

## The clusters

- **Fukuoka, 1936: the defaulter's own group.** Five of the seven Fukuoka kō in the open half of the Nōrinshō volume put the arrears on his 部落, クビリ, 小組合 or co-obligors from his 組 ([[Who answers for a ko member's default was drafted not given]]), and the blind-coded kō 7 (宗像郡吉武村) does the same: 一、時間內掛金不足ノ際ハ講クビリヨリ立替ヘルコト, with three named クビリ fixed in the rule itself.
- **Tottori and Shimane, 1915: the managers, without limit.** In the Finance Ministry's examples ([[Okurasho 1915 - a Finance Ministry survey of mujin with eleven rulebooks]]) two 鳥取縣 model rulebooks and one 島根縣 rulebook share one pattern, as quoted in the re-test coding notes: 管理人 who 本講ノ事務ヲ整理シ債權債務ニ關シ無限ノ責任ヲ有ス, who pay each pot 講金ノ集否ニ拘ラス, and a separate head for 認可取消 caused by the 管理人's fault. The Shimane rule says it outright: 空掛人退講シ掛金ヲ爲サヽルトキハ管理人之ヲ負擔シ他ノ講員ニハ更ニ損失ヲ掛ケサルモノトス. The 認可 clause marks a licensing regime, and the shared wording marks a template. **They are one witness, not three.**
- **Elsewhere, single drafts.** Officers guaranteeing pots in Aomori (講長及世話人ハ懸金ノ遲怠者ノ有無ニ不拘糶取金支拂ヲ保證), the 世話人 guaranteeing the convener in a Shizuoka kō of 1929, all members bearing a shortfall in an Akita kō of the 1936 volume and in a Saitama kō of 1912, the last two at low confidence. None recurs.

## Why it matters

- **For the census.** A characteristic recording the residual bearer would encode the drafting regime more than the institution, which is part of why `RD-test` could not pass a test built from the existing columns ([[A residual-bearer characteristic failed two blind tests for want of cases not for redundancy]]).
- **For the book.** Who absorbs the tail of a member's default — his neighbours by residence, the officers without limit, or nobody named — is set by drafting practice and regulation, not by the kō as such. The licensing template moves the residual onto named individuals with unlimited liability, which is the opposite of entity shielding: the fund is protected by making its managers personally ruinable.

## Where this is soft

- **Few cases, two selectors, one country, 1915–1936.** Three clusters, two of them from small numbers of rulebooks.
- **The regime is inferred from the text.** The Tottori and Shimane rules show a 認可 clause and shared wording; the licensing ordinances themselves have not been read ⚠️ verify against the prefectural 取締規則.
- **Selection.** Every rulebook here was chosen by a ministry, and a ministry may have chosen rules that matched its regulations ([[Refusals are observations of the filter not inferences from survivors]]).
