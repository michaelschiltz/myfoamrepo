# The isqa's bifurcation belongs to the Sasanian substrate question

The *pelga milveh u-pelga pikkadon* formula is attributed to **Rav Idi bar Avin**, a fourth-century Babylonian amora. Same polity, same century, same substrate the project already invokes for the *qirāḍ* in [[The qirad's antecedents are Sasanian not Sogdian]] — and that note does not use it.

The opening is concrete rather than gestural, on three grounds.

**The rabbinic leg is already inside the named expert's programme.** Macuch's stated research agenda is pre-Islamic Iranian law and its impact on *Rabbinic*, Eastern Christian and Islamic law in formation. The *qirāḍ* note approaches her for the Islamic leg only. The rabbinic leg is the one she has published toward, so it is the cheaper ask and the stronger one.

**The method exists and has been run on this corpus.** Irano-Talmudica — Elman, Secunda and successors — has spent two decades reading Bavli sugyot against Sasanian legal and religious material. The specific question here is narrow and answerable: does the loan/bailment bifurcation correspond to a Sasanian partnership category, and does a Middle Persian technical term survive in the sugya, of the kind Macuch (1989) traced between Ishoʿbokht's Syriac code and the Sasanian law book? That is a *Leitfehler* test with a defined corpus, not a gesture at secondary literature.

**The corpus is better preserved than the Middle Persian originals.** This is the strategic point. The Sasanian commercial law the *qirāḍ* argument needs survives mostly at one remove, through East Syriac transmission. The Bavli survives whole, is exhaustively indexed, and is commentated continuously from the geonim forward. If a partnership *Leitfehler* is findable anywhere, it is findable there first and most cheaply. **The rabbinic route may be the least-cost place to run the philological test the qirāḍ genealogy actually turns on** — which is a work-package argument, not merely a scholarly one.

## Evidentiary status — unaffirmed and unchecked

No expert has been approached on this and **no literature check has been run**. It is entirely possible the sugya has already been read against Sasanian partnership law and the result was negative; that would itself be worth knowing. Do not present this as a novel observation until the Irano-Talmudica bibliography has been swept. Treat the note as a lead.

The structural caution from the parent note carries over: even if the substrate claim holds for both forms, it establishes common descent within the western family, which the project needs only in the weak form — [[The western family needs only plausible common descent not proven genealogy]].
