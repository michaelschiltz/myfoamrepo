# The double refusal of the sea loan is evidence about the filter not about independence

I floated the double refusal of the sea loan as a candidate independent-recurrence datum of the *Leitfehler* type — the same form refused in two traditions, with wholly divergent stated rationales. That framing is wrong, and this vault's own notes show why. The correction is worth more than the original claim.

## Why the independence reading fails

[[Refusals are observations of the filter not inferences from survivors]] makes the refusal informative on one condition: that **availability** be established, so that an absence is a decision and not an evidence-gap. It states, correctly, that the sea-loan case is strong precisely because Mediterranean entanglement establishes availability.

But availability-through-contact is exactly what destroys independence in the sense of [[Galton's problem]]. The co-refusal cannot be both informative *as a refusal*, which requires contact-established availability, and evidence of *independent arrival*, which requires the absence of contact effects. Asking it to do both is having it both ways.

## What the case actually establishes

Reframe it as a natural experiment and it becomes stronger, not weaker.

Both traditions inherited the same object — the *foenus nauticum*, permitted in Roman law as an exception to the interest ceiling [**verify**]. Two doctrinal machineries with no common apparatus were then applied to it: *riba* and *gharar*, with breach-of-trust reasoning behind them, on one side; canon-law usury doctrine and the decretal *Naviganti* on the other. Both returned the same verdict. Both then produced *different* workarounds, which is the point [[Naviganti - Latin Christendom rejected the sea loan too]] already makes — Latin Europe unbundled the composite into the bill of exchange and free-standing marine insurance, while the Islamic world met the same function through the profit-and-loss partnership and never unbundled at all.

Hold the object constant, vary the doctrine, observe the same selective outcome. **The divergence of the rationales is what makes this evidence about the filter rather than about doctrine.** Had the reasoning agreed, one would suspect a shared juristic inheritance doing the work. Because it diverges completely, whatever the two traditions were responding to lies outside both doctrinal systems — which is precisely the claim Objective 1 needs to make good.

## And this is why it survives Galton entirely

Not by establishing independence, but by not requiring it. The selection question is separable from the origination question: see [[The theory predicts recurrence not independent origins]]. Even granting full contact and a common Roman inheritance, a co-refusal is a datum about the *filter acting*, and the filter is what the project is trying to characterise. Transmission of the object is not merely compatible with the argument here; it is the premise that makes the observation possible.

That makes this the most robust comparative evidence in the project — more robust than the East Asia/West seam, because it does not depend on an independence claim that the Sasanian material cannot yet support. Worth weighing whether it belongs in Part 1 alongside the *ortoq*, which carries a heavier evidentiary burden for a weaker conclusion.

## Verify

- The Roman status of the *foenus nauticum* as an exception to the interest ceiling, and whether both traditions can be shown to have received it as such rather than encountering the form independently in practice. The claim of a *common inherited object* is doing real work here and should not be asserted casually.
- The *Naviganti* citation itself still carries a verify flag on the vault's Naviganti note.
