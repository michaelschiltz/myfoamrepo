# The Khalili Bactrian documents — a private collection as the archive

**More than 150 documents in Bactrian, of the fourth to eighth centuries, came to light in the 1990s and are in the Khalili Collection, London** — a private collection. Edited by **Nicholas Sims-Williams**, *Bactrian Documents from Northern Afghanistan*, in three parts (legal and economic documents; letters and Buddhist texts; plates), 2000–2012.

**They are contracts.** The Invisible East inventory of about thirty of them lists types including **"contract for the purchase of an estate", "contract for a loan of money"** and undertakings between parties, dated between **629 and 771/772 CE**.

## Why the custody matters

**The documents surfaced on the market during the Afghan wars and their findspot is not recorded.** ⚠️ **The Khalili Collection's page read for this note does not say how they were acquired**, and this survey did not research the provenance debate. **What is certain is the shape: an unexcavated corpus, privately held, published by one scholar.**

**That is the acquisitions pattern of [[MOC - Acquisitions and the antiquarian trade]] in its strongest form** — stronger than the Geniza dealers, the Huizhou market or the Shanxi collector, because **here the private collection is the archive of record** and there is no public counterpart.

**For the census**: a body of dated Central Asian contracts in a language with one principal editor. **Any use of them is a use of Sims-Williams's readings**, which is the dependence problem in its purest form — one witness, however good.
