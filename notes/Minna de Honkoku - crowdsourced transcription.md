# Minna de Honkoku — crowdsourced transcription

みんなで翻刻, <https://honkoku.org/>. Run by the **National Museum of Japanese History (Rekihaku)**, the **Earthquake Research Institute of the University of Tokyo** and the **Kyoto University historical-earthquake research group**, where it began in 2017.

**More than 10,800 participants and more than 48 million characters transcribed.** Rebuilt in 2019 and again in August 2025; editing supported by AI *kuzushiji* recognition from Toppan and CODH.

## Why it matters

**It is Japan's one large layer 3 made from manuscripts by people rather than by a model**, and it began with **historical earthquake records** — so its first corpus was chosen by seismology, not by history. **Pattern 1 again: legibility follows the funder's question.** It has since widened to other collections. ⚠️ The current mix of collections and the licence of the transcriptions not captured.

**For the census: a place to put a *kō* rulebook.** A project that needs a handful of village documents transcribed, checked and published could do it here in public. **The trade-off is the same as for any crowd layer**: speed and openness against uneven reading of numerals and amounts — the fields [[Kuzushiji OCR - the NDL and CODH stack]] identifies as highest-risk.
