# `C 66` — the Patent Rolls and the charters

At [[The National Archives - Kew]]. ⚠️ The series reference is carried from the survey's first pass and should be confirmed against Discovery.

## What is here

Letters patent under the great seal, enrolled — **including the charters of incorporation** by which English trading companies were created. The `chartered_corporation_england` row is about bodies constituted by exactly these instruments, and Harris quotes the operative clauses from acts and charters.

## Why the enrolment matters more than the charter

**An enrolled charter is a dated, official text of the constitutive act**, and the roll is serial: charters can be read against one another over time rather than one at a time from a printed edition.

That is the difference between describing a form and observing its drafting change — the same distinction that made the Antwerp phase split possible in [[The Antwerp phases separate on two cells and the boundary is textual]]. **A serial constitutional record is what lets a boundary be textual rather than asserted.**

⚠️ Whether the calendared *Calendars of the Patent Rolls* cover the relevant period and are full-text online is the practical question. **If they are, the description layer is at layer 3 for a series whose originals are not imaged** — the pattern already met at [[Archives departementales du Nord - the Chambre des comptes de Lille]], and the cheapest route in.
