# Bernoulli split Sempronius's cargo by the logarithm and Roy by the probability of disaster

The merchant's rule of spreading cargo across ships was formalised twice, and the two formalisations fall on the two limbs of the project's apparatus.

**Bernoulli 1738.** The *Specimen* includes a merchant, Sempronius, with goods at home and goods abroad that can only come by sea, on a route where about one ship in ten is lost. Bernoulli computes his position under logarithmic utility with the cargo in one ship and divided between two, and concludes that goods exposed to danger are better divided into several portions than risked together [verify section numbers, the figures and the wording in the Sommer translation before citing]. Under multiplicative wealth the logarithm is the ergodicity transform, so Bernoulli's diversification argument is the **variance limb**: splitting raises the expected logarithm, which is the time-average growth rate. It is the earliest formal statement of Antonio's rule, a century and a half after Antonio.

**Roy 1952.** "Safety First and the Holding of Assets" (*Econometrica* 20 (3): 431–49) has the investor minimise the probability that wealth falls below a disaster level, not maximise an expected utility. That is the **ruin limb**: the criterion is $p$, and spreading lowers it. Markowitz's "Portfolio Selection" appeared the same year and formalised the rule as variance reduction for a given mean. [Verify page ranges.] Scott's peasants reach Roy through Roumasset ([[Scott 1976 on the subsistence ethic as safety-first]]).

## Why the pair matters

- **It answers the "EE is Bernoulli" charge on the project's own material.** Bernoulli's logarithm explains why splitting cargo pays when fortunes compound. It does not explain why splitting pays when gains add and a loss is final; that needs Roy's criterion, and the vault's [[Under absorption pooling raises the probability of ruin to the power of the pool]] shows the two can be obtained from one act of pooling. Bernoulli covered one limb, and his successors in economics took the other as a separate criterion rather than a consequence of the dynamic.
- **It is a historiographical opening.** Neither of the theme-issue papers on Bernoulli engages the Sempronius passage ([[Peters and Adamou 2026 on the time interpretation of expected utility theory]]). A paper setting Antonio, Sempronius, Roy and the notarial record side by side would place the project's argument inside the history of the practice it explains.
