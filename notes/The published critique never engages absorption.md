# The published critique never engages absorption

All three published objections to ergodicity economics attack the *variance* limb and none reaches the *ruin* limb. [[Doctor Wakker and Wang 2020 on the ergodicity problem]], [[Toda 2023 on ergodicity economics as pseudoscience]] and [[Ford and Kay 2023 on growth-optimal decision making]] — with the eleven-author reply to the last — argue throughout about the Jensen gap between time-average and ensemble-average growth. Absorption, invariant sets and the decomposition of a state space appear in none of them.

**But the interesting explanation is not that the critics missed something.** It is that nothing in the models any of them discusses has an absorbing state at all.

## The dispute is conducted over processes that never absorb

Geometric Brownian motion never reaches zero. The Peters coin toss never reaches zero. Wealth approaches it asymptotically and the state space is never decomposed; the invariant σ-algebra stays trivial throughout. So the critics are not neglecting a live feature of the models under discussion — the feature is not there.

They do discuss ruinous *outcomes*, and at length. Ford and Kay treat the coin toss extensively, quote Kelly's own qualification that the answer "would depend on the relative values (to the gambler) of being broke or possessing a fortune," and gesture at survival — "in general the sort of people who find such gambles attractive have not survived." Hulme et al. state that most probable wealth goes to zero while expected wealth diverges. That is **asymptotic impoverishment**, and it is not absorption. In a process where wealth approaches zero without reaching it, the only available source of time/ensemble divergence is the non-linearity of the observable, which is why the entire dispute is a Jensen dispute.

**The absence in the critical literature is downstream of an absence in the defended literature.** Peters led with geometric Brownian motion; the critics accepted the terrain. Neither side has been arguing about processes that terminate.

Not quite neither side, however — see [[Galton-Watson is the extinction branch Peters named and did not develop]], where Peters names extinction as "a different kind of ergodicity breaking," credits it to 1875, and then develops the other kind for the next thirteen decades of his own account.

## Why this reframing is stronger than the bibliometric one

Reading the silence as an oversight makes the project's position look lucky, or evasive: *our limb happens to be undefended*. The accurate statement is different and better.

**The published dispute concerns a class of processes without absorbing states. The project works with a class that has them, because institutions die, and dying is not a limit.** A *commenda* that loses the ship is not asymptotically poor. A house whose lineage fails does not converge on failure. The barrier argument of [[An absorbing barrier breaks ergodicity by construction]] is therefore not an unanswered claim within the dispute; it is a claim about a different problem, and its ergodicity failure is obtained by definition rather than by aggregation.

That is also why the instruction already recorded at that note — **where the historical argument can be run off ruin rather than off variance, it should be** — now has an external warrant. Running off variance puts the argument on the terrain where the framework has been publicly and partly successfully attacked, and where defending it costs the concessions catalogued at [[The growth-rate criterion is asymptotic and the horizon comparison cuts both ways]] and [[Jensen supplies the gap but only the dynamic privileges the logarithm]].

## The three cautions

**The barrier claim carries its own qualification.** Stated at [[An absorbing barrier breaks ergodicity by construction]]: a single absorbing state gives a unique invariant law, so the decomposition claim needs at least two absorbing states, or must be stated over the killed process or the path-space shift. A critic who reaches the barrier argument will start there, and the branching case supplies the clean two-set version.

**Absence of engagement is not endorsement, and the silence is explained.** All three critiques address a decision theory for individuals facing gambles, where absorption is a boundary case rather than the central object. They had no reason to address the project's setting. Reading a critique of one thing as a warrant for another would be a straightforward fallacy.

**The pattern should prompt suspicion of our own framing.** The selective limb escapes all three critiques — see [[The selective claim is falsifiable where the prescriptive claim is not]] and [[EE cannot rank across dynamics and the comparative project must not need it to]]. Either that is a real feature of the position or the selective framing is doing too much work. And it raises the sharpest question available against the project, which is not any of the published ones: [[Absorption without ergodicity economics is just ruin theory]].
