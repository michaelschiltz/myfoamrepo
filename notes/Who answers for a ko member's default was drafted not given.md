# Who answers for a kō member's default was drafted, not given

In twenty-one Shōwa rulebooks, the party who bears a winner's default once his own security fails is **written into the rules, and the rules differ**. Some put it on all members, some on the defaulter's own small group, some on the officers, and most say nothing. Two of the alternatives appear in one ministry class, which is the comparison that matters.

## The evidence

## What it shows

- **The first layer is everywhere the winner's own security**, except in 36. That layer is what `RB3` already codes. Most rules make an officer the formal creditor (取締人, 世話人, 總代, 講長, 藏元), which is the trustee holding of [[Yui 1934 - the law of ko and who bears a member's default]].
- **The residual layer is drafted in eleven of twenty-one, and the drafts take four forms.** Some name all members jointly (11), some the defaulter's own group (19, 28, 29, 36, and 30 and 41 through co-obligors), some the officers (34, 30), and one leaves it to the members' later decision (42). Kō 8 states an officer's liability only for handling contrary to the rules, which is Yui's negligence default written out.
- **Two forms meet in one ministry class.** Kō 34 and 36 are both organisation-run. In 34 the 世話人 carry absolute responsibility and winners give guarantors. In 36 payouts are unsecured and the small group answers. These are opposite allocations of the same hazard, the members' failure.
- **Group liability clusters in Fukuoka**: five of the seven Fukuoka kō read (19, 28, 29, 30, 36), and the ministry's glosses point to a sixth among the reserve. Elsewhere only Yamaguchi's 41 uses it. One editor chose all four prefectures, so this may be a regional drafting practice or the selection. Kō 10 and 36 share a village and do not share the device.
- **The unwon member's default is a different question.** The answers here are forfeiture to the fund, expulsion with a refund at the end, sale of his claim, or a fine paid to that round's winner. They move the loss in different directions from the winner's default, and must not share a characteristic with it.

## What it does not show

- **Silence is not the default.** Ten rules state no residual bearer. Under the 1934 case law their members would answer as co-owners, but that is post-1898 doctrine, and a silent rule codes `.NR`.
- **The read half is open, not blind.** These are the cases the question was found on. A characteristic fixed from them must be tested on the reserve, which is the reason the reserve exists.
- **It is Shōwa practice under regulation**, chosen by a regulator. It says nothing directly about a Tokugawa 仕法書.
