# Archivo de la Corona de Aragon — Barcelona

<https://www.cultura.gob.es/cultura/areas/archivos/mc/archivos/aca/presentacion.html> · catalogue via [[PARES - the access layer for the Spanish state archives]]

**The medieval Mediterranean archive of the Spanish state system**, and the one that reaches back far enough to matter for the *comanda*. Where the Castilian archives begin to be useful in the sixteenth century, the ACA is continuous from the twelfth.

## The sections that matter

**`REAL CANCILLERIA`** — the chancery registers of the Crown of Aragon. Privileges granted to merchant bodies, the *Consolat de Mar*, and the maritime ordinances.

**`REAL PATRIMONIO / MAESTRE RACIONAL`** — the royal accounting office. Volumes run from the fourteenth century and record the Crown's commercial and fiscal dealings in detail, including with merchant syndicates.

**`REAL AUDIENCIA`** and **`CONSEJO DE ARAGON`** — litigation and conciliar deliberation.

**`GENERALITAT DE CATALUNYA`** — the corporate record of the Catalan estates' standing body, itself an institution with perpetual succession and its own fisc.

## Why it matters for the commenda strand

The Catalan *comanda* is one of the western family's clearest cases, and the coding in `loss_mitigation_forms` already separates unilateral from bilateral variants — [[Unilateral and bilateral commenda collapse on profit and separate on loss]] and [[Ragusan collegantia is unilateral and Venetian collegantia is bilateral]]. The Catalan evidence is the third leg of that comparison and is currently thinner in the vault than the Venetian and Ragusan.

**But the instruments are not here.** The ACA holds the Crown's record; the *comandes* themselves are notarial and are at [[Arxiu Historic de Protocols de Barcelona]]. Use the ACA for the legal and institutional frame — charters, ordinances, jurisdiction — and the protocols for the clauses. Confusing the two wastes a trip.
