# Sogdian commercial documents — thin but not empty

**The vault records, from specialist advice, that the Sogdian network's "own commercial-legal record is too thin, and too Near-Eastern-inflected, to stand as an independent legal tradition" — [[The ortoq is the discriminating instrument]]. This pass asked what the record actually is.**

## What survives

- **The Ancient Letters**, found by Stein in 1907 in a watchtower west of Dunhuang, written about **312–313 CE**, at the British Library (**Or. 8212/92, Or. 8212/95**). Commercial correspondence of Sogdian merchants in China: goods — gold, silver, camphor, pepper, musk, wheat, silk, cloth — **money left on deposit, a father's property, instructions to partners**; Letter II reads in part as a will. **Two of the letters were written by women.**
- **The Mount Mugh archive**, early eighth century: **seventy-four Sogdian documents, one Arabic, one in Turkic runic script and several Chinese**, at the IOM in St Petersburg — [[Institute of Oriental Manuscripts - St Petersburg]]. It includes **legal documents** — a marriage contract and a slave-sale contract are the famous ones — **letters and economic records**, among them a commercial letter (A-1) listing textiles and leather goods dispatched by the merchant Āpānak. **Edited by Freiman, Livshits, Bogolyubov and Smirnova, and being re-edited now**: a 2026 reinterpretation of A-1's commodity terms replaced an earlier reading of them as gemstones.
- **Scattered Sogdian documents from Khotan, Turfan and Dunhuang**, inventoried by [[Invisible East - an open corpus for the eastern Islamicate documents]] across the BnF, the British Library, the National Library of China, Renmin University's museum, the Afrasiab Museum in Samarkand and the IOM.

## The assessment this pass reaches

**"Thin" is right and "empty" is wrong, and the difference matters for one specific use.** **There is no Sogdian partnership contract corpus** — nothing here is a body of commercial instruments comparable to the Geniza's or the Uyghur contracts'. **What there is, is a handful of letters and legal documents, dispersed, edited by a small number of scholars, and still being re-read.**

**So the vault's claim survives**, and with a sharper form: **the Sogdian record cannot bear a legal-tradition comparison, and it can bear questions about agency, deposit and family capital** — which is what the Ancient Letters are about. ⚠️ Read entirely through museum and project summaries; **no edition consulted**, and the readings quoted are contested in the way the A-1 revision shows.
