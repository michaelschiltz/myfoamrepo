# Tōyō Bunko and the Digital Silk Road — the expedition library, not the finds

**The Tōyō Bunko's Silk Road contribution is a library, not a manuscript collection**, and it is free.

**The Digital Archive of Tōyō Bunko Rare Books**, run by the **National Institute of Informatics with the Tōyō Bunko** as part of the **Digital Silk Road Project**: **245 rare books by 82 authors, 72,591 pages**, digitised cover to cover, with full-text, page, image and map search. The breakdown is **163 expedition records, 44 academic studies, 18 maps, 12 volumes of photographs, 7 historical records** — **Stein's own reports among them.**

## The honest assessment

**Useful and not an archive.** What is here is **the published output of the expeditions that removed the documents**, not the documents. For this project its uses are narrow and real:

- **Provenance.** An expedition report is where a fragment's findspot is recorded, and findspot is what the shelfmark lost — see [[Silk Road documents - one site's archive in twelve institutions]].
- **Free full text** of works that are otherwise in closed stacks, searchable, in a Japanese institution.

**What the Tōyō Bunko does *not* hold, on this pass's evidence, is Inner Asian manuscript material of the kind at Ryukoku or Berlin.** ⚠️ Its manuscript and rubbing holdings were not surveyed; **this note establishes what the digital archive contains and nothing more.**
