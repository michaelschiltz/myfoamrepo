# The Bodmerei shield was a default rule and Amsterdam contracted out of it

Schuster makes limited liability the *eigentliche Besonderheit* of the Bodmerei. The contract creates
*lediglich eine auf das Pfand beschränkte, dingliche Haftung des Schuldners*, and *an das persönliche
Vermögen des Reeders kann sich der Gläubiger grundsätzlich nicht halten* — *reine Sachhaftung*. That
is precisely what separates it from the ancient sea loan, where the debtor *haftete stets
unbeschränkt mit seinem gesamten Vermögen* and the pledge secures without constituting: *konstitutiven
Charakter hatte es nicht*.

Stated that way it is a rule of the institution, and a rule of the institution predicts nothing about
what any particular contract says. Schuster's own footnote supplies the qualification and it is easy
to read past: the debtor may discharge himself by ceding the pledge — *sich durch Abtretung des
Pfandes von jeder weiteren Haftung zu befreien*, a right still in the ALR II 8 §2427 — but only
*wenn nicht besondere vertragliche Vereinbarungen hinzutraten*. **The shield is dispositive.** The
Statutes of Marseille of 1253/55 already have personal liability arising by special agreement.

Amsterdam takes that escape as routine. Of thirty-six notarial *contracten van bodemerij* from
1601–21, eight bind the borrower's person and whole estate, present and future, in the same breath as
the sea-risk clause; one adds three sureties bound *als principaele* who renounce *beneficium
excussionis et ordinis*, which is empty drafting if liability is in rem; and the protests execute
against *die personen van den voorn. schipper ende sijn reders ende alle haere andere goederen*.

A second and independent clause type points the same way, and it is what lifts this above a recourse
habit. Rossi's pair for settling a marine loss is **average** — compensation for the actual damage —
against **abandonment**, in which the party cedes the thing-at-risk for the full sum and so avoids
the liquidation of damages. Abandonment is the shape of Schuster's *Abtretung des Pfandes*. Six
Amsterdam acts do the opposite: the lender takes *pro rato van 't suver gesalveerde*, a proportional
share of the net salvage. Average, not abandonment.

So the divergence runs along two axes at once. **Amsterdam drafting converts an in-rem,
all-or-nothing settlement into a personal, proportional one.**

The generalisable point is about what a differentia is evidence of. A rule stated as the essence of
an institution is a claim about its **default**; a corpus of instruments is evidence about what
parties **did with that default**. Here the two disagree without either being wrong, and both
available shortcuts destroy the finding — reading the contracts as falsifying Schuster, or reading
the statute as describing the contracts. What the disagreement actually shows is that the default was
worth contracting around, which is a fact about neither the rule nor the practice alone. The census
holds it as a pair: `LS3=0` for the Bodmerei as such, `LS3=1` for Amsterdam.

Rossi supplies the standing objection and it should not be argued away: we may be aware of a practice
and be unsure *whether that was the customary position or just an isolated episode*. Eight acts in
thirty-six state the general charge, and the notary abbreviates the remainder to *Hieronder
verbindende etc.* — so eight is a floor rather than a count. The protests are what carry the weight,
because enforcement is not formulary.
