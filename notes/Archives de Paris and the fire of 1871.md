# Archives de Paris and the fire of 1871

<https://archives.paris.fr/>

**The third catastrophic loss in the survey, and the one with the most instructive aftermath.**

## What happened

On **24 May 1871**, during the suppression of the Commune, the Hôtel de Ville burned and with it the archives of the city of Paris. The *état civil* of Paris before 1860 was destroyed, together with the municipal record it sat in.

## Why it is different from Ypres and Naples

Because of what came next. The city undertook a **reconstruction** — the *état civil reconstitué (XVIe siècle – 1859)* — assembled after the fact from surviving copies, notarial records, family papers and printed sources, and it is now published on the archive's own site.

**A reconstructed archive is a much more dangerous object than an absent one.** [[Archief Ieper - one charter and five accounts]] leaves a hole that announces itself; a reconstitution leaves a *usable series with an invisible selection rule*. What survived the fire indirectly was whatever had been copied elsewhere — and what gets copied is not random: it is the propertied, the litigious, and the families who later cared enough to supply documents.

**Any count taken from the reconstituted series measures the reconstitution.** That is the sharpest available instance of [[Refusals are observations of the filter not inferences from survivors]], because here the filter was applied twice — once by the fire, once by the reconstructors — and only the first is visible.

## What this costs the project

The Parisian *juridiction consulaire* and the municipal commercial record are in the destroyed portion. ⚠️ Establish what of the merchant court's archive survives, if any — see [[Juridictions consulaires - the merchant courts]]. The [[Minutier central des notaires de Paris]] is **not** affected: the notarial minutes were held separately and survive, which is why Paris still has an instrument archive at all.
