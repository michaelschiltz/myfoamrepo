# Perpetuity splits into adaptation and petrification

Perpetuity is not one property. Two institutions can both aim at permanence and reach it by opposite mechanisms, and the mechanism decides how the corpus behaves under a changing environment.

The corporation persists **by re-deciding**. A self-governing body can repurpose its rules, assets, and activities by collective decision — which is why the *naties* survived six centuries and the abolition of their own legal category (see [[The naties are a six-century Lindy institution]]). The *waqf* persists **by freezing**: inalienability plus perpetually binding founder-conditions immobilize the corpus, to the point that jurists had to invent escape valves (*istibdāl*, the cash-*waqf*) to relax it, and Kuran reads that rigidity as a structural drag (see [[Kuran's brake is a refusal of immortal entities]]).

Under regime change the adaptive corpus is the more time-robust even though both were built for permanence — a robust-yet-fragile versus antifragile contrast. This is the ergodic gloss on perpetuity: petrification optimizes for a fixed environment and cannot re-optimize; adaptation re-optimizes across states. Perpetuity-by-freezing is a bet that the world holds still.

Guard the inverse error, as against Kuran: the claim is not that the *waqf* is worse, only that it purchased permanence by a different mechanism at a stated cost in adaptability (see [[Guard against reverse-teleology]]).
