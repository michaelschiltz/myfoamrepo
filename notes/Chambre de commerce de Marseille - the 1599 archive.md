# Chambre de commerce de Marseille — the 1599 archive

Archives of the CCI Aix-Marseille-Provence. <https://www.cciamp.com/>

**The oldest chamber of commerce in France**, founded 1599, with its own historical archive — inventoried in the nineteenth century by Octave Teissier, whose *Inventaire des archives historiques de la Chambre de commerce de Marseille* is a printed finding aid and is available digitally.

## Why a chamber of commerce is an archive this project wants

Because the Marseille chamber was not a lobby. It **administered the French trade with the Levant**: it appointed and paid consuls in the *échelles*, it levied duties on its own members to fund them, and it governed the terms on which Marseille merchants could trade.

**That is a compulsory pool administered by a merchant corporation for a collective good** — which is `PY0=collective-good` and `MB3=compulsory` in the census's own vocabulary, the configuration that `averia_pool` occupies and that the standing open question asks about. A second instance under a different crown, with its own archive and a printed inventory, is exactly what that question needs.

⚠️ **This is a reading of an institution, not of its records.** Nothing here has been checked against the inventory, and whether the chamber's levy was compulsory on all Marseille merchants or only on its members is the question that decides whether the paragraph above survives.

## The finding aid is the asset

Teissier's printed inventory means **the description is at layer 3 even if the records are at layer 1** — the same situation as the Genoese San Giorgio and the *inventaires sommaires* on Gallica. A printed nineteenth-century inventory is a filter, but it is a *searchable* one, and for a corporation-held archive that is often the only route in.

⚠️ Access conditions for the CCI's archive are unestablished; expect the non-standard terms that come with corporation custody, as at [[Arxiu Historic de Protocols de Barcelona]].
