# The Sarta company held no common fund

The Manresan *societas* of Jaume Sarta, Berenguer Amargós and Des Valls, formed in the 1340s and renewed after the Black Death, kept no company capital in its records. Commendas raised from investors were "invariably received in the name of one of the partners rather than in the name of the company"; when "the company" bought something, each partner covered a third of the cost; and the partners cemented the partnership by placing commendas with one another — Pere (II) Sarta's 6,500 s. "racione societatis" to Pere de Muncunill in 1327, Berenguer Amargós's 10,000 s. to Jaume Sarta in 1348 (Fynn-Paul 2018, ch. 4 nn. 124–126). Fynn-Paul calls it "the interpersonal commenda … in lieu of a 'limited liability' partnership structure", and concludes that the company "added little in terms of structure to the individual means of the partners", except that counterparties knew three wealthy families stood behind each deal.

**What it would test.** A Catalan *societas* coded from these papers would take `CI1` at its first instance in the Crown of Aragon with a declared answer of `none` or `several-accounts` rather than `common`, and would leave the asset-partitioning cells at `.NR` or `0` on a positive statement, not on silence — the opposite of the Florentine `compagnia`'s subscribed *corpo*. It is a candidate, not a coding: one family's papers, read through one historian, and the archival phase would have to see the acts.

**Where this is soft.** "The records indicate that the company did not in itself hold any capital" is an inference from what the Sarta books record; a company ledger, if one existed, has not survived in them. Survivorship applies, as at [[The land comanda gave way to the censal after 1348]].
