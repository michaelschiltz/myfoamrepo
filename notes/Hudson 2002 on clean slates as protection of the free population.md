# Hudson 2002 on clean slates as protection of the free population

Michael Hudson, "Reconstructing the Origins of Interest-Bearing Debt and the Logic of Clean Slates," in Michael Hudson and Marc Van De Mieroop (eds.), *Debt and Economic Renewal in the Ancient Near East* (Bethesda: CDL Press, 2002), 7–58. Read in full (October 2026); the page numbers below are the volume's.

**The one argument in the sweep that ties a debt rule to the survival of a polity. It is a barrier argument in all but name, and it is not formalised. Reading the text adds something the first version of this note missed: the line between cancelled and spared debts is a line of outcome-coupling.**

## The argument, verified

- **The sort (p. 28).** "Mesopotamian rulers responded by annulling barley debts, while leaving commercial debts intact." The reason is given just before: "Most commercial debts could be paid without creating social strains", whereas agricultural debts "led to economic disorder by causing the forfeiture of family members and, by the second millennium, land rights."
- **Commercial debts were already state-contingent (pp. 28–29).** "Commercial debts were forgiven when the merchandise was lost through no fault of the merchant." Hammurapi §48 extended the principle to the farmer whose field the storm-god flooded: debtors were not liable for "acts of God" (p. 29). Local enforcement of that relief in agriculture "proved difficult". The conclusion repeats the contrast: when trade ventures failed, "creditors shared in the loss", while barley debts "had to be paid out of the debtors own labor, other income, or by selling off assets" (p. 52).
- **The polity's own base (pp. 29–30).**
  - Rulers had to stop debts mounting "to the point where they threatened to expropriate the community's fighting force."
  - "If the indebted rural citizenry were to survive along customary lines, priority could not be given to creditors" (p. 29).
  - Large-scale foreclosure "would have disenfranchised the cultivators who manned the army, dooming debt-ridden realms to defeat by outsiders in short order." Hammurapi §§26–39 accordingly barred the forfeiture of soldiers' lands to private creditors (p. 30).
  - The argument recurs on p. 37, with Aeneas Tacticus on promising debt cancellation to a besieged town.
- **The dynamic (p. 41).** The ancients "perceived that debts grow autonomously under their own dynamic by the exponential curves of compound interest rather than adjusting themselves to reflect the ability of debtors to pay."
- **The instruments.** *Mīšarum* and *andurārum* proclamations, issued customarily in a ruler's first full year, cancelled barley debts, freed bondservants and cleared land of liens (p. 30).

## Why it matters

- **The absorbing state is explicit.** Bondage and the loss of subsistence land are absorption for the household. The clean slate is a re-randomisation after absorption, not a pooling device: the same operation as [[Warichi re-randomises after absorption rather than diversifying]].
- **The sort is by outcome-coupling, not only by barrier.**
  - The debts left alone were the ones whose repayment already moved with the outcome: the merchant owed nothing if the cargo was lost.
  - The debts cancelled were fixed claims, paid from labour or assets whatever the harvest.
  - The clean slate supplies, after the fact and in bulk, the state-contingency that the agrarian contract lacked. That is the second binding of [[Lock-in and outcome-coupling are distinct bindings]].
  - It lines up with Scott's fixed against variable claims ([[Scott 1976 on the subsistence ethic as safety-first]]) and with Glaeser and Scheinkman's consumption/production split ([[Glaeser and Scheinkman 1998 on interest restrictions as social insurance]]).
- **The polity is the second trajectory.** The ruler's interest in households not being absorbed is the two-level structure of [[Taleb secures the collective by exit and has no theory of pooling]], with a third mechanism added: neither exit nor pooling, but periodic reset.
- **The dynamic is half right.** Hudson sees the multiplicative side: compound growth against an economy that does not compound at the same rate. He does not draw the trajectory consequence, that a fixed claim against a fluctuating multiplicative income raises the probability of absorption even at equal means. That step is the project's.

## Cautions

- **Partisan framing.** Hudson is an advocate of debt jubilees. Carry the Assyriological apparatus with him: Van De Mieroop, and Bottéro 1961 on Old Babylonian debt annulment (cited in the chapter's bibliography).
- **Debated reconstruction.** His thesis that interest-bearing debt originated in public institutions (p. 28) is contested and is not needed for the barrier reading.
- **Economics companions.** Rosenberg and Weiss 2000 read the Jubilee's return of land as anti-monopoly law under imperfect credit markets. Schaefer and Noell 2005 apply contract theory to the sabbatical year.
- **Descendants.** Biblical *shemitah* and *yovel* descend from these proclamations, and the prosbul is the rabbinic workaround. The sweep found no functional study of the prosbul.
