# Hudson 2002 on clean slates as protection of the free population

Michael Hudson, "Reconstructing the Origins of Interest-Bearing Debt and the Logic of Clean Slates," in Michael Hudson and Marc Van De Mieroop (eds.), *Debt and Economic Renewal in the Ancient Near East* (Bethesda: CDL Press, 2002), 7–58 [pagination unverified].

**The one argument in the sweep that ties a debt rule to the survival of a polity. It is a barrier argument in all but name, and it is not formalised.**

**The argument** [verify against the text]. Bronze Age Mesopotamian royal proclamations (*mīšarum*, *andurārum*) cancelled agrarian debts, freed debt-servants and restored forfeited land. Commercial debts were not cancelled. The rationale was the polity's own base: the free, land-holding population owed corvée and military service. Interest-bearing debt compounds faster than an agrarian economy grows, so without periodic release the creditors' accumulation would strip the ruler of the people he depended on.

**Why it matters.**

- **The absorbing state is explicit.** Debt bondage is absorption for the household. The clean slate is a re-randomisation after absorption rather than a pooling device, the same operation as [[Warichi re-randomises after absorption rather than diversifying]].
- **The commercial/agrarian split is a doctrinal sort by barrier.** The rule cancels the debts whose default absorbs a household and leaves alone those whose default does not. That is a sharper statement than anything in the usury literature, and a testable one.
- **The polity is the second trajectory.** The ruler's interest in households not being absorbed is the two-level structure of [[Taleb secures the collective by exit and has no theory of pooling]], with a third mechanism: neither exit nor pooling, but periodic reset.

**Cautions.** Hudson is an advocate of debt jubilees and the framing is partisan; carry the Assyriological apparatus with it (Van De Mieroop; Bottéro 1961 on Old Babylonian debt annulment). The economics companions are Rosenberg and Weiss 2000, who read the Jubilee's return of land as anti-monopoly law under imperfect credit markets, and Schaefer and Noell 2005, who apply contract theory to the sabbatical year. Biblical *shemitah* and *yovel* are the Israelite descendants, and the prosbul is the rabbinic workaround. The sweep found no functional study of the prosbul.
