# A residual-bearer characteristic failed two blind tests for want of cases, not for redundancy

`RD-test` — who bears a drawn member's default once his own security fails — failed both blind tests of discriminant validity on rule 6's **second** limb: no eligible pair of rulebooks agreed on all the predictors, so no pair could show either that the existing columns predict the new one or that they do not. That is a failure of the evidence to reach the question, not a finding that the characteristic is redundant. The distinction decides what to do next.

## The two results

| test | rulebooks | substantive `RD-test` | eligible | pairs agreeing on all predictors |
|------|-----------|------------------------|----------|----------------------------------|
| 1936 Nōrinshō reserve | 17 | 5, one of each value | 4 kō, 3 villages | none |
| 1915 Ōkurashō and c. 1942 court reports | 19 | 6: `officers` 5, `all-members` 1 | 6 rulebooks | none |

## Why the tests could not bite

- **Silence dominates.** The written rules stop at the drawer's own guarantors or pledge in 25 of 36 rulebooks. A test of a residual-layer characteristic draws its cases from the minority that write the layer down.
- **The predictors are many and fine-grained for that few cases.** The pass condition asks two rulebooks to agree on four or five nominal cells; with four to six eligible rulebooks that almost never happens, so the test spends its cases on the conditioning and has none left for the outcome. See [[Count degrees of freedom not cells]].
- **When the new column varies, it varies by drafting regime, which no predictor records.** In the re-test it barely varied (five `officers`), and three of those came from one licensing template ([[Where ko rules name a bearer beyond the drawer's security it follows the drafting regime]]). A characteristic whose values track an unrecorded variable will fail a test built from recorded ones whatever its merit.
- **One predictor contained the outcome.** `LS1 = residual-on-fund` restates `RD-test = fund`, which made the first test's `fund` kō unscorable; the re-test removed `LS1` from the pass test.

## What follows

- **Do not adopt, and do not record the characteristic as redundant.** The census's rule is that discriminating power is free and a new characteristic earns its place by repairing a conflation; neither test showed a conflation, and neither showed collinearity.
- **A third test needs selection on the residual layer**, which the blind protocol forbids if done by reading: rulebooks must be chosen by a property knowable before reading (a regime, a prefecture's licensing rules, a cooperative's model rules) and then read blind. Selection on the outcome would make any pass worthless.
- **The descriptive finding survives the failed test.** The rules usually leave the residual to custom (two 1936 rulebooks say so in words: 地方賴母子ノ慣例), and when they do not, the answer is set by who drafted them.

## Where this is soft

Both tests ran on Japanese rulebooks of 1915–1936 chosen by government offices. A residual bearer may be drafted more often, and more variously, in instruments not selected by a regulator, and nothing here speaks to Tokugawa 仕法書.
