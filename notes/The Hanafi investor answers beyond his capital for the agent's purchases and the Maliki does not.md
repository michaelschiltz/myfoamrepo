# The Hanafi investor answers beyond his capital for the agent's purchases and the Maliki does not

**When the capital is lost after a purchase and before the price is paid, Ḥanafī law sends the agent back to the investor for the price, and does so again if the second sum is lost. Mālikī law lets the investor walk away.** The capital provider's liability is capped in one school and not in the other.

## The rules

- **Ḥanafī.** The agent buys as an agent and carries the contract liability (*ʿuhda*), *yarjiʿu bi-mā yalḥaquhu min al-ʿuhda ʿalā rabb al-māl ka'l-wakīl* (al-Sarakhsī, *al-Mabsūṭ* 22, 19). If the thousand is lost after the purchase, "he recovers another thousand from the investor and pays it over" (76). The recovery repeats, and the capital grows by each sum paid in (172–175; at 175 the agent recovers 4,500). Debt the agent incurs beyond the capital on his own initiative is his alone (*bāb al-istidāna*, 178). Within the mandate, then, the investor stands behind every purchase.
- **Mālikī.** "It is said to the investor: if you wish, pay the price, and the goods are *qirāḍ* as before; and if he refuses, the price falls on the agent and the goods are his" (Saḥnūn, *al-Mudawwana* 3, 641). Mālik gives the same rule in the *Muwaṭṭaʾ* (2055): the investor may pay in, or "be quit of the goods". "If you dislike it, nothing is owed by you."

## What it does in the matrix

**Nothing, and that is the finding.** In both rows the agent's estate is open to the seller, so `AP3` is 0 and `LR1` is `unlimited-several` on each. Since 2026-08-18 those characteristics have been located on the labour party, the individual repeatedly exposed. The capital provider's exposure, where the schools actually part, has no column. `LS3`, the loss-side analogue, is uncoded on the alloc rows by the family precedent.

The difference is recorded in both rows' `AP3` notes and in logbook 6 (2026-10-14 (ii)). It is not proposed as a characteristic: repairing the vocabulary in the batch that found the gap would be retrospective selection.

## Why it matters for the argument

The *qirāḍ* is usually described as shielding the agent and leaving the investor's loss "capped at the capital advanced". The cap holds for loss to the venture, in both schools. It does not hold, in Ḥanafī law, for the venture's debts incurred within the mandate after the capital has gone. There the investor is in the position of a principal to his agent, without limit. What partitions the Ḥanafī investor's estate from the venture is the mandate's boundary, not the capital. In the Mālikī *qirāḍ* the capital is the boundary.

If the commenda family is ever re-coded on owner shielding of the capital provider, this pair is the case that shows the question is not empty. The decision belongs to the whole family and to logbook 1, not to this pair.

## Where this is soft

- **One text per school.**
- **Recovery is not equity.** The Ḥanafī recovery runs through the agent's right against his principal; the seller's claim stays against the agent. The investor answers the agent, not the creditor.
- **Ḥanafī doctrine outside al-Sarakhsī is not surveyed.**
