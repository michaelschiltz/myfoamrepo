# Archivio di Stato di Lucca

<https://archiviodistatoinlucca.cultura.gov.it/>

Lucca's merchants ran one of the earliest and most widely dispersed European banking networks, and the city's archive is unusually complete for a small republic. The archive publishes an *archivio on line* and a set of edited inventories.

## Why it is worth a note

**The Corte dei Mercanti.** A merchants' court with its own jurisdiction, which puts Lucca in the same family as the Spanish *consulados* ([[Consulado de Burgos - the universidad de mercaderes]], [[Archivo Foral de Bizkaia - the Consulado de Bilbao]]) and the Florentine *Mercanzia*. ⚠️ Series, extent and dates unverified.

**Early insurance.** Lucchese and Tuscan merchants are in the first generation of European marine insurance, and the argument about when peril stopped being shared and started being priced — [[Pricing risk does not remove it]] — needs more than the Genoese and Florentine evidence it currently rests on.

**The *Diplomatico*** is served through a dedicated interface, so some of the medieval material is at layer 2.

## The honest assessment

For this project Lucca is a **corroborating site, not a primary one**. Its value is that a claim resting on Florence and Genoa alone rests on two cities whose sources the same historiography has worked over for a century; Lucca is the cheapest available third. That is a real use, and it is a smaller one than the note's length might suggest.
