# Archives nationales — Paris and Pierrefitte

<https://www.archives-nationales.culture.gouv.fr/> · catalogue: the *Salle des inventaires virtuelle* at <https://www.siv.archives-nationales.culture.gouv.fr/>

The archives of the French state before and after the Revolution, split between Pierrefitte-sur-Seine (post-1790) and the Paris site (ancien régime and the notaries).

## The series that matter

**`MC` — the Minutier central des notaires de Paris.** Its own note: [[Minutier central des notaires de Paris]].

**`X` — Parlement de Paris.** Its own note: [[Parlement de Paris - serie X]].

**`Z/1d` — Amirauté de France**, cotes `Z/1d/1` to `Z/1d/141`, with a published *répertoire numérique*. The admiralty jurisdiction at the centre; the local admiralties are in the departments — see [[The amirautes - a series across the coastal departments]].

**`G7` — Contrôle général des finances**, and the fiscal series of the ancien-régime monarchy. The French counterpart to Simancas's `CONSEJO Y JUNTAS DE HACIENDA`; where the Crown argued about the terms on which it borrowed.

**`E` — Conseil du roi**, including the *arrêts en commandement* by which trading companies were chartered.

⚠️ Extents, dates and digitisation unverified for all but `Z/1d`.

## The structural point about French company charters

The French chartered companies — the Compagnie des Indes orientales and its successors — were **creatures of the royal council rather than of a representative body**, which makes their constitutive documents different in kind from a VOC charter granted by the Staten-Generaal or an English charter under the great seal. The granting instrument is an *arrêt du Conseil*, and the Conseil's own record is here.

For the census that matters because [[Transmission versus independent recurrence is the load-bearing test]] turns on what the drafters had in front of them, and the French companies were explicitly modelled on the Dutch. **France is a transmission case, not a recurrence case** — and the archive of the transmitting act is in série `E`.
