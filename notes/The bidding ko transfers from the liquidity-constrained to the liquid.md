# The bidding kō transfers from the liquidity-constrained to the liquid

Namimatsu states the mechanism plainly. In a *tanomoshi* allocated by 入札, the members in distress have the highest demand for money, so they bid early and bid high; the gap between what they have paid in 掛銀 and what they actually receive is the implicit interest. Members with cash to spare bid late, and by then the pot carries the accumulated discounts of everyone who bid before them, so their receipt **exceeds** their contributions. His conclusion is worth quoting in full: たとえ無尽が高利貸資本に対抗する自衛的な相互扶助組織として成立したとしても、結果的に金銭に余裕のある富裕層が、親なし無尽を通じて、生活困窮者から利子部分に相当する金額を獲得することになってしまう.

An institution founded *against* usury capital that transfers systematically from the poor to the rich, by the ordinary operation of its allocation rule and without anyone defecting.

**This makes variance-amplifying pooling a class rather than a peculiarity of the tontine.** The two cases share the outcome and share nothing else. The tontine concentrates a fixed aggregate onto survivors: the sorting variable is mortality, the transfer runs from short trajectories to long ones, and the mechanism is survivorship ([[The tontine is pooling in form and dispersal in function]]). The bidding kō concentrates by liquidity: the sorting variable is urgency of need, the transfer runs from the constrained to the unconstrained, and the mechanism is a price auction over position in a queue. Neither is defeated by decorrelated hazards, so neither is caught by the correlation test ([[Pooling's sign is set by the correlation of its baskets]]). A framework that names only ρ certifies both as sound.

The reason this instance is worth more than the tontine's is that the sorting variable is **wealth itself**, so the arrangement is regressive in exactly the population it was built to protect. Under multiplicative dynamics that is not merely unfair: it moves the constrained members toward the absorbing barrier while moving the unconstrained away from it, which is the opposite of what pooling is supposed to do to a trajectory.

Three things follow that are testable rather than rhetorical. **The regulatory response fits the mechanism.** From the 1930s prefectures imposed floors on the winning bid — fifty per cent of the payout in Kagoshima, sixty in Nagano and Mie, seventy in seven prefectures, eighty in Tochigi. A floor on the bid is a ceiling on the implicit interest, aimed precisely at the transfer described here, and it says the authorities had identified the same mechanism. **Lot allocation should not show it**, since 抽籤 severs the link between urgency and position; so the lot-versus-bid distinction is not a detail of procedure but the presence or absence of the transfer. **And the zero-interest case is the control**: 救済講, where the 親 is a named distressed person, normally carried no interest at all, which removes the mechanism by construction.

Against the temptation to read this as decline: Namimatsu explicitly refuses the stage account. Having set out 澁谷's progression from 親無尽 through 親なし無尽 and 取退き無尽 to 富籤, he adds 日本全体でこのように段階的に変わったわけでなく、これらの形態がいわば重層的に存続していた — the forms persisted in layers, not in sequence. The transfer is a property of an allocation rule that coexisted with others, not a late corruption of an earlier purity ([[Guard against reverse-teleology]]).
