# The proverbs of diversification state the rule and omit its conditions

Historians have observed the practice of spreading risk across ventures many times, and the proverbial record states it across traditions. What the sayings carry is the rule. What they leave out are the three conditions under which the rule pays — that the perils fall separately, that the venture grows on average, and that there is a loss from which there is no return — and the size of the gain when they hold. Those come from the dynamic: [[Under absorption pooling raises the probability of ruin to the power of the pool]] and [[Pooling pays only where ventures grow on average]].

## The record

**Shakespeare, *The Merchant of Venice* I.i (written c. 1596–98; line numbering varies by edition).**

> My ventures are not in one bottom trusted,
> Nor to one place; nor is my whole estate
> Upon the fortune of this present year.

Three axes in three lines — hull, place, year — read at [[Diversification runs across bottoms not across partners]]. The only saying in the set that names independence along more than one dimension.

**Ecclesiastes 11:1–2 (Authorised Version).**

> Cast thy bread upon the waters: for thou shalt find it after many days.
> Give a portion to seven, and also to eight; for thou knowest not what evil shall be upon the earth.

The first verse has long been read as an allusion to sea trade [verify the commentary tradition before relying on it]. The second gives the reason as ignorance of which evil will come, which is the independence condition stated as uncertainty.

**Babylonian Talmud, Bava Metzia 42a, in the name of Rabbi Yitzḥak.**

> לעולם ישליש אדם את מעותיו: שליש בקרקע, ושליש בפרקמטיא, ושליש תחת ידו
>
> A person should always divide his money in three: a third in land, a third in merchandise, and a third in hand.

[Verify wording against the Vilna edition.] The division runs across asset classes exposed to different perils, and the third kept in hand is a reserve: distance from the barrier, $s$ in the formula, held deliberately.

**Zhanguo ce, "Qi ce" 4: Feng Xuan to the Lord of Mengchang.**

> 狡兔有三窟，僅得免其死耳。
>
> The wily hare has three burrows, and that only just lets it escape death.

The burrows are refuges, not investments, and the stated purpose is survival, not return: the saying is about ruin avoidance, and the claim that one refuge is not enough. [Verify the chapter reference.]

**"All one's eggs in one basket."** The English proverb is recorded in seventeenth-century collections and is commonly traced to Torriano's Italian proverbs (1666) and to English translations of *Don Quixote*; the egg wording may be the translators' rather than Cervantes's [verify both before citing].

**The counter-proverb.** Twain, *Pudd'nhead Wilson* (1894): "Put all your eggs in the one basket and—WATCH THAT BASKET." Concentration with monitoring, discussed at [[Diversification runs across bottoms not across partners]].

**Japanese.** The egg-basket saying in Japanese (卵を一つのかごに盛るな) is a modern calque. The native merchant maxims that come to mind — 石橋を叩いて渡る (tap the stone bridge before crossing), and the house codes' prohibitions — counsel verification and avoidance rather than spreading. If that holds up across the house codes, it is a difference in which term of the ordering the Edo merchant world wrote down ([[Under absorption the time-average criterion is an ordering not a maximand]]); proverb corpora are too unsystematic to establish it [verify against the house codes].

## What the sayings show and do not show

- **They show the rule was articulated in its own idiom, independently or not**, across Hebrew, Aramaic, Chinese and English sources. For the coding commons this is the "articulated by the tradition's own advocates" field: the function is stated, not only visible to the analyst.
- **They do not show that anyone computed the gain.** A proverb is a policy, and the policy can be right without the belief behind it — [[Rationality is a property of the policy not of the belief that produces it]], [[Time-average optimization is a survivorship property not an intention]].
- **They are silent on when the rule fails.** None of them warns that spreading across ventures struck by one storm buys nothing, or that no spreading saves a declining trade. Antonio's speech is followed by exactly that failure, which is the playwright's addition, not the proverb's.

## The two formalisations

The same practice was formalised twice, along the two limbs — [[Bernoulli split Sempronius's cargo by the logarithm and Roy by the probability of disaster]].
