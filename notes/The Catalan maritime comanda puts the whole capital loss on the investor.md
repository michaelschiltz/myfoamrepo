# The Catalan maritime comanda puts the whole capital loss on the investor

In the comanda *ad usum maris* the investor bears the whole loss of capital to peril, and the commendatary answers only for fault. This is said in the tradition's own words, in both the normative and the transactional layers.

**Normative.** The *Consolat de Mar* ([[The Consolat de Mar comanda chapters]]) has the commendatary owe nothing when the comanda is lost without his fault on the agreed voyage (cap. 209), or by reprisal, seizure or enemy ships (210). Commendataries carry nothing of their own and "lose nothing" when comandas are lost (211). "No one receives a comanda to his own loss" (88). A comanda taken "a ús e costum de mar e a risch de mar e de males gents" sets the default (254).

**Transactional.** A Vic act of 1240 sends goods to Flanders or France "ad tuum resecum et fortunam" (García Sanz 1963, 329; Martínez Gijón 1974, 266). In Hancock's 1,432 Barcelona and Mallorca contracts the clause *ad redegum / risicum (et fortunam)* appears in 352 (24.6 %), mostly placing the risk on the investing party alone, and a Mallorcan notary in 1348 calls that allocation customary, *ut moris est* ([[Hancock 2025 on trust in Barcelona and Mallorca contracts]], 191–192). The profit split is commonly 3:1 to the investor, *ad quartum lucri* (192–193; Cuadrada & López Pérez, 78–79).

**Doctrine.** Martínez Gijón states the rule for the unilateral maritime comanda, which he finds predominant in Spanish law ([[Martinez Gijon on the comenda in Spanish law]], 1966, 386, 397–398). The commendatary's unilateral guarantee on all his goods, which is usually present, secures his duty to render account, not the capital against peril (401).

**The other side of the rule is fault, and the burden of showing it falls on the agent.** He answers for everything if he deviates from the agreed voyage (209), gambles or squanders the comanda, or passes it to another (213), or buys what he was not told to buy (216). If he cannot "show just reasons" for a loss, he must return the comanda and the gain or go to prison (220). His account is received and, on suspicion, sworn; then he is believed unless the investor proves otherwise (279).

**What this does not show.** Three quarters of Hancock's contracts carry no risk clause at all, so the clause is not the rule's only carrier: the *Consolat* supplies the default. Nothing here bears on the investor's exposure to the commendatary's creditors.

The allocation is the Italian one, and the drafting descends from it; agreement with `commenda` is **parallelism**, not convergence. Compare [[The commenda and the qirad do not separate on loss allocation]].
