# Hof van Holland — the civil litigation record

`3.03.01.01`, *Inventaris van het archief van het Hof van Holland, 1428–1811*, at [[Nationaal Archief - The Hague]].

**Nearly four centuries of civil judgments from the province where the instruments were written.** The *Civiele sententies* are separately indexed by the Nationaal Archief, as are the fifteenth-century *Memorialen* (1428–1463).

## Why litigation is the right series, and not a second-best one

Every coded characteristic in `loss_mitigation_forms` and `organizational_forms` is a property of a drafted instrument. **A contract states what the parties agreed; a judgment states what the agreement turned out to mean when it failed** — and for the shielding characteristics especially, failure is the only condition under which the answer is visible at all.

This is the same reasoning that makes `Giudici di Petizion` the series to want at [[Archivio di Stato di Venezia]] and the *Mercanzia* the one at [[Archivio di Stato di Firenze]]. The Dutch case is stronger than either because the court is provincial rather than municipal, the run is continuous from 1428, and the *sententies* are indexed.

## What it might settle

[[The creditor limb of entity shielding has no witness]] records that the census has no evidence on whether members' *creditors* could reach a partnership's assets, as distinct from whether members could — and that a source can be rich on one limb and silent on the other. **A judgment on a creditor's claim against a partnership's assets is a direct witness on exactly that limb**, and it is the kind of evidence the doctrinal literature does not supply because the doctrinal literature is arguing about categories.

⚠️ This is a hypothesis about a series, not a finding. Whether the *sententies* reach commercial partnerships often enough, and whether the index is searchable by subject rather than by party, are both unestablished and both decide whether this is a week's work or a year's.

## The obstacle

Seventeenth-century Dutch court hands, at volume, with no transcription layer. Unlike the notarial archive next door — [[Stadsarchief Amsterdam - the notarial archive]] — nothing here has been through HTR. ⚠️ Whether the Amsterdam notarial models transfer to this material is an open and answerable question, and worth asking before any manual reading is budgeted.
