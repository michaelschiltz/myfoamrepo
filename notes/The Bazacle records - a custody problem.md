# The Bazacle records — a custody problem

⚠️ **The flagship pre-modern share case, and the survey could not identify its fonds from a desk.** This note records what was established, what was not, and what to do — which is more useful than the placeholder it replaces.

## Why it matters

[[Le Bris Goetzmann Pouget 2023 on convergent evolution toward the joint-stock company]] rests on the Bazacle, and the census codes `bazacle_mill`. It is the project's longest continuous series of share prices and the case that most directly tests whether transferable claims on a perpetual body predate the chartered company. **A row this load-bearing should not have an unidentified source base.**

## What was established

**The records are almost certainly split in two, by date and by custodian.**

- **The medieval and early-modern material** — the *uchaux*, the transfers, the deliberations that Germain Sicard used for *Les moulins de Toulouse au Moyen Âge* (1953) — is with the [[Archives departementales de la Haute-Garonne]] and/or the municipal archives of Toulouse. ⚠️ **Cote not identified.** FranceArchives indexes at least one description under *Bazacle, moulin du*, and the departmental *état des fonds* includes an *archives privées* (série `J`) section, which is where a company fonds would sit.
- **The modern company archive** has a specific and testable destination: the Société toulousaine du Bazacle was a **hydroelectric** company by the twentieth century, and French electricity was nationalised on **8 April 1946**. **EDF's historical archive holds a *Fonds Ex-Sociétés*** — the archives of the companies nationalised in 1946 — at <https://archives.edf.com/>. ⚠️ Whether the Bazacle's papers are in it is unconfirmed, but it is a named collection with a matching remit, which is a far better lead than a series letter.

**This is the same structure as [[Natie archives - where the Antwerp nations records are]]**: a long-lived institution whose archive followed its corporate successor rather than its territory. Two of the project's most important continuity cases have that shape, which suggests it is a pattern rather than a coincidence — **institutions that survive keep their own papers, and institutions that survive are exactly the ones a persistence argument wants.**

## Why the desk search failed, which is itself worth recording

The Haute-Garonne catalogue refused automated access; FranceArchives requires JavaScript to render a description; the Persée full text of Sicard's monograph is closed to automated fetching; the modern article's publisher rate-limited. **None of these is a closed archive. All of them are interfaces built for a human with a browser**, and the cumulative effect is that the French finding aids are the least remotely-workable in the survey. See [[FranceArchives and the French access layers]].

## What to do, in order

1. **Read Sicard's source list.** The 1953 monograph names its cotes; that is a library task and it settles the medieval half in an afternoon.
2. **Write to EDF's archive service** about the Bazacle in the *Fonds Ex-Sociétés*.
3. Only then approach the Haute-Garonne archives, with a cote rather than a question.
