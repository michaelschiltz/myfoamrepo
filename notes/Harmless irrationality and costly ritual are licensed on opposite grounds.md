# Harmless irrationality and costly ritual are licensed on opposite grounds

**Taleb licenses superstition because it costs nothing and praises ritual because it costs a great deal. The two arguments cannot both be the reason for the same practice. "Irrationality is allowed as long as it is harmless" covers decorative belief. It does not cover fasting, sacrifice, dietary law or formal ceremony, which are expensive by design and need a defence that runs through their cost. Filing them under one licence hides the variable that separates them, and *Skin in the Game* itself sorts by two different variables in successive chapters.**

## Licence one: no cost, no jurisdiction

*Skin in the Game* ch. 18 ("Ocular Deception", "Ergodicity First"): no criterion of rationality has been built for actions that bear no cost. The columns of a temple lean so that they look straight, and a child's Santa Claus is innocent unless it does harm. Three consequences, the third of which narrows the licence.

- **The burden falls on the critic.** Removing a costless belief is itself an intervention, with its own iatrogenics. Ch. 19 ("Rationality, Again") calls obstructing a superstition that serves ergodicity irrational; *Antifragile* ch. 7 makes the general case.
- **Small-cost error generates discovery** (ch. 18, "Revelation of Preferences"). This is a second reason for tolerance, and it too needs the cost to be small.
- **"Harmless" is assessed on the repeated exposure, not the single act.** Ch. 19 ("Repetition of Exposures"): one cigarette is benign and the habit is not; every risk taken shortens life expectancy. Taleb uses this against psychologists who test a subject once. It binds his own licence too. A belief harmless in the instance may not be harmless as a lifelong policy. The licence is for practices harmless *along the trajectory*.

## Licence two: the cost is the point

Ch. 16 ("The Gods Do Not Like Cheap Signaling"): belief requires an entry fee, and Pascal's wager fails because belief cannot be a free option. Sacrifice is revealed preference. *Antifragile* ch. 22 describes Orthodox fasting as harrowing, close to two hundred days a year. None of this is offered as harmless. Its defences are commitment that cheap talk cannot fake (ch. 16), stressor (*Antifragile* ch. 22), and the loss of a sense of entitlement (ch. 22).

## Two variables, two chapters

Ch. 17 ("Religious in Words") sorts by a different variable. Atheism is measured in deeds, by distance from an atheist's action *on matters where life is at stake*. Prayer, ceremony and the Pope's renunciations are classed as differences that do not touch survival. On that test most Orthodox and Catholic Christians are atheist in action. Lent then counts as decoration in ch. 17 and as skin in the game in ch. 16. The inconsistency is informative, because the two chapters are measuring different things.

- **Ch. 16 measures cost.** Does the practitioner pay?
- **Ch. 17 measures barrier-relevance.** Does the practice change action where ruin is in play?

| | Off the barrier | On the barrier |
|---|---|---|
| **Costless** | Decorative belief: Santa Claus, prayer by the hospital bed. Licence one; nothing to explain. | Heuristic: the refusal to sleep under dead trees. Defended by its error structure ([[A heuristic is defended by its error distribution not by its accuracy]]). |
| **Costly** | Ritual proper: fasts, dietary law, ornament, ceremony. Needs a function that runs through the cost ([[Ritual carries its function in its form and its circularity not in its content]]). | Where practice converges: the Pope goes to the Gemelli clinic. Belief yields to the secular action, which is ch. 17's whole point. |

The bottom-right cell is the empirical content of ch. 17: where cost and ruin coincide, the religious and the secular act alike. The top-right cell is where a "superstition" is a heuristic in disguise. Only the bottom-left cell is ritual in MS's sense, and it is the one cell neither licence covers.

## Rule for the vault

Never write "harmless irrationality" of a costly practice. Place a practice in the grid first: costless or costly, on the barrier or off it. Each cell takes a different argument, and the argument for one cell proves nothing about another.
