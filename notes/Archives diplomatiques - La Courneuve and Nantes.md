# Archives diplomatiques — La Courneuve and Nantes

The foreign ministry's archives: the central series at La Courneuve, the **posts** — embassies and consulates — at Nantes.

## Why the consular series matters

**The French consuls in the Levant were appointed and paid by the Marseille chamber of commerce**, and their correspondence is the administrative record of a trade governed by a merchant corporation under royal capitulations. See [[Chambre de commerce de Marseille - the 1599 archive]], which holds the other half of the same relationship.

A consulate is also an **identity-wrapper institution** in the sense the vault separates at [[Identity-wrapper and perpetual-monopoly are distinct shielding facets]]: a body that gives a merchant community a legal personality and a jurisdiction inside a foreign polity. The census reads that form from the Low Countries *naties* and the Castilian consulates at Bruges; the French consulates in the Ottoman Levant are the same form under a different treaty regime, and they are documented in two archives rather than lost in successor custody.

**That makes them the best-documented member of the family** — which is the opposite of the situation at [[Natie archives - where the Antwerp nations records are]], and a reason to start here rather than there.
