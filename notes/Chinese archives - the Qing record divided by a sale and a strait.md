# Chinese archives — the Qing record divided by a sale and a strait

**The structural fact about the Chinese imperial record: the central state's archive exists, is enormous, and sits in three institutions on two sides of a political border, under opposite access regimes.**

## How it was divided

- **In Beijing: [[First Historical Archives of China - Beijing]]** — about **10 million** Ming and Qing documents, the Palace Museum's archive department of 1925 renamed in 1955. **About 4.7 million opened** when the new building opened on 6 July 2022.
- **In Taipei, by purchase: [[Academia Sinica IHP - the Grand Secretariat archives in Taipei]]** — about **310,000** items of the Grand Secretariat's archive, **sold as waste paper around 1921** and bought back by scholars — see [[The eight thousand sacks - the Grand Secretariat archives sold as waste paper]] — then taken to Taiwan.
- **In Taipei, by evacuation: [[National Palace Museum Taipei - the Qing palace memorials]]** — **150,000 palace memorials and 190,000 Grand Council files**, removed with the Palace Museum's collections at the end of the civil war.

**No series is whole on either side.** A memorial may be in Taipei and its Grand Council copy in Beijing; a Grand Secretariat run may be split between the institute that bought the sacks and the archive that kept what was never sold.

## What it does to the evidence

**1. The legibility map is a political map.** The Taipei holdings are catalogued online and largely imaged; the Beijing holdings are read on site, **with photography forbidden and photocopying capped at twenty documents per research trip** on a 2023 practitioners' manual — see [[PRC archival access - photography forbidden and catalogues curated]]. **The first pattern of [[Handoff - the non-European archive survey]] — the funding map is a legibility map — becomes a map of which side of the Strait a document ended on in 1949.**

**2. The central record is a record of administration, not of commerce.** Memorials, edicts, judicial reviews, granary and price reports. **The census's Chinese forms — shares, partnerships, native-place associations — are private institutions**, and the state wrote about them only when they came to court or to its revenue. See [[Chinese contracts - the private document boom and the market that feeds it]].

**3. But the central record holds the survey's best denominator.** The monthly grain-price reports sent to the throne from 1736 have been compiled into a database of about 2.19 million entries — [[Qing grain price database - a denominator from the memorials]].
