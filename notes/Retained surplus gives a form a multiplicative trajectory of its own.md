# Retained surplus gives a form a multiplicative trajectory of its own

**A hypothesis to develop during the project, deliberately kept out of the application (decision of 2026-11-21): it is not formalised, and its test is a coding exercise the project has not yet run.**

The tempting version is chronological: older cooperative forms push the absorbing barrier back, as in Vanhoyweghen, Verbeken and Ginis; corporate forms do that too, and additionally exploit multiplicative dynamics, as in Peters, to grow. It fails three ways, and what survives is a claim about two variables, not about two families of forms or two periods.

## Why the chronological version fails

- **It is the ascent narrative.** A sequence from barrier-only to barrier-plus-growth is a sequence from primitive to advanced — [[The ascent narrative is the Jensen error as historiography]], [[Guard against reverse-teleology]]. It also contradicts Part 1's commitment to compare by dynamic regime rather than by period.
- **It is historically inverted in Europe.** The corporation entered as a continuity device for bodies that had to outlive their members — churches, towns, universities — with no interest in compounding: [[Persona ficta made the corporation a-moral ab initio]], [[The Roman universitas makes the JSC case parallelism not convergence]]. Its business use for accumulating capital came later. Perpetual growth-bearing forms are also old (the Toulouse mills), and cooperative forms are often young.
- **The shield does not suppress variance; it lets the entity carry more.** See [[Shielding raises the variance an entity can carry]]. Pooling across decorrelated ventures and shielding push variance in opposite directions, and the corporation does both.
- **Older forms carry the multiplicative limb too**, in a different place: the *commenda* compounds in the investor's portfolio across successive contracts, while the form itself is deliberately non-perpetual.

## The version that holds

Two variables decide what a form does:

- **Whose trajectory it protects** — the member's, or a new trajectory belonging to the entity.
- **What happens to the surplus** — distributed to members, or retained and compounded inside the form.

| | Surplus distributed | Surplus retained and compounded |
|---|---|---|
| **Member's trajectory** | *kō*, *mujin*, mutual-aid funds, general average, *warichi* | the investor's portfolio of successive *commendas* |
| **Entity's trajectory** | *waqf*: perpetual, income spent, principal untouchable | lineage estates that bought land; the Edo merchant house; the business corporation |

**Where surplus is distributed, a form can act only on the barrier**: it keeps members alive and leaves growth to them. **Where surplus is retained, the form acquires a multiplicative trajectory of its own**, and that trajectory must be protected — from the members' ruin (entity shielding) and the members from its ruin (owner shielding). The corporate bundle is the cell in which an entity trajectory is both compounding and shielded on both sides.

The application already holds the first variable: Part 2 distinguishes the two component families "by the unit whose trajectory they protect, the participant's and the enterprise's". The surplus variable would supply a reason for the distinction — participant protection works through the barrier, enterprise protection becomes necessary once the enterprise compounds.

## The prediction

**Retention co-occurs with shielding; distribution does without it.**

- The *waqf* fits: perpetual, but income spent and principal protected by inalienability, so it neither compounds nor approaches a barrier — the consistent rule read at [[Kuran's brake is a refusal of immortal entities]] and [[Pooling approaches the infinite ensemble and perpetual succession the infinite horizon]].
- The *kō* distributes and has no shield.
- The Edo merchant house retained and compounded, and protected itself by household discipline and by partition of assets across domains — [[The fictive household supplied partition and perpetuity without legal personality]].

**What would count against it:** a form that retained and compounded a common fund over generations, with no shielding in either direction, and survived. Codable from the five-component matrix plus one new characteristic, the disposition of surplus (distributed / retained / mixed), registered before the cases are coded [check against the HistorEE_codebooks schema].

## A correction made in drafting

In conversation the *warichi* was placed as the opposite pole of the corporation: periodic reallocation resetting holdings and holding the village near its ensemble. **That is wrong.** *Warichi* re-deals parcels, not shares; share holdings were traded and inequality grew (Brown, at [[Brown tests equality where a risk reading predicts dispersion]]). What it reallocates is the **incidence** of an irreversible loss — [[Warichi re-randomises after absorption rather than diversifying]], [[Warichi and general average spread the incidence of an irreversible loss]]. It belongs in the member-trajectory, distributed cell, as a barrier device, and says nothing about the divergence of wealth.
