# The German *Wirtschaftsarchive* — a distributed business archive

**Germany's business archive is not one institution but a network of regional ones**, each attached to its chambers of commerce: the **Rheinisch-Westfälisches Wirtschaftsarchiv** at Cologne (<https://www.rwwa.de/>), the **Bayerisches Wirtschaftsarchiv**, the **Wirtschaftsarchiv Baden-Württemberg**, and others.

## The comparison that makes this a note

| Country | Business archive |
|---|---|
| France | [[Archives nationales du monde du travail - Roubaix]] — one national institution |
| Netherlands | [[IISG and NEHA - Amsterdam]] — one institution, non-state |
| Belgium | none; private archives registered through Archiefbank |
| Germany | **a regional network, chamber-attached** |

**Four countries, four different answers to the same problem** — where do a firm's papers go when the firm has no reason to keep them — and none of the answers is about firms. The German solution is federal because Germany is; the French is national because France is.

**This is the custody pattern of [[Fuggerarchiv - Dillingen]] seen from the other side.** Where a firm survives, its archive stays private; where it does not, the papers reach a public or semi-public repository if a regional business archive exists to take them. **So the archives of the failed are public and the archives of the durable are private**, and a project about durability is systematically pushed toward the custodians with the most control over access.

⚠️ Holdings, coverage and access terms unverified for every institution named.
