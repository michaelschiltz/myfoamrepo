# Kamer van Assurantie en Averij — Amsterdam

Records at [[Stadsarchief Amsterdam - the notarial archive]]. ⚠️ Toegang number not yet transcribed.

**A specialised court for marine insurance, established by the city in 1598**, whose commissioners adjudicated disputes between underwriters, the insured and brokers, and which ran for roughly two centuries.

## Why this is a first-rank institution for the project and not merely a court

The project's central distinction is between a peril **shared** and a peril **priced** — see [[Pricing risk does not remove it]] and [[MOC - Risk-sharing vs risk-pricing]]. A city that in 1598 creates a standing tribunal *specifically for insurance disputes* has already made that distinction institutionally: it has decided that priced peril is a separate kind of business needing its own forum, separate from ordinary commercial jurisdiction and from average.

**The date is the point.** 1598 is four years before the VOC charter. The institutional machinery for pricing maritime peril was in place in Amsterdam before the institutional machinery for pooling capital in a permanent company — which cuts directly against any staging that treats pricing as a late development out of pooling. The census already holds one such finding: the 2026-08-07 tontine split concluded that **pricing is orthogonal to mechanism rather than a stage of it**. This is a second, institutional, instance of the same shape.

⚠️ That paragraph is an argument the archive might support, not a finding. It rests on a foundation date and nothing else, and it should not be repeated until the records have been read. Sabine Go's work on the chamber's first decades (1598–1621) is the obvious entry.

## What to look for

Rulings, and behind them the underlying policies. The distinction that matters when reading them is whether the commissioners were pricing a **risk** — a quantified distribution — or adjudicating a **hazard** met by sharing. The census's own terminology rule keeps those apart deliberately, and a source that calls both "risk" will hide the shift by naming its endpoint.
