# The *jajmani* system has no archive

**The grant names the *jajmani* network among the South Asian institutions in scope. This pass found no archive for it, and a literature that disputes whether it existed as described.**

## What the term refers to

The hereditary arrangement by which village service castes — barber, washerman, potter, priest — served landholding families and were paid in grain shares at harvest rather than in cash per transaction. **It entered scholarship through village ethnography of the 1930s and after**, and was read as a system of customary, non-market distribution.

## The problem

**C. J. Fuller, "Misconceiving the grain heap: a critique of the concept of the Indian jajmani system", in Parry and Bloch (eds), *Money and the Morality of Exchange* (Cambridge, 1989), chapter 2**, argues that the concept was built by anthropologists out of local practices that varied and were not a system. ⚠️ Read from the chapter listing and repository record only; **the argument here is stated from its title and reputation, not from the text.**

## The archival consequence, which is the point of this note

**The evidence for *jajmani* is fieldwork.** Its record type is **the anthropologist's notebook, census and village survey**, not an institution's own papers: there is no *jajmani* contract, no fund, no register. **Where the census can code an institution from its own instrument — a *shihōsho*, a contract, an inscription — here it would code from an observer's reconstruction**, with `articulation=analyst-imposed` and an unresolved dispute about whether the object exists.

**Two consequences, stated and not acted on.**

1. **For the census**: *jajmani* should not be a row. **Not because the practice did not exist, but because what would be coded is a mid-twentieth-century analytic category** — the strongest form of the problem the census's `articulation` field exists to record.
2. **For the grant**: the term appears in the project's self-description and, on this pass's evidence, cannot be given an archival base. **The ancient *śreṇi* named alongside it can** — see [[Indian inscriptions - the endowment recorded in stone]]. **Recorded for Decision_log D4, which this pass does not decide.**
