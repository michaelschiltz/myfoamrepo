# The Business Archives Council and the UK business archives

**Britain's sixth national answer to the question of where a firm's papers go**, and it is the least institutional of the six.

| Country | Business archive |
|---|---|
| France | [[Archives nationales du monde du travail - Roubaix]] — national, state |
| Netherlands | [[IISG and NEHA - Amsterdam]] — one institution, non-state |
| Germany | [[The German Wirtschaftsarchive - a distributed business archive]] — regional, chamber-attached |
| Sweden | [[Centrum for Naringslivshistoria - Stockholm]] — a foundation funded by the depositing companies |
| Belgium | none; private archives merely registered |
| **Britain** | **no national business archive; a survey body, a register, and company custody** |

Britain has the **Business Archives Council**, which surveys and advocates but does not hold, and the **National Register of Archives**, now inside Discovery, which records where things are. **The records themselves stay with companies, or reach a university or local repository if someone intervened.**

## Why this belongs in the argument rather than in a list

**Six countries, six arrangements, and not one of them was designed around what historians would need.** Each is a by-product of a national administrative tradition — French centralism, German federalism, Swedish corporatism, British voluntarism.

Put beside the five instances of successor custody the survey has now found — the Fuggers, the Bazacle, the Antwerp *naties*, the livery companies, the Bank of England — **the conclusion is that the archival record of durable private institutions is governed by arrangements that vary by country and were chosen for reasons unconnected to research.**

**That is a selection effect on the comparative corpus operating at the level of the state**, and it is the general form of what [[The VOC-EIC digital asymmetry is an access regime not a survival difference]] argues for two companies. Worth stating in those terms in the methods section, with the six-row table as the evidence.
