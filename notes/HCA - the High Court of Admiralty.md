# `HCA` — the High Court of Admiralty

At [[The National Archives - Kew]]. **The British member of the maritime-jurisdiction family, and the one with a nineteen-year digitisation programme attached to part of it.**

## What the court did

Maritime jurisdiction for England: **average and its distribution, bottomry and respondentia, marine insurance disputes, wrecks, prize.** The same docket as the French *amirautés* ([[The amirautes - a series across the coastal departments]]), the Hamburg `Admiralitätskollegium` ([[Staatsarchiv Hamburg]]), and the Amsterdam average court ([[Averijgrossen - the general average adjudications]]).

**With Britain the set is complete across five maritime powers**, and that is the configuration the loss census most wants: one institution-type, five jurisdictions, five legal traditions, all producing serial adjudications of the same perils.

## The prize side, which is where the money went

`HCA 32` and the associated series hold the papers seized from captured ships. That material is the subject of [[The Prize Papers - HCA 32 and the German money]] and is being digitised at scale.

**Note the division carefully, because it decides what is reachable.** The *prize* material is being opened; ⚠️ the *instance* side — the ordinary civil jurisdiction over average, bottomry and insurance — is, so far as this pass established, not part of that programme. **The series the census needs and the series being digitised may not be the same series.** Establish this before assuming the Prize Papers solve the British problem.
