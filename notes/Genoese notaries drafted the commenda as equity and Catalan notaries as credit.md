# Genoese notaries drafted the commenda as equity and Catalan notaries as credit

The security clauses in the commenda acts form a gradient from Genoa through Marseille to Catalonia, and the gradient is one of drafting formulary, not of who bears the peril. The allocation of peril is identical in all three. What differs is whether the notary wrote the contract in the formulary of equity or of credit.

**Genoa: security omitted by design.** The commenda pilot's stage-5 comparison found the same notaries writing a pledge, a surety or an obligation of goods into 85 % of Scriba's sales and 91 % of his sea loans, and into 89 % of Cassinese's sales, 64 % of his sea loans and 75 % of his *mutua*, but into none of Scriba's ten commendas and seven of Cassinese's 142, none of which secures the advance. Under the two-limb rule of 2026-10-07 that is an observed `none`: the absence is a drafting choice, not a recording gap.

**Marseille: security for fault only.** Amalric's notulae carry sureties, a father binding himself for his son, but only *in omni defectu quem invenires culpa*. Blancard's abridgement *obligans etc.* hides whatever obligation formula stood in the full act.

**Catalonia: the general pledge as usual.** Personal and real guarantees over all the commendatary's goods, *me et omnia bona mea mobilia et inmobilia habita et habenda*, are "usually included" in unilateral and bilateral acts (Martínez Gijón 1966, 401; Vic 1240 at 384). Hence `comanda_alloc RB3 = general-estate`, the value's first instance in the loss census, against `commenda_alloc RB3 = none`.

**What the Catalan pledge secures.** Not the capital against the peril. Martínez Gijón is explicit that it secures the duty to render account and restore what was not lost by an excused cause, and the same pledge, prison clause and renunciations stand in the Barcelona deposit acts (Martínez Gijón 1964, 72–74): it is the boilerplate of credit instruments. That fits his classification of the comanda as a credit relation between the parties (1966, 406) and García Sanz's finding that the only common element of the contracts called *comanda* is trust *intuitu personae* (1959, 289–290). See [[Comanda names several contracts in the Catalan sources]].

**Two consequences.**

- **A characteristic defect.** `RB3` asks what secures "the advance" and does not distinguish security for the capital against the peril from security for the agent's performance. The Catalan value is correct under the present definition and misleading in comparison, since it puts a performance guarantee in the column that holds the *bodemerij*'s hypothecation of the ship. The pilot already reported `RB3` as a misfit ("secures what, against what"); this is the case that shows it.
- **A transmission question.** If Amalric's unprinted acts carry a general obligation behind *obligans etc.*, the gradient is a Ligurian–Occitan–Catalan boundary in drafting practice. Drafting boundaries are where transmission leaves arbitrary particulars ([[Divergent arbitrary particulars are the independence diagnostic]]), so this is testable on the documents and bears on how the formulary reached Barcelona.

## Where this is soft

The Catalan figures were read from OCR and await the page images. Hancock's 54.9 % general-pledge figure is for all contract types, not comandas alone. The Genoese counts come from an unadjudicated stage-5 recoding by one model. Pilot entries 19 and 21 and comanda adjudication 1 must be ruled together before this is cited.
