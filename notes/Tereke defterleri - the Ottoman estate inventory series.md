# *Tereke defterleri* — the Ottoman estate inventory series

**The post-mortem inventory drawn up by the court's *kassam* and entered in the *şer'iyye sicilleri*.** It lists the deceased's property and **the debts owed to and by the estate**, and the division among heirs. Recent work calls the Ottoman inventories "akin to the Continental post-mortem inventories with their extensive asset coverage (realty, capital goods, financial assets and liabilities, consumer durables)".

## Scale, as the quantitative literature reports it

- **8,810 *tereke*** from the court registers of two adjacent north-west Anatolian provinces, Hüdavendigâr and Saruhan — mainly the Bursa and Manisa courts — **covering 1460–1870**, in Canbakal and Filiztekin, "Wealth inequality in northwestern Anatolia under the Ottomans, 1460–1870", *European Review of Economic History* 29:1 (2025), 88–129.
- The same authors' database paper — "Wealth and demography in Ottoman probate inventories: a database in very long-term perspective", *Historical Methods* 54:2 (2021), 94–127 — describes the corpus as a long-run database. ⚠️ Its total size and town coverage were not established in this pass.
- **Town-level studies exist in quantity** — Bursa, Ankara, Kastamonu, Ayntab, Rusçuk, Burdur — mostly in Turkish, mostly in local-history journals.

**Where they are**: inside the court registers, therefore wherever those are — [[ISAM Library Archive - the court register image collection]] for images, [[Istanbul Muftulugu Seriyye Sicilleri Arsivi]] and [[The Anatolian court registers - museum to National Library to State Archives]] for originals.

## Why the survey came back for this

**Because it is the Ottoman answer to the question [[The record of non-survivors survives where failure was administered]] tells every pass to ask.** A *tereke* is made because heirs, creditors and the treasury have claims on an estate; **it is the administration of an ending.** And it is serial, dated, quantitative and already partly in databases.

**Two uses, in order of strength.**

1. **A denominator.** The census's standing complaint is that it can count instruments and not populations — [[Never let the units slide from instruments to populations]]. **An inventory series is a population**: everyone in a town whose estate passed through the court, with wealth and debt attached. ⚠️ With the usual qualification that it is a population of the *dead whose estates were inventoried*, which is not everyone.
2. **The insolvent tail**, which is the part that bears on absorption: [[Gurema - the insolvent estate distributed among creditors]].

⚠️ **Nothing here was read beyond abstracts and catalogue records**, and the script is the one the Ottoman pass already flagged: inventories are in *siyakat*-inflected hands.
