# The Silk Road layer 3 is a national editorial programme

**Images are pooled internationally; texts are edited nationally.** IDP put the fragments in one database — [[IDP - the digital reunification of a dispersed archive]] — but **the transcriptions that a historian actually codes from are produced by national projects, each working on the collection its own country holds or has access to.**

## The programmes

- **China on the British collection**: 郝春文 (Hao Chunwen), 《英藏敦煌社会历史文献释录》 — a long-running transcription series of the **social and historical** documents in the British Library's Dunhuang collection, with **volume 18 published** by 2025. ⚠️ Announcement page robots-blocked; volume count and series scope taken from a search result and not verified.
- **China and Russia on Khara-Khoto**: 《俄藏黑水城汉文文献释录》 (2023), **four volumes in five books, nearly 500 social documents** from Xixia to Yuan, edited by Du Jianlu of Ningxia University with Irina Popova of the IOM, from the St Petersburg originals, over five years' work.
- **China on Turfan**: 《吐鲁番出土文书》, the standard edition of the Astana finds. ⚠️ Not examined in this pass.
- **Japan on the Uyghur documents**: Yamada Nobuo, *Sammlung uigurischer Kontrakte* (Osaka University Press, 1993), after Radloff and Malov's *Uigurische Sprachdenkmäler* (Leningrad, 1928) — [[Old Uyghur contracts - the documentary layer under the ortoq]].
- **Japan on the Chinese legal documents**: Niida Noboru, 《唐宋法律文書の研究》 (1937), and Ikeda On's 《中国古代籍帳研究》 — **the Tang contract corpus was put in order in Tokyo.** ⚠️ Named from bibliographic records only.
- **Russia and Britain on the Iranian material**: Livshits on the Mount Mugh documents; Sims-Williams on Sogdian and Bactrian.
- **Oxford, open and recent**: [[Invisible East - an open corpus for the eastern Islamicate documents]].

## What it does to the evidence

**1. The language of the edition selects the reader.** **Two of the four biggest editorial programmes are in Chinese and one is in Japanese** — so the corpus is open to this project in proportion to its East Asian command, which is where it is strongest.

**2. Editions are selections.** "Social and historical documents", "economic documents", "contracts" are the editors' categories; **what was not thought social or economic was not transcribed.** The Chinese pass's warning about compilations applies here too — see [[Zhang Chuanxi 1995 - the contract compilation behind the census's Chinese rows]].

**3. There is no machine-text layer.** No Silk Road counterpart to [[MiDRASH - the machine transcription of the Geniza]] was found. **Twenty scripts, small corpora per script: the economics that produced kuzushiji and Modi models do not hold here.** ⚠️ No survey of HTR work on Central Asian scripts was done.
