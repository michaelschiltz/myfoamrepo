# Toda 2023 on ergodicity economics as pseudoscience

Source note for the second published attack on ergodicity economics, and the more dangerous of the two.

**Bibliographic facts.** Alexis Akira Toda, "'Ergodicity Economics' is Pseudoscience," arXiv:2306.03275v1 [q-fin.GN], 5 June 2023, dated 7 June 2023. Library key `IRVRS9A4`. Toda is a tenured associate professor of economics at UC San Diego and a co-editor of the *Journal of Mathematical Economics*; the paper recites these credentials in its second footnote, which is itself a datum about the register.

**Status flags.** The item is a **preprint**, and whether it has since been peer-reviewed is unresolved — this matters, because an unrefereed polemic and a published critique carry different weight in a referee's hands. The ar5iv rendering of the same identifier is dated August 2026, which indicates **a revised version exists**; the text read here is v1. Resolve both before responding in print. [verify]

## The argument

**§I.** Popper's demarcation criterion, plus a list of six "indicators of possible pseudoscience" **taken from Wikipedia**. The paper's thesis: EE is pseudoscience because (i) it has produced few testable implications and is therefore not falsifiable, and (ii) it meets many of the indicators.

**§II.** Toda declines to define EE — "I am not an expert on this field" — and reads it from Peters 2019 as prescribing growth-rate optimisation, noting the priority of Kelly (1956) and Latané (1959), and that dynamic expected-utility treatments of optimal investment came with Merton and Samuelson (both 1969).

**§III.** The technical core. Merton's portfolio problem: risky asset following geometric Brownian motion with drift $\mu$ and volatility $\sigma$, safe asset at $r$, fraction $\theta$ in the risky asset, continuously rebalanced, investor caring only about terminal wealth $W_T$.

- EE maximises $r + (\mu-r)\theta - \tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^2\theta^2$, giving $\theta^{*} = \dfrac{\mu-r}{\sigma^{2}}$.
- EUT with CRRA coefficient $\gamma$ maximises $r + (\mu-r)\theta - \tfrac{1}{2}\gamma\sigma^2\theta^2$, giving $\theta^{*} = \dfrac{\mu-r}{\gamma\sigma^{2}}$ — Merton's 1969 result.

The two coincide at $\gamma = 1$. Toda notes that criticising the growth-optimal approach *for* coinciding with log utility only persuades those who already accept EUT, and explicitly sets that critique aside.

**§IV.** The falsifiability section. Concedes that the portfolio problem *is* a testable implication of EE. Then poses a three-period consumption–investment problem with labour income $Y_t$ and dynamic budget constraints, observes that any first-year PhD student can formulate and solve it under economic theory, and asks what EE has to say about it. Notes that Peters and Gell-Mann promised a generalisation beyond purely additive or multiplicative dynamics in 2016 and that none has appeared. Concludes: until EE provides testable implications, stay away from it.

**§V.** The six indicators applied. Analysed below.

**§VI.** Concluding remarks, including concern about "researchers who lack proper training in the subject matter" who "attack economics based on ignorance," and about dissemination through popular and social media.

## The six indicators, item by item

Mostly not worth answering, and the vault should not spend words on them. Recorded so the judgement is on file.

- **(i) Vague or exaggerated claims.** Lands. The quoted Peters–Gell-Mann line about resolving "the fundamental problem of decision theory, therefore game theory, and asset pricing" is indefensible, and the vault forbids the equivalent move at [[Do not claim history-dependence as the novelty]].
- **(ii) Improper collection of evidence.** Three specific citation allegations. The sharpest content in the paper; see [[Toda's citation allegations must be checked before we cite Peters]].
- **(iii) Lack of openness to testing by other experts.** Measured by counting how many of Peters's papers appeared in economics journals. This is not a measure of openness but of disciplinary gatekeeping, and it is circular — a field declining to publish you becomes evidence that you are unscientific. Ignore.
- **(iv) Absence of progress.** The Kelly and Latané priority point is correct and the vault already concedes it at [[Nothing in the ergodic theorem fails in geometric Brownian motion]]. Conceding it again costs nothing.
- **(v) Personalization of issues.** Note that §VI personalises — "lack proper training," "ignorance," social-media dissemination — and §II footnote 2 recites the author's credentials at length. The charge and the method are the same. Do not say so in print; the observation is for our own calibration, and returning fire is exactly the trap.
- **(vi) Misleading language.** The complaint that "indiscriminate assumption of ergodicity" is unclear is, awkwardly, fair — and the vault's answer already exists at [[The word ergodic carries four incompatible senses]], which is the note that makes the phrase precise. Our own hygiene work answers this one.

**Method note on the indicators.** A paper invoking Popper's demarcation criterion and then sourcing its operational test from a Wikipedia list has a problem it does not acknowledge. Worth exactly one clause in any reply, and no more; the tone of the source is an invitation to respond in kind, and doing so would forfeit the technical points below.
