# German archives online — the federal access layers

<https://www.archivportal-d.de/> · part of the Deutsche Digitale Bibliothek

**Germany inverts the French pattern, and the inversion is the whole structural fact.**

France has one classification frame and a national archive; the material this project wants is scattered across a hundred departments but described identically everywhere. **Germany has no frame and no national archive for the period at all** — the Bundesarchiv begins with the North German Confederation — and archival competence sits with sixteen *Länder* and, crucially, with the cities.

**And the cities are where the material is.** The Hanse was civic. South German merchant capital was civic. The territorial states of the Holy Roman Empire are not where a merchant's papers ended up; the *Stadtarchiv* is. **So the German answer to "where is the commercial record" is: in about twenty city archives, none of which is subordinate to any of the others.**

## The layers

| Layer | Where |
|---|---|
| National aggregation | Archivportal-D, over the DDB |
| *Land* information systems | each runs its own: **Arcinsys** (Hessen and others), **archive.nrw.de** (North Rhine-Westphalia), the **GDA** for Bavaria, and so on |
| City archives | their own systems again — Hamburg's *ActaPro*, Lübeck, Cologne, Augsburg, Nuremberg |
| Full text | [[Deutsches Zeitungsportal - the German full-text layer]] |

**Archivportal-D is the only thing that sees across all of it**, and what it aggregates is description, not images.

## How this compares

Italy has three unreconciled national systems; Germany has closer to twenty, but they are *tiered* rather than overlapping — each covers a defined jurisdiction, and the aggregator sits above them honestly. **That is a better position than Italy's and a worse one than France's**, and the practical difference is that a German search needs to know which tier the institution belonged to, not which of several competing catalogues to try.

⚠️ Reuse terms, IIIF and bulk access are set institution by institution and are unestablished throughout. Do not generalise from the DDB's own open-data posture to the archives it aggregates.
