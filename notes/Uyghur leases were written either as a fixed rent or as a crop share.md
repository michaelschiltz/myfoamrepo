# Uyghur leases were written either as a fixed rent or as a crop share

**In Mongol-period Turfan the same land market wrote its leases in two forms: the *yaqa* lease, which fixes the rent at signature, and the *anuq* lease, which fixes no rent and divides the harvest.** The choice between a priced fixed claim and an outcome-coupled share is therefore visible *at the level of the instrument*, among the same people and in the same decades — which is what the chronicles behind the *ortoq* cannot show. Tax incidence is written as a separate clause and varies independently of the rent form.

## The corpus

**Thirteen leases, sigla A–M, all in the Berlin Turfan collection** (U 5267–U 5279), seven previously published (USp, Malov, Clark) and six first edited here. Zieme dates them, by prosopography with USp Nr. 14 and the animal-cycle years, to the first half of the fourteenth century, most likely towards its middle; the second half of the thirteenth is the only alternative, and a pre-Mongol date is excluded (Zieme 1980, 204–207). **"Nicht das Material einer örtlich und zeitlich fest bestimmbaren Archivsammlung"** (205): a scatter, not an archive. ⚠️ Leases in the other Turfan collections were, in 1980, absent or unpublished (204).

## The two forms

- **The *yaqa* texts — fixed rent.** The relation is expressed as *yaqaqa tut-*, "receive against rent", and the rent is set at signature in kind or in paper money (Zieme 1980, 214, 217):
  - **A**: 2 *šïq* (grain unspecified);
  - **B**: ½ *šïq* broomcorn millet and 2 [*küri*] sorghum;
  - **D**: 50 *quanpo*;
  - **E**: 10 *tang* of cotton;
  - **G**: 12 *sïtïr* of *čao* (paper money);
  - **M**: amount lost (perhaps *quanpo*).
- **The *anuq* texts — crop share.** No rent is named; the lessor takes a part of the expected harvest (217–219):
  - **C, H, K, and uncertainly J**: the harvest divided 1:1. Formula (C): *bo yirkä näcä uruy badsar ikägü teng ündürüp tarir-biz bulmiš tüš-in teng ülä-sür-biz* — "what seed we put into this land we both raise in equal parts and sow; the fruits obtained we divide equally". **Seed is contributed in the same proportion as the yield is divided.**
  - **L**: 2/5 to the tenant, 3/5 to the lessor for 2 *šïq* of barley, and a further passage dividing 3 *šïq* of barley 1:1.
- ⚠️ **A misprint in the source**: p. 214 lists the *yaqa* texts as "A, B, C, D, E, G und M" against "sechs Urkunden"; the rent table on p. 217 has A, B, D, E, G, M, and C is an *anuq* text on p. 217. Read C as *anuq*. I and J cannot be assigned from the formula (214); F appears in neither list.

**Zieme's parallels**: the half share to the lessor was general under the Tang and Song (Gernet), and a Tibetan document from Dunhuang (BM Or. 8212 (184a)) divides a millet harvest "in halves" (218 nn. 116–117).

## Tax incidence, separately written

- **The lessor bears the taxes in D, E and F**, which Zieme compares with the half-share tenancy of modern East Turkestan (220).
- **The tenant alone bears them in A and B**, with a further clause dividing the "great tax".
- **Divided in C, H, J and perhaps K**: half each. **In L**: two-thirds the tenant, one-third the lessor. Text L (TM 103a, ll. 14–16; p. 238): "if levies fall due on this land, I, Tilik Tuγrul, give two shares; I, Bulda, give one share".
- J: "if levies fall due, we shall both be responsible in equal parts and give in equal parts" (236).

**The fixed-rent form does not settle who carries the fiscal risk**: A and B put the tax on the tenant, D and E on the lessor. Rent form and tax incidence are two characteristics, not one — [[Count degrees of freedom not cells]].

## Co-ownership

Three leases (A, J, K) are made by a lessor who holds the land "in equal shares" (*teng ülüš-lüg*) with a partner: "the land of Qayımtu, which he holds in Čangïq in equal shares with Ïlčï" (A 5; Zieme 1980, 213). **Joint ownership of land is written down; joint venture capital is not.**

## What it does for the comparison

- **It is the documented counterpart to the inferred crop-share at Bamiyan** — [[The Afghan corpora offer loss allocation in tenancy not commenda]] — two centuries later and with the terms on the page.
- **It puts the risk-sharing / risk-pricing choice inside one contract genre**, which is the cleanest test the vault has outside the Mediterranean of whether the choice tracks exposure: [[Scott 1976 on the subsistence ethic as safety-first]].
- **It is not an *ortoq* document.** It confirms that the Turfan contract corpus records tenancy, sale and loan, not commercial partnership — [[The Afghan corpora show partnership as traces because their archives are a landlord's and a granary's]].
- ⚠️ **Thirteen texts, several fragmentary, field sizes unknown**: Zieme says nothing general can be said about rent levels (217). The note claims the existence of the two forms and their clauses, not their frequency.
