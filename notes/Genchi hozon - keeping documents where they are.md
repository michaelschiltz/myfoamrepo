# *Genchi hozon* — keeping documents where they are

**The collecting policy that fixed the Japanese map.** The Ministry of Education's Shiryōkan, founded in May 1951 to rescue historical materials endangered by war and postwar upheaval, **collected originals at first and, from the 1970s, shifted to filming them and leaving them with their holders**, to support preservation in place (the NIJL Archives' own account). The principle is usually called *genchi hozon* 現地保存. ⚠️ The term is the survey literature's; the NIJL page describes the policy without naming it.

## What it does to the evidence

**1. It keeps the record dispersed by design.** Europe's national archives absorbed provincial and private material over two centuries; **Japan decided, deliberately and recently, not to**. The union layer is therefore a catalogue of holders and a microfilm set, not a building.

**2. It makes the microfilm the national copy.** The NIJL Archives list **329 fonds held only on microfilm**, beside 637 owned. **A document filmed in 1975 and later lost in a flood or a house clearance survives as film** — the Ottoman İSAM pattern, at a smaller scale and fifty years earlier.

**3. It makes local custody carry the risk.** A policy of leaving papers in private *kura* and municipal store-rooms places them where earthquakes, tsunami, amalgamation of municipalities and house sales reach them. See [[Shiryo Net and the disaster survival rule]].
