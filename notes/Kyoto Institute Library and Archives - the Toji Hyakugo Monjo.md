# Kyoto Institute, Library and Archives — the *Tōji Hyakugō Monjo*

京都府立京都学・歴彩館. 東寺百合文書WEB: <https://hyakugo.pref.kyoto.lg.jp/>

**About 25,000 documents of the temple Tōji, eighth to eighteenth century, densest in the fourteenth to sixteenth.** Named for the hundred paulownia boxes the daimyō Maeda Tsunanori gave to house them. **Bought from Tōji by Kyoto Prefecture in 1967**; National Treasure 1997; on UNESCO's Memory of the World register. Searchable online by box, era and date. ⚠️ Image completeness and licence not captured in this pass; the site is widely reported as fully imaged under an open licence.

## Why it has a note

**It is the medieval model of what the census's early-modern material lacks**: an institutional archive, kept together for centuries by its creator, transferred whole to a public body, and published whole. **The opposite of the house archive**, and the reason medieval Japanese credit is sometimes better documented than Tokugawa village credit.

## The honest assessment

**Marginal for the census on present knowledge.** No coded row draws on it. Its manor records carry medieval credit, levies and the *tokusei* debt cancellations that `ko_horyuji_50kan`'s clause guards against. ⚠️ Whether any *tanomoshi* material is in it was not searched. **A search here is the cheapest way to test whether the census's two fifteenth-century *kō* rows are typical.**
