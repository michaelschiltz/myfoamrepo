# Historiographical Institute — the Shiryō Hensanjo and the *Dai Nihon* series

東京大学史料編纂所, University of Tokyo. <https://www.hi.u-tokyo.ac.jp/>

**The state's historical compilation office since 1869**, now a university institute. **More than 1,200 volumes of sources published since 1901** in the national series — *Dai Nihon shiryō* (chronological), *Dai Nihon komonjo* (documents), *Dai Nihon kokiroku* (diaries), *Dai Nihon kinsei shiryō* (early-modern). About **200,000 original items** plus its reproductions — including the **Shimazu house documents (15,133 items, National Treasure)** — and a large set of *eishabon* hand-traced copies made from documents in private and temple custody. Databases under the SHIPS portal. ⚠️ Free-access terms for each database not captured.

## What it is for, for the census

**1. It is layer 3 in print for the national compilations**, and the *Dai Nihon kinsei shiryō* carries the Edo town magistrates' *Shoton'ya saikōshirabe* (15 vols) — see [[Kyu-bakufu hikitsugisho - the Edo town magistrate records]].

**2. Its copies are a survival layer.** An *eishabon* traced from a temple's documents a century ago survives if the originals did not. **The institute's copy collection is Japan's older equivalent of İSAM's image archive** — made by hand, by a state office, selected for national history rather than local economy.

**3. The medieval *kō* rows sit nearest it.** `ko_horyuji_50kan`, `ko_meio_1498` and `ko_yamakuni_1555` are coded from Miura 1959, who worked from printed source collections and temple documents of exactly the kind the institute copied. ⚠️ Whether the Hōryūji *kishiki* and the Nihōji shrine *okitegaki* exist in its copies not checked.
