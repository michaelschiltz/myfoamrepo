# Forms are not independent observations when a village runs a portfolio

The census codes one form at a time and counts each as a case. That is sound where the forms come from unrelated communities. It fails where a single village runs several at once, because then the forms are not independent draws — they are components of one arrangement, and the shape of each is conditioned by what the others already cover.

Echigo is the instance to hand. Brown documents *warichi* — periodic reallocation of arable — across the province; Matsunaga documents *tanomoshi-kō* in villages of southern Echigo. Land and credit, two pooling institutions, same province, different scholars, neither aware of the other's object. Add *yui*, the labour-exchange institution general to rural Japan, and the village holds three arrangements **segregated by medium**: land, labour, money. (The third is an inference from the institution's general distribution; no source read here places *yui* in a named Echigo village, and that should be checked before it is asserted.)

**Read as a portfolio, the individual forms look different.** A village that reallocates land periodically has already addressed the risk that a household's holding becomes unworkable; its credit institution therefore need not do that work, and can specialise on liquidity. A village with strong labour exchange can tolerate a landholding pattern that would otherwise be unworkable at the harvest peak. Each institution's coded profile is partly a residue of what the others left undone — which is a statement about the *set*, not about any member of it.

**This is character non-independence with a form-level twin.** The census already caught the characteristic version: `AP3`, `LR1`, `CF2` and `LR6` all separated the *ʿisqa* from the *qirāḍ*, and all four recorded one fact — the manager is a debtor on the loan half. Four cells, one degree of freedom. The same logic applies upward. Three forms coded from one village may express one settlement of how that village handles shortfall, and counting them as three cases inflates *n* exactly as counting four collinear characteristics inflated the evidence there ([[Count degrees of freedom not cells]]).

**A second instance, on the European side.** Scattering fragments a holding across many plots and buys yield-variance reduction at a cost McCloskey puts near a tenth of mean yield. Part of that cost is labour: travel between plots, fragmented supervision, harder mobilisation at the peak. Open-field villages met it with communal ploughing, common grazing and shared work. On that reading scattering and communal labour are **complements** — the first creates the coordination problem the second absorbs — and treating scattering alone as "the insurance institution" mistakes one component for the package. Worth checking against Allen and Clark before asserting, but it follows from McCloskey's own cost estimate.

**What would test it.** Co-occurrence rather than presence: which pooling institutions appear together in the same community, and which never do. The interesting prediction is substitutive — a village lacking one arrangement should show another doing more work, so absence of *warichi* should correlate with something else carrying the incidence problem. That is a claim about distributions across villages and is answerable from Brown's 113-village sample joined to the kō literature, which nobody has attempted because the two literatures do not read each other.

**What this does not license.** It is not an argument against coding forms singly — the alternative, coding villages, would lose everything the characteristic set is for. It is an argument for **recording co-occurrence** so that independence can be assessed rather than assumed, and for refusing to treat forms drawn from one community as independent evidence in any similarity or configurational claim. Description is unaffected; comparative claims are not ([[The morphological matrix is dictated by the shape of the feature space]]).
