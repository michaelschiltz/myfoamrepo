# Princeton Geniza Project — the documentary database

<https://geniza.princeton.edu/> · data: <https://github.com/princetongenizalab/pgp-metadata> and `pgp-text`

**The union catalogue of the documentary Geniza**, running since 1986 and now directed from Princeton's Geniza Lab (Marina Rustow); the current site built by Princeton's Center for Digital Humanities.

## What it holds

From the tabular export of February 2026 (release v1.1):

| Table                            | Records |
|----------------------------------|---------|
| documents                        | 35,855  |
| fragments                        | 36,172  |
| footnotes (links to scholarship) | 24,412  |
| people                           | 1,802   |
| sources                          | 712     |
| places                           | 486     |

**Letters, legal deeds, accounts and lists**, with shelfmarks, dates, descriptions, and **transcriptions and translations for a subset**. ⚠️ The number of documents carrying a transcription was not established; an older figure of 4,600 transcriptions is out of date. **Images are displayed from the holding libraries through IIIF**, not re-hosted. Goitein's research notes and the India Book materials sit in separate repositories (`goitein-notes`, `goitein-india-traders`) not examined.

## Why it matters more than its size

**It is the only place the Geniza exists as documents rather than fragments.** A PGP record is a *document*, possibly spread over several fragments in several libraries, typed and dated. **That is the unit a frequency could be counted over** — the Geniza's nearest thing to a series.

**It is also the Geniza's layer 3 made by scholars, not machines**, and **its data are downloadable** — updated with every database change. In the European survey only the Sound Toll registers offered a genuine denominator; **this is the second, with the caveats the deposit imposes**: see [[Cairo Geniza - the deposit and its survival rule]].

## Rights — the column is not settled

**The site footer shows CC BY; the export's README says CC BY-NC 4.0.** For a project that will publish derived data, **the non-commercial clause matters**. ⚠️ Treat as **CC BY-NC** until the Lab confirms.

## A correction the grant needs

**Decision_log A5 lists "Geniza materials via Princeton Geniza Project" under database access**, as though a licence were needed. **None is.** The database, the export and the images of the JTS collection are free. **A5 can drop the line**; what the Geniza needs is reading time, not subscriptions.
