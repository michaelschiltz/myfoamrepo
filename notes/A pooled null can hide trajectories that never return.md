# A pooled null can hide trajectories that never return

Nguyen, Brakman, Garretsen and Kohl (2025) rebuild the West German bombing case with synthetic controls for 53 cities. Between 50 and 70 per cent of the cities mean-revert; the remainder behave as unit roots, with positive and negative impacts roughly balanced. A pooled coefficient on the same cities reads as mean reversion. The ensemble returns; a third of the trajectories do not, and their deviations cancel in the average.

That is the time–ensemble distinction in its plainest empirical form, and it disqualifies a class of candidates for the survivor case. Miguel and Roland (2011) — no long-run local poverty traps from the American bombing of Vietnam — is a pooled cross-sectional null at a single date, which is precisely the design in which opposite trajectories are invisible. Yamada and Yamada (2021) find no robust long-run effect of the same campaign in southern Laos but negative effects in the north forty years on, which is what a pooled null over heterogeneous components would conceal.

A null persistence coefficient is therefore not evidence that the sub-process is ergodic. It is consistent with ergodicity, and equally with a decomposition whose components drift in opposite directions. See [[Ergodicity is indecomposability not randomness]].
