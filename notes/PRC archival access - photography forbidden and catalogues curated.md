# PRC archival access — photography forbidden and catalogues curated

**The access regime for mainland Chinese archives, as practitioners report it.** Principal source: Nasser and Foreman, *The Chinese Archive: A Pocket Manual* (CLIR, 2023); with Carter 2020.

## The rules as reported

- **Photography "strictly forbidden"** at PRC archives, with rare exceptions.
- **Photocopying capped**: at the First Historical Archives, **twenty documents per research trip, one trip per calendar year**; municipal archives set daily and monthly page limits.
- **Transcription by hand is the only reliable method**; the manual advises doubling the time one would plan for a US archive.
- **Letters of introduction** from a Chinese institution, and in some localities from a government authority.
- **The Foreign Ministry Archive** closed after 2013 and reopened with about 90 per cent of earlier material redacted; **provincial archives such as Sichuan's removed post-1949 records from public access**.

## The one that matters most for a register

**"Digitisation has the added benefit from the state's perspective of erasing any proof that restricted documents exist."** A paper catalogue lists closed fonds as closed; a digital system simply does not show them. Files consulted at the Beijing Municipal Archives in 2018 were not available in 2023. **In a curated digital catalogue, a null is not even a fact about the catalogue** — it may be a fact about what was withdrawn. The rule at [[Refusals are observations of the filter not inferences from survivors]] applies with a twist: **the filter can be changed after the observation.**

## Scope for HistorEE

**Most of this bears on post-1949 material, and the Qing record is less sensitive.** The First Historical Archives has admitted foreign researchers since about 1980, with closures for borders, nationalities and medical prescriptions. **But the photography and copy rules apply to Qing documents too**, and they make a Beijing archive a transcription site, not a capture site. ⚠️ Current practice at the First Historical Archives after the 2022 move not checked with the archive.

**The alternatives the manual names are the ones this survey found for the census**: Taiwan, "easily accessible, friendly, and welcoming"; Hong Kong; overseas collections; and **the market for documents** — see [[Chinese contracts - the private document boom and the market that feeds it]].
