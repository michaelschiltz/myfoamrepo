# Archief Ieper — one charter and five accounts

<https://archief.ieper.be/>

**The most extreme archival loss in the European survey, and it should be in the register as a methodological case rather than as a repository.**

## What happened

The belfry tower in which the Ypres archive was kept **burned on 22 November 1914**. Of the old city archive of one of the great medieval cloth towns of Flanders, the modern municipal archive holds **one charter and five eighteenth-century accounts**. Fragments survive from some surrounding villages, and most of the Charitable Institutions archive came through. The archive today manages over six kilometres of material, but it is a **reconstructed** collection, not the pre-war one.

## Why it belongs in this hub

Ypres was a cloth town of the first rank with a documented civic and commercial life from the thirteenth century. **Its absence from any comparative corpus is total, and it has nothing whatever to do with the institutions it once documented.**

This is the same structure recorded at [[Archivio di Stato di Napoli]] and it is worth stating twice because the discipline is unnatural. [[Refusals are observations of the filter not inferences from survivors]] treats absence as evidence about the filter — but a filter must have a *shape* for that reading to work. **A fire has no shape.** Flemish gaps of this kind license neither an inference about the past nor an inference about selection, and they must be declared rather than reasoned from.

The practical consequence: **any claim about the geographical distribution of a form in the Low Countries has a hole at Ypres that cannot be estimated.** Where a form is attested at Bruges and Ghent and not at Ypres, that is not evidence about Ypres. Say so in the text rather than letting a map imply otherwise.

## The second-order point

The survival of the **Charitable Institutions** archive while the civic archive burned is itself a selection: what remains of Ypres is disproportionately its charitable and welfare record. A researcher working the surviving material would conclude that charitable institutions loomed unusually large in Ypres, and would be describing a fire.
