# The land comanda gave way to the censal after 1348

Fynn-Paul's Manresa series has the land comanda at its height c. 1280–1348, more than halving after the Black Death, and slowing to a trickle by the 1380s ([[Fynn-Paul 2017 on the land commenda of Manresa]], 102–103, fig. 4).

**His explanation.** Interest rates in Catalonia fell from 15–20 per cent before 1348 towards 5 per cent after the 1370s. A third of the profit stopped being worth the commendator's risk. Meanwhile municipal and royal *censals* and *violaris* absorbed savings, in denominations too large for small investors (102–106). On his account small savers lost an instrument and were not given another. He sets this against Kedar's and Aurell's mentality explanations (103 n. 43).

**Why it matters here.** This is a substitution case. A contract sharing profit and loss gives way to an annuity with a fixed claim on a public debtor. The participation was also structured differently on the two sides: merchants split their capital into units of about 100 s. across several merchants, while rustics and tradesmen placed theirs with one (100–101). That is the spread-against-concentrate distinction the project's time-average argument turns on, recorded as behaviour rather than as theory.

**Cautions.** The series is two families' notarial books, which survive and were kept for a reason, so this is survivorship. The interest-rate series (fig. 5) is his own, from Barcelona council records and Manresa books. The 91 commendas mix deposits with trading comandas ([[Comanda names several contracts in the Catalan sources]]). The decline is therefore a decline in a word's frequency until the series is split by type.
