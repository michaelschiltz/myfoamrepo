# Acta Turcarum — the Ottoman series at Dubrovnik

At [[Drzavni arhiv u Dubrovniku]], reference code `HR-DADU-07.2`, within the fonds *Acta et diplomata* (`HR-DADU-07`). **161 boxes, 17 linear metres, about 15,000 documents, fifteenth to nineteenth century.** The count the Croatian pass recorded is confirmed; what that pass could not establish is below.

## The arrangement

Four subseries, and the rest unclassified:

- `7.2.1` — sultans' documents: *ahdnames*, *fermans*, *berats*
- `7.2.2` — orders of the Bosnian governors and the Herzegovinian *sancakbeys* (*buyuruldus*)
- `7.2.3` — letters of governors to the Ragusan authorities
- `7.2.4` — official documents of *kadis* — see [[Kadijski spisi - the kadi documents at Dubrovnik]]

**The unclassified remainder is the largest single fact about this series.** The arrangement above covers the diplomatic and judicial acts; the commercial paper of Ragusan merchants inside the empire, if it is here, is most likely in what nobody has arranged. ⚠️ Share of the 15,000 that sits outside the four subseries not established.

## Finding aids

- The working inventory *Acta Turcarum* by **Fehim Efendić and Jovan Perović, 1930s**, partly summary and partly analytical. Efendić's cataloguing of 1933–1941 is what gave the series its name and its present shape.
- **Vesna Miović's analytical inventory of `7.2.4`, 2019**, published by the archive — the one subseries described to item level on current standards.
- The **eighteenth-century dragomans' own indexes**, which are themselves part of the record: *Indice degl'Arzi* (268 petitions), *Indice dei Hoggetti* (348 *hüccets*), *L'indice delle Buiuruldi* (159), *Materie col'Indice* (1785). See [[The Dubrovnik Ottoman documents in three scripts]].

Description from the JArchiveS survey platform, <https://arhiv.jas-center.eu/>, which also records Armenian and Hebrew script and Ladino among the series' languages — a reminder that Ragusa's Ottoman correspondents included the empire's Jewish and Armenian merchants.

## The survival rule is written down

This is the thing the European survey taught to look for, and here it exists. Miović and Selmani 2007 reconstruct the chain:

- The documents were kept by the **Turkish Chancery** (*Turska kancelarija*, *Secretaria Turca* from the 1730s) in the Rector's Palace, which grew out of the Slavonic Chancery.
- **Losses after the fall of the Republic in 1808**, through the French administration and the years of dispersal, and a **fire in March 1817**.
- **About one-sixth sent to the Imperial archives in Vienna in 1833**; about **860 documents sent to Zadar in 1836** as *Carte Turche*. The Vienna material returned after the Second World War; nine misfiled boxes were later retrieved from Zadar.
- **About five-sixths never left Dubrovnik.**

**So the gap has a shape and the shape is published.** A later researcher who finds a thin decade can ask whether it went to Vienna, rather than inferring that nothing was written. ⚠️ Whether every Vienna transfer came back is not established by what was read.

## The Istanbul side

The Porte kept its own register of the relationship. See [[Duvel-i Ecnebiye - the Istanbul side of the Ragusan relationship]]: the same privileges, entered by the issuing chancery, in a series that has already been set against this one — and the two do not agree.
