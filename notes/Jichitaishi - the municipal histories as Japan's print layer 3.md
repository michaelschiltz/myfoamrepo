# *Jichitaishi* — the municipal histories as Japan's print layer 3

自治体史: histories of prefectures, cities, towns and villages, compiled by committees of academic historians appointed by the local government, typically in **narrative volumes (通史編) and source volumes (資料編/史料編)** that transcribe the locality's documents from the earliest records to the modern period.

## Why this is the most important layer in Japan

**Because it is where Japanese village and merchant documents are at layer 3**, and there are a great many of them. A source volume is a selected, transcribed, annotated edition of documents that in the original sit in private *kura*, municipal store-rooms and school cupboards — see [[Japanese archives - custody follows the house]]. **Where Europe's layer 3 is a handful of machine-transcribed corpora and Japan's archival digitisation stops at books, Japan's documents are transcribed in print, district by district, across the whole country.** ⚠️ Total number of titles not established; the national bibliographies of local histories (全国地方史誌総目録) are the place to count.

## The funding map, municipal edition

**What is transcribed is what a municipality paid to have transcribed, when it paid, and what its committee chose.** Three consequences:

- **Selection by committee.** A source volume prints what the editors judged representative or important. **A *kō* rulebook is in it if an editor thought credit associations mattered to the town's history.**
- **Selection by date.** The compilation boom — broadly the postwar decades — fixed which documents were found, filmed and transcribed. Documents that surfaced afterwards, like the Chōkōji-mura papers found in a ceiling, may never have been.
- **Selection by amalgamation.** A town merged out of existence may have no history, or a history of the larger unit.

**Every Japanese row in the census was coded from a printed transcription or a historian's quotation of one** — Miura from 『旧高野領内文書』 and 『丹波国山国庄史料』; the Echigo and Settsu rows from articles working local source collections. **The census's Japanese evidence is a sample of Japan's local-history editing, not of Japan's *kō*.**

## Access

- **Many are out of commerce, and so behind [[NDL transmission service for individuals - the residency wall]].**
- **Some municipalities pay to publish their histories free online** — for example through the NPO Saitama SOHO's 自治体史デジタルアーカイブシステム, at ¥850,000 for the system and about ¥1 million per thousand pages of text. **Free for readers; paid for by the town; so available where the town paid.**
