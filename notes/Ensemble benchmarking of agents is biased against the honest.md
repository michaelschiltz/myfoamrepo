# Ensemble benchmarking of agents is biased against the honest

The time/ensemble distinction enters the agency problem twice. Once in the deterrent, where the folk theorem's continuation value is an expectation over possible futures while what binds an agent is his own realised path. And once here, in the *benchmark* — which is the less obvious entry and the more damaging one.

A principal judges an agent by comparing his realised result against the spread of results across agents. That is an **ensemble** operation. The agent's exposure, meanwhile, runs **along his own trajectory**. Under multiplicative dynamics those two quantities are precisely the ones that diverge: the ensemble average exceeds what the typical trajectory attains, and the gap is the volatility drag.

So an agent benchmarked against the ensemble mean **underperforms it in the typical case, with no misconduct whatever**. The principal, applying a standard drawn from the wrong average, reads ordinary trajectory-level bad luck as underperformance or worse. Reputation under non-ergodicity is therefore not merely a weak instrument. It is a **biased** one, generating false positives for misconduct at a rate that rises with the drag.

The consequence for selection is worse than noise. Entrustment is withdrawn from agents whose trajectories were merely typical, and retained by those whose trajectories were lucky or whose appearances were managed. Selection continues, and it selects for the wrong thing — the same failure mode reached by a different route in [[Decoupling substitutes proxy legibility for outcome legibility]], and one that [[Outcome-coupling is the condition of legibility]] does not by itself avoid.

## Two institutional responses the framework predicts

**Benchmark the agent against his own past rather than against his peers** — substitute a time average for an ensemble average. Long relationships, repeated entrustment, cumulative record, the preference for a known correspondent over a better-performing stranger. On this reading the persistence of long dyadic ties in pre-modern trade is not merely a solution to information cost; it is the correct estimator.

**Refuse to infer fault from loss at all**, and shift the question to conduct. This is exactly what the *amāna*/*ḍamān* structure does: the *muḍārib* holds the capital on trust and is not liable for loss as such, but only where he was negligent or exceeded instructions — *taʿaddī*, *taqṣīr*, *mukhālafa*. The doctrine institutionalises a refusal to read misconduct off outcomes. See [[Skin in the game is asymmetric in the mudaraba]].

That second reading is worth pressing. The received explanation of the negligence-and-instructions doctrine is that it restores the agent's thin skin in the game. The reading here is complementary and sharper: under ruin-dominated dynamics an outcome-based liability rule would convict honest agents at a high rate, so the tradition moved the test from the outcome to the conduct. The fiqh is not compensating for weak incentives; it is declining an inference it had reason to distrust.
