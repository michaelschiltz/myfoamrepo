# Pooling's sign is set by the correlation of its baskets

Hamilton's cooperation–synchrony paradox and the risk-pooling result at the core of ergodicity economics look contradictory only until pooling is disaggregated. Hamilton: pooling *raises* synchrony and inflates aggregate volatility. The insurance result ([[Skin in the game]], Peters–Adamou): pooling is precisely what lifts each agent's [[Time-average|time-average]] growth. Both hold, because pooling is not one operation. Its sign on aggregate volatility is fixed entirely by the pairwise correlation ρ of the exposures pooled.

Under the synchrony-extended variance Var(r|N) = (s²/N)[1 + (N−1)ρ], pooling across independent baskets (ρ→0) recovers the law of large numbers and averages risk away; pooling across a shared exposure (ρ→1) averages nothing and merely relabels N members as a single unit. The same act — aggregating capital or fate under one decision-maker — desynchronizes or synchronizes according to what sits inside the baskets. The insurance result already encodes the constraint: pooling raises the time-average growth rate *only* when the parties' fluctuations are partly independent. Cooperation across a common environment is Hamilton's trap; diversification across decorrelated ventures is the escape. They are the same equation read at opposite ends of ρ. Lower ρ is bought, not given: it is deliberate desynchronization, and via the [[Jensen gap]] it raises the time-average by capping the variance that would otherwise drag a multiplicative trajectory toward the [[Absorbing barrier|absorbing barrier]].

The Fugger *konzern* is the historical anchor. Capital split across several baskets — Tyrolean and Hungarian mining, Antwerp exchange, papal transfers — rather than concentrated in one. Read correctly this is not a scaling strategy but a desynchronization strategy: they purchased a lower ρ and paid for it in forgone scale, because in a personal-capital regime genuine decorrelation is bounded by what kin and factors can monitor. This reframes the price of asynchrony as a monitoring limit, not a scale limit.

> **Verify / candidate split.** The Fugger baskets plausibly shared a concentrated counterparty — Habsburg sovereign credit — so their *activity* was diversified while their *absorbing barrier* was not; the Spanish suspensions from 1557 would then have struck every basket at once. If so, the Fugger illustrate the intention of desynchronization and the failure mode of counterparty concentration simultaneously. Worth its own note (working title: *The Fugger diversified activity while concentrating the barrier*), and a check against Harris's chapter.

The corollary belongs to the shielding axis: staying small-and-decorrelated (the family firm) and scaling-behind-firewalls (the shielded corporation) are two institutional solutions to one dimensional-collapse problem — one pays in scale, the other in the legal machinery of the partition.
