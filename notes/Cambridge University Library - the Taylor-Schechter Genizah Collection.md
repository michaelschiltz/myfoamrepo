# Cambridge University Library — the Taylor-Schechter Genizah Collection

<https://cudl.lib.cam.ac.uk/collections/genizah/1> · Taylor-Schechter Genizah Research Unit, <https://www.lib.cam.ac.uk/collections/departments/taylor-schechter-genizah-research-unit>

**About 193,000 fragments** removed by Solomon Schechter in 1896–97 — nearly half the Geniza, and the largest single collection of medieval Jewish manuscripts anywhere. Hebrew, Judaeo-Arabic, Aramaic and Arabic.

**Two further Cambridge collections:**

- **The Jacques Mosseri Collection, about 7,000 fragments**, on a twenty-year loan since 2006 — so due to end in 2026. ⚠️ Whether the loan has been renewed or converted was not established, and the census's Geniza literature cites Mosseri shelfmarks.
- **The Lewis-Gibson Collection, about 1,700 fragments**, formerly at Westminster College, bought jointly by Cambridge and the Bodleian. ⚠️ The library's page gives 2014; other accounts give 2013.

## Layer

- **Layer 2, and nearly complete.** Cambridge Digital Library states that **all Taylor-Schechter items and the Genizah-derived items in the Or. and Add. series are online**, with about 4,000 Mosseri items. **IIIF manifests** are published. ⚠️ An older page of the Research Unit's gives "more than 21,000 fragments" online; the collection page is the more recent statement. ⚠️ Image licence not captured.
- **Layer 1:** item descriptions by the Genizah Research Unit, founded in the 1970s for conservation, cataloguing and research.
- **Layer 3:** by machine, through Ktiv — see [[MiDRASH - the machine transcription of the Geniza]] — and for the documentary share, selectively, through [[Princeton Geniza Project - the documentary database]].

## What it holds for the census

**The documentary classes are where the partnership instruments are.** Ackerman-Lieberman cites T-S 10J and T-S NS shelfmarks among his partnership agreements. ⚠️ The division of the T-S classes into documentary and literary was not reconstructed in this pass; Goitein's apparatus is the place to do it.

## The honest assessment

**The one Geniza trip that could be justified — and it is not yet justified.** With the collection imaged and machine-read, a visit answers physical questions only: a verso, a join, an erasure, an Arabic text under a Hebrew one. **Plan the reading from the desk and the visit for what the images cannot show.**
