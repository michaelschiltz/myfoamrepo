# The Afghan corpora show partnership as traces because their archives are a landlord's and a granary's

**No partnership instrument survives in the medieval Afghan documentary corpora: no *sharika*, no *muḍāraba* or *qirāḍ*, no Hebrew-script deed of association, no *muzāraʿa*. Partnership is attested only as traces — a legal query that presupposes it, levy entries "with partner", fractional co-ownership, joint debtors.** The absence is a fact about what the find contexts could hold, not about what Bamiyan and Ghur practised. Reading it as absence of the institution is the deficit reading: [[The deficit reading of absence is the scalar ranking in evidentiary form]].

## What the archives are

- **Bamiyan Papers, Group I (395–430/1005–1039)**: the papers of one Jewish landlord and merchant, Abū Naṣr Yehuda b. Daniel, and his son Siman-Ṭov. *Iqrār* deeds of debts owed *to* him, Judaeo-Persian letters, account books (Ms. Heb. 8333.205–207, 8333.220), one lease of three parcels (8333.15, 430/1039). The account books record some ten plots against nearly a hundred peasants' names, mostly as debtors for seed, loans and grain; Haim reads Yehuda as landowner, grain merchant, moneylender and possibly tax-collector (Haim 2023, 206, 210–216, n. 26). **A creditor's file** (Haim 2019, 72–73). Haim himself once suggested that debtors who recur in the account books **"were perhaps commercial partners (*shurakā-yi tijārī*) of Abū Naṣr b. Dāniyāl, or had taken land from him on hire"** (Haim 2019, 417: "longtime business partners of Abū Naṣr b. Dāniyāl or … they leased lands from him"; Persian trans., 83). ⚠️ A conjecture from name recurrence, not a document.
- **Bamiyan Papers, Group II (549–618/1155–1221)**: nearly ninety sheets connected to two storehouse managers (*anbār-dār*) of the royal granary (*anbār-i khāṣṣa*), Abū Bakr b. ʿUmar (1155–1185) and ʿAlī b. Abū Bakr (1206–1219), forty-seven of them issued by the pair: tax receipts (*barāt*), payment orders, *iqrār*s, correspondence with *shiḥna* and diwan, lists; over twenty receipts from 1169 alone. The granary also **managed loans to the rural population**. **A fiscal file** (Haim 2019, 74–76; Haim 2023, 206–207; Ludden and Azad 2024).
- **Firuzkuh Papers (573–618/1177–1221)**: about a hundred documents in New Persian — petitions to landlords who are probably *iqṭāʿ* holders, levy lists, sales. **A seigneurial and fiscal file.**
- **Khalili Persian documents from Istīw (593–617/1197–1220)**: sixteen, mostly legal — sale of land, marriage, loans (Haim 2023, 203–204). ⚠️ Not checked.
- **Khalili Bactrian documents (629–771/772)**, list A–P: marriage contract, receipts, deed of gift, manumission, purchase or lease of estates, loans of grain and wine, undertakings to keep the peace, slave sale. No partnership. ⚠️ Q–Y not checked.

**None is a merchant's partnership file and none is a *qāḍī*'s register.** A partnership deed would survive here only if a partner kept the archive, or if the sheet were reused as scrap — the route by which Arabic-script documents survive in the Cairo Geniza ([[Cairo Geniza - the deposit and its survival rule]]). Reuse is common in Group II, so the second route is open. It has not yet produced a partnership deed.

## What the traces are

- **IEDCID1188** (Ms. Heb. 8333.102): the only item the Invisible East typology files as *Legal: Partnership*. An *istiftāʾ* on whether the other partners' testimony stands once one partner is a litigant. It presupposes a commercial partnership and is not one. Ed. Ofir Haim, unpublished. ⚠️ Read through a summary.
- **IEDCID248** (Firuzkuh 60) and other levy lists (IEDCID240, 264, 275, 1154): entries "with partner" (*sharīk*). ⚠️ Whether joint cultivation or joint liability for the levy is not established.
- **Ms. Heb. 8333.78** (Muḥarram 565/1169; Azad 2025, Doc. 2): "The partners (*shurakā*) of Būtiyān have delivered the statutory grain [tax] to the state warehouse" — 672 *mann*, with five named contributors below: 130, 225, 237, 60 and 20 *mann*, which sum to the total. **A joint fiscal obligation with its internal shares written down.** Azad reads *shurakā* as the representatives of a community or village; another farmer of Būtiyān paid alone in the same month (Ms. Heb. 8333.168) (Azad 2025, 213–215, 154–155). Groups of farmers also appear as the "*mardumān* of Ghūr-i Karūd" and eleven men of Kafshān; Azad asks whether they were agricultural cooperatives and leaves it open (154–155).
- **Ms. Heb. 8333.145** (undated, Phase II): **eleven partners (*sharīkān*) sold hay for a total of 100 silver dinars**, amounts itemised per partner; the legible names include Abū Bakr Sarikhān, ʿAlī b. Muḥammad-i Lang, Khwāja Sharaf, Khwāja ʿUthmān and Ḥusayn Ṣarrāf (Azad 2025, 121–122). A joint sale with separate contributions; the terms between the partners are not stated.
- **IEDCID260** (Firuzkuh 14, 1169): a widow sells her half of a yearling bull to her co-owner. **IEDCID111/112** (Firuzkuh 7–8, 1195/1207): one-sixth of a bathhouse sold, then a debt secured on the share. ⚠️ Read through summaries.
- **Ms. Heb. 8333.216** (401/1011; two debtors; 7 *shiyānī*) and **8333.217** (409/1018; three debtors; 75 *shiyānī*): several debtors in one *iqrār* (Haim 2019, 78). ⚠️ Joint or several liability is for the edition to settle.
- **IEDCID1281** = BL Or. 8212/166 (Du1), Judaeo-Persian, Dandan-Uiliq near Khotan, late eighth century; a fragment of at least three pages, 38 lines surviving. "Please give his three shares (*rasad*). [Please give] that of mine" (l. 4); "they understood my profit (*sūd*) and loss (*ziyān*)" (l. 14); "they bought all the sheep on my behalf" (l. 15); "It is a heavy loss" (l. 17); "if for me the profit shall have been the fate" (l. 20); "don't worry about (your) own account" (l. 21). Local sheep trade; the recipient addresses the sender as "Rabbi" (Zhang Zhan 2022–23, 105–134, esp. 110–112). **Shares, profit and loss in a joint purchase, but the form of the arrangement is not stated.** Not from Afghanistan.

**The tenancy negative needs qualifying.** Azad writes that the corpus "is devoid of such contracts" and lacks land deeds, cadastral surveys and tax-rate inventories (Ludden and Azad 2024, PDF pp. 12, 17). But Phase I holds at least one written lease, **Ms. Heb. 8333.15** (430/1039), edited by Haim (2019 *JRAS*, 432–434) and discussed in Haim 2023, 211–212: see [[The Afghan corpora offer loss allocation in tenancy not commenda]]. The negative holds, at most, for Phase II and Firuzkuh; for partnership it stands.

## The same shape further east

**The Old Uyghur contracts are typed Sa, Lo, Pl, Ad — sale, loan, pledge, adoption** ([[Old Uyghur contracts - the documentary layer under the ortoq]]). ⚠️ Whether any SUK type is a trading partnership is still unestablished; the check is the vol. 2 type table and the glossary under *ortaq*. **The *ortoq* itself rests on chronicles**: Enkhbold 2019 reads Juvaynī, Rashīd al-Dīn, Vaṣṣāf and the Song envoys Peng Daya and Xu Ting, not documents ([[The Silk Road record is documents and the census codes chronicles]]).

**From Bactria to Turfan the documentary record is sale, loan, lease, pledge and receipt; partnership lives in law and in narrative.** The asymmetry recurs because the archives that were kept are households', landlords' and treasuries', and it is a property of the filter.

## Consequence

- **No *commenda* or *qirāḍ* coding can be drawn from these corpora**, and none should be promised in Part 1 or Part 2.
- What they can supply is the subject of [[The Afghan corpora offer loss allocation in tenancy not commenda]].
- The negative is a datum for [[Refusals are observations of the filter not inferences from survivors]]: a filter whose composition is known — a creditor's file, a granary's file — predicts which genres are missing.
