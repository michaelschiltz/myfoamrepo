# The record of non-survivors survives where failure was administered

**Survivorship bias in the archive is not a constant of the past. It is a function of whether a jurisdiction maintained a procedure for winding up failed bodies, and whether that procedure's own archive survived.** Where somebody held an enforceable claim on a failing enterprise, failure produced a *better* record than success — because an officer impounded the papers.

This is the general statement of what the archive survey found case by case, and it qualifies the survey's own seventh pattern, that failure is emptiest.

## The instance that makes the claim concrete

[[Insolvente Boedelskamer - Antwerp]]: the city required the papers of **insolvent** merchants and firms to be impounded to protect creditors, and the result is **about 204 linear metres, over 3,000 fonds from 1518 onwards, more than 150 trading enterprises, with complete firm archives preserved whole.** [[Desolate Boedelkamer - Amsterdam]] is the same institution in the other city.

**Read that against the ordinary case.** A successful early modern firm's papers survive if the family kept them, if no fire took them, if a descendant gave them to an archive — the conditions of [[Japanese archives - custody follows the house]]. **A failed Antwerp firm's papers survive because a magistrate seized them.** The failures are in better order than the successes, and they are in one place with a finding aid.

## Three generators, and they are not equally good

**1. Somebody had a claim — insolvency chambers, probate and orphan chambers, estate inventories.** The papers are impounded *as evidence of what is owed*, so **the firm's own books come in with them**. This is the only generator that preserves the running record of a non-survivor.

**2. The state was the claimant — confiscation and liquidation.** [[Orta sandigi records - the guild and Janissary chests]]: after the abolition of 1826 the Janissary chests' assets went to the Treasury, **and the liquidation created the surviving series** — inventory and auction registers, with the loan book surviving chiefly as the debts outstanding in June 1826, recorded by the confiscating creditor. **What survives is the liquidator's summary, not the institution's own account of itself.**

**3. Nobody had a claim and the climate did the work — abandonment.** The sealed cave, the desert fort, the warehouse: [[Silk Road documents - one site's archive in twelve institutions]], [[Cairo Geniza - the deposit and its survival rule]], the Batavia council's papers found in a Jakarta warehouse in 1995 ([[Leiden University Library - the Kong Koan archive of Batavia]]). **Systematic in no respect; a lottery whose prizes are extraordinary.**

**And the control case, where no generator operated.** [[Chettiar banking - the enquiry is public and the account books are not]]: a financial system of some 2,500 firms across three countries collapsed in the 1930s with **no insolvency chamber impounding anything**, and its ledgers are gone. **Same kind of failure, no administrator, no archive.**

## Why this matters to this project more than to most

**The project's dynamics are organised around an absorbing barrier, and the standing complaint of [[Persistence samples are conditioned on non-absorption]] is that the available evidence is evidence about units that did not hit it.** An insolvency archive is a **serial, inventoried register of absorption events with the absorbed units' own papers inside**. There is no other source class in the survey of which that is true.

It also reaches a specific hole: **[[The creditor limb of entity shielding has no witness]]**. A chamber that ranks claimants in a concrete estate cannot avoid the creditor limb, because ranking them is its business.

## Three cautions, and the second is the one that bites

**1. The selection is inverted, not removed.** A bankruptcy series is conditioned on failure as tightly as the persistence literature is conditioned on survival. **Its use is as the complement of a sample whose other half is everywhere**, and any rate — a hazard, a failure probability — needs a denominator that the chamber does not hold: how many firms there were.

**2. The record is made at the winding-up and for the winding-up.** Valuations are liquidation values; inventories are snapshots taken at the worst moment; correspondence is retained for what it proves about debts. **A cell coded from such a series may be a cell about liquidation rather than about the going concern** — the objection already recorded against coding the Janissary chest's loan book, and the standing check for anything coded from this source class.

**3. Cross-jurisdiction counts confound the institution with its archive.** More recorded failures in Antwerp than in Lyon or Osaka may mean only that **Antwerp had a boedelkamer**. The presence of an insolvency procedure is itself the variable, and a comparison that does not hold it fixed is measuring the procedure.

## What follows for the survey and for the coding

- **A standing question for every regional pass**: did this jurisdiction administer failure — insolvency, confiscation, probate — and where is that administration's archive? **Asked and answered, the region's "failure is emptiest" verdict is replaced by a location.** ⚠️ Not yet asked systematically outside Europe; the Ottoman *muhallefat* series and the estate inventories in the *şer'iyye sicilleri* are the obvious next place, and were not examined.
- **Pattern 7 is qualified, not overturned.** Institutions that ended without a claimant leave nothing — the guild that dissolved, the *kō* that completed its cycle, the association whose members simply stopped meeting. **What the boedelkamers show is that the class "non-survivor" is not uniformly dark: it has a lit corridor wherever creditor rights were enforced.**
