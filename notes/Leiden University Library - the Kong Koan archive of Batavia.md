# Leiden University Library — the Kong Koan archive of Batavia

吧城公館檔案. Leiden University Libraries, UBL209. <https://collectionguides.universiteitleiden.nl/resources/ubl209>; scans at <https://digitalcollections.universiteitleiden.nl/kongkoanpapers>

**The archive of the Chinese Council of Batavia — the body of Chinese officers (*majoor*, *kapitein*, *luitenant*) through which the Dutch administered the city's Chinese** — 646 volumes, 13 metres, 1761–1968, bulk 1774–1949. **Found in a warehouse in Jakarta in 1995**, entrusted to Leiden, transferred to the University Library in 2016.

## The homonym trap, stated first

**公館 *kong koan* is not 公司 *kongsi*.** The census's `gongsi` row already warns that *gongsi* is also the modern word for "company"; **the Hokkien romanisations of the Council and of the Bornean mining federations differ by one syllable and are routinely confused in catalogues and search results.** The Kong Koan was **a colonial administrative council**, not a shared-capital venture and not a native-place association. **Nothing here evidences `gongsi`, and nothing here should be coded under `huiguan`.**

## What is in it

- **Council minutes**, 1787–1920
- **Marriage registers**, 1772–1919, and marriage documents from 1761
- **Cemetery registers**, 1811–1935, and **grave purchase records**, 1840–1905
- **Temple records**, including the Kim Tek Ie temple's donations and accounts, 1875–1907
- **Financial ledgers and annual accounts**, 1812–1945
- **Population and immigration registration**, 1852–1913

In Dutch and Chinese.

## Layers and access

- **Layer 2, the whole archive**: microfilmed 2001–2003, **scanned 2012–2013 under Metamorfoze, and online**. **No access limitations**; originals by the curator's permission.
- **Layer 3**: none beyond the published minutes projects. ⚠️ Blussé and Chen, *The Archives of the Kong Koan of Batavia* (Brill, Sinica Leidensia 59, 2003) is the guide; the minutes have been published in Chinese in a multi-volume series whose extent was not checked.
- **Script**: nineteenth-century handwritten Chinese and Dutch. **The binding constraint is hand, not access.**

## The honest assessment

**Marginal for the census's existing rows, and the best-served Chinese institutional archive in the survey.** Its value is as **the one place where a Chinese community body's burial and temple finances survive in series, imaged and free**: cemetery registers, grave purchases, a temple's donations and accounts. **If a future batch asks whether Chinese community burial grounds were funded by fees, donations or levies, this is the cheapest archive to read in the world.** It does not bear on the census's share and partnership rows at all.

**Successor custody again, with a twist**: **a colonial intermediary's archive, abandoned in its own city and rescued to the former colonial power's university.**
