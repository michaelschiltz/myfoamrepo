# Under absorption the time-average criterion is an ordering not a maximand

The project's settled position is a single objective throughout: variance suppression and ruin avoidance are expressions of one criterion pursued under absorbing-barrier dynamics, not different goals. Vanhoyweghen, Verbeken and Ginis give that position formal backing, but **what they derive is a lexicographic ordering, not a scalar**: growth weighted by survival where escape is possible; expected time to absorption where it is certain; finite-horizon hitting probability where neither is defined; and, as the cost of ruin grows without bound, ruin probability first (§6). A lexicographic order on $\mathbb{R}^n$ has no real-valued representation, so "single maximand" is formally false in exactly the regime the project cares about.

## Consequence for the text

Write *one objective* or *a single ordering principle*, never *single maximand*. Part 1, Objective 2 was changed accordingly on 2026-11-20 ("one objective pursued against different binding barriers"), and Part 2 §0 cites Vanhoyweghen, Verbeken and Ginis 2026 for the ruin-avoidance clause. A mathematician on the panel would have caught the old wording.

## Historical gain

The limiting case is the useful one. When ruin dominates, the ordering reduces to minimising ruin probability, which is what rule-based ruin avoidance in household codes does: a rule that forbids a class of ventures outright is a lexicographic preference, not a high weight on variance. The Edo merchant house codes are the obvious material [verify which codes state such prohibitions before citing any]. The objective does not change; the regime changes which term of the ordering binds.
