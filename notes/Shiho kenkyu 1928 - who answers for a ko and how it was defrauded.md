# Shihō kenkyū 1928 — who answers for a kō, and how it was defrauded

遠藤剛一・吉野淑計・熊谷直之助・藤原末作「頼母子講及之に関する犯罪の研究」, 『司法研究報告書集』第8輯5 (司法省調査課, December 1928), 67 frames. NDL Digital pid 1907434, **restricted to libraries and individual transmission**; read on screen through MS's logged-in session, 21 October 2026, §§7–8 (pp. 87–101) only. Not downloaded.

## What it says

**No national statistics.** The history section states that no reliable national count of kō exists. Its one figure is 池田龍藏's: 33,634 kō with 74,461,163円 lent in Tokyo and 26 other prefectures, from a 大正5 (1916) survey by the Ministry of Finance and the Bank of Japan, covering under half the country (p. 8). **It is not the source of the litigation counts the vault cites** (821 suits in 1907 against 2,991 in 1916, and so on), which must come from the judicial statistics; those are still unverified.

**Crime runs along three roles** (pp. 88–91):

- **Members.** They offer worthless or another person's property as security for the pot, or forge a guarantor's seal. These are ordinary frauds.
- **The 講元 or 世話人 as manager.** Embezzlement of contributions, repayments and pledged security; breach of trust in paying the pot without the security the rules require; and fraud by **幽霊講員**, ghost members created to draw the pot.
- **The licensed operator.** Fraud at formation, or by joining secretly, or planting confederates, to win early rounds and then stop repaying (p. 92).

**The legal line between a mutual kō and a business** (pp. 92–98) is drawn on three tests, and the first matters most for the census:

- **Who is the subject.** In a 組合講 the members are the subject. Rights and duties run *between members*, so one member's arrears fall directly on the others, who can sue the defaulter. In a 営業無盡 the operator is the subject, and each member is bound to the operator alone.
- **How it is formed.** A 組合講 forms by the members' mutual consent, an operator's mujin by separate contracts with the operator.
- **What it is for.** A 組合講 is for each member's purpose, an operator's mujin for the operator's profit.

**Two observations from practice.** Many kō rules then in use put the loss from members' arrears **entirely on the 世話人 or 發起人**, which makes them look like business mujin (p. 93). And a kō formed in the name of a shrine, temple or charity, where a third party is the real subject and takes the profit, is a business mujin whatever it is called (p. 98).

**The investigators' checklist** (pp. 99–101) names what went wrong in practice: lot and bid procedures rigged to exclude proper recipients, the 入札差金 (bid discounts) not distributed, 缺口 mishandled, the **minimum payout (最低手取金高) not kept**, and payment refused on the pretext of insufficient security.

## Why it matters

- **It names a fact no characteristic holds: who answers for a member's default.** The answer can be the members jointly, the 世話人 alone, or the operator, and the 1928 authors use it to tell a mutual kō from a business. The 1936 rulebooks state it outright: the Akita 甲野乙造企無盡 binds its members 相互不可分連帯 ([[Norinsho 1938 - forty-three ko rulebooks in print]]). The census records security (`RB3`) and who bears a peril, not who bears a co-member's failure. This is the second hazard to the *fund* the vocabulary cannot record, after the 徳政 cancellation in `ko_horyuji_50kan`. [[Yui 1934 - the law of ko and who bears a member's default]] gives the case law: in the mutual kō the fund is co-owned and the members hold the claims, the 世話人 guarantees the payout only where the rules or a proved custom say so, and the 1915 statute makes the licensed operator guarantee it.
- **The religious-name test is 三浦's 徳政 argument seen from the prosecutor's side.** A sacred purpose was a cover that changed who the kō answered to, in 1928 as in the fifteenth century.
- **The checklist corroborates the bidding-kō mechanism from enforcement.** A minimum payout is a floor on the winning bid, and undistributed 入札差金 is the transfer of [[The bidding ko transfers from the liquidity-constrained to the liquid]] taken by the organiser instead of the late drawers.
