# The isqa refuses the agent's shield

The *ʿisqa* is the **unshielded member** of the Mediterranean partnership triad, and that is what makes it worth a chapter rather than a footnote.

Because half the advance vests in the manager as a debt (*pelga milveh*, BT *Bava Metzia* 104b), his exposure is not capped at what he contributed — it runs against his estate. In the *qirāḍ*/*muḍāraba* the *mudārib* holds capital as *amāna* and is liable only on *taʿaddī*, *taqṣīr* or *mukhālafa*; monetary loss falls on the *rabb al-māl* alone ([[Skin in the game is asymmetric in the mudaraba]]). In the unilateral [[commenda]] the tractator contributes nothing and can lose nothing; in the bilateral *societas maris* his loss is pro rata to a contribution he chose. All three cap the agent at his stake. The *ʿisqa* alone does not.

This is precisely the ground on which **Udovitch (1962)** excludes the *ʿisqa* — and the Byzantine *chreokoinonia* on the same reasoning — as ancestors of the Western commenda: absence of agent liability is the commenda's *differentia*, and both candidates impose liability.

## The dynamic reading

The *qirāḍ* hands the agent a call: bounded below at zero, unbounded above. It lifts the [[Absorbing barrier|absorbing barrier]] off his trajectory entirely, which is what makes repeated exposure to multiplicative venture risk rational for a capital-poor but skilled man. The *ʿisqa* leaves the barrier in place — a bad voyage moves the manager toward it, not merely back to where he started.

That generates a falsifiable prediction. **The *ʿisqa* should select for managers who already hold a wealth buffer; the *qirāḍ* should select for the capital-poor.** Testable against Geniza and responsa prosopography. If it fails, the shielding argument has a problem and it is better to find that here than at referee stage.

## Where this is soft

The unlimited-liability reading is taken from the codes. A halakhist will answer that individual *shetarot* and communal enforcement norms capped the manager in practice. The claim needs documentary evidence, not doctrine — which is exactly the terrain of the Udovitch/Goitein versus Ackerman-Lieberman dispute over whether Geniza Jews transacted in Islamic or Talmudic forms.
