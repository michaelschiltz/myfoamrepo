# MICOLL — Padua ERC tracing commercial-law terms is a transmission control, not a rival

**ERC Consolidator Grant (ERC-2020-COG, GA 101002084) at the Università degli Studi di Padova, 1 Oct 2021 – 31 Mar 2027, €1,610,593: traces the migration of commercial-law terms by historical linguistics, centred on Venice, from the eleventh-century commercial revolution to the early modern period, against the "myth" of a single shared *lex mercatoria*. It is the nearest live ERC project to the European commercial-law strand, and the overlap is tangential: MICOLL follows the *words*, HistorEE codes the *functions*.**

## Project data

- Acronym: MICOLL
- Full title: *Migrating commercial law and language. Rethinking lex mercatoria (11th–17th century)*
- Grant agreement: 101002084; CORDIS [cordis.europa.eu/project/id/101002084](https://cordis.europa.eu/project/id/101002084)
- Programme: H2020 ERC; call ERC-2020-COG
- Host: Università degli Studi di Padova (sole beneficiary)
- PI: Stefania Gialdroni, Dipartimento di diritto privato e critica del diritto (named on the Padua *Vox Mercatoria* podcast page, not on the CORDIS fact sheet)
- Sources in scope: commercial letters, contracts, statutes
- Stated deliverables: a glossary of medieval commercial-law terms; an interactive map of the land routes linking Venice with northern Europe and the Tyrrhenian
- Questions: did merchants in different regions use the same legal terms, and did those terms carry the same legal meaning?

## Why it is not competition on method

MICOLL's finding is the history of a term. HistorEE's unit is the instrument coded against the five corporate components, the four loss components and the time-average criterion; the term is one input to that coding, recorded in the multilingual lexicon. The two projects share a target — the *lex mercatoria* as a harmonised, customary, pan-European law — and both reject it, which places MICOLL alongside De ruysscher's position (regionally divergent law built from custom, legislation and doctrine) rather than against ours.

## Why it is useful to us

A shared loanword with divergent arbitrary particulars is the *Leitfehler* of [[Transmission versus independent recurrence is the load-bearing test]]: positive evidence of descent. MICOLL's glossary is therefore a ready-made transmission control for the western cluster — where a term demonstrably migrated, the matching function is coded as connected by descent, not counted as an independent trial. It strengthens the precaution already taken in Part 2 (the western forms held as non-independent under Galton's problem) without touching the East Asia / western-cluster test.

## The answer if a panel raises it

One sentence: MICOLL traces how commercial terms travelled; HistorEE tests whether pooling institutions answer to the danger of the venture, and uses evidence of travelled terms as its transmission control. Do not name MICOLL in Parts 1–2 (no room, and naming it invites the comparison); keep this note as the prepared answer.
