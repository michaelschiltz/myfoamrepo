# Service historique de la Défense — Lorient and the Compagnie des Indes

<https://www.servicehistorique.sga.defense.gouv.fr/> · Lorient: the *centre historique des archives de la Marine en océan Indien*

**The French East India Company's archive is with the defence ministry**, not with the national archives — because Lorient was the company's port and became a naval one, and the papers followed the place.

## Why the Compagnie des Indes is the case France contributes

The census codes the VOC in three period rows and the English chartered corporation in one. **The French company is the third member of that set and is uncoded** — and it is the most interesting of the three for the project's central test.

The Dutch and English companies are argued over as candidates for independent invention. **The French company is explicitly derivative**: founded 1664 on Colbert's initiative in conscious imitation of the Dutch, restructured repeatedly, and chartered by royal council rather than by a representative assembly. [[Transmission versus independent recurrence is the load-bearing test]] says the discriminating evidence is a case where transmission is *known*, and here it is not in dispute.

**A known-transmission case is the control the comparison needs.** It shows what copying actually looks like in the drafting — which is the only way to judge whether the Dutch–English resemblance looks like copying or not.

⚠️ The company's records are split between Lorient, [[Archives nationales d'outre-mer - Aix-en-Provence]] and the Archives nationales, and the division is unestablished. *Mémoire des hommes* publishes a digitised *Compagnies des Indes* section ⚠️ whose scope should be checked first, since it may cover the crew and voyage registers rather than the constitutional record.
