# Path dependence literature

Bibliographic companion to [[Path dependence requires naming the non-commuting operation]]. Zotero collection `path dependence — theory` (`PVC9C4RC`), fifteen items. DOIs below were resolved through CrossRef and the returned titles checked against the intended item; anything marked **unverified** must be confirmed before it reaches a footnote.

Prior to this session the library held **one** item on path dependence proper. For a book arguing sequence-sensitivity to referees at a major press, that was an exposed flank.

## The disciplining formalism

**Page, 'Path Dependence', *Quarterly Journal of Political Science* 1:1 (2006), 87–115.** `10.1561/100.00000006` — the single most useful item. Separates path dependence from *state dependence*, *early-mover advantage*, *phat dependence* (dependence on the *set* of past events, not their order) and *tipping*. His path/state distinction **is** the non-commuting-operator test in another vocabulary, and the phat-dependence category is the one most historical claims collapse into once examined. Citing him inoculates against the charge of using the term loosely. No open-access PDF.

## Reinforcement and attractor lock-in

**Arthur, 'Competing Technologies, Increasing Returns, and Lock-In by Historical Events', *Economic Journal* 99:394 (1989), 116–131.** `10.2307/2234208` — the multiple-attractor result. Source of the sense of *lock-in* that must be kept apart from capital lock-in; see [[Arthur's lock-in and capital lock-in are different objects]].

**Arthur, Ermoliev & Kaniovski, 'A generalized urn problem and its applications', *Kibernetika* 19:1 (1983), 49–56.** English translation in *Cybernetics* 19:1. **Unverified — translated-series pagination not confirmed; the Russian/English pairing is inconsistently cited in the secondary literature.** Check the printed volume.

**Hill, Lane & Sudderth, 'A Strong Law for Some Generalized Urn Processes', *Annals of Probability* 8:2 (1980), 214–226.** `10.1214/aop/1176994772` — the almost-sure convergence theorem doing the actual work behind Arthur's argument. *Note: the neighbouring DOI `…4773` is a different paper on renewal processes; the two are one digit apart and easily confused.*

**Pemantle, 'A survey of random processes with reinforcement', *Probability Surveys* 4 (2007), 1–79.** `10.1214/07-PS094` — definitive technical survey. Cite for the $\sum 1/n^2 < \infty$ machinery rather than reconstructing it.

**David, 'Clio and the Economics of QWERTY', *American Economic Review* 75:2 (1985), 332–337.** Papers and Proceedings; no DOI. **JSTOR stable number recorded in the Zotero entry is unverified.** Obligatory to cite, thin as theory, and empirically contested — see immediately below.

## The objection to pre-empt

**Liebowitz & Margolis, 'Path Dependence, Lock-In, and History', *Journal of Law, Economics, & Organization* 11:1 (1995), 205–226.** `10.1093/oxfordjournals.jleo.a036867` — dismantles the QWERTY case and introduces first-, second- and third-degree path dependence, only the third being genuinely inefficient. A referee working on institutions will raise this. The reply is in [[Absorption is a path-dependence mechanism distinct from reinforcement]]: their critique lands on the reinforcement mechanism and passes over absorption, because the barrier argument never claims inefficiency.

## Historical-institutionalist uptake

**Pierson, 'Increasing Returns, Path Dependence, and the Study of Politics', *APSR* 94:2 (2000), 251–267.** `10.2307/2586011` · **Mahoney, 'Path dependence in historical sociology', *Theory and Society* 29:4 (2000), 507–548.** `10.1023/A:1007113830879`

Cite for disciplinary positioning, not for mechanism. Both are looser than Page, and both import Arthur's increasing-returns assumption without testing whether the material supports it.

**Allen & Donaldson, 'Persistence and path dependence: A primer', *Journal of Economic Geography* (2022).** Already held (`55YZEXIZ`), now refiled to the collection. Useful on separating persistence from path dependence — a distinction the historical literature routinely elides.

## Barriers, ruin, and non-commuting composition

No canonical paper states the affine non-commutation result because it is too elementary to publish, so the citation for [[Flows make the dynamic affine and affine maps do not commute]] must be constructed rather than borrowed. Nearest anchors:

- **Kelly, 'A New Interpretation of Information Rate', *Bell System Technical Journal* 35:4 (1956), 917–926.** `10.1002/j.1538-7305.1956.tb03809.x` — time-average optimality under a ruin constraint. *Pagination unverified.*
- **Breiman, 'Optimal Gambling Systems for Favorable Games', *Fourth Berkeley Symposium* 1 (1961), 65–78.** No DOI. *Pagination unverified.*
- **Merton & Samuelson, 'Fallacy of the log-normal approximation to optimal portfolio decision-making over many periods', *Journal of Financial Economics* 1:1 (1974), 67–94.** `10.1016/0304-405X(74)90009-9` — the counter-tradition. Cite it; the objection it raises is the one an economist referee will reach for.
- **Furstenberg & Kesten, 'Products of Random Matrices', *Annals of Mathematical Statistics* 31:2 (1960), 457–469.** `10.1214/aoms/1177705909` — the general non-commuting case, needed if the state is ever made multidimensional.

## Shielding

**Hansmann & Kraakman, 'The Essential Role of Organizational Law', *Yale Law Journal* 110:3 (2000), 387–440.** `10.2307/797521` — already load-bearing elsewhere in the project, filed here because barrier relocation is the bridge between the two literatures. See [[Absorption is a path-dependence mechanism distinct from reinforcement]].

## Gaps

Not yet acquired, and each is a plausible referee reference:

- **Blair on capital lock-in** — the legal-form sense of the term, needed to make [[Arthur's lock-in and capital lock-in are different objects]] properly cited on the legal side. Currently the note carries the distinction without a citation for its second half.
- **Bougerol & Lacroix**, *Products of Random Matrices with Applications to Schrödinger Operators* (1985) — the book-length treatment behind Furstenberg–Kesten.
- **The sequence-of-returns risk literature in pension economics** — Milevsky and others. Unsurveyed; likely contains the affine result stated in an applied register.
- **Vergne & Durand** on the methodological conditions for testing path dependence empirically. Unverified from memory, and the strongest available challenge to using the concept in historical work at all.
