# The Brussels experiment - Vanhoyweghen et al 2022 and its sequels

Source note. **"Brussels" is Arne Vanhoyweghen's group at the Vrije Universiteit Brussel, and what it manipulates is time pressure.** The name is Vanhoyweghen's own: his blog post calls it a "similar but much simpler" experiment than Copenhagen, run in Brussels. Different group, different manipulated variable, different claim from [[The Copenhagen experiment - Meder et al 2021 and its pre-registered successor]]; the side-by-side table is at [[The laboratory tests the choice and the archive tests the pool]]. Peters is not an author.

## The paper

Vanhoyweghen, Arne, Brecht Verbeken, Cathy Macharis and Vincent Ginis. 2022. "The Influence of Ergodicity on Risk Affinity of Timed and Non-Timed Respondents." *Scientific Reports* 12: 3744. DOI `10.1038/s41598-022-07613-6`. Zotero `PMCRYLQW`. First presented at the Ergodicity Economics 2021 online conference.

## Design

- **The manipulated variable is time pressure, between groups, crossed with the dynamic.** One group chooses under perceived time pressure, one without; both meet additive and multiplicative gamble pairs in randomised order.
- **Stated choice between described bets.** Pairs of 50/50 bets with equal expected value and different variance, outcomes shown explicitly in euros or as multiplicative factors (the Peters coin toss, ×1.5 or ×0.6). No trial-by-trial wealth updating [verify]; incentive scheme and sample size not yet checked [verify].
- **Sample.** Undergraduate economics students at the VUB — trained in the expected-value reasoning the design sets against intuition, which the authors treat as a strength.
- **The measure** is the share of choices for the growth-optimal option.

## Result

Under additive dynamics time pressure has no effect, and both groups sit near risk neutrality. Under multiplicative dynamics the timed group is significantly more likely to choose the growth-optimal bet, while the untimed group stays closer to expected-value indifference. **The claim is about intuition**: the fast, untrained response tracks the time average, and deliberation as taught pulls it back towards the expectation.

## Critique

- **Ford and Kay, Appendix B**: the safer lottery second-order stochastically dominates, so any risk-averse expected-utility maximiser makes the same choice, and the design cannot discriminate. See [[Ford and Kay 2023 on growth-optimal decision making]].
- **What that objection leaves standing.** Stochastic dominance explains a *level* of safe choices, not the *interaction*. If the additive pairs are equally mean-preserving spreads, dominance holds there too, yet time pressure moves choices only under multiplicative dynamics. That interaction is the reported result, and dominance alone does not predict it. This is my reading and is untested against the paper's tables [verify that the additive pairs are mean-preserving spreads]. An expected-utility theorist can still absorb it by letting curvature depend on frame and time pressure — the same escape as at Copenhagen.
- **Described, not experienced, dynamics.** A stated choice tests intuition about a dynamic one is told about, not adaptation to one lived through. Copenhagen is the stronger design on exactly this point, Brussels the stronger on the intuition question.

## Sequels from the same group

- **Vanhoyweghen and Ginis 2023.** "Human Decision-Making in a Non-Ergodic Additive Environment." *Proceedings of the Royal Society A* 479 (2278): 20230544. DOI `10.1098/rspa.2023.0544`. Zotero `M78ALGIF`. Risk of ruin introduced into additive dynamics; risk aversion varies with distance to ruin. **The laboratory analogue of the absorption limb**, and the nearest experimental neighbour of [[An absorbing barrier breaks ergodicity by construction]].
- **Vanhoyweghen, Macharis and Ginis 2025.** "Investigating the Origins of Redistributive Behaviour in Non-Ergodic Contexts." *Proceedings of the Royal Society A* 481 (2326). DOI `10.1098/rspa.2025.0598`. Zotero `HFZQ27RX`. Two iterations, n = 200 each, thirty multiplicative gambles: subjects redistribute gains and losses against expected gain, read as self-interested growth rather than altruism. **The only experiment in the set about pooling**, so it must be read before anything is written about the laboratory and cooperation. It remains an individual's stated preference over sharing; it does not observe a pool surviving.
