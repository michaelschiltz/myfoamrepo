# Relief kō for a named person is claimed to be unattested outside Japan

Arimoto, Fujie and Senda make a singleton claim about the *tanomoshi-kō*, and it is worth holding open rather than adopting. Their sentence is このように特定の困窮者の救済を目的とした講の組織化は、著者らの知る限り、日本以外では報告されていない — organising a rotating association for the purpose of relieving a *specific* distressed person is, to the authors' knowledge, not reported outside Japan.

**The hedge is theirs and must be carried.** 著者らの知る限り is "to the authors' knowledge", not "nowhere". A negative existential over a global literature stated with an explicit epistemic qualifier is a research prompt, and it turns into a fact by repetition unless the qualifier travels with it.

The claim is narrower than it first appears, which is what makes it testable. It is not that Japanese kō had welfare functions — that is unremarkable and widely attested for rotating associations. It is that the association was **constituted in advance around a named beneficiary**, so the identity of the first recipient is a term of the arrangement rather than an outcome of it. Arimoto and colleagues set the contrast precisely: an ordinary ROSCA member who meets misfortune bids for the pot, whereas the 救済講 was organised beforehand to relieve someone already known to be in difficulty. The supporting structure is that 親有り講 — associations with a designated 親 — were 66.3 per cent of the 1935 census, and that 救済講 where the 親 was a distressed person normally ran at **zero interest** (Kojima 2011, via Arimoto et al.).

*[Corrected 2026-10-21, against the survey itself (農林省経済更生部『農村金融資料 第1号 頼母子講ニ関スル調査』, 1935, NDL pid 1077357, read on the page images). The figure is right — 親有ル講 198,331 of 299,094, 66.3 per cent (p. 3) — but it does not measure what the paragraph uses it for. The survey defines the 親 in its 凡例 十: 講親トハ講ニ依リテ救済ヲ受ケタル者ニシテ第一回ノ講會ニ於テ抽籤、入札等ノ方法ニ依ラズシテ講金又ハ其ノ他ノ給付物ノ給付ヲ受ケタル者ヲ指稱ス. **Relief is built into the category by definition**: every kō whose first pot went to a designated recipient without lot or bid is counted as relieving that recipient, whoever he was — a lord levying a domain kō, a temple raising repair money, a merchant funding trade. So the 66.3 per cent is the share of kō with a designated first recipient, not the share organised to relieve a distressed person, and it cannot support the singleton claim as stated. It also shows where the claim's vocabulary comes from: the Japanese administrative category already equated the 親's first draw with 救済 in 1934. Whether 有本ほか or 小島 read the category that way is not established; their zero-interest finding for 救済講 does not come from this survey, which has no interest question.]*

Two things would settle it, and neither requires new archives. The comparison set is already named by Namimatsu: Korean *kye* (契), Chinese *hui*, Indonesian *arisan*, Vietnamese *hụi*, Thai *chae*, and the West African *esusu* and *dashi* families. If any of those admits a designated distressed beneficiary fixed at formation, the claim fails. Second, the negative may be an artefact of what gets reported rather than what exists: the development-economics ROSCA literature is built around savings and lumpy purchase, following Besley, Coate and Loury, and a relief-organised variant might simply fall outside what that literature counts. **An absence in a literature is not an absence in the world** ([[Refusals are observations of the filter not inferences from survivors]]).

If it survives, what it buys is specific and limited. A designated-beneficiary constitution is an *arbitrary particular* — nothing in the function of rotating credit requires it, and it could as easily have been absent — so its presence in one tradition and absence elsewhere is the kind of detail that carries evidential weight about descent rather than about function ([[Divergent arbitrary particulars are the independence diagnostic]]). It would not show independent origination on its own; it would show that the Japanese line acquired a feature the others did not, which is a claim about *this* lineage and not about the recurrence of rotating credit generally ([[The theory predicts recurrence not independent origins]]).

Until checked, cite it as Arimoto and colleagues' claim, with their qualifier, and not as a finding.
