# The Sound Toll Registers

Held at [[Rigsarkivet Denmark - Daisy and Arkivalieronline]]. Database: **Sound Toll Registers Online**, `soundtoll.nl`. **UNESCO Memory of the World, inscribed 2007.**

**The single most valuable serial source in the European survey, and it is not close.**

## What it is

Denmark levied a toll on every ship passing the Sound, and recorded **every ship and every cargo entering or leaving the Baltic through the Danish straits**. The oldest register is **1497**; the series runs **1557 to 1857**, when the toll was abolished, and is **almost complete from 1574**.

**Sound Toll Registers Online** transcribed it into two linked databases — pre-1634 and 1633 onward — built by Veluwenkamp and van der Woude and published by Tresoar in 2009, with a subsequent **STRO 2.0** re-engineering. Openly accessible.

## Why it matters more than any archive in this survey

**Because it is a denominator.**

Every methodological warning this project has accumulated — [[The VOC-EIC digital asymmetry is an access regime not a survival difference]], [[The QDL corpus is conditioned on its funder's geography]], the prohibition on frequencies across national corpora — has the same root: **the project can usually count instances but never the population they came from.** A source that records *every* passage for three centuries removes that problem for one domain.

Concretely: a claim about how often a peril was insured, or how often a voyage of a given kind was undertaken, becomes computable against a known total. **Nothing else in the survey offers that.**

## The caveats, which are not small

⚠️ **STRO is explicitly "an interpretation of the STR and not a direct copy"** — a reduction of the original registers, made by transcribers with their own coding decisions. The database's own documentation says so. **Any analysis inherits those decisions**, and they have to be read before, not after.

⚠️ It records **passages**, not ventures, firms or contracts. It tells you a ship passed with a cargo; it does not tell you how the voyage was financed or how its peril was allocated. **It is a denominator for the loss census's questions, not an answer to them** — and the temptation to let the countable source redefine the question is exactly what [[The VOC-EIC digital asymmetry is an access regime not a survival difference]] warns against, arriving this time as an opportunity rather than a constraint.

⚠️ The toll was levied by a state with an interest in the count, and evasion existed. Establish what the literature says about under-registration before any figure is used.
