# Restate the cost puzzle as a question about a trajectory

A large class of problems in economic history shares one form:

> *Why does X occur, given that X is costly?*

Sovereign borrowers repay when default appears cheaper. Lenders hold ranks in a moratorium when defection pays. Parties buy insurance whose premium exceeds its actuarial value. Merchants pool with kin at a discount to the return available alone.

The form is not neutral. It presupposes that the behaviour must be justified by a payoff comparison **at a moment**, and when the comparison comes out negative the explanation must be supplied from outside the dynamics — reputation, punishment, norms, altruism, risk-aversion as a taste. The supplement is doing the work the framing made necessary. This is a state question in the sense of [[The split is over whether the explanandum is a state or a trajectory]].

**The move is to restate it.**

> *What becomes of an entity that does X repeatedly, indefinitely, and cannot step outside its own path?*

Under non-ergodic dynamics the two questions have different answers, and the second frequently returns *X* as a dominance result with no supplement required. The comparison is then not between two payoffs but between a payoff and a rate, and a one-period gain set against a permanent change in growth rate is not a close contest.

The vault already contains worked instances rather than merely the schema. [[The moratorium held because leaving the pool is not a one-period cost]] runs it on Genoese refusal, and finds the restatement lowers what the enforcement story must carry and accounts for a residual it cannot — persistence across twenty-one years and changed personnel. [[A single throw of dice cannot be evaluated by its expectation]] runs it on the Armada, and finds the authors' own contrast reversed. Peters and Adamou's insurance and cooperation results are the same move made in a model rather than an archive.

## The discipline that keeps it honest

Two constraints, both already imposed elsewhere in the vault, and neither optional.

First, the restatement is **not an imputation of intent.** No historical actor computed a growth rate. The claim is selective, per [[Time-average optimization is a survivorship property not an intention]]: arrangements whose realised trajectories tracked the time average persisted, and the alternatives were removed. Any sentence implying deliberation will be destroyed.

Second, the restatement **must be earned in each case, not assumed.** It requires naming the multiplicative variable and showing it compounds — the requirement [[Bad luck is what the ensemble average calls the modal trajectory]] flags as unmet for Spanish decline and deliberately leaves open. A schema applied without that step produces the mirror-image vice of the one it corrects: explaining everything by growth rates is no better than explaining everything by expectations.

## Why it is worth having as a schema

Because the puzzles are generated in bulk by the state framing, and answering them one at a time concedes the framing each time. Naming the reformulation as a general move lets a single argument retire a class of puzzles, and makes visible that the supplements — reputation, punishment, preference — were called into being by a modelling choice rather than discovered in the record.
