# Asiatisk Kompagni and the Danish trading companies

Records at [[Rigsarkivet Denmark - Daisy and Arkivalieronline]], with a themed digitised collection.

## The case

Denmark chartered a sequence of overseas companies — the Danish East India Company and its successor the **Asiatisk Kompagni** (1732), and the West India and Guinea Company — all built on the Dutch and English models by a small state that could not have projected force on its own account.

## Why a small-state company is the useful kind

The entity census codes the VOC, the English chartered corporation, and the deed-of-settlement company — **all of them bodies of great powers.** The French company at [[Service historique de la Defense - Lorient and the Compagnie des Indes]] is the first known-transmission case; the Danish companies are the second, **and they add something the French do not: scale.**

A chartered company that is structurally a copy of the VOC but an order of magnitude smaller separates the form from the circumstances. **If the apparatus of entity shielding, capital lock-in and transferable claims appears in a company that could never have done what the VOC did, the apparatus is not explained by what the VOC did.** That is a straightforward and rather strong argument, and it needs a small-state case to run.

⚠️ Unread. The companies' charters, their capital structures and their relation to the crown are all to be established, and the claim above is a reason to look.

## The thing that is not separable

The Danish West India and Guinea Company's business was the slave trade and the tropical colonies, and Rigsarkivet's Dansk Vestindien guide exists because that record is consulted for that history. Same statement as at [[Zeeuws Archief - the Zeeland chamber and the MCC]] and [[Archives departementales de la Loire-Atlantique - Nantes]].
