# The Dubrovnik Ottoman documents in three scripts

**The layer question at Dubrovnik has to be asked about script, and it has three answers, one per period.** This is the first place the non-European survey meets the problem [[Handoff - the non-European archive survey]] predicted, and it is milder here than it will be anywhere else.

## 1. Before c.1525: Cyrillic, and already edited

The Porte's earliest correspondence with Ragusa was not in Ottoman Turkish. Emir Filipović counts **about 139 imperial documents and about 65 acts of Ottoman officials, 10 July 1430 to 1525**, written in the Štokavian dialect in **Cyrillic diplomatic cursive** — *ahdnames*, *fermans*, *buyuruldus*, tribute receipts, privileges.

- Truhelka 1911 edited some 80 sultanic acts and 40 governors' letters
- **Stojanović 1934 edited 220 documents**, the standard edition
- Bojović 1998 translated 129 into French

**For the fifteenth century the script barrier is a Slavist's, not an Ottomanist's, and the corpus is at layer 3 in print.** ⚠️ Filipović places them under Stojanović's numbering as *Prilozi arhivskim serijama* IV b, outside `HR-DADU-07.2`; the current reference code was not confirmed.

## 2. From the sixteenth century: Ottoman Turkish in Arabic script

The bulk of the 15,000 — see [[Acta Turcarum - the Ottoman series at Dubrovnik]]. *Dīwānī* for the chancery acts of the Porte, the cursive court hands for the *kadis'* documents. **Here the European rule fails**: an image is readable by nobody on the team without paid transcription, and Decision_log A6 is the line that pays for it.

## 3. The eighteenth century: the dragomans' Italian

The Republic's Turkish Chancery translated and indexed what it received, and the volumes survive as part of the record:

- *Registro di Comandamenti Imperiali, Hattiscerifi, e Capitulazioni* (1784)
- ***Traduzioni di Capitulazioni e Fermani***, four volumes, 1784–1785
- *Copia Lettere*, four volumes, 1729–1782
- *Dona Turcarum*, two volumes, 1566–1667
- per-document Italian *regesta* on many originals — see [[Kadijski spisi - the kadi documents at Dubrovnik]]

**That is a Latin-script layer over a large part of the Ottoman series, made at the time.** It is the single most useful practical fact this pass found about Dubrovnik.

## The trap in the third layer

**A dragoman's translation is the Ragusan apparatus reading the Ottoman one.** The *Traduzioni* were made by the Republic's officials, for the Republic's purposes, at a date when the privileges they rendered were live instruments in its dealings with the Porte. Used as a crib to the originals, they are a finding aid. **Used in place of the originals, they collapse the two apparatuses the site is valuable for into one** — which is exactly the failure [[Independence of apparatus is not independence of question]] warns about, arriving through the reading room rather than through the research design.

The rule that follows: **the Italian may locate a document; only the Ottoman text may witness it.** Where the two differ, the difference is data about Ragusan reception, not noise.
