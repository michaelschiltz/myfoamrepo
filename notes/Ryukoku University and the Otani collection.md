# Ryukoku University and the Ōtani collection

Ōmiya Library, Kyoto. **About 9,000 items** — the largest Japanese share of the Ōtani expeditions' finds, including **8,000 items from two wooden boxes** that stayed in Kyoto.

**Ryukoku is one of IDP's seven founding partners and has hosted the Japanese IDP centre since a collaborative agreement in 2005.** As of August 2023 the Japanese centre had digitised **8,465 manuscripts, 6,745 of them in Chinese and 4 in Uyghur script**.

## Why this matters for the project's Japanese base

**The Silk Road is one of the two non-European regions where Japanese institutions hold originals, not just editions** — the other being the Ōtani material now in Tokyo, Lüshun and Seoul ([[The Otani dispersal - Kyoto, Tokyo, Lushun, Seoul]]). **A Kyoto-based reader can work on Inner Asian documents in Kyoto**, with the images of the dispersed remainder on IDP.

⚠️ **Access terms, catalogue coverage and what is served publicly versus on site were not established.** The Ryukoku Museum shows some of the material; the library holds it.
