# Social immunity and superorganism immunity - loci

Loci from the hive sweep of 8 November 2026 (Undermind workspace *HistorEE — the hive: emergence, social immunity, rewriting the game*, deep search *Social immunity colony-level defence superorganism*). Items marked † were read in full; the rest on abstract and metadata.

## Concept and definition

- **Cremer, Armitage and Schmid-Hempel 2007**, "Social Immunity," *Current Biology* 17 (16): R693–R702, doi:10.1016/j.cub.2007.06.008. Coins the term for collective anti-parasite defence, by explicit analogy with the immune system of a body.
- † **Cremer, Pull and Fürst 2018**, "Social Immunity: Emergence and Evolution of Colony-Level Disease Protection," *Annual Review of Entomology* 63: 105–23, doi:10.1146/annurev-ento-020117-043110. Definition at 107 (collectively performed behavioural, physiological and organisational defences; "the immune system of the colony"). Strict social immunity reserved for colonies where selection has shifted to the colony through a germline–soma division (107–8); families and communal breeders excluded (fig. 1). No claim that individual immunity is reduced.
- † **Cremer and Sixt 2009**, "Analogies in the Evolution of Individual and Social Immunity," *Phil. Trans. R. Soc. B* 364: 129–42, doi:10.1098/rstb.2008.0166. Three-stage scheme (border, soma, germline defence; fig. 2); premise that the colony as a whole, not any individual, produces offspring (131); analogies declared phenomenological, not mechanistic (129); disanalogies: worker diversity, expulsion and nest abandonment, no social autoimmunity (131, 134, 138).
- † **Pull and McMahon 2020**, "Superorganism Immunity: A Major Transition in Immune System Evolution," *Frontiers in Ecology and Evolution* 8: 186, doi:10.3389/fevo.2020.00186. Colony immunity as a defence system operating at the level of the new individual (2); functions include policing internal cheaters and maintaining colony identity through colony-specific cuticular hydrocarbons (3, 7); individual defences co-opted and elaborated, not reduced (10, fig. 2); distributed and self-organised (15).
- **McMahon 2017**, "Blurred Boundaries between Society and Individuality" — termite case from the cockroach-like ancestor [venue to verify].

- Resin collection: Simone, Evans and Spivak 2009, *Evolution* 63: 3016–22, doi:10.1111/j.1558-5646.2009.00772.x.
- † Social vaccination: Konrad et al. 2012, *PLoS Biology* 10: e1001300, doi:10.1371/journal.pbio.1001300.
- † Organisational immunity: Stroeymeyt et al. 2018, *Science* 362: 941–45, doi:10.1126/science.aat4793.

## Chemical social immunity as a spatial configuration

- † **Fernandes, Levina, Frost and Carter 2026**, "Spatially Organized Chemical Defences Shape Microbial Communities in Honey Bee Colonies," *ISME Communications* 6 (1): ycag247, doi:10.1093/ismeco/ycag247. Twenty-four *A. mellifera* colonies, five apiaries at one site (Tocal, NSW), one sampling event; amplicon sequencing, broth-microdilution assays against seven microbes, ¹H NMR.
  - Framing: antimicrobial social immunity as a colony-level property emerging from spatial organisation and functional integration, not the property of any single material (2; again 10).
  - Distance decay across ranked compartments: microbial communities (bacteria R² 0.170, fungi 0.069) and antimicrobial activity (R² 0.217), the latter driven by hive pathogens and absent for non-hive microbes (3–6, figs 1–2).
  - Mediators: H₂O₂ in honey (ρ 0.570), flavonoids in propolis and wax, polar aliphatics in pollen (5–8). Defences both foraged and endogenous (2); honey's peroxide not attributed to bee glucose oxidase in the paper.
  - Stressed hives show weaker spatial structure (bacteria R² 0.138 against 0.236; fungal decay negligible), read as loss of partitioning; spatial order proposed as a marker of colony health (3, 10).
  - Apiary explains most variance in activity (R² 0.34); hive health and chalkbrood history not significant.
  - Limits stated by the authors (11): ethanol extracts need not match in-hive concentrations; power is low; causal links remain inferential. Two further cautions of ours: the compartments are *ranked* by ecological and functional proximity, not measured distance, so part of the decay is built into the ordering, and the microbiome and activity analyses use different orderings; "permitting beneficial species" is interpretation, since beneficial microbes were not assayed.

## Individual against collective defence

- † Masson et al. 2024, *Nature Communications* 15, doi:10.1038/s41467-024-53527-4. *Lasius niger*: systemic individual immunity against bacteria, none against fungi; grooming and group living protect against fungi only. Erosion of antifungal immunity by relaxed selection is proposed, not tested (8).
- López-Uribe et al. 2016, *Biology Letters* 12: 20150984 (encapsulation response falls with *colony size* across eleven lineages; the predictor is colony size, not eusociality as such); He et al. 2021, *Proc. R. Soc. B* 288: 20203168 (termites).
- Barribeau et al. 2015, *Genome Biology* 16: 83 — the depauperate bee repertoire *precedes* sociality. The general trade-off is unproven.

## Genetic diversity

- † Tarpy 2003, *Proc. R. Soc. B* 270: 99–103, doi:10.1098/rspb.2002.2199. Chalkbrood inoculation, colonies of single-drone against pooled-semen queens (final n = 18 and 8). Means did not differ for disease, colony mass or final population; *variances* did — disease prevalence (Bartlett p < 0.0001), brood viability, hygienic behaviour, final population. Tarpy frames polyandry as bet-hedging on "reducing the variance in fitness over time" (after Gillespie 1976): monandrous colonies "are more likely to fail." Chalkbrood is benign (maximum ca. 2.3% of brood infected). The finding is variance, not growth; Seeley and Tarpy 2007, *Proc. R. Soc. B* 274: 67–72, doi:10.1098/rspb.2006.3702; Oldroyd and Fewell 2007, *TREE* 22: 408–13.
- Caution: Soper, Ekroth and Martins 2021, *BMC Ecology and Evolution* 21, doi:10.1186/s12862-021-01925-3 — direct evidence only in eusocial Hymenoptera, small samples, publication bias.
