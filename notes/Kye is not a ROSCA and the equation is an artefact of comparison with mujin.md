# Kye is not a ROSCA and the equation is an artefact of comparison with mujin

The Japanese-language literature on Korean popular finance treats 契 (*kye*) as a rotating savings and credit association whose Japanese counterpart is the 頼母子講. Kim Jingyu opens by classing it among ROSCAs alongside Japanese *tanomoshi*, Chinese *hui*, Indonesian *arisan* and Thai *chae*; Lee Myonghwi writes that 契と無尽は共通的に ROSCAS と呼ぶことができる. **The Korean literature does not carve it that way.**

Choi Jae-seok's survey gives six functional categories — economic (*nonggye*, *bogye*, *singnigye*, *guugye*), political (*donggye*, *boan'gye*), welfare (*honsanggye*), religious (*chonggye*, *munjunggye*), educational (*hakkye*, *sŏdanggye*) and recreational (*sigye*, *tonggapkye*) — and states that apart from the *singnigye* specifically, lending at interest as a *purpose* was rare. Rotation appears once, as one of four arrangements for lending out the association's property: to members only, to non-members only, to both, or 윤번제, by rotation. **The rotating-credit form is a subtype of one category, not the definition of the institution.**

So "kye = Korean ROSCA" is a comparative concept built for the Japanese comparison and then read back as a description. It is the `AP4` and `RB3` error at the scale of a national literature: a category native to one tradition exported to another, where it selects the fraction that resembles the exporter and discards the rest. The institution that most of the Korean material describes is a general-purpose cooperative association holding common property — the rotating fund is what a Japanese observer would notice.

**The dating collapses on the same inspection.** Kim and Lee both assert a late-Goryeo origin in tax obligation, flatly and without a primary source. Choi lists **twenty competing theories** — primitive-communal, *ture*, *hyangdo*, *kabae*, guild, *hyangyak*, the late-Goryeo *hopo* levy, the Joseon *kunp'o* levy, self-defence against feudal misrule, resistance to pre-capitalist usury, spontaneous emergence — and concludes that the list alone shows 얼마나 혼란되어 있는가, how confused the scholarship is. Proposed origins run from before the Three Kingdoms to Joseon, and pre-Joseon evidence is scarce. **What the Japanese-language articles present as settled is one position among twenty.**

**Provenance explains both.** Choi's bibliography names the colonial ethnography the Japanese framing rests on: Zenshō Eisuke, 『朝鮮の契』, 朝鮮總督府 1926 — the source of the survey figures Lee cites — with 猪谷善一 (1924), 李學鍾 (1923) and 四方博 (1944), all Government-General publications. The categories *and* the counts are colonial products, and they reach the present-day Japanese literature intact.

**And the colonial state was not a neutral observer of what it counted.** Choi: 일제는 하층계급의 금융개선을 꾀한 식리계·산통계·작파계 등을 금압하였다 — the authorities suppressed precisely the lower-class credit-improving kye, while promoting the *singsan'gye* as a subordinate organ of the 금융조합. The surviving statistics therefore describe the forms the state permitted, which is a filter observation and not a census ([[Refusals are observations of the filter not inferences from survivors]]).

**What follows for the project.** Korea cannot serve as an independent-arrival case for rotating credit: 無尽 was transmitted from Japan and licensed under the 朝鮮無尽業令 of 1922, and Lee records that 契 itself 次第に金融的性格を強化し、推薦方式などを採択しながら無尽との類似点が多くなった — the indigenous form grew *more like* the transplant under contact. Any resemblance between late kye and *tanomoshi* is downstream of a documented channel ([[Galton's problem]]).

But that is what makes it useful. A named parent, a datable transplant, a regulatory shock and a forced monopolisation in 1939 make Korea a **calibration case**: it shows what transmission preserves and what it lets drift, on a pair where descent is not in doubt ([[Transmission versus independent recurrence is the load-bearing test]]).

**Two details worth carrying into the coding.** The *kyech'ŏp* has the same documentary architecture as a 仕法帳 — 序/完議 stating purpose and history, 立議 the rules, 座目 the member roster, 跋 the account of founding, plus receipts and a property list. And the Kajwa-dong *kŭmsonggye* of 1778 rules 추가가입은 되나 탈퇴는 안 됨: entry permitted, exit forbidden — a membership condition no coded form in the census yet exhibits.
