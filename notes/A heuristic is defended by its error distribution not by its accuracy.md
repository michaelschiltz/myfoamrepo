# A heuristic is defended by its error distribution not by its accuracy

**A heuristic is not a cheap approximation to an optimum, and to defend it as one is to concede the wrong point. Where the payoff carries an absorbing barrier, the estimator that minimises mean error is not the one that minimises ruin. A rule biased away from the barrier, with low dispersion, dominates an unbiased rule with high dispersion. The heuristic is judged by the distribution of its errors along the trajectory, not by how close it comes on average.**

## Taleb's three arguments, ranked

**1. Bias against variance** (*Skin in the Game* ch. 18, fig. 3). The biased shooter with low dispersion stays away from the periphery; the unbiased one with high dispersion does not. Rigorously: if crossing the barrier is unboundedly costly, the loss function is lexicographic, and the estimator that minimises expected loss is biased away from the barrier. This is the strong argument, and it is the [[Rationality is a property of the policy not of the belief that produces it]] point applied to estimation.

**2. The asymmetry of the two errors** (ch. 19, "Rationality, Again"). Refined paranoia costs little when wrong and saves everything when right once; how often one is right is irrelevant (*Antifragile* ch. 23, "The Problem of Frequency"). The precise condition is that the false alarm is an *additive* cost on the trajectory while the miss is absorption. Being wrong nearly always is then the visible face of a correct criterion, which is the sense in which the "irrationality" of a heuristic can be rational. The argument fails where the cost of the false alarm compounds. A rule that refuses every bet forgoes growth multiplicatively and decays against one that sizes its bets, and that is a Kelly question, which no heuristic of refusal answers ([[Antifragility is defined by an ensemble Jensen gap]]). Taleb half-concedes this in "Love *Some* Risks": risk-loving, yet averse to ruin. The strong defence therefore covers the barrier, not the sizing.

**3. Teachability** (ch. 18, "What Is Religion About?"). Binary, unconditional rules are easy to teach and to enforce. This is the most neglected of the three and, for the project, the most usable. A rule that is not conditional on a calculation transmits with less copying error and does not depend on the competence of whoever applies it. That is a property of the rule as a transmitted instrument, not of anyone's cognition, and it can therefore be used without breaking [[Never let the units slide from instruments to populations]] or [[Time-average optimization is a survivorship property not an intention]].

## A prediction the third argument makes

If unconditional statement is how a rule survives transmission, rules guarding absorbing exposures should be stated categorically, as prohibitions, more often than rules governing additive risks, which can afford grading and cost–benefit tests. The doctrine corpus can test this. The obvious difficulty is that *gharar* is itself graded, the minor tolerated and the gross forbidden [verify the doctrinal formulation], while *ribā* is categorical. Whether that split tracks barrier-exposure is a question, not a finding.

## Haug and Taleb 2011

Option traders price with a Gaussian formula adjusted by varying a single parameter to fudge tails and skew, not with Black–Scholes–Merton (*Journal of Economic Behavior & Organization* 77, no. 2: 97–106, DOI `10.1016/j.jebo.2010.09.013`, Zotero `RNXVCCFB`). One free parameter cannot be overfitted. The paper's historical claim, that the practice predates the formula, has not been read [verify].

## What not to claim

- **Do not take the survival of heuristic-holders as the warrant.** Ch. 18 ends "What Is Religion About?" by inferring from our survival through tail risks that it cannot have been random: the backward inference refused at [[Taleb reads survival as evidence about the survivor and we read it as evidence about the filter]]. The defence above is forward. It specifies the error structure under a stated dynamic before looking at who survived.
- **Do not impute the heuristic to anyone's mind.** The laboratory finding that intuition under time pressure tracks the time average is about choice. A codified rule is its analogue in function only ([[The laboratory tests the choice and the archive tests the pool]]).
