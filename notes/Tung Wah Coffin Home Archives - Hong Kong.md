# Tung Wah Coffin Home Archives — Hong Kong

東華義莊文獻. Tung Wah Group of Hospitals, Records and Heritage Office. <https://www.twmarchives.hk/coffin_home_archives_overview.php?lang=en>

**The operating record of a Chinese native-place network's burial function — the record the steles do not carry.** The Tung Wah Coffin Home received the coffins and exhumed bones of Chinese who died abroad and forwarded them to their home districts. The archive is **234 items, tens of thousands of pages, 1910s to 1970s**: letters to Tung Wah from Chinese organisations and individuals overseas, **coffin home guarantee certificates and application forms, temporary depository contracts, coffin and bone registers, receipts and bills of lading**.

## What the archive shows

**The senders were native-place and clan associations** in California, Canada, Australia and South-East Asia, working through Hong Kong's district chambers and the home towns' charities. A 2015 account of Yip Hon-ming's reading of about **10,000 pages** puts the number of coffins and sets of bones returned through Hong Kong in the first half of the twentieth century at about **100,000**. ⚠️ Read through a university press summary, not from the study.

**This is the first place in the survey where a Chinese association's hazard function appears in serial operating documents**: a death abroad triggers a claim on an association, which pays for exhumation, shipment and delivery. **Whether the overseas associations did so from a fund built on members' contributions — `CN` and `PY` in the census's terms — is answered by their own accounts, not by Tung Wah's**; Tung Wah's registers give the flow and its timing, the senders' letters give the arrangement. ⚠️ No letter read.

## Layers and access

- **Layer 2**: **300 letters of the 1920s–1930s** are online as images with descriptions.
- **Layer 1** for the rest: the registers and contracts are described, not imaged. ⚠️ Whether the full 234 items are catalogued item by item online was not checked.
- **On site**: the Records and Heritage Office's reading room, by application. Text may be used for research or private study with attribution; **non-text materials need prior written consent**.
- **Script**: handwritten Chinese letters and printed forms, twentieth century. **Not a script problem.**

## What it bears on

- **`huiguan`** — uncoded because no source shows a fund. **This archive does not show one either, but it shows the payout event and who sent it**, and so says where the fund's record should be looked for: **the overseas associations' own papers, in San Francisco, Vancouver, Melbourne and Singapore** — a trail that starts here and leaves the Chinese survey. See [[Guild stele inscriptions - the huiguan's surviving record]].
- **Successor custody** — the archive survives because the charity survives; **the most continuous Chinese association record in the survey is held by a Hong Kong hospital group, not an archive**.
