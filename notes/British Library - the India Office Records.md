# British Library — the India Office Records

The archive of the East India Company and of the India Office. The census's VOC rows have no English counterpart coded, and this is where the counterpart would come from.

## The access position, already argued in the vault

[[EIC digital corpus - what is online and on what terms]] and [[The QDL corpus is conditioned on its funder's geography]] set it out: **images at best, and mostly behind a subscription**, with the Qatar Digital Library opening the portion relevant to the Gulf.

`IOR/B` — the minutes of the Courts of Directors and Proprietors, **1599–1858** — is the serial constitutional record, and it is the paywalled one. **That series is the EIC's answer to Van Dam**, and the comparison in [[The VOC-EIC digital asymmetry is an access regime not a survival difference]] turns on it.

## What the survey adds now

Two things.

**First, the third funder.** With [[The Prize Papers - HCA 32 and the German money]], Britain has three major early-modern commercial corpora opened by three non-British funders — a commercial publisher, a Gulf foundation, a German academy. **The pattern is now a pattern.**

**Second, a warning the other countries have made concrete.** The survey has repeatedly found that *what a funder digitises is organised around the funder's question*, and that the resulting corpus has no denominator. Qatar's selection is honest and stated; Adam Matthew's is commercial; Göttingen's is a research programme about prize. **None of the three was organised around the question this project asks**, and a British corpus assembled from all three would be a union of three other people's samples.
