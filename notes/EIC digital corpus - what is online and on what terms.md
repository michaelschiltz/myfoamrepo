# EIC digital corpus — what is online and on what terms

The English company's records survive at a scale comparable to the Dutch. Almost none of it is freely available as text. What is online arrives through three channels — a commercial publisher, a sovereign-wealth partnership, and a handful of derived datasets — and each imposes its own filter.

## Where the archive is

The **India Office Records** (IOR) at the British Library: the archives of the East India Company (1600–1858), the Board of Control, the India Office and Burma Office. ⚠️ The "nine miles of shelving" figure is repeated everywhere and unsourced here; verify against the BL's own collection description before printing.

The classes that matter for this project:

- **IOR/A** — charters, deeds, statutes, treaties, 1600–1947.
- **IOR/B** — minutes of the Courts of Directors and Proprietors, 1599–1858. The constitutional record.
- **IOR/D** — minutes and memoranda of the general committees.
- **IOR/E** — correspondence with the factories.
- **IOR/G** — Factory Records.
- **IOR/L/AG** — **Accountant General's records, c.1601–1974, c. 8,200 storage units**. Ledgers and journals of the commercial operation. This is where the financial series actually lives.

## Channel one: catalogue, not content

Descriptive access runs through <https://searcharchives.bl.uk> and, for the majority of official IOR collections, duplicated records on TNA **Discovery**.

**The catalogue has been unstable since October 2023**, when the Rhysida ransomware attack took the Library's systems down. Read-only catalogue restored 15 January 2024; a rebuilt main catalogue launched 15 December 2025. ⚠️ Confirm the present state of IOR-level description and of any digitised-item links before relying on a search result as evidence of absence. For two years an empty result at the BL meant nothing at all.

## Channel two: Adam Matthew, paywalled

Adam Matthew Digital's *East India Company* has imaged the core constitutional series — **IOR/A, IOR/B, IOR/C, IOR/D, plus IOR/G Factory Records**, 1599 to c.1950.

- Institutional subscription only; free at BL reading rooms (St Pancras, Boston Spa).
- Images with catalogue-level metadata. ⚠️ Whether full-text search rests on keyed transcription, HTR, or neither is unverified — decisive for whether it can be used quantitatively.
- No bulk export. A commercial platform is a reading room with a search box, not a corpus.

⚠️ Check whether Hokkaido holds a subscription. If not, this is a London-residency question, not a desktop one.

## Channel three: the Qatar Digital Library

<https://www.qdl.qa> — Qatar Foundation, Qatar National Library and the British Library, from October 2014. Free, high-quality, with catalogue records and Arabic/English description.

- Over **2 million** images overall; ⚠️ the "c. 475,000 pages from India Office Records and Private Papers" figure comes from QDL's own About page and looks stale against a reported ~2.5 million total by 2024. Do not cite either number without rechecking.
- **The selection principle is geographic and funder-driven**: Gulf and Arabian Peninsula material, mid-eighteenth century to 1951. It is not a series, it is a cross-cut.

This last point is not a caveat, it is a methodological constraint. See [[The QDL corpus is conditioned on its funder's geography]].

## Derived datasets

- **H. V. Bowen, *The East India Company: Trade and Domestic Financial Statistics, 1755–1838***, UK Data Service **SN 5690**, from the ESRC project "British economic, social, and cultural interactions with Asia, 1760–1833". Free on registration; documentation covers the Company's accounting year, commerce journals, invoice values, export and import series. **This is the EIC's answer to Boekhouder-Generaal Batavia** and the only structured financial series on the English side that can be used without going to the manuscripts.
- Genealogical indexing of ecclesiastical returns and service records via commercial providers. Not relevant here.
- ⚠️ Ships' journals (IOR/L/MAR/B) — digitisation status unresearched; partially relevant via the CLIWOC climate work.

## The gap, stated plainly

There is **no machine-readable text corpus of the EIC records**. Bowen's dataset stops at aggregates and stops in 1838; Adam Matthew supplies images behind a paywall; QDL supplies excellent free images of a geographic slice. `IOR/L/AG` — the ledgers — is effectively untouched.
