# Prospect theory's parameters are neither population constants nor individual traits

§5 of [[Doctor Wakker and Wang 2020 on the ergodicity problem]] offers prospect theory as the field's answer to the failures of expected utility. Measured by the idiosyncratic preference it imputes, the answer goes further than the patches it replaced. EU confined its anomalies to one function, the curvature of utility. Cumulative prospect theory (Tversky and Kahneman 1992) needs curvature over gains and losses (α, β), a loss-aversion coefficient (λ), two weighting-function parameters (γ, δ), and a reference point that the theory itself leaves to the modeller. Each anomaly is answered with a further dimension of taste — the move Stigler and Becker (1977) warned against, since an explanation by variation in tastes can explain anything.

**The defence, and why it fails.** The standard reply is that these are not idiosyncratic tastes but psychophysical regularities: diminishing sensitivity in the Weber–Fechner sense, with the 1992 medians (λ ≈ 2.25, α ≈ 0.88) functioning as population constants. The evidence sustains neither reading of what a parameter is.

- *Not a population constant.* The meta-analysis of Brown, Imai, Vieider and Camerer (2024) finds loss aversion present on average but widely dispersed and sensitive to design. Walasek and Stewart (2015) move measured λ by changing the range of stakes offered, which makes it a property of the choice set rather than of the chooser. Gal and Rucker (2018) contest loss aversion as a general principle.
- *Not an individual trait.* Pedroni et al. (2017) elicit risk preferences from the same subjects by several standard methods and obtain mutually inconsistent estimates.

A parameter that is neither a constant of the population nor a stable property of the person is a fitted quantity of the design. For a theory offered as the replacement of EU, that is the worst available combination.

**The limit.** This is not the claim that prospect theory is wrong, which [[Answering EU's failures with more psychology instantiates the complaint]] forbids, and the prohibition stands: the qualitative regularities are robust. The claim is narrower. The parameters are not primitives, so pointing at them cannot settle whether an observed risk attitude is a taste or a property of the dynamic. Nor is design-dependence evidence *for* the dynamic reading — a range effect is not a wealth dynamic, and Walasek and Stewart must not be recruited as a witness. What the instability removes is the objection that the dynamic reading is gratuitous because a stable preference account already exists. None does.

**The counterweight.** The framework is not preference-free in practice either. The Copenhagen data were fitted with individual isoelastic η, dispersed around the predicted values; the testable claim is the direction of the shift between dynamics, not each subject's level [verify against `JE86VR89`]. Beyond the pure additive and multiplicative cases, the free parameter reappears as the dynamic the agent faces or assumes — the which-dynamic problem logged under Known vulnerabilities at [[MOC - Defending the ergodicity claim]] and stated at [[Jensen supplies the gap but only the dynamic privileges the logarithm]]. The parsimony advantage asserted at [[Toda's own example makes EE more falsifiable than expected utility]] holds only while the dynamic is specified independently of the choices it is meant to explain.

**What this means for our use.** For historical actors the asymmetry is decisive. No elicitation is possible, so a preference-parameter account of a twelfth-century investor is unidentifiable by construction, whereas contract terms, compounding and loss-sharing rules survive in the archive. The discipline that keeps this from becoming circular is the one at [[Ford and Kay concede the condition under which their objection lapses]]: the dynamic is coded from the instrument's terms, never from its fate.

## Source

Prospect-theory session, September 2026, following the question whether economists at large accept DWW's claim that the field has moved from EU to prospect theory. Short answer, not filed separately: only the descriptive branch has; applied work and graduate training keep EU or its recursive extensions, and EU remains the normative benchmark — see Barberis (2013).
