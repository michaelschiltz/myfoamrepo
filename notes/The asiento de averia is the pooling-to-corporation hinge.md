# The asiento de averia is the pooling-to-corporation hinge

The comparative programme asserts a hinge between the pooling of loss and the pooling of capital — Objective 1 and Objective 2, the same time-average logic in its distributive and its temporal face — and it has, so far, no case in which the one is documented turning into the other. The *asiento de avería* is that case, if Hierro Anibarro's genealogy holds.

The sequence is short and unusually well recorded. A compulsory *ad valorem* levy on the Indies trade, raised from 1521 to pay for the armed escort of the fleets, is progressively devolved by the Crown to private contractors; the capital assembled under those contracts acquires patrimonial autonomy; and the resulting body is, by the 1620s, indistinguishable from a chartered company to the officials whose job was to tell the difference. A pooling institution becomes a corporate one, by a mechanism — the negotiated fixed-sum contract with the fisc — that is visible in the documents.

Three things make this worth more than another European case in a literature already saturated with them.

It runs **outside the Dutch–English canon** on which the entire corporation debate is built, and outside the Toulouse–Genoa counter-canon that [[The corporation is not the omega - the naties are an undisplaced niche]] contests. A third genealogy, Iberian and fiscal rather than commercial or municipal, is a genuine addition to the decomposition.

The pooled object is **protection**, not profit. That distinguishes it from every instrument currently in the frame and pushes at [[The determinate-contingent typology carries the richness]]: the hazard insured against is an adversary who responds to the defence mounted, not a storm. Whether the typology can accommodate strategically generated risk is a real question and better asked now.

And the pool **fails**. From the 1620s the Seville *asiento* consortia performed increasingly badly and the 1640 contract went bankrupt as fewer and fewer merchants paid — free-riding, fraud and contraband unravelling a compulsory pool in the most fiscally documented commercial system in early modern Europe. That matters structurally: the retained negative cases are both non-European, so Europe supplies the baseline and the successes while the failures are all elsewhere. A European pooling institution that collapses, with numbers, is worth more to the falsifiability case than three more that succeed. It also puts the framework a question it must answer rather than illustrate — if pooling raises every participant's time-average growth rate, why must it be compulsory, and why do participants defect? The answer is available, since protection is non-excludable and the private and pooled optima therefore diverge, but the framework has to be made to give it.

**Status.** The genealogical claim rests on [[Hierro Anibarro 2005 on the asiento de averia and the privileged company]], read from the abstract only. If *autonomía patrimonial* turns out to mean segregated accounting rather than entity shielding, the hinge weakens to an analogy and this note should be downgraded.
