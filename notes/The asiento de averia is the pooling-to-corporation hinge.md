# The asiento de averia is the pooling-to-corporation hinge

The comparative programme asserts a hinge between the pooling of loss and the pooling of capital, and has had no case in which the one is documented turning into the other. The *asiento de avería* is that case — but not by the route the abstract of [[Hierro Anibarro 2005 on the asiento de averia and the privileged company]] advertises, and the correction is worth more than the original claim.

The sequence is short and archivally dense. A compulsory *ad valorem* levy on the Indies trade, raised from 1521 to pay for the armed escort of the fleets, is devolved by the Crown to private contractors through the *asientos* from the 1610s. What the contractors then build is not an entity-shielded corporation. It is a **term partnership carrying four corporate components assembled by private capitulation**: limited liability in fully modern form; capital lock-in for the term; a fund divided into aliquot *puestos* with voting proportional to contribution; and the depersonalisation of the contributor, of whom "no es importante la calidad... sino la de su crédito." Perpetuity is absent by design, and entity shielding is not evidenced at all.

That inventory is the finding, and it cuts against the *asiento* being a corporate ancestor while confirming the decomposition thesis in its strongest form. **The components were available, in developed and explicit form, in a vehicle that was never made perpetual — and it left no descendants.** The Compañía del Norte (1624) and the Compañía de las Indias Orientales (1628), which Hierro Anibarro derives from the *asiento*, were both short-lived. This is the Roman diagnosis a second time and in better documents: transferability and shielding that are never bundled with the continuity-securing functions leave nothing behind. See [[The corporation is not the omega - the naties are an undisplaced niche]].

Three further properties earn the case its place in WP1 as well as WP2.

The pooled object is **protection**, not profit, and the hazard is an adversary who responds to the defence mounted — not a storm. Where that sits on [[The determinate-contingent typology carries the richness]] is an open question and better asked than assumed.

The pool is **compulsory**, which every other instrument in the comparative frame is not. That puts the framework a question rather than an illustration: if pooling raises each participant's time-average growth rate, why must it be enforced, and why do participants defect? The answer is available — protection is non-excludable, so the private and pooled optima diverge — but it has to be given.

And the pool **fails**. Hierro Anibarro's own account has the capital chronically hard to assemble, with "no verdaderos interesados en mantener las flotas," merchants sparing and mistrustful, and the *avería* practically extinct by the time the *Recopilación* of 1681 codified forty-six laws for it. A European pooling institution that collapses, with fiscal records, is worth more to the falsifiability case than three more that succeed — and it corrects the structural asymmetry by which Europe currently supplies the baseline and the successes while the negative cases are all non-European.
