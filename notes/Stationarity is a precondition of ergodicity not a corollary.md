# Stationarity is a precondition of ergodicity not a corollary

For stochastic processes the ergodicity definition is carried by the shift operator on path space: a **strictly stationary** process is ergodic if and only if the shift-invariant σ-algebra under its law is trivial. Stationarity enters as a precondition of the definition, not as a consequence of it.

The consequence is unforgiving and it is the point at which WP3 is most exposed. **A non-stationary process is not "non-ergodic"; it lies outside the domain over which the definition is stated.** Any formulation that describes historical processes as non-ergodic *because* history does not repeat has therefore conceded the argument before it begins — it has used a word with a precise technical meaning to assert something the word does not say. The referee who notices this does not need to engage the substantive claim at all.

The repair is to attach the ergodicity requirement to a **named observable** rather than to "history". The persistence literature's space-for-time substitution assumes that the cross-sectional distribution of some quantity at a fixed date coincides with the distribution of that quantity along a single unit's path over time. That is a genuine ergodicity requirement, statable and falsifiable — but only once the quantity is specified: institutional quality, a growth rate, a survival probability. Stated over a named observable the criterion is a technical claim; stated over "history" it is [[The word ergodic carries four incompatible senses|Davidson's fourth sense]] and inherits its vulnerabilities.

This also disciplines our own usage. Where the underlying increments are stationary and the failure is one of aggregation rather than of the ergodic theorem, we should say so — see [[Nothing in the ergodic theorem fails in geometric Brownian motion]]. Where the failure is genuine decomposition of the state space, we should say *that*, because it is the stronger claim and it is available to us — see [[An absorbing barrier breaks ergodicity by construction]].
