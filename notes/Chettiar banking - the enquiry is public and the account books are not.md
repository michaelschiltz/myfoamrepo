# Chettiar banking — the enquiry is public and the account books are not

**The Nattukottai Chettiars of Chettinad financed rice, teak and rubber across Burma, Ceylon, Malaya and South India** from the nineteenth century to about 1930. **What they were is documented; what they wrote is not available.**

## The scale, as the enquiries recorded it

From the Madras, Burma and Ceylon banking enquiry reports of 1929–1934, as summarised in a working paper read for this pass: working capital of the community estimated at **795 million to 1,200 million rupees**; **243 firms** in South India, **1,650 in Burma** (1929), **700 in Ceylon** (1916) falling to **556** (1934); **deposits from other Chettiars 60–80 per cent of all deposits**, proprietors' own capital 10–20 per cent.

## The arrangements, and why they are probably out of scope

- **Communal rate-setting**: a monthly meeting fixed the *nadappu* rate, the benchmark for deposits between Chettiar firms.
- **Mutual liquidity**: a trusted member in difficulty could borrow within the community below the outside rate — described in the paper as "informal deposit insurance".
- **Information**: blacklists of bad debtors circulated through the community.
- **A crisis that did not become a collapse**: the Ceylon "Chetty crisis" of 1925, after one over-levered firm failed.

**On the census's own precedent this is a coalition, not a pool.** The Geniza *ṣuḥba* was recorded as out — no contributed capital, no fund, no liability rule — and **the Chettiar arrangements are the same shape: reciprocal credit on reputation, with no standing fund and no contribution schedule.** They belong to the vault's argument about coalitions and information, not to the matrix. ⚠️ Read at one remove, from one working paper; if a fund is ever evidenced the determination changes.

## The archive question

**The account books — *kadai kanakku* — stayed with the firms**, and the classic study worked from books shown by informants rather than from a repository. **Burma's nationalisations and the 1942 evacuation destroyed or stranded much of the overseas record.** ⚠️ Stated from the general literature, not verified. **The public record is the enquiry** — see [[The colonial enquiry as South Asia's layer 3]] — **and the temple and caste-association records of Chettinad**, which were not surveyed here.
