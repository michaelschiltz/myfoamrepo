# National Records of Scotland

<https://www.nrscotland.gov.uk/>

**A separate archive of a separate legal system**, and for this project that is the whole reason it matters rather than an administrative footnote.

## The holdings

The records of the Scottish state before 1707 and of Scottish institutions after it: the Court of Session, the registers of deeds and sasines, the burgh records, and the archives of Scottish companies.

**The Register of Sasines and the Books of Council and Session** are registration systems of a kind England never had — **public registers of private deeds** — which means Scottish instruments are findable in a way English ones are not. ⚠️ Extent and coverage unverified, but the structural point is not in doubt: **a jurisdiction with a general register of deeds produces a locatable instrument record.**

## The claim this repository supports

Scots partnership law gives a firm **legal personality separate from its partners**, and English law does not. That is a live doctrinal fact, treated in the Scottish Law Commission's partnership work and in the academic literature, and it is a difference on the census's most theoretically loaded axis — inside one state, one commercial economy, one currency.

**It is argued at [[Scots partnership has legal personality and English partnership does not]]**, which is where it belongs, because it is a claim about institutions rather than about custody.
