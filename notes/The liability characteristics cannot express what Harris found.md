# The liability characteristics cannot express what Harris found

[[Harris 2020 on the late plurality of limited liability]] is held in the vault as intellectual
history: limited liability became a separate corporate attribute only around 1800 and a uniform one
only in the twentieth century. **Harris 2000 is the same argument met from inside the instrument,
and there it registers as a coding failure.**

Coding `chartered_corporation_england` blind, `AP3` and `LR1` both returned `.NR` — and not on the
source's silence. They returned `.NR` on **the source's own finding**, stated in terms: limited
liability is *"not … a binary legal feature that a corporation or an era has or lacks … a matrix
with several dimensions"* (printed 129). It is instrument-dependent throughout; it is **expressly
optional by statute after 1825** (6 Geo. IV c.92 cl. 2, at 265, 270–271); and it is **still excluded
from the privileges of incorporation in 1844** (285). A phase split was considered and refused,
because both phases would still be `.NR`.

## Why this is a result and not a gap

`LR1`'s value set asks which of a small number of liability regimes a form instantiates. Harris's
finding is that for this form, over this period, **the question has no answer at the level of the
form** — the regime is a property of the instrument, and the instrument varies within the type. A
characteristic that cannot take a value against the best monograph on its own period is not
under-read; it is mis-specified for the object.

That inverts the usual reading of a `.NR`. Ordinarily `.NR` records that the source did not reach
the question, and the repair is acquisition. Here acquisition will not repair it: **on present
holdings the `.NR` should be expected to stand**, and it is a defensible resting place precisely
because it rests on a stated finding rather than on silence. Compare
[[The deficit reading of absence is the scalar ranking in evidentiary form]]: an absence read as a
deficit is a ranking in disguise, and the same error is available one level down, where an
unfillable cell is read as a hole in the reading rather than as news about the vocabulary.

The teleological payoff is the one the vault already argues. If the attribute usually named as the
corporation's telos cannot be coded as an attribute at all for the English chartered corporation
across 1720–1844, the ascent narrative loses its measure — see
[[The corporation is not the omega - the naties are an undisplaced niche]] and
[[Whiggish history of finance names the ascent narrative]].

## Where this is soft

The row's period is `1720-1844`, argued from the cells rather than from the literature's
conventional break: neither 1600 nor 1720 is a change point in the form, and `CI1`, `CI2`, `FP2` and
`MG3` each change value before c.1680. But **every cell rests on evidence from c.1660–1837 and none
on 1720–1740**, which is the half of the period where the holdings are thinnest and the one
monograph covering it — DuBois 1938 — is unobtainable and, in any case, upstream of Harris rather
than independent of him ([[Galton's problem applies to the apparatus not only to the institutions]]).
