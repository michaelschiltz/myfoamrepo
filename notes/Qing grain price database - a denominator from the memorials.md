# Qing grain price database — a denominator from the memorials

清代糧價資料庫, Institute of Modern History, Academia Sinica (MHDB). <https://mhdb.mh.sinica.edu.tw/>

**About 2.19 million entries**: the **monthly grain prices of every prefecture and independent department** reported by provincial officials to the throne, **from 1736 to 1911**. Compiled by **Wang Yeh-chien** (1930–2014) from 1977 — at Kent State, Academia Sinica's Institute of Economics and the Chinese Academy of Social Sciences — and completed at the Institute of Modern History at the end of 2008. **Free, with online registration renewed annually**; unregistered use gives limited results. The Institute calls it the richest, most reliable and longest consistent statistical series in Chinese history before 1912.

## Why it gets its own note

**It is the third genuine denominator in the survey**, after [[The Sound Toll Registers]] and [[Princeton Geniza Project - the documentary database]] — **and the only one that is a time series of a hazard.** A price series across 175 years and every prefecture is a measured record of dearth.

**What it is an interpretation of**: price *reports*, filed by officials with reasons to report prices within expected bounds. **The reporting apparatus is the survival rule**, and the literature on grain-price reporting (Wilkinson on price quotations) is where its biases are discussed. ⚠️ Not read.

## What it bears on

**Nothing the census codes, and one argument the vault makes.** Against a covariate peril the only mitigation is across time — the vault's `A perfectly correlated peril leaves only the time average`. **The Qing state's ever-normal granaries were the institution of that argument at imperial scale, and this database is how their environment can be measured.** Parked with the Edo *shichibu tsumikin* as a pair — logbook 2, 2026-09-11 (vii).
