# The three published critiques do not agree with each other

The vault now holds all three published attacks on ergodicity economics. They are routinely cited together, and they make incompatible charges. Treating them as a bloc — in either direction — is an error available to us and to a referee, and worth foreclosing.

**Toda: EE is a trivial special case of expected utility.** The objective functions coincide at $\gamma = 1$ in the Merton portfolio problem, growth-rate optimisation dates to Kelly and Latané, and twelve years have added nothing. See [[Toda 2023 on ergodicity economics as pseudoscience]].

**Ford and Kay: growth-optimal behaviour is necessarily incompatible with expected utility.** Extending Samuelson (1971), no utility function whatever rationalises growth-optimal choice across additive and multiplicative settings; the approach therefore violates the von Neumann–Morgenstern axioms outright. See [[Ford and Kay 2023 on growth-optimal decision making]].

**Doctor, Wakker and Wang: EE gets preferences wrong where EUT gets them right**, with a counterexample intended to show the growth criterion producing an absurd ranking. See [[Doctor Wakker and Wang 2020 on the ergodicity problem]].

A framework cannot simultaneously be a trivial special case of expected utility theory and be incompatible with every utility function.

## The resolution, and why it favours the framework

The tension resolves, and the resolution is the eleven-author reply's central technical point: the mapping between the models holds **if and only if the utility function is the ergodicity transformation**, $u = f$, and $f$ is fixed by the dynamic.

Toda works inside a single dynamic — geometric Brownian motion, one portfolio problem — where $f = \log$ and the coincidence at $\gamma = 1$ follows. Ford and Kay work across dynamics, where $f$ changes with the process while EUT requires one agent to carry one utility function, so no single $u$ can track $f$. Both are correct about their own domain. Neither is entitled to the sweeping form in which the charge is made.

What the resolution shows is that **the disagreement between the critics is precisely the framework's own thesis**: that the transformation is set by the process rather than by the person. Toda sees agreement because he holds the process fixed; Ford and Kay see incompatibility because they vary it. If the transformation were a taste, as EUT holds, varying the process would not vary it and the two critics would agree. This is [[Defending expected utility by axiomatics forfeits its normative claim]] demonstrated from the outside, by opponents, without collusion.

## How to use this, and how not to

**Use it narrowly and once.** The observation is that citing "the published critiques" as a settled verdict misdescribes a literature in which the critics contradict each other on the central question. That is a fair and checkable point, and it does not require asserting that any of them is wrong.

**Do not use it to dismiss them.** Each critique contains at least one thing that lands and is conceded elsewhere in this cluster: Toda on the Kelly–Latané priority; Doctor, Wakker and Wang on the overclaiming and on the Meder experimental design; Ford and Kay on completeness across dynamics and on the finite-horizon status of the "guarantee." Mutual inconsistency at the level of the headline claim is compatible with each being right about something, and a reply that traded on the inconsistency to avoid the substance would be the mirror of the move the vault objects to at [[Answering EU's failures with more psychology instantiates the complaint]].

**One further asymmetry, recorded rather than pressed.** Only Ford and Kay drew a reply from the ergodicity-economics group, and only Ford and Kay conceded an error of their own in return. That exchange is the one to cite as the state of the argument; the other two stand unanswered on both sides.
