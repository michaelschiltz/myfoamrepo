# Doctor Wakker and Wang 2020 on the ergodicity problem

Source note for the leading published critique of ergodicity economics, and the base for the replies filed alongside it.

**Bibliographic facts, now verified and superseding the flag carried at [[Nothing in the ergodic theorem fails in geometric Brownian motion]].** Doctor, Jason N., Peter P. Wakker and Tong V. Wang, "Economists' views on the ergodicity problem," *Nature Physics* 16, no. 12 (2 December 2020): 1168. DOI `10.1038/s41567-020-01106-x`. Matters Arising to Peters, "The ergodicity problem in economics," *Nat. Phys.* 15 (2019): 1216–21. Peters replies in the same issue at page 1169, DOI `10.1038/s41567-020-01108-9`.

**Where the argument actually is.** The Matters Arising is a *single page* carrying one sentence of content — that Peters's "remedies are unjustified and give an inaccurate impression of the economics field as a whole." The whole substance sits in the freely available Supplementary Information, an eleven-page document dated February 2020. Anyone who cites the article without the SI has not read the objection. Wakker is the corresponding author and the argumentative voice is recognisably his.

**Reading status.** The SI is read in full. The article body and **Peters's reply are paywalled and unread beyond their opening lines** — the reply opens "I thank Doctor et al. for their interest in our work. Their Matters Arising suggests that they disagree with me, but I'm not sure where the disagreement lies." No characterisation of the reply's argument should be made from this vault until the text is obtained through Hokudai. [verify]

## The six sections

**§1. EU does not need parallel universes.** Expected utility represents choice over probability distributions; the EU value need not be realised or consumed, and there may be no outcome yielding it. Peters's "multiverse clones" line is read as a misunderstanding that the EU number must be physically instantiated. Ramsey, von Neumann–Morgenstern and Savage involve no averaging over persons or time.

**§2. Mathematical similarity without requiring ergodicity.** The isomorphism between intertemporal, interpersonal and inter-event aggregation is old news — Keeney and Raiffa ch. 9, Wakker 2010 Appendix D. Peters's own theory needs the ergodicity assumption; EU does not.

**§3. The growth rate does not fit preferences.** A constructed counterexample, plus the objection that real dynamic problems involve intermediate consumption and life-cycle patterns that no single multiplicative factor captures, and that growth rates are undefined for non-quantitative outcomes. Analysed at [[The Wakker counterexample refutes expected log utility too]] and [[The growth-rate criterion is asymptotic and the horizon comparison cuts both ways]].

**§4. Confusing ubiquity and explanatory power.** Time is ubiquitous, but so are risk, other people, neurons and molecules; a physicist could as well demand everyone study physics. Wakker's annotated bibliography files this under the keyword "own small expertise = meaning of life."

**§5. Falsifications of EU are well known.** Allais, Hershey–Schoemaker, Starmer's survey; prospect theory and the 2002 and 2017 Nobels. Attacking EU therefore does not touch economics, which abandoned EU decades ago. An Appendix argues that Meder et al. applied EU and prospect theory *statically* to a dynamic experiment, that their equation (10) omits probability weighting and is therefore not prospect theory, and that ambiguity aversion confounds the treatment difference.

**§6.** Restates: economics is a big field, EU is a small part of it, and criticising EU does not refute economics.

## What must be conceded

Three of these land, and the vault's existing discipline already requires conceding them.

- **§1 and §2 are correct as stated about the axiomatics.** The von Neumann–Morgenstern representation is a theorem about a preference relation and carries no ensemble interpretation. The aggregation isomorphism is genuinely old. What both sections leave untouched is the normative question — see [[Defending expected utility by axiomatics forfeits its normative claim]].
- **§4 lands on real overclaiming.** The quoted lines about economics having "drifted in a direction that places too much emphasis on psychology" are indefensible, and the vault already forbids the equivalent move at [[Do not claim history-dependence as the novelty]] and [[Separate the sociological and the methodological claims about economics]]. §4 is what overclaiming costs, and should be read as evidence for those guardrails rather than against the framework.
- **The Appendix's technical criticisms of Meder et al. appear correct** on the omission of probability weighting and on ambiguity aversion as a confound. Note however that the SI is dated February 2020 and targets the 2019 working paper; the published version is in the library as `JE86VR89` (2021) and **may have addressed these points. Compare the two before repeating either the criticism or the defence** [verify].
