# Þjóðskjalasafn Íslands — Iceland

The National Archives of Iceland.

## The honest note

**Iceland is almost certainly outside this project's evidentiary reach, and saying so is the note's content.**

Iceland had no towns, no merchant corporations, no notariate and, for most of the relevant period, **no legal internal commerce at all**: the Danish trade monopoly of 1602–1787 vested all Icelandic trade in licensed Danish merchants. The institutional forms the census codes did not exist there, and the records of the trade that did exist are Danish and are at [[Rigsarkivet Denmark - Daisy and Arkivalieronline]].

## Why it is worth a note anyway

Two reasons, and the second is the real one.

**Iceland has the best-documented pre-modern legal culture in northern Europe** — the sagas, *Grágás*, the Commonwealth's assembly institutions — and it is routinely used in the institutional-economics literature as a case of stateless order. **A project arguing about institutional durability will meet that literature**, and it is worth knowing in advance that the Icelandic material is a legal and literary corpus rather than a commercial-documentary one.

**And a monopoly is an institutional form.** The Danish trade monopoly is a *perpetual-monopoly* arrangement of the kind [[Identity-wrapper and perpetual-monopoly are distinct shielding facets]] separates out — imposed rather than granted to a merchant body, which makes it the limiting case. ⚠️ Not proposed; recorded because the limiting case is where a characteristic's boundary is tested.
