# First Historical Archives of China — Beijing

中国第一历史档案馆, 9 Qinian Street, Dongcheng. <https://fhac.com.cn/>

**About 10 million Ming and Qing documents** — nearly half of China's estimated 20 million historical files. Roughly 80 per cent Chinese and 20 per cent Manchu, with more than 50,000 Mongolian items and Western-language documents. **77 collections**, 44 searchable in the online catalogue. Origin in the Palace Museum's archive department of 1925; present name 1955.

## Access

- **New building opened 6 July 2022, with about 4.7 million items opened** — the largest single opening in years; the other half remains unopened.
- **Digitised documents and microfilm are read in the electronic reading room on site**; visits booked through the website or WeChat.
- Foreign researchers admitted since about 1980.
- **Copying capped at twenty documents per trip; no photography** — see [[PRC archival access - photography forbidden and catalogues curated]].

## What it holds for the census

**The state's view of private institutions, and the survey's best denominator.**

- **Grain-price and granary reports** — the source of [[Qing grain price database - a denominator from the memorials]], with the Taipei memorials.
- **Board of Revenue and judicial records** in which partnerships, native-place associations and pawnshops appear when they reached the state. ⚠️ Fonds not identified.
- **Baodi county's yamen archive** is among its holdings. ⚠️ Not verified in this pass.

**It is not where the census's Chinese rows come from.** Every coded Chinese cell rests on private contracts in a printed compilation — see [[Zhang Chuanxi 1995 - the contract compilation behind the census's Chinese rows]].
