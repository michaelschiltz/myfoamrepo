# Chancery and the equity pleadings

Series `C` at [[The National Archives - Kew]].

## Why equity rather than common law

Because the English business forms the census codes were **held together by trust and partnership**, and those are equity's subject matter. The deed-of-settlement company was a partnership whose capital was vested in trustees; its internal disputes, its dissolutions and the reach of its creditors were argued in Chancery.

**Chancery pleadings state the parties' case in narrative**, at length, with the deed usually recited — which makes them a far better source for what an arrangement was understood to do than a common-law judgment reporting an outcome.

## The specific hope

[[The creditor limb of entity shielding has no witness]] records that no coded source answers whether members' *creditors* could reach a firm's assets. The Antwerp answer to that gap is an insolvency archive — [[Insolvente Boedelskamer - Antwerp]]. **The English answer, if there is one, is here**: a creditor's bill against a partnership's assets is pleaded, answered, and decided, in a series that runs for centuries.

⚠️ **This is a hypothesis about a series and the series is enormous.** Chancery is notoriously difficult to search by subject, the finding aids are by party name, and the volume is such that a badly specified question will not return. **Establish whether a subject route exists before treating this as reachable**; if it does not, the realistic route is through the secondary literature's footnotes, as recommended for Simancas at [[Archivo General de Simancas]].
