# Dunhuang *she* associations — bylaws for a funeral fund

**社 / 社邑.** Lay associations at Dunhuang, documented by **their own papers** from the second half of the ninth century through the tenth. **This is the best-shaped census candidate the non-European survey has found, and it is the only one whose rulebooks, circulars and death-benefit schedules all survive in the same archive.**

## The record

Three document types, in the manuscripts themselves:

- **Bylaws (社條 *shetiao*)** — the rules of a named association.
- **Circulars (社司轉帖 *shesi zhuantie*)** — the officers summoning members to a meeting or an event.
- **Administrative papers** — applications, notices, accounts.

**Officers**: president (社長), manager (社官), secretary (錄事), sometimes an elder (社老).

**What the members owed each other.** One circular fixes the contribution at a death as **麵一斗、粟一斗、色物半疋** — a peck of flour, a peck of millet, half a bolt of coloured cloth. A women's association's bylaws require, at the death of a member's relative, **"one vat of oil, one catty of white flour, and one peck of millet"**, plus food and wine, with full members attending the funeral in white mourning garments. **Breach is punished**: a banquet for the whole association, and for a member who tries to leave, **"three whacks of the stick" plus the banquet, "absolutely no exceptions."**

**Scale of one association**: the four surviving women's bylaws show **twelve to twenty-six members**; the bylaws of **959** list fifteen.

**Dates and shelfmarks of those four**: **658 or earlier (Turfan, 67 TAM 74); 876 or 936 (draft, BD 14682); 959 (S. 527, British Library); 968 (P. 3489)** — **which is the dispersal in one footnote: London, Paris, Beijing and a Turfan tomb.**

## Why it matters to the census

**Every element the census asks for is in the instrument**: a defined membership, a written rule, **a contribution schedule in kind**, a payout **triggered by a death**, a penalty for default, and named officers. **The *kō* acquisition rule — no *kō* moves a cell without a *shihōsho* — is satisfied before the work starts: the *shetiao* is the *shihōsho*.**

**And it answers, from the other end, the failure the Chinese pass recorded.** `huiguan` is uncoded because a native-place association's surviving record is stone that names donors and gives no fund — [[Guild stele inscriptions - the huiguan's surviving record]]. **Here is a Chinese association, seven centuries earlier, whose paper survived because the cave was sealed.** ⚠️ Whether the *she* is an ancestor, a cousin or an unrelated form of the later *huiguan* is a question this note does not answer.

## Where the texts are

Manuscripts at the British Library, the BnF, the National Library of China and the IOM; **transcriptions in the Chinese editorial programmes** — Ning Ke and Hao Chunwen's compilation of *she* documents and Hao's ongoing series on the British collection. ⚠️ **The size of the whole *she* corpus was not established in this pass**; the compilation's contents were not seen. English-language treatments: Teiser 2020 on the women's bylaws, Hao Chunwen 2021 in *Hualin International Journal of Buddhist Studies* 4.2.
