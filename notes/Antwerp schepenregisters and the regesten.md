# Antwerp *schepenregisters* and the regesten

At [[FelixArchief - Antwerp city archive]]. Finding aid: the *Regesten van de schepenregisters*.

**The Antwerp equivalent of a notarial archive**, and it was folded into the FelixArchief note in the first Belgian pass when it has a finding-aid problem of its own.

## The series and its calendar

The *schepenregisters* record the acts the aldermen passed: conveyances, obligations, sureties, partnership agreements, and the *certificatieboeken* in which the city certified commercial facts for use elsewhere. Coverage begins in the fourteenth century. The Rijksarchief separately runs a **volunteer project analytically processing aldermen's deeds for the Antwerp region**, so a second body of derived description is accumulating outside the city archive.

## The problem, which is the reason for the note

**The *regesten* are a calendar, not the series.** They are abstracts compiled by editors over the nineteenth and twentieth centuries, selecting and summarising, and a researcher who works the regesten is working a nineteenth-century reading of the fifteenth.

That is exactly the hazard [[Refusals are observations of the filter not inferences from survivors]] names, with a twist that makes it worse: **the calendar is searchable and the registers are not**, so the filter is also the path of least resistance. Anything counted in the regesten is a count of what nineteenth-century editors thought worth abstracting — and their interests were topographical and genealogical far more than commercial.

**The operative rule: cite the register, not the regest.** Use the calendar to find, never to count.
