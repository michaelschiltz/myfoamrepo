# Insurance is a split-off from the sea loan not a coordinate mechanism

Harris's three-way distinction treats allocation, spreading and pooling as coordinate mechanisms —
three ways of meeting a peril, to be compared across cases. For two of the three that is a category
error about their relation. Premium insurance did not arise beside the sea loan; it came **out of**
it, under a specific and datable pressure.

Lohsse's conclusion:

> In der Tat hat sich … die Versicherung in der mittelalterlichen Rechtswissenschaft **unter dem
> Einfluss des Zinsverbots aus dem Seedarlehen abgespalten**. … Die **Trennung von Kredit- und
> Versicherungszweck** … wird so zu einem besonders ansprechenden Beispiel dafür, welche
> Fortschritte die Kanonisten des 13. und 14. Jhs. in der Anpassung des Rechts an die
> wirtschaftlichen Bedürfnisse ihrer Zeit erzielt haben.

The sea loan fused two functions: it advanced capital and it transferred the peril of the voyage.
The usury prohibition made the fused instrument unlawful in its priced form, and the canonist
response was not to abandon it but to **separate the purposes** — leaving a credit contract on one
side and a contract for bearing peril alone on the other. Insurance is the residue of that
separation. Allocation and spreading are connected by descent, and the descent event has a
mechanism, a cause and a century.

## What follows for the comparison

A typology whose members stand in a relation of descent cannot be used to sort cases as though they
were alternatives faced by an actor. Asking whether a given form "allocates or spreads" presumes the
two were simultaneously available options; before the separation they were not distinguishable, and
the sea loan sits on the near side of a line that had not yet been drawn.

This is why the sea loan satisfies both descriptions at once and why no reading settles it. The
lender is named by contract, which is allocation; he is also a party with no operational stake paid
an ex-ante premium for carrying the peril, which is spreading. The instrument is the common
ancestor, and a common ancestor answers to the description of both descendants.

Zeno reached the same place from the documents ninety years earlier, without the juristic material.
His Palermo charter-parties, in which the *patronus navis* lends to the merchant chartering his
vessel *ad risicum maris et gentium*, he reads as *una forma larvata di assicurazione associata al
noleggio* — concealed insurance attached to a charter. His evidence is proportional: the advance runs
at a near-constant fraction of cargo value, and on total loss the merchant loses the cargo but keeps
the money, which is an indemnity paid in advance.

## The terminological care

The relation is **descent**, and institutions are transmitted as texts and drafting practices — a
formulary modified under doctrinal pressure, not a lineage with a heritable disposition. The
reserved-word rule holds: see [[Never let the units slide from instruments to populations]]. Nor is
this convergence in any sense; there is nothing independent about it.
