# Adamou Berman and Peters 2026 on the two growth rates

Source note. GDP per capita growth is an ensemble-average rate; the rate an individual income experiences is a time average; the gap between them is an inequality measure. The paper that could turn the Great Divergence corollary from a horizon into a calculation.

**Bibliographic facts.** Adamou, Alexander, Yonatan Berman, and Ole Peters. 2026. "The Two Growth Rates of the Economy." *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society A* 384 (2334): 20250163. DOI `10.1098/rsta.2025.0163`. Zotero `RKQBDUYJ`; citekey `adamouTwoGrowthRates2026`. LML, King's College London (Berman), Santa Fe Institute (Peters). Received 18 November 2025, accepted 10 June 2026. Supplementary material `10.6084/m9.figshare.c.8748623`. Pp. 1–6 of 12 read from page images; the USA and France application unread.

## What it does

- **Two rates under random multiplicative growth (§2).** The growth rate of average income, $\Delta \ln \langle x \rangle / \Delta t$, approximates GDP per capita growth and is *plutocratic*: each person's growth rate is weighted by income share (eq. 2.12). The time-average rate, $\langle \Delta \ln x / \Delta t \rangle$, weights everyone equally and is the growth rate of the geometric-mean income, named "democratic domestic product" per capita (eq. 2.22).
- **The ergodicity gap.** The difference between the two rates; under multiplicative growth it is the mean logarithmic deviation, Theil's second index.
- **Cross-sections suffice (eq. 2.20).** The time-average rate needs only the mean log income at the start and end of the period, not panel data, and is independent of rank mobility.
- **The distinction is older than the programme.** Footnote 4 credits Chenery et al. with first articulating it, as weighting of quintile growth rates; existing names include the "people's growth rate".

## What it does for the project

- **It makes the Great Divergence corollary measurable.** Part 2 raises, as a fenced corollary, that polities scored as laggards were suppressing variance under absorbing-barrier dynamics, so that an ensemble-averaged wealth metric misreads them. Maddison-type comparisons of GDP per capita are ensemble averages; social tables and ancient-inequality reconstructions supply the dispersion needed for the time-average rate. Part 2 now says "measurable as the gap between ensemble- and time-average growth" and cites the paper. The corollary remains fenced.
- **It is a second instance of the ascent narrative as a Jensen error.** See [[The ascent narrative is the Jensen error as historiography]].

## What must be said before using it

The paper estimates a time-average quantity from two cross-sections by invoking ergodicity of the growth rate under an i.i.d. multiplicative null (eq. 2.19). That is a licensed space-for-time step, and a referee who has read Objective 3 will notice it. Filed at [[The time-average growth rate from cross-sections is a licensed space-for-time step]].
