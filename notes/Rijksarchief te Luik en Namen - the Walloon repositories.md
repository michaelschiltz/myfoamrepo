# Rijksarchief te Luik en Namen — the Walloon repositories

Part of [[Rijksarchief in Belgie - the state archives network]]. Liège: <https://www.arch.be/> · Namur: same network.

**The first Belgian pass covered Flanders and Brabant and omitted Wallonia entirely**, which is a defect worth naming rather than quietly repairing.

## What is there

**Liège** was a prince-bishopric — a separate polity from the Habsburg Netherlands until 1795, with its own law, its own coinage and its own commercial institutions. That independence is the point: it is a **neighbouring jurisdiction under a different sovereign**, which is the configuration this project's comparative method most wants and most rarely gets inside one modern country.

**Namur** and the Walloon repositories hold the county and seigneurial records.

⚠️ Series, extent and digitisation all unestablished. Nothing here has been checked.

## Why the omission mattered

Not because Liège is likely to be rich in maritime or company material — it is landlocked and its economy was metallurgical. **Because a survey that quietly follows a language boundary has imported a nineteenth-century political fact into a study of the fifteenth.** The census's own terminology rules exist to stop exactly that kind of anachronism entering through a word; this is the same error entering through a search strategy.

The honest statement: Wallonia is probably a low-yield region for this project, and *probably* is doing work in that sentence that no evidence supports yet.
