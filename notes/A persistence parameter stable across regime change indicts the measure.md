# A persistence parameter stable across regime change indicts the measure

Clark's headline finding is that the decay of status correlation with lineage distance is **unchanged from 1600 to 2022** — across enclosure, industrialisation, mass literacy, universal suffrage, the welfare state and two world wars. He reads the constancy as evidence of a biological substrate. The reading runs the other way.

## The argument

An invariance that survives every regime change in modern history is either a very deep constant of the object or a property of the apparatus measuring it. The project's own framework supplies the prior: a genuinely non-ergodic historical process, in which the structural mapping from initial condition to outcome is itself rebuilt by each regime, **should not** yield a stable transmission parameter. Stability of that kind is what one expects from a measure whose units are defined by the institution generating them.

Clark's identification runs on surnames. Surname-linked status is not an observation of the classified population; it is an observation of a classification system which the same regime changes did *not* dismantle. Read that way, the constancy is evidence for the persistence of the ranking apparatus rather than of anything ranked by it.

This is the strongest available objection to the paper and it belongs to this project rather than to the sociologists, because it follows from the framework's own commitments rather than from a general suspicion of quantification.

## Relation to the other two objections

Three distinct attacks, and they should not be run together.

The **covariance attack**, at [[Never let the units slide from instruments to populations]]: an environment transmitted along the same lineage generates the same correlation structure as additive transmission, so the character has no discriminating power. This attacks the inference from the decay curve to the mechanism.

The **dispersion attack**, at [[Fat tails do not require fat-tailed inputs]]: outcome dispersion under multiplicative dynamics with a barrier is uninformative about endowment dispersion. This constrains the interpretation but does not touch the decay curve, which is Clark's actual evidence.

The **invariance attack**, here: the stability of the parameter across regimes is itself the anomaly, and the natural explanation implicates the measure. This is the only one of the three that engages the finding Clark actually leads with.

## Where to place it

Probably not in the application. It reads as an attack on a named living scholar working adjacent to the heritability literature, and Part 2 has no need of one; the WP3 case list is already carrying Voigtländer–Voth and Nunn. Better held for the methodological flagship article, where the argument can be made at the length it needs and without the grant's political exposure. The same judgement recorded at the end of [[Never let the units slide from instruments to populations]].

## Credit where due

Clark states explicitly that observational data cannot establish genetic causation, and that the transmission has only a *form* which parallels additive determination under strong assortative mating. That disclaimer is real and the note should not be written as though it were absent. The objection is that foregrounding the parallel while disclaiming the mechanism produces a structure which predictably survives translation into the stronger claim — the same defect as the title-text gap in [[Voigtlander and Voth measured the relaxation time and reported a mixture]].
