# isqa

The Jewish investment partnership defined at BT *Bava Metzia* 104b by Rav Idi bar Avin's formula *pelga milveh u-pelga pikkadon* — **half loan, half bailment**. An investor (*baʿal ha-maʿot*) advances capital to a manager (*ʿosek*); half vests in the manager as a debt he owns and owes, half remains the investor's property held on deposit. Profit and loss follow ownership on each half.

The bifurcation is engineered against two doctrinal failure modes at once. All-bailment yields the investor a return on capital contributed without labour, and has the manager labouring gratuitously on another's money — *avak ribbit* on both counts, with no [[Skin in the game|skin in the game]] to boot. All-loan makes the capital the manager's outright, so any stipulated return to the investor is *ribbit ketzutzah*, flat. The split threads between them, and the residue is mopped up by requiring a wage as an idle worker (*sekharo ke-poʿel batel*) or an asymmetric profit/loss ratio favouring the manager.

The structural consequence is the important one: the manager is a **debtor** for half the stake, so his exposure is not bounded by his contribution. Contrast the *qirāḍ*, where the *mudārib* risks labour and not capital ([[Skin in the game is asymmetric in the mudaraba]]), and the [[commenda]], where the agent's downside is capped at what he put in. See [[The isqa refuses the agent's shield]].
