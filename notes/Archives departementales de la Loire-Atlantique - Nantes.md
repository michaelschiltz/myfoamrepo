# Archives départementales de la Loire-Atlantique — Nantes

<https://archives.loire-atlantique.fr/>

Nantes was France's principal slave-trading port, and its departmental archive holds the *armement* records — the fitting-out of voyages — together with the *amirauté de Nantes* in série `B`.

## What an *armement* record is, and why the census wants it

A French Atlantic voyage was financed by a **société d'armement**: a partnership formed for one voyage, with subscribed shares, a managing *armateur*, and a distribution at the end. **That is the *partenrederij* and the *commenda* under a third name**, and the census codes both.

Nantes therefore offers a French instance of a form the census currently reads from Dutch and Mediterranean sources. [[A migrated institution collects local names not local forms]] is the standing warning against treating the name as the form; the corresponding positive test is whether the *clauses* match, and it can be run here against [[Gemeentearchief Zaanstad - the partenrederij country]] and the Italian material.

## The thing that cannot be left implicit

The voyages whose financial structure makes this archive valuable were slaving voyages, and the *armement* papers are the accounting of that trade. **The structure and the cargo are not separable in the sources and should not be separated in the writing.** Same statement as at [[Zeeuws Archief - the Zeeland chamber and the MCC]] and [[Archives nationales d'outre-mer - Aix-en-Provence]]; it is being made a third time because it applies a third time, not as a formula.
