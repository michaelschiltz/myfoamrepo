# Indian merchant account books — *bahi khata* and *kadai kanakku*

**The indigenous commercial record of South Asia is the firm's own book** — the cloth-bound *bahi* of the north and west, kept in *mahajani* script and its regional cousins, and the *kadai kanakku* of the Nattukottai Chettiars in the south. **There is no institutional corpus of them.**

## Where they are, in order of how much there is

- **With families and firms.** The default. Rudner's account of Chettiar banking rests partly on "account books from Nakarattar banking houses" and on documents collected by an informant, not on a repository.
- **In state archives, as princely-state administration.** [[Rajasthan State Archives - Bikaner and the princely bahis]] holds *bahiat* among the Rajput states' records: **these are the state's books, not the bazaar's.**
- **In foreign university libraries, by purchase.** Indiana University's Lilly Library holds a **Rajasthan princely state collection, mid-1700s to the 1940s** — Jaipur, Karauli, Tonk, Uniara, Bharatpur — with petitions, correspondence, ledgers, receipts and *bahīs* in Persian, Marwari, Urdu and Gujarati, in *shikasta* and a western Nagari variant. **Boxes 1–7 minimally processed, advance permission required, the rest closed pending processing.** ⚠️ Acquisition route not stated in the source read.
- **In the colonial record, as evidence.** Books seized, summoned or copied in litigation and enquiry. ⚠️ The Deccan Riots Commission of 1875–76 is the obvious instance and **was not verified in this pass**.

## What this does to the evidence

**1. Survival is by litigation, enquiry or the market** — the acquirer-custody pattern of [[Chinese contracts - the private document boom and the market that feeds it]], **without China's fifty years of institutional collection.** Nothing here resembles the Huizhou corpus.

**2. The unit is lost first.** A firm's books make sense as a set; a single *bahi* bought at auction is an instance with no context, the problem the Chinese pass put as the household box.

**3. The script and the accounting are both specialist.** *Mahajani* numerals and abbreviations, and a bookkeeping vocabulary of *nam*/*jama* entries, need training that few historians have. **This is the Chinese commercial-numeral problem and the Ottoman siyakat problem in a third tradition.** ⚠️ Stated from the general literature, not from books read.
