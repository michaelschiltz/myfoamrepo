# Three immune functions mark a pool that has become a person

**Cremer and Sixt's three stages of colony defence — at the border, within the body, around the germline — read across to institutions as three functions an individuated arrangement should perform and a mere pool need not: border defence against outsiders' claims, policing of members who would draw on the whole for themselves, and control of membership. Their joint presence is a codable criterion for the line between a pool and a person. It is a coding probe, not a metaphor, and it inherits Cremer and Sixt's own restriction to phenomenological analogy.**

## The correspondences

- **Border defence → entity shielding.** The pool's assets are protected from the personal creditors of its members, so that one member's ruin does not infect the whole (Hansmann, Kraakman and Squire 2006, *Harvard Law Review* 119: 1333–1403). [[Entity shielding and owner shielding protect different trajectories]]; [[The creditor limb of entity shielding has no witness]] names the evidential difficulty.
- **Policing internal cheaters → anti-lopsidedness doctrine.** Rules that forbid one party to draw the pool's gains while shedding its losses: *al-ghurm bi-l-ghunm*, the Roman prohibition of the *societas leonina*, the *ʿisqa*'s mandatory exposure ([[Skin in the game is asymmetric in the mudaraba]], [[The isqa refuses the agent's shield]]). Withdrawal restrictions — capital lock-in — police the defector who leaves with the store.
- **Self/non-self recognition → membership rules.** Admission, expulsion, merger and transfer of a member's place: who counts as inside.

## The prediction

A form showing all three has crossed into individuation; one showing none is a pool however long it lasts; partial cases locate the drafting stage of the transition. The test is a composite over existing components of the five-part decomposition (entity shielding, capital lock-in, transferable claims) plus the loss-rule and membership fields [check against the HistorEE_codebooks schema before proposing a column]. It also predicts an order: if [[Legal scaffolding gives a pool collective properties it does not yet own]] is right, border defence is first supplied from outside — by a court or a charter — and only later held by the unit itself.

## The limit

Colonies can expel members and abandon nests, which bodies cannot; institutions can do both. The correspondence is therefore closer for institutions than for organisms in one respect, and that is a reason to code it, not to celebrate it.
