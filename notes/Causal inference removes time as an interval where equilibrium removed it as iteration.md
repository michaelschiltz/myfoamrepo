# Causal inference removes time as an interval where equilibrium removed it as iteration

The Post-Keynesian and complexity challenges to equilibrium never reached the persistence literature, and not because they were refuted. The empirical turn did not run on equilibrium theory. It ran on the potential-outcomes framework, which disposes of time differently: a unit, a treatment, an outcome, and time as the gap between the date of treatment and the date of measurement.

Three erasures now stand side by side. Arrow–Debreu makes time a coordinate ([[Arrow-Debreu dissolves time rather than neglecting it]]). The line from Galton to Clark makes it the iteration of a fixed law ([[Time-homogeneity not stationarity is what removes time]]). The causal-inference reading makes it an interval. None treats time as an irreversible process in which the operator itself is rebuilt.

The third erasure explains what otherwise looks paradoxical in the persistence literature. It celebrates that history matters — the long shadow, the deep root — and owes nothing to equilibrium; yet it needs the cross-section to be an ensemble of exchangeable units standing for the trajectory of one. That is the fork of [[A significant persistence coefficient is evidence against its own licensing condition]]: on the temporal reading the licence is contradicted by the finding; on the causal reading the estimand is a contemporaneous contrast and every temporal word is decoration. The critique therefore cannot be aimed at an equilibrium the literature does not hold. It must be aimed at the exchangeability it does hold. See [[The split is over whether the explanandum is a state or a trajectory]].
