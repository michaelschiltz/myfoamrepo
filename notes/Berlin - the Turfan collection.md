# Berlin — the Turfan collection

**Four German expeditions brought about 40,000 text fragments to Berlin**: Grünwedel, December 1902 – April 1903; von Le Coq, November 1904 – December 1905; Grünwedel and von Le Coq, December 1905 – June 1907; von Le Coq, June 1913 – February 1914.

**Where they are now, in one city and three institutions:**

- **Berlin-Brandenburgische Akademie der Wissenschaften** — about **12,000 Middle Iranian and Old Turkic fragments**, including **6,000 Uyghur** and **3,500 Manichaean**.
- **Staatsbibliothek zu Berlin, Oriental Department** — about **6,000 Chinese, 8,000 Sanskrit, 4,000 Tocharian, 300 Syriac, 200 Tibetan, 100 Mongolian**.
- **Museum für Asiatische Kunst** — murals, paintings, textiles, sculpture.

**IDP records 31,713 manuscripts for the German collections** as of August 2023.

## The war, and what it did

The fragments were put in salt mines during the Second World War; **"precise figures for losses of texts during the war cannot be determined."** ⚠️ **The wartime removals to the Soviet Union, and what returned, were not researched in this pass** and are the first thing to check before any claim about completeness.

## Layers

**Layer 2**: the **Digitales Turfan-Archiv** at the academy publishes images of the Iranian and Turkic fragments. ⚠️ Image counts and licence not established. **Layer 3**: the German editorial tradition — *Berliner Turfantexte* and the catalogue series. ⚠️ Not examined. **The Uyghur contracts in the U series are edited mainly in Japan** — [[Old Uyghur contracts - the documentary layer under the ortoq]].
