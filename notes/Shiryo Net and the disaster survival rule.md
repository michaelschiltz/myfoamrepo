# Shiryō Net and the disaster survival rule

<https://siryo-net.jp/> — 歴史資料ネットワーク, founded after the **1995 Great Hanshin–Awaji earthquake** to rescue privately held historical materials in disaster areas.

## The Japanese instance of pattern 3, and it is serial

The European survey found five catastrophic losses, each a single fire. **Japan's record is exposed to a recurring hazard**, and the survival rule is written by the recurrence:

- **1923, the Great Kantō earthquake.** The Tokyo Imperial University library and the law, letters and economics buildings burned (the university's own record of 3 September 1923); the Ministry of Finance's records were largely lost, a small set of burnt survivors reaching the National Archives. ⚠️ No volume counts established.
- **1945, the air raids.** The Mitsui Bunko's building at Togoshi burned on 24 May 1945 — see [[Mitsui Bunko - the Mitsui house archive]].
- **1995, Hanshin–Awaji.** Shiryō Net founded. **The Kōnoike family's decision to donate its papers to Osaka City was taken in the earthquake's wake** — see [[Osaka Museum of History - the Konoike family documents]]. A disaster moved a merchant house's archive from private to public custody.
- **2011, Tōhoku; 2018, Typhoon Jebi; 2024, Noto; 2025, Aomori** — all on Shiryō Net's own record of responses, with regional networks in more than twenty prefectures.

**And a slow loss beside the fast ones: municipal amalgamation.** The Heisei mergers of 1999–2010 roughly halved the number of municipalities, and the loss of their records is a recognised concern. ⚠️ The halving is from general knowledge; the scale of record loss not established.

## What that does to inference

**A disaster has a shape the European fires lacked: it is geographic and dated.** A gap in the surviving *kō* record of coastal Miyagi after 2011, or of Kobe after 1995, can be asked about. **But the rescue creates its own selection**: what is rescued is what volunteers reached, and what reaches public custody after a disaster is what a family chose to give. The Kōnoike donation is the model case — **the survival rule includes the donor's decision**.
