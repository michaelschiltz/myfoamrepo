# *Bodmerei* — where the German bottomry records are

**A custody note for a coded row.** `bodmerei` is in `loss_mitigation_forms`, alongside `bodemerij_amsterdam`, and the pair is one of the census's clearest near-identical-form comparisons across two legal traditions.

## The asymmetry between the pair

The Dutch side is in excellent shape: [[Stadsarchief Amsterdam - the notarial archive]] is at layer 3, the instruments are notarial, and the census's row was coded from a printed edition drawn out of that archive.

**The German side has no notariate of the same kind.** In the Hanseatic cities the civic register did the notary's work — the *Niederstadtbuch* at [[Archiv der Hansestadt Lubeck]], the equivalent books at Hamburg and Bremen. **So the two rows are not sourced from the same kind of document even though the form is the same**, and that is a coding fact, not a filing one.

⚠️ This should be checked before the pair is used for any comparative claim. A difference between `bodemerij_amsterdam` and `bodmerei` may be a difference between a notarial minute and a civic register entry — **a difference in what the record type records, not in what the parties agreed.** That is exactly the failure mode [[Independence of apparatus is not independence of question]] names.

## Where to look, in order

1. **The printed law.** Hamburg's eighteenth-century systematic treatments of *Assekuranz und Bodmerei* are digitised and full-text. Cheapest entry; see [[Staatsarchiv Hamburg]].
2. **The admiralty adjudications** at Hamburg, and their Bremen and Lübeck equivalents ⚠️.
3. **The civic registers** — the Lübeck *Niederstadtbuch* and its counterparts, which is where individual contracts sit.

⚠️ Nothing in this note has been confirmed against a finding aid. It records the shape of the problem and the order of attack.
