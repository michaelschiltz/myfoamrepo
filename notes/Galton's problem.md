# Galton's problem

**Concept anchor.** In 1889 Edward Tylor read a paper to the Royal Anthropological Institute correlating marriage and descent customs across some three hundred societies. Francis Galton, in the discussion, asked the question that has carried his name ever since: are these cases independent, or are many of them historically related, so that the correlation rests on far fewer observations than the count suggests? Correlation across cultural units presupposes that the units are separate draws. Where they share ancestry or have been in contact, they are not, and the effective *n* collapses toward the number of lineages rather than the number of cases.

The canonical formulation matters here because it is **a rule about the burden of proof, not a caveat about noise**. For contiguous, contemporaneous, contact-exposed traditions the default is non-independence. Independence is the thing that has to be earned, case by case, and a comparison that treats it as the unmarked condition will manufacture the recurrences it sets out to discover. See [[The western family needs only plausible common descent not proven genealogy]].

Two routes earn it, and the project uses both.

**Calibration.** Document a clean case of transmission so that the signature of real diffusion is in hand, and read the remaining cases against it. This is the work the *ortoq* does: see [[The ortoq is the discriminating instrument]].

**Internal evidence.** Look past functional agreement, which is weak evidence of anything because function is constrained by the problem, to the arbitrary particulars, which are not. See [[Divergent arbitrary particulars are the independence diagnostic]].

## The reflexive point, which is the interesting one

Galton's problem is the hazard Objective 1 faces. Non-ergodicity is the hazard Objective 3 attacks. **They are the same defect.** The persistence regression treats a spatial cross-section as a set of independent realisations of one process; the cross-civilisational comparison treats a set of traditions as independent arrivals at one solution. In both cases an inference from a population to a mechanism is licensed only if the units are what the inference assumes them to be, and in both cases that condition is asserted rather than shown.

This is worth saying out loud rather than hoping no one notices. A project whose methodological objective is that quantitative history covertly assumes independence, and whose empirical objective covertly assumes independence, has a problem. A project that names the single defect, shows it operating in both literatures, and applies to its own comparison the discipline it demands of the persistence programme, has a thesis. The second is available for the cost of a paragraph, and the paragraph belongs in Part 1.
