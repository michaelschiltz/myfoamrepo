# Labour exchange is the pooling type the census cannot see

`loss_mitigation_forms` holds nineteen forms and not one of them pools labour. Every coded arrangement moves money, goods or land: contributions to a fund, an ex-post assessment, a survivorship draw, a reallocation of arable. Reciprocal labour is a pooling institution with the same functional profile and it has no representation at all.

The pair is named in the literature already. Suzuki Eitarō's 1963 article sets 契 against Japanese 講 for financial cooperation and Korean **품앗이 (*pumasi*)** against Japanese **結 (*yui*)** for labour cooperation — treating the two halves as parallel, and both as instances of the same underlying principle. Whatever one makes of the financial half ([[Kye is not a ROSCA and the equation is an artefact of comparison with mujin]]), the labour half names two institutions that are simply missing from the matrix.

**And *pumasi* is not an informal courtesy — it has an accounting rule.** Suzuki: if A offers his labour to B, B is required to return the equivalent labour to A; the principle extends to mutual help among more than three members; and **the value of the labour is calculated in accordance with the differences in sex and years of age of the labourer.** That is an explicit equivalence schedule converting heterogeneous labour into commensurable units — a unit of account without money.

Which is what makes it interesting rather than merely absent. The characteristic set was built around funds: `CN1` asks when contributions fall due, `CN2` how they are measured, `LS2` whether members hold a residual claim on the fund. A labour-exchange association has no fund, and the census has already met one form with that property — *warichi*, whose `CN1` is `.NA` precisely because it reallocates an existing asset rather than assembling one. Labour exchange would be a second, and for a different reason: the obligation is bilateral and outstanding rather than pooled and held. There is nothing to be a residual claimant *of*.

**Three things it would test.** Whether `CN2 contribution basis` can express an equivalence schedule graded by sex and age — `equal`, `proportional-to-stake` and `proportional-to-capacity` were written for money and the third is closest, but a tariff is not a proportion. Whether the census can represent an arrangement whose accounting unit is time rather than value. And whether the pooling facet survives contact with a form that pools without a pool.

**One caution, and it cuts against Suzuki.** He writes that all four institutions rest on 'the supposition that all human beings are equal', two sentences after describing a labour tariff graded by sex and age. The tariff is evidence of the opposite: labour was explicitly *not* treated as fungible across persons, and the schedule records whose hour counted for how much. Anyone coding *pumasi* or *yui* should treat the equality claim as the author's framing and the tariff as the datum ([[Refusals are observations of the filter not inferences from survivors]]).

**Not a proposal to code it yet.** 結 is attested across the Japanese material already in hand and could be coded from sources the team reads; *pumasi* could not, for the reasons that apply to kye generally. Coding the Japanese half alone would put a single labour form in the census with no comparator, which is how a facet acquires zero variance and stops carrying information. Better to code 結 and *pumasi* together when the Korean side is properly sourced, or neither.
