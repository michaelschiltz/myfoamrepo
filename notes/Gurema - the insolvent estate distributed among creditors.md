# *Guremâ* — the insolvent estate distributed among creditors

**Where an estate's debts exceeded its assets — *borca batık tereke* — the Ottoman court did not pay the creditors in full: it distributed what there was among them proportionally, by the *guremâ* procedure, and recorded the distribution in the register.**

**This is the Ottoman creditor limb, in a serial source.** It answers, case by case and in writing, the question the census has been unable to source: **when the assets run out, who reaches what, and in what order or proportion.**

## What has been established

- **The procedure**: pro-rata distribution among the *garīm*, the creditors, when the estate cannot pay in full; studied "according to fatwa collections and court records", which means **the ground of the ranking is discussable and not only the outcome** — the condition [[The coding commons must record stated rationale not only component presence]] sets, and the open question left at [[Insolvente Boedelskamer - Antwerp]] and [[Desolate Boedelkamer - Amsterdam]]. ⚠️ Whether the *register entries themselves* state reasoning was not established; the studies read do.
- **Two worked local studies**, both 2024 and both in Turkish: Muazzez Gündüz Oğul, "Tereke kayıtlarına göre guremâ usulü ve servet-borç ilişkisi: Bursa örneği (1703–1710)", *Osmanlı Mirası Araştırmaları Dergisi* 11/29, 205–223 (DOI 10.17822/omad.1458244); and Kaan Ramazan Açıkgöz, "Guremâya kalan borca batık terekelerin sosyoekonomik açıdan dönemsel analizi: Kastamonu örneğinde (1753–1889)", *Sinop Üniversitesi Sosyal Bilimler Dergisi* 8/2, 702–731 (DOI 10.30561/sinopusd.1513947).
- **A finding worth the census's attention**: the Bursa study reports that debts went unpaid **irrespective of the size of the estate**, and that credit relations ran across social classes and confessional communities.

⚠️ **What is not established: how many.** Neither abstract gives the number of insolvent estates against the number of inventories, so **the failure rate — the thing this source could in principle supply — is unknown.** That is the first question to put to the two articles.

## The shape of the Ottoman failure record

**Three regimes, not one**, and they answer different questions:

1. **Death with debts** — *guremâ*, here, in the *şer'iyye sicilleri*, from at least the seventeenth century.
2. **Confiscation** — [[Muhallefat and musadere - confiscation as an archive]], for the elite, to 1839.
3. **Commercial bankruptcy** — after the commercial code of 1850 and the *ticaret mahkemeleri*, the Ottoman state acquired a bankruptcy procedure for merchants proper. ⚠️ **Its records were not located in this pass**; provincial commercial-court studies exist (Beirut, İzmir, Salonica, Ankara) and are the way in.

**Only the third is a boedelkamer in the strict sense — a procedure for the failure of a going concern rather than for the estate of a dead man.** The first two are what the empire had before it, and they are far better served.
