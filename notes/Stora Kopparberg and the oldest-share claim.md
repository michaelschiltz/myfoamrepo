# Stora Kopparberg and the oldest-share claim

**The claim is that a deed of 1288 makes Stora Kopparberg the world's oldest joint-stock company. The document will not carry that, and the census should not code it on that basis.**

## What the document actually is

Bishop **Petrus Elofsson** of Västerås transferred **one-eighth of the copper mountain then called Tiskasjöberg** to his nephew **Nicolaus Christineson**, **in exchange for a loan** Nicolaus had made when Peter took up the see. It was sealed by King Magnus Birgersson and eight bishops. The original is at Riksarkivet, Stockholm, and it is a ***bytesbrev*** — an exchange deed.

**So the transaction is a debt being settled with a fractional interest in a mine.** That is the whole of it.

## Four things it does not establish

**It does not mention a share.** The deed records a fraction of a thing, not a unit of participation in an enterprise. The legal-historical objection is exactly this: *how the division into eighths came about is not stated, and nothing in the document speaks of shares.*

**It does not establish transferability as a property of the interest.** One conveyance, between an uncle and a nephew, in settlement of a debt, is a transfer — not evidence that transfer was general, free of consent, or customary.

**It does not establish who held it.** Under canon law the question of whether this is the bishop's property or the chapter's is live, and it is not settled by the deed. **An institution whose shareholder may be a corporation sole is not straightforwardly a shareholding enterprise.**

**It does not establish a going concern with governance.** The Falun heritage account asserts that quarter-parts (*fjärdeparter*) were bought and sold and that holders met at a *bergsting*. ⚠️ **That is a local heritage narrative, not a scholarly finding**, and it asserts a practice as already existing without showing it. It may well be right; it is not evidence yet.

## Why this matters beyond one case

**The oldest-share claim is a first-instance claim, and first-instance claims are where this project is most exposed.** [[Whiggish history of finance names the ascent narrative]] describes the failure mode; [[Guard against reverse-teleology]] is the discipline. **Reading a thirteenth-century fractional conveyance as an early share is reading the endpoint backwards into the document**, and doing it to support a claim about origins is the exact error the project exists to refuse.

**The Bazacle comparison makes the point sharper, not weaker.** [[Le Bris Goetzmann Pouget 2023 on convergent evolution toward the joint-stock company]] rests on a *series* — six centuries of share prices and dividends — not on a founding document. ⚠️ **No comparable series has been located for Stora**, and its absence is the difference between the two cases. A founding charter supports a claim about a moment; a price series supports a claim about a form's operation.

## What Stora is good for

**Continuity, not origin.** The enterprise ran from the thirteenth century into a modern listed corporation, which is a Lindy observation of the kind [[The naties are a six-century Lindy institution]] makes — and it is subject to the same caution as [[The isqa is Lindy because captive not because robust]]: **continuity of name is not continuity of form.**

If Stora is coded, it should be coded for what can be evidenced — and on present knowledge the better-evidenced instance of the *form* is German. See [[The Kux carries transferability with unlimited liability]].
