# The Consolat de Mar comanda chapters

Source note for the normative layer of the Catalan maritime comanda. The *Llibre del Consolat de Mar* carries a run of chapters on the comanda that state, in the tradition's own words, who bears the capital loss, when the commendatary is answerable, how the comanda is kept apart from his own goods, and how his account is believed. It is the only Crown of Aragon text that regulates the mercantile comanda at all: the *Furs de València* (1240) and the *Costums de Tortosa* (1272) regulate the comanda as Roman deposit (García Sanz, *Ausa* 1959, 168–169; see [[Garcia Sanz on the Vic commendas of the thirteenth century]]).

## The chapters, in Capmany's numbering

Cited from Capmany/Font Rius 1965 (Zotero XWHD38V2, the file "…mar 1.pdf"; printed page = PDF page − 66, verified at PDF 247, 309, 321, 335, 338). Pardessus numbers each chapter one higher, in brackets; Martínez Gijón 1974 cites Molina's 1914 edition, which follows Pardessus (his cap. 215 is Capmany 214).

- **209, *De comanda a viatge cert*** (254–255). A comanda for a fixed voyage or place, lost entirely without the commendatary's fault: he owes nothing. Carried to another voyage or place and lost: he answers for all. Profit made on an unagreed route goes wholly to the investor, beyond the share agreed.
- **210, *De empediment a comanda*** (255–). Loss by reprisal, seizure by lords or enemy ships at the agreed place: no liability. A commendatary who knew of the danger and entered anyway answers for all.
- **211, *Declaració del precedent capítol*** (257; Pardessus [212]). The ship's patron who carries comandas is in no worse condition than another commendatary, because commendataries "go about the world" with nothing of their own in what they carry, and if the comandas are lost "they lose nothing, since it costs them nothing of their own". This is the tradition's own statement of the capital-labour asymmetry and of the commendatary's exemption from capital loss.
- **213, *Ítem, de comanda*** (262; Pardessus [214]). A comanda the commendatary may carry wherever he goes: if lost, it is lost to the investor, unless he gambled, spent or lost it by his fault, or passed it to another.
- **215, *Ítem, de comanda*** (263; Pardessus [216]). A commendatary who invests the comanda together with his own money shares gain and loss "per sou e per liura"; one who invests only his own money owes the investor a pro-rata share of the gain.
- **216, *De comanda en diners*** (264–; Pardessus [217]). Instructions bind: he must take witnesses if he cannot find the goods he was told to buy; deviation puts the loss on him and the gain on the investor; loss by mischance without fault is the investor's.
- **219, *De comanda que algú pendrá en lo comú, o sparsa*** (269–; Pardessus [220]). A comanda taken separately ("sparsa") by someone who also carries goods of a company is accounted separately and not mixed into the common stock unless the contract says so; commingling without consent lets the investor claim the highest price obtained.
- **220, *De comanda qui-s perdrá e lo comandatari se abatrá*** (272–; Pardessus [221]). Lost for any cause not excused above, the commendatary returns comanda and gain "unless he can show just reasons"; if he can neither prove nor pay, he is imprisoned until he settles.
- **254, *De comanda feta a ús de mar*** (274–). A comanda received with no terms beyond being taken "a ús e costum de mar e a risch de mar e de males gents": he must sell where he makes port; if no reward was agreed he may retain nothing, and the investor rewards him "segons que guanyará e segons lo maltret", at the investor's discretion.
- **279, *Com comandatari deu ésser cregut per son sagrament*** (279–; Pardessus [280]). The commendatary's account of gain or loss is received; on suspicion he may be put on oath, and then he is believed unless the investor proves the contrary.
- **88** (181; quoted by Martínez Gijón 1974, 267–268): a ship's patron who receives a merchant's goods in comanda carries them "saving mischance", because "no one receives a comanda to his own loss" ("negú no reb comanda a son dan").

## What the chapters do not say

They say nothing about the investor's liability to the commendatary's creditors, nothing about juridical personality, and nothing about a pool across investors. They regulate the comanda *ad usum maris* only.

## Cautions

Fynn-Paul (2017, 90 n. 17) warns that the compilation is late (fifteenth-century as printed, incorporating older law) and Barcelona-specific; [[Fynn-Paul 2017 on the land commenda of Manresa]]. The chapters fix defaults; notarial acts could and did contract around them.
