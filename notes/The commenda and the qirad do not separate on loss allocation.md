# The commenda and the qirad do not separate on loss allocation

Coded blind against the `loss_mitigation_forms` characteristics, the *commenda* and the *qirāḍ* return **identical values in every cell**: peril to the capital provider, market loss to the capital provider, no pledge, obligation discharged on total loss, no peril priced ex ante, participation voluntary, no fund. Nine characteristics, no contrast.

This is not an artefact of coarse coding. It is what three independent literatures say when they are read for this facet alone. Harris (2007) treats the two as one institution under two names and his migration argument requires it. Çizakça (2014) writes that "Islamic *mudaraba* was called *commenda* by the Europeans". Ackerman-Lieberman (2011, 663) glosses "the *commenda* (*qirāḍ* or *muḍāraba* in Arabic)" inside a single parenthesis while arguing against Udovitch about everything else.

## What this does to the triad

[[Isqa qirad and commenda are a liability-variable triad]] holds route, cargo and information environment fixed and varies agent liability. The design survives — but the variation runs **between the Jewish form and the other two**, not among three. The *ʿisqa* separates on substantive values (loss shared, obligation not discharged: [[The isqa refuses the agent's shield]]); the *commenda* and the *qirāḍ* do not separate from each other at all.

So the triad is 2 + 1. Written as three cases it would report the number of names in use, not the number of liability regimes.

## Where the difference actually lives

Not nowhere — but not on this axis. In `organizational_forms` the two still coincide on `AP3`, `LR1`, `LR6` and `CF2`. What differs between them is doctrinal apparatus rather than loss incidence: the *ḍamān*/*amāna* structure has no Latin counterpart, and the Latin form carries an asymmetric entity shielding the fiqh does not theorise.

**And one thing that is neither doctrine nor loss incidence: how the payout ratio is formed.** In the *qirāḍ* "any proportional division of net proceeds agreed to by the partners was deemed acceptable", the manuscripts contemplating splits from half-and-half to 1/20–19/20, so "the cost of capital among eastern Mediterranean long-distance participants was a function of market conditions and, of course, of one's position in the traders' network". In Genoa the ratio was customary and invariant — 4,860 ties across dozens of destinations, "only a handful" departing from it — until the last quarter of the thirteenth century (van Doosselaere 2009, 66–68, reading Udovitch 1970, 190–6).

Negotiated against customary is a real structural contrast and it is not a loss-allocation one. Van Doosselaere draws a strong consequence from it that is worth testing independently: a fixed split lowered the cost of entering a partnership with a stranger, since there was nothing to bargain about, while a negotiable one "favored the formation of clusters, as partners were more likely to build on previous experience and to do away with the bargaining". If that holds, the same institution is network-opening in one tradition and network-closing in the other, on a variable invisible to any loss characteristic.

## Where this is soft

The identity is a **doctrinal** identity. Ackerman-Lieberman (2011, 664) reports that Geniza legal documents for investment partnerships "allocate both partners a share of both profits and losses, with very few documents indemnifying the active partner against losses". If those instruments are *qirāḍ* in practice rather than *ʿisqa* in Jewish dress, then the practised *qirāḍ* shares loss and the identity coded here is an identity between two law books, not two instruments. The dispute is Udovitch and Goitein against Ackerman-Lieberman and it is not settled here. The unit has to be the clause structure, never the name on it — [[Natie is a false cognate]], [[Never let the units slide from instruments to populations]].
