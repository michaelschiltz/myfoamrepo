# The isqa is Lindy because captive not because robust

The *ʿisqa* is in continuous documented use from the amoraic period to Israeli banking practice today — some seventeen or eighteen centuries, dwarfing [[The naties are a six-century Lindy institution]]. It is the longest-lived instrument the project is likely to touch, and it is a trap.

Survival here is not evidence of fitness. The form persisted because it was **mandatory for a population with no permitted alternative**. Lindy is a survivorship inference and inherits the selection problem in full — [[Refusals are observations of the filter not inferences from survivors]], [[Time-average optimization is a survivorship property not an intention]].

Split the tag before a referee splits it for you:

- **Lindy-because-tested** — the form survived competition against permitted alternatives. Licenses the inference to robustness.
- **Lindy-because-captive** — the form survived because an exogenous rule constrained the choice set. Licenses nothing about the form, and quite a lot about the rule.

The *ʿisqa* is the second, and the [[The heter iska restores the fixed return by evidentiary barrier|heter ʿisqa]] is the direct evidence. The moment the form's cost bit hard enough, the community did not abandon it — it re-engineered the instrument into a fixed-return coupon while keeping the name and the clause structure. **What persisted was the label, not the function.** A pure duration count would score that as eighteen centuries of institutional success; it is closer to eighteen centuries of one institution wearing another's paperwork.

The general rule this yields is worth more than the case: **duration is only informative jointly with the choice set that was available at each moment.** Any Lindy claim in the project needs the counterfactual menu specified, or it is measuring the constraint and reporting it as a property of the form. That applies to the *naties* note as much as this one, and to the *kabu-nakama* material, where the choice set was licensed from above.
