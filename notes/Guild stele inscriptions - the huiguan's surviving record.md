# Guild stele inscriptions — the *huiguan*'s surviving record

**Much of what survives from Ming and Qing native-place associations (会馆) and trade associations (公所) is carved in stone.** Associations recorded their founding, their donors, their rules, their property and their charitable works on steles in their halls, and **the steles — or rubbings of them — outlived the halls and their papers**. The inscriptions are at layer 3 in print in regional compilations, among them:

- 《明清苏州工商业碑刻集》 — Suzhou (1981)
- 《上海碑刻资料选辑》 — Shanghai (1980)
- 《明清以来北京工商会馆碑刻选编》 — Beijing

⚠️ Editors, years and inscription counts not verified in this pass; the titles are standard and the details should be checked before citation.

## Why this explains a census failure

**`huiguan` is uncoded because "no source here shows" a fund** — its own `key_source`. **The record type is part of the reason.** A stele records **that** the association existed, **who gave**, **what it owned** and **what rules it declared** — founding, donation, prohibition, commemoration. **It does not record a contribution schedule, a claim, a payout or a loss.** The census's `CN`, `PY` and `LS` characteristics ask about exactly what stone does not carry. **Pattern 4 again: the record type sets what can be coded.**

**The operating record — account books, burial registers, correspondence — survives where an association's papers survived**, and the survey found that more often **overseas**: see [[Tung Wah Coffin Home Archives - Hong Kong]] and [[Leiden University Library - the Kong Koan archive of Batavia]].
