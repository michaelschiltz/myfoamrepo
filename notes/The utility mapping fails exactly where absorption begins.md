# The utility mapping fails exactly where absorption begins

The correspondence between growth optimality and expected utility holds only where the transformed process is Brownian motion on the whole real line. Once there is an absorbing boundary, the time-average ordering cannot be represented by any fixed utility of wealth.

**The argument.** Vanhoyweghen, Verbeken and Ginis show that under absorption the ordering runs on growth weighted by survival, then on expected absorption time, then on finite-horizon hitting probability, and that any utility reproducing it must be antisymmetric about the *initial* state and curved according to which boundary lies on which side (pp. 9–10). That representation moves with the starting position, with the location of the boundary and, in two of their regimes, with the horizon. A Bernoulli agent carries one function of terminal wealth through every decision. No single function does what the ordering requires.

**So the Bernoulli identity is confined to boundary-free dynamics**, and the place where it ends is the place where the project's material lives: bankruptcy, a lost ship, a lineage that dies out, an estate partitioned at inheritance. The inference is not drawn in either paper. Peters and Adamou's correspondence fails at the boundary; Vanhoyweghen's framework begins there; the two papers sit in the same issue without citing each other.

## Why this is the answer to the relabelling charge

[[Every utility function is growth-optimal under some dynamic]] leaves the framework's content in the observability of dynamics. This note adds a second, stronger answer: there are orderings the time-average criterion produces that **no expected-utility agent with stable preferences can produce at all**. A prediction indexed to distance from ruin, and reversing when the boundary moves without any change in wealth, distinguishes the frameworks; a prediction indexed to the curvature of a fixed function does not. Part 1 §1 now carries the sentence, citing Vanhoyweghen, Verbeken and Ginis 2026.

## Caution

The ordering itself contains an ensemble step — [[Conditioning on survival is an ensemble operation]]. The claim made here concerns representability, which survives that caution; it does not endorse the $g_\theta$ formula.
