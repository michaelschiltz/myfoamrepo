# *Juridictions consulaires* — the merchant courts

⚠️ **A series note recording an expectation, not a holding.** Nothing below has been checked against a finding aid.

France established **merchant courts staffed by merchants** — the *juridictions consulaires*, from the Paris foundation of 1563 — in the commercial towns. Their records are in série `B` of the departmental archives alongside the *amirautés*, and for Paris with the Archives de Paris ⚠️ *see the loss recorded at* [[Archives de Paris and the fire of 1871]].

## Why they belong in the register

Because they are the French member of a family the survey has now met four times: the Castilian *consulado* ([[Consulado de Burgos - the universidad de mercaderes]], [[Archivo Foral de Bizkaia - the Consulado de Bilbao]]), the Florentine *Mercanzia*, the Lucchese *Corte dei Mercanti*, the Bilbao ordinances.

**Merchants judging merchants by merchant custom is an institutional form in its own right**, and it is not in `organizational_forms`. A court whose judges are the parties' peers, which applies usage rather than learned law, and which was constituted by royal grant, has an entity structure the census could describe — and does not.

⚠️ Whether that is worth a coded row is a question for a selection brief, not for this note. Recorded here because four countries have now produced the same institution and the census has no row for it.
