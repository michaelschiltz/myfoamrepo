# The fraterna and compagnia diverge in formation but converge at the barrier

The two foundational Italian partnership forms differ sharply in *how they come into being* and converge exactly where it matters for survival: neither partitions ruin. Both carry unlimited liability that fuses the members' personal patrimonies to the firm's obligations into a single shared [[Absorbing barrier|absorbing barrier]]. The interesting contrast is therefore not in the exposure they impose but in the kinship-and-contract logic by which they assemble it — a case of [[Entitlement by liability versus entitlement by membership]].

## Formation: default versus contract

The **fraterna** (Venetian type) is involuntary. It arose automatically when brothers (*fratelli*) declined to divide the paternal inheritance; the undivided household patrimony simply *continued* as a trading unit. Membership was co-heirship — an entitlement by kinship-membership, not a subscribed act. There was no firm capital distinct from household wealth: firm and estate were one, and dissolution meant partition, not the expiry of a term.

The **compagnia** (Tuscan/Florentine type, from *cum pane*, "sharing bread") is deliberate and contractual. A term-limited, renewable instrument with articles and formal bookkeeping, it began among family but admitted outside partners and outside money. The separation of the **corpo** (the partners' committed capital) from the **sopracorpo** (additional deposits and loans, including from non-partners) let it draw external funding while keeping control among the principals. Entry here is entitlement by liability — subscribing to the contract and its exposure. In the mature *super-companies* (Bardi, Peruzzi, Acciaiuoli) this became branch networks and salaried agents.

## Convergence: the unpartitioned barrier

Neither form separated firm assets from the personal creditors of the members, and neither capped a member's downside. Under joint (compagnia: *in solidum*) liability the partners' whole estates and the firm's debts formed **one absorbing barrier**: a loss large enough anywhere propagated multiplicatively across every patrimony with nothing to arrest the cascade. This is precisely the vulnerability that later [[Entity-shielding]] (protecting firm assets from members' personal creditors) and owner shielding ([[limited-liability|limited liability]]) were built to compartmentalize — converting one system-wide barrier into isolated cells so failure in one need not be terminal for the whole. The compagnia, by isolating a *corpo* and admitting outside capital, at least gestures toward the accounting separateness on which entity shielding would later be built; the fraterna, with no firm/household boundary at all, sits one step further from shielding.

The Bardi and Peruzzi collapses of the 1340s are the canonical demonstration: sophisticated, branched compagnie with no ruin-partitioning, wiped out through unlimited joint liability by a single sovereign default (Edward III). Scale amplified rather than dampened the fragility — size was leverage on the shared barrier, not insurance against it. The pattern is the partnership-form analogue of [[The Fugger diversified activity while concentrating the barrier]]: variety of operations does not decorrelate the ruin-bearing exposure.
