# *Taiwan Shihō* — the colonial survey of Chinese associations and shares

臺灣私法. The report of the **Provisional Council for the Investigation of Old Customs in Taiwan** (臨時臺灣舊慣調查會), the Japanese colonial government's survey of Qing customary law in Taiwan, published in Japanese in **1910–1911**, with appended volumes of documents. ⚠️ Volume count and dates from general knowledge; a later Chinese translation exists and its edition was not checked.

## Why a printed report has a note

**Because it is the one place found in this pass where Chinese share-holding associations were described systematically, by an administration that needed to decide whether they were legal persons.** It defines the **deity association (神明會)** as a body formed by people of the same native place, trade, scholarly circle or kin to worship a deity, **"sometimes also serving the members' individual interests"**, and treats it beside the **ancestral trust (祭祀公業)**. **Members held shares (會份), and shares were inherited** — on a later sociological reading, by the eldest son, and not wholly excluding daughters, unlike the ancestral trust's male line. The colonial state recognised both as legal entities and later withdrew that recognition. ⚠️ All of this read through a 2003–2004 National Science Council project report that quotes the survey, not from the survey.

**And the institutions did not end.** Deity associations and ancestral trusts are **still registered in Taiwan today**, with model bylaws issued by municipal civil affairs bureaux; the report found 181 registered deity associations in the counties it could reach, and **81 bodies carrying the ancestral-trust name as a shell** for other purposes. ⚠️ The governing statute (a 2007 act on ancestral trusts) is from general knowledge.

## What it bears on

- **`shenhui_gu` and `shenhui_gu_alloc`** — coded from **one Shanxi contract via one compilation via one article** (see [[Zhang Chuanxi 1995 - the contract compilation behind the census's Chinese rows]]). **The 神明會 is the same institution under a near-identical name, in another province, described by a third party with legal reasons to be precise about shares, inheritance and property.** It is **the obvious second instance** — and, on the dependence rule, **an independent one**: a different record type (a legal survey, not a contract) and a different custody.
- **The layer question.** Layer 3 in print, in Japanese, **which the PI reads**. Its appended documents are transcriptions of Qing Taiwanese contracts. **A colonial survey is a selection by the colonial legal agenda**, and it described customs to be governed — the same caution as a regulator's record at [[Kyu-bakufu hikitsugisho - the Edo town magistrate records]].
- **A companion source to find, not yet placed**: the Japanese surveys of North China village custom of the 1940s (中国農村慣行調査, printed after the war). ⚠️ Named from general knowledge; not searched.
