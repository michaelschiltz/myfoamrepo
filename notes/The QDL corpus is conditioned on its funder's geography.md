# The QDL corpus is conditioned on its funder's geography

The Qatar Digital Library is the best free access anyone has to the India Office Records, and it is a **sample drawn on one criterion**: relevance to the Gulf and the Arabian Peninsula, because Qatar Foundation paid for it. The selection is honest and openly stated. It is nonetheless a selection, and it does not respect series boundaries.

## Why the distinction is load-bearing

A digitised *series* — IOR/B complete, IOR/L/AG complete — supports frequency claims within itself, because the denominator is known. A digitised *cross-cut* does not. Counting mentions of anything in QDL yields a number whose denominator is "material a Gulf-focused selector judged relevant", and no amount of statistical care recovers what was left behind.

The structure is familiar: the sample is conditioned on a criterion correlated with the thing being measured. [[Persistence samples are conditioned on non-absorption]] states it for survival; here the conditioning variable is a funder's regional interest, and the correlation with commercial and maritime subject matter is obviously non-zero. Treat any QDL-derived frequency as **descriptive of QDL**, never of the IOR.

## The use it is good for

Depth on the Gulf trade, the maritime and residency records, and — for this project — the Company operating as a governmental and diplomatic actor rather than a trading one. Where the question is *what did the Company do in the Gulf*, QDL is close to a complete answer. Where the question is *how did the Company constitute itself*, it is a convenience sample of a constitutional record that lives elsewhere and behind a paywall.
