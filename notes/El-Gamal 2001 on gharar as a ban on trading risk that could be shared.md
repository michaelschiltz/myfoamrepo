# El-Gamal 2001 on gharar as a ban on trading risk that could be shared

Mahmoud A. El-Gamal, "An Economic Explication of the Prohibition of Gharar in Classical Islamic Jurisprudence," *Islamic Economic Studies* 8:2 (2001), 29–58 [volume and issue unverified]. Companion: "An Economic Explication of the Prohibition of Ribā in Classical Islamic Jurisprudence," Rice University working paper (2001). Synthesis in *Islamic Finance: Law, Economics, and Practice* (Cambridge, 2006).

**The nearest neighbour to the project's reading of Islamic contract doctrine, and like the rest of the literature it is an efficiency argument.**

**The argument** [verify the mechanism against the text]. *Gharar* is not a ban on uncertainty as such. It bans the **trading** of risk — bilateral transfer for a price — where a risk-sharing arrangement could carry the same exposure, because the risk-trading allocation is inferior to the risk-sharing one. The classical exceptions (*salam*, *istisnāʿ*) mark where need outweighs the prohibition. Al-Suwailem 2000 (*Islamic Economic Studies*, game-theoretic) attempts an objective measure; Berg and Kim 2014 (*Journal of Economic Behavior and Organization*, DOI 10.1016/j.jebo.2014.02.016) offer a rival signalling-and-screening explanation.

**How it sits beside the vault's reading.** [[Gharar excludes designed-in unverifiability]] gives *gharar* a verification function; El-Gamal gives it a risk-allocation function. The two are not rivals. A contract whose payoff turns on an unverifiable state is usually also one that prices a risk it could have shared, and both readings predict the refusal of the sea loan.

**Where the project departs.** El-Gamal's "inferior" is judged in expected utility, across the ensemble. The time-average version is stronger and needs no preferences: a fair transfer of risk lowers both parties' growth rates ([[A fair wager lowers both trajectories which maysir forbids and skin in the game permits]]). That gives an argument from dynamics for the same doctrinal line. Cite El-Gamal as the efficiency precedent the dynamic argument sharpens, not as support for it.
