# Archivio di Stato di Pisa

<https://aspisa.cultura.gov.it/>

**The sea-loan archive.** Pisa's *Constitutum Usus* (twelfth century) is one of the earliest statutory treatments of maritime lending in Latin Christendom, and the project's argument about the sea loan — [[The sea loan is a contingent claim not a loan]], [[Naviganti - Latin Christendom rejected the sea loan too]], [[The double refusal of the sea loan is evidence about the filter not about independence]] — turns on exactly the material this archive holds.

## The fondi

**`Comune di Pisa`, Divisione A (11th century – 1406)** — the commune's own record, with a published inventory. The maritime magistracy, the *Ordo maris*, belongs to this world. ⚠️ Whether its records survive as a distinct series is unestablished and is the first thing to check.

**`Archivio notarile distrettuale di Pisa`** — described in SIAS. The instruments.

**`Diplomatico`** — the parchments.

## Why the early date carries the weight

Same reasoning as at [[Archivio di Stato di Genova]]: the transmission-versus-recurrence test is decided by the earliest instances, not the typical ones. Pisa and Genoa are the two twelfth-century Tyrrhenian witnesses, and **they are rivals rather than a single tradition** — which makes divergence between them more informative than agreement. [[Divergent arbitrary particulars are the independence diagnostic]] is the rule; two neighbouring maritime republics with separate statutory traditions is close to a designed experiment.
