# Source editions — Cairo Geniza

The printed and online editions that bring Geniza documents together in transcription, translation or calendar form. **The image and database layers are already recorded** — [[Princeton Geniza Project - the documentary database]] and the rest of [[MOC - Geniza archives]]; this note is the book layer beneath them. Where a volume is cited in the PGP's "Scholarship" tab, its editions of individual documents can often be read there.

## Merchant letters and deeds — the core

- **Moshe Gil, *Be-malkhut Yishmaʿel bi-tequfat ha-geʾonim* (*In the Kingdom of Ishmael*)**, 4 vols (Tel Aviv: Tel Aviv University, Diaspora Research Institute publ. 117–120, 1997). **846 documents** — most of them eleventh-century merchant letters of the Ibn ʿAwkal, Tustari and Nahray b. Nissim circles, with contracts and court decisions — **transcribed and translated into Hebrew**; vol. 1 is a study (English tr. *Jews in Islamic Countries in the Middle Ages*, Brill 2004, **study only**). Not digitised. **The largest edition of Geniza commercial correspondence, and the one the census's `isqa`/`qirad` evidence most needs.** ⚠️ Split of documents across vols 2–4 unverified.
- **Moshe Gil, *Palestine during the First Muslim Period (634–1099)***, 3 vols, Hebrew (Tel Aviv University & Ministry of Defence, 1983; DRI publ. 56–58); vols II–III are numbered Geniza documents with Hebrew translation. English tr. (CUP 1992) is of the study volume only.
- **Goitein & Friedman, *Sefer Hodu* ("India Book")**, Hebrew, Jerusalem: Ben-Zvi Institute (Oriens Judaicus A): I *Joseph Lebdi* (2009); II *Maḍmūn Nagid of Yemen* (2010, c. 735 pp.); III *Abraham Ben Yijū* (2010); IV/A–B *Ḥalfon the Traveling Merchant-Scholar*, with Amir Ashur (2013). **Volumes V–VII (209 documents) are being edited by the Princeton Geniza Lab for open-access print and the PGP**, with new finds to 668 documents in all and English translations of vol. IV.
- **Goitein & Friedman, *India Traders of the Middle Ages: Documents from the Cairo Geniza ("India Book")*** (Leiden: Brill, 2008; Études sur le judaïsme médiéval 31), xxix + 918 pp. English translation of India Book I–III. Fulcrum (ACLS HEB), subscription.
- **Goitein, *Letters of Medieval Jewish Traders*** (Princeton UP, 1973; Legacy reissue 2016). 80 letters in English translation, no originals.
- **Stillman, "The Eleventh-Century Merchant House of Ibn ʿAwkal"**, *JESHO* 16 (1973), 15–88 — the earliest merchant-house archive (61 documents). ⚠️ How many are edited in the article unverified.

## Legal deeds, courts, marriage

- **Gershon Weiss, "Legal Documents Written by the Court Clerk Halfon ben Manasse (dated 1100–1138)"**, PhD, University of Pennsylvania, 1970, 2 parts. **Note: Gershon, not Gideon.** A single clerk's run of Fustat deeds — partnerships, releases, debts. ProQuest (paid); PGP cites it document by document.
- **Phillip I. Ackerman-Lieberman, *The Business of Identity: Jews, Muslims, and Economic Life in Medieval Egypt*** (Stanford UP, 2014), **with an appendix of fifteen legal documents concerning partnership.** The closest printed match to the census's partnership coding. ⚠️ His 2007 Princeton dissertation not checked.
- **Geoffrey Khan, *Arabic Legal and Administrative Documents in the Cambridge Genizah Collections*** (CUP, 1993; CUL Genizah Series 10), xviii + 567 pp. **Arabic-script** sales, leases, **endowments**, debt acknowledgements, powers of attorney, petitions — the Islamic-court side of the deposit, with translation.
- **M. A. Friedman, *Jewish Marriage in Palestine*, 2 vols** (Tel Aviv & JTS, 1980–81) — ketubbot with dowry valuations; and his *Jewish Polygyny in the Middle Ages* (Hebrew, 1986).
- **Judith Olszowy-Schlanger, *Karaite Marriage Documents from the Cairo Geniza*** (Brill, 1998) — with trousseau values.
- **Moshe Gil, *Documents of the Jewish Pious Foundations*** (Brill, 1976) — **already held**; the *heqdesh* candidate rests on it.
- **Mark R. Cohen, *The Voice of the Poor in the Middle Ages*** (Princeton, 2005) — 90+ translated alms lists, donor lists and petitions; pairs with Gil 1976.

## Finding aids

- **Shaul Shaked, *A Tentative Bibliography of Geniza Documents*** (Paris & The Hague: Mouton, 1964) — the only route to pre-1964 editions scattered in journals.
- **Cambridge University Library Genizah Series** — catalogues and hand-lists; vol. 14 (Shivtiel & Niessen, T-S New Series Arabic/Judaeo-Arabic) is the one for documentary fragments.

## Corrections to what the project had recorded

- **Goldberg 2012 has no documentary appendices**; her corpus is c. 900 documents (990–1080), 697 in the commercial-letter core — a study, not an edition.
- **The PGP holds 6,716 transcriptions** (about 2,200 of them Goitein's unpublished editions) across its 35,855 catalogued documents, CC-BY.
