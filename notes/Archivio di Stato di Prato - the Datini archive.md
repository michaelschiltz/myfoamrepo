# Archivio di Stato di Prato — the Datini archive

<http://datini.archiviodistato.prato.it/en/> · archive: <https://archiviodistatoprato.cultura.gov.it/>

**The best-documented single merchant enterprise of the European middle ages**, and the reason it belongs in this hub is narrower than its fame: it is one of very few places where a *company* can be observed as a running system rather than reconstructed from its constitutive documents.

## What survives

Francesco di Marco Datini's business papers, kept nearly entire: **letters** in the tens of thousands, **books of account**, insurance policies, bills of exchange, partnership agreements. Catalogued to item level on the *Fondo Datini* site — layer 1 at unusually fine grain, with imaging ⚠️ of unverified extent.

## Why it is the right archive for a specific question

The coding scheme records what an institution's constitutive documents *say*. Datini lets a second question be asked: what the parties **did** across many transactions over decades. Where the two diverge, the divergence is the finding — and it bears directly on [[The coding commons must record stated rationale not only component presence]], because Datini is where stated rationale can be checked against practice at scale.

The insurance material in particular is the point at which the Tuscan trade had **priced** perils that other traditions were still **sharing**. That is the cut [[Pricing risk does not remove it]] and [[MOC - Risk-sharing vs risk-pricing]] are drawn on, and Datini is one of the few corpora dense enough to date the transition rather than assert it.

## The caution

A single firm's archive surviving nearly whole is a survivorship artefact of the first order. Whatever is inferred from Datini describes **Datini**, and the sample of one is conditioned on precisely the thing that makes it valuable — that the business succeeded and the papers were kept. [[Persistence samples are conditioned on non-absorption]] is the general statement; here it is a rule about not generalising from the best-documented firm to the population of firms.
