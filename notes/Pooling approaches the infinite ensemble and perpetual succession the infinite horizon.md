# Pooling approaches the infinite ensemble and perpetual succession the infinite horizon

**Hypothesis, to be tested, not asserted. Non-ergodicity is the failure of two limits to commute: $t \to \infty$ and $N \to \infty$. The project's two strands may be institutional approaches to the two limits. Pooling with a sharing rule approaches the ensemble: it *raises* the growth rate from $\mu - \sigma^2/2$ toward $\mu$. Perpetual succession approaches the horizon: it does not raise the rate, it lets the rate be *lived* — uninterrupted by death, partition and inheritance — by a person whose trajectory is long enough for the time average to govern it. If that is right, the cooperation strand and the corporate-decomposition strand share one axiom rather than two, and a differential prediction follows: perpetuity is survivable only where the barrier is refused or shielded.**

## The two limits do different work

- **$N \to \infty$ changes the rate.** A pool of $N$ decorrelated members re-sharing each period grows at $\mu - \sigma^2/2N$ ([[The expectation value describes a pool that re-shares not a population]]). Size and sharing buy back the Jensen gap.
- **$t \to \infty$ realises the rate.** A finite life truncates the trajectory, and mortality resets it: dissolution on a partner's death, partible inheritance, the *farāʾiḍ* fragmenting an estate are all re-starts that prevent compounding from running. A perpetual person converts the time-average growth rate from an asymptotic property of an imagined trajectory into the operative description of an actual one.

The two are not substitutes. Perpetuity without pooling is a single trajectory living the long run at the full drag of $\sigma^2/2$; pooling without perpetuity buys a higher rate that is cut short at every death. The combination — a perpetual, pooled person — is the case in which both infinities are approached at once.

## The barrier is the cost of perpetuity

A trajectory extended without end is exposed to the [[Absorbing barrier|absorbing barrier]] without end. Perpetuity therefore cannot be granted to a risk-taking vehicle unless something stands between it and ruin. The historical solutions sort cleanly on that condition, and the sorting is the test.

- **Perpetuity by refusing risk.** The *waqf* is perpetual and inalienable: it cannot be sold or pledged, so it cannot be lost. Islamic law grants perpetuity to the pious collective and refuses it to the private accumulation vehicle ([[The waqf and the nación flamenca diverge only at legal personality]], [[Kuran's brake is a refusal of immortal entities]]). On this reading that is not a brake but the consistent rule: the actual infinite in time is licensed only where the barrier has been removed by forbidding the operations that could reach it.
- **Perpetuity by household discipline and jurisdictional partition.** The Tokugawa *ie*, including the fictive household, persists irrespective of whose body occupies its name, and partitions assets across domain borders ([[The fictive household supplied partition and perpetuity without legal personality]]). House codes restraining speculation would be the risk-refusal side of the same settlement [verify against the Mitsui and Kōnoike house rules].
- **Perpetuity by shielding.** The business corporation keeps risk-taking and interposes the shield: limited liability moves the barrier off the members, transferable shares let members exit while the person continues, and the person itself can fail without taking its members with it ([[The ensemble average is the local descriptor of a barrier-shielded agent]], [[The real cut is shielded-and-pooled versus unshielded]]).

**Differential prediction.** Forms granted perpetuity should show either inalienability/risk-refusal or barrier-shielding. Perpetuity combined with unshielded risk-taking should be rare, short-lived, or quickly hedged by one of the two. Forms that pool without perpetuity — *qirāḍ*, *commenda*, *kō* — should renew on a short cycle, re-forming the pool rather than extending one person. Both are codable from the five-component matrix plus a risk-refusal flag [check against the HistorEE_codebooks schema].

## Why the corporation looks like an endpoint

The corporation is the form in which both limits are approached together, which is why the ascent narrative reads it as the summit ([[Whiggish history of finance names the ascent narrative]]). The two-limit reading removes the summit without removing the achievement: it is one combination among several, each approaching one infinity at a stated cost, and the forms that approach one limit only are solutions in their own right ([[The corporation is not the omega - the naties are an undisplaced niche]]).

## Weak points

- The perpetuity leg has no formal result of the kind Peters and Adamou supply for pooling. The claim that mortality-induced resets lower *realised* long-run growth needs its own model — splitting at death is a resetting process, and the literature on stochastic resetting may supply it [verify].
- The mapping risks reading legal perpetuity as economic horizon. A perpetual charter is not a long-lived enterprise; most chartered companies died young. The test must use realised duration of the person, not its charter terms.
- Aristotle's distinction — the ensemble needs an *actual* infinity of co-present copies, the trajectory only a *potential* one — is the philosophical gloss that prompted this note. It is illuminating and must not be leaned on: *Physics* III is not evidence about institutions.
