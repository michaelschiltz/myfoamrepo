# Lloyd's and the marine insurance records

**The Lloyd's Marine Collection** is at [[Guildhall Library and the City livery companies]]; the **Captains Register, 1869–1947**, at [[The London Archives]].

## What the collection holds

- ***Lloyd's List*, 1740 to the present** — the shipping newspaper: vessel movements, voyages, **casualty reports**. On microfilm, with annual alphabetical indices 1838–1926 and Voyage Record Cards from October 1927.
- **Loss records** — Missing Vessel Books 1873–1954, war loss records, marine loss records 1939–1970, with cargo and position.
- Weekly shipping indexes 1880–1917, voyage supplements, **Lloyd's Confidential Index 1886– (30-year embargo)**, Mercantile Navy Lists, Board of Trade inquiries and casualty returns.

⚠️ Some material requires 48 hours' notice.

## Why *Lloyd's List* from 1740 is the interesting object

**It is a serial record of maritime casualty published by the market that priced the risk.** For the project's central distinction — peril shared against peril priced — that is a source of an unusual kind: **the pricing institution's own running account of the losses it was pricing.**

Set beside the Amsterdam average adjudications at [[Averijgrossen - the general average adjudications]], which record how a realised loss was *allocated*, *Lloyd's List* records how losses were *observed and reported* to a market. **Allocation and observation are different acts and the census codes the first**, but the second is what makes ex-ante pricing possible at all, and [[Pricing risk does not remove it]] is about the moment the two come apart.

⚠️ **Microfilm is not a digital layer.** A run from 1740 on film, indexed annually from 1838, is layer 1 with a finding aid — usable, and not remotely. The early century, 1740–1838, has no annual index at all.
