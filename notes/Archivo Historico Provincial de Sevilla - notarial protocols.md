# Archivo Historico Provincial de Sevilla — notarial protocols

Catalogue via the Junta de Andalucia's archive system, **not** via [[PARES - the access layer for the Spanish state archives]].

**Where the instruments are.** The Archivo General de Indias holds the state's record of the Carrera; the notarial protocols hold the *contracts*. Marine insurance policies, *prestamos a riesgo de mar*, *companias*, freight charters and the partnership agreements behind individual voyages were drawn by Sevillian notaries and survive in their protocol books. For a project whose unit of analysis is the instrument rather than the institution, this is the more important of the two Sevillian repositories.

## Why it is the right archive for the coding

The characteristics in `loss_mitigation_forms` are drafted-clause characteristics. They can only be coded from documents that contain clauses. An *averia* account records that a pool existed; a notarial *poliza de seguro* records on what terms the peril was allocated, which is the question [[A route clause conditions the investor's peril in three traditions]] and [[Which peril gets a clause is set by the tradition's doctrinal problem]] are actually asking.

## Scale and finding aids

Sevilla's notarial archive is among the largest in Spain, running from the fifteenth century. The classic route in is the printed *Catalogo de los fondos americanos* and the *Indices de los Protocolos Notariales de Sevilla*, which index by notary and year rather than by instrument type. ⚠️ Volume, exact date range and the current custodial split between the Archivo Historico Provincial and the *Archivo de Protocolos Notariales* need confirming — the two names are used interchangeably in the literature and are not obviously the same body.

## Digitisation

⚠️ **Unverified and probably thin.** Assume layer 1 with printed indices, and budget for on-site photography. The permission regime for self-photography in Andalusian provincial archives is not documented here and should be established before any trip is costed.

## The finding-aid problem

Notarial protocols are indexed by notary, not by subject. There is no route from "find me the marine insurance policies" to a shelf mark except through the printed indices or through a secondary work that already cited one. This makes the archive expensive in exactly the way [[MOC - Acquisitions and the antiquarian trade]] describes: the scarce input is the finding aid, not the access.
