# Gallica and RetroNews — the French full-text layer

Gallica: <https://gallica.bnf.fr/> · RetroNews: <https://www.retronews.fr/>

**France's full-text layer is split in two, and the split is a licensing decision rather than a technical one.**

## Gallica

The BnF's digital library: books, manuscripts, maps, periodicals, at very large scale, free, with an IIIF API and a long-standing open-reuse posture. For this project its most useful holdings are **the printed legal and commercial literature** — the *ordonnances*, the treatises on insurance and average, the *inventaires sommaires* of the departmental archives themselves (several are on Gallica in full, which makes a printed finding aid searchable when the archive's own catalogue is not).

**That last point is worth acting on.** Where a departmental série `B` inventory was printed in the nineteenth century and is on Gallica, the finding aid is at layer 3 even though the records are at layer 1.

## RetroNews

The BnF's historical press platform — and it is **a paid subscription service over public-domain newspapers**. The criticism this attracted in France is on the record and is not this project's to adjudicate; the operational fact is what matters.

**Set the three national press corpora side by side:**

| Corpus | Scale | Terms |
|---|---|---|
| Delpher (NL) | 18M+ pages | Free; 1618–1879 texts downloadable as open data |
| BelgicaPress (BE) | 1.2M+ pages | Free |
| RetroNews (FR) | large ⚠️ | **Subscription** |

**Three neighbouring countries, three different answers, none of them about the press.** Any cross-national frequency claim drawn from newspapers would be measuring national library policy — the same prohibition already standing for the VOC and EIC in [[The VOC-EIC digital asymmetry is an access regime not a survival difference]], now with a third data point that makes the pattern undeniable.

⚠️ Establish whether institutional access through Hokkaido or the LML partners covers RetroNews before budgeting for it.
