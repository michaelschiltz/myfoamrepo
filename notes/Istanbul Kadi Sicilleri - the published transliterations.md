# *İstanbul Kadı Sicilleri* — the published transliterations

**The one Ottoman court corpus at layer 3, and it is a selection whose rule is not published.** Free online at <https://kadisicilleri.org/>.

## What it is

- **Forty volumes, presented 20 December 2012**, prepared by İSAM with the Istanbul 2010 European Capital of Culture agency and the Istanbul Metropolitan Municipality, under M. Akif Aydın and Coşkun Yılmaz.
- **Forty registers of the İstanbul, Galata, Eyüp and Üsküdar courts**, each transliterated into Latin-script Turkish beside a facsimile, Arabic entries translated.
- **About 24,000 pages and more than 40,000 entries**; the online database opened on 25 January 2014 with word search.
- **The series continued beyond forty.** Volume 57 is İstanbul Mahkemesi register 22 (1107–08/1695–97); volume 98 is register 211 (1278–79/1862–63). A second site, <https://kadisicilleri.istanbul/>, run by İBB Kültür A.Ş. with Medipol University, presents 60 volumes for 1557–1911. ⚠️ Total volumes published to date not established; the two sites' counts do not agree.

Contents, in the project's own list: guild regulations, price lists, **waqf deeds**, sales, donations, **loans**, partitions of inheritance, wills, marriages and divorces, imperial decrees.

## What layer 3 means here

**It removes the palaeography and leaves the language.** A Turkish reader with legal vocabulary can use it; nobody else can without translation. It is word-searchable, so a question like "every *avarız vakfı* entry in these registers" is a search, not a reading programme.

**It does not remove siyakat.** Where an entry is an account, the transliteration renders the figures; whether the reader can audit them against the facsimile is another matter.

## The selection, and why it matters to a row that already exists

**Forty registers from about 9,870 is 0.4 per cent; even a hundred volumes is about 1 per cent.** The project describes the registers as chosen "from different periods". **No selection rule is published**, and [[Handoff - the non-European archive survey]]'s third pattern — ask not only what survived but what the rule was and whether anyone wrote it down — applies to editions as much as to fires.

**And the census's `avariz_fund` row may already rest on it.** Kars 2021 works İstanbul Mahkemesi registers 3, 10, 12, 18, 20 and 22, spanning 1618–1697. **Register 3 (1027/1618), register 12 (1073–74/1663–64, volume 16), register 18 and register 22 (1107–08/1695–97, volume 57) are in the edition**, and the article's span is exactly register 3's first year to register 22's last. ⚠️ Registers 10 and 20 not checked; register 18 confirmed only from a bookseller's listing; whether Kars read the originals or the edition is not settled — his bibliography cites registers by number, which is also how the edition is cited.

**If the six are the edition's six, the Istanbul half of that row's evidence is the edition's sample**, and what looks like a court-register finding about seventeenth-century Istanbul is a finding about the registers a publication programme chose to print — begun on European Capital of Culture money and continued under sponsorship this pass did not establish ⚠️. (Register 12 is in the first forty; register 22, as volume 57, is not, so the Kars registers span both phases.) **That is the funding-map argument of [[MOC - European archives and digital collections]] arriving, for the first time in this survey, on a coded row.** It does not make the coding wrong. It makes the row's scope narrower than "Istanbul".
