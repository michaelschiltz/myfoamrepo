# Independence of apparatus is not independence of question

Harris 2000, *Industrializing English Law, 1720–1844*, was acquired to discharge a named
recruitment hazard. Both sources standing behind `deed_of_settlement_company` are sceptics of the
Hansmann–Kraakman–Squire framework in whose vocabulary the entity-shielding characteristics are
written, so the census had recruited its evidence from one side of a live dispute and needed a
witness from outside it.

**Harris is measurably outside it.** Across the full text: `hansmann` 0, `kraakman` 0, `squire` 0,
`asset partitioning` 0, `entity shielding` 0. On the frame test he is exactly the corrective that
was wanted.

**And he discharged nothing.** He never asks the creditor-ranking question. `AP1` on
`chartered_corporation_england` came back `.NR`, so the row can neither corroborate nor contradict
the `compagnia`'s `AP1=1`, and the recruitment hazard on `deed_of_settlement_company` stands
untouched. `creditor` returns 13 hits in 331 pages, seven of which are the *public* creditors of the
State.

## The claim

**A recruitment bias is discharged by a witness who asks the same question from a different frame.
A source that is merely differently framed adds a row and no evidence.** Independence of apparatus
is a necessary condition and does no work on its own, because a framework is a way of asking, and
two authors can decline the same question for opposite reasons.

This is why an acquisition list has to be built from the question rather than from the frame. The
revised list for this row makes the point in its own shape: the entry that would move `AP1` and
`AP2` is not a named work at all but a description — *anything asking the creditor-ranking question
of an English chartered corporation* — because **no work in Harris's own bibliography asks it**, and
so the acquisition has to be found by search rather than named from an apparatus.

## Why the failure is hard to see

The corrective that fails this way **looks like a success from every angle that gets checked**. It
produces a coded row, a green suite, a new form in the census and a legitimate entry in the
CHANGELOG. Nothing in the instrument reports that the hazard it was bought to discharge is still
open; only the cell values say so, and they say it in `.NR`, which reads as an absence rather than
as a failed test. The discharge has to be asserted separately and checked separately, which is why
the row now carries the correction and a forward pointer rather than a silent `.NR`.
