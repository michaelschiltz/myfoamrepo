# Institutions substitute state-verification for outcome-inference as ruin exposure rises

The formal/informal axis cannot carry this argument. Formal and informal enforcement interpenetrate everywhere — notarial practice is embedded in reputational communities, guild courts are both at once, the *kadı* court served merchants already bound by dense ties. Any prediction stated as "formality replaces reputation" will be refuted by the first case examined.

The axis that does carry it is **outcome-inference against state-verification**. Reputation, entrustment, guild sanction and ostracism are all outcome-inference mechanisms: they read conduct off realised results. Witnesses to lading, notarial registration, port certificates, collateral held outside the venture, guarantors whose liability does not turn on the venture's fate, the placement of kin, and the *amāna*/*ḍamān* shift from *did you lose?* to *did you exceed your instructions?* — these establish what happened independently of how it came out. Either kind can be sociologically formal or informal. That is why the old axis fails and this one does not.

## Three barriers, running in two directions

Absorbing barriers enter the enforcement problem at three points, and the earlier draft of this argument used one of them and missed the others.

**In the venture.** The ship sinks, the caravan is taken. Frequent, natural ruin collapses the informativeness of outcomes: see [[Non-ergodicity collapses the likelihood ratio on outcomes]]. This *degrades* outcome-inference.

**In the agent's own wealth.** A ruined agent has no future for the threatened loss of future dealing to bite on. The folk theorem's continuation value is an expectation over futures that a typical trajectory does not reach. This also *degrades* outcome-inference.

**In the sanction.** Expulsion from a closed trading community, where there is no outside option, is itself absorbing — and an absorbing loss is not a large cost in time-average terms but an unbounded one, dominating any finite one-off gain. This *strengthens* outcome-inference, and strengthens it more under the framework than under expected utility.

The framework therefore predicts no blanket shift. It predicts a **substitution governed by a ratio**: institutions shift weight from outcome-inference to state-verification as the venture's exogenous ruin hazard rises *relative to the absorbency of the community's sanction*. Where a community can make exclusion reliably terminal, outcome-inference survives and verification devices stay thin. Where it cannot, and ruin is frequent and natural, verification devices proliferate.

## What this buys and what it costs

It re-describes the Maghribi–Genoese contrast without invoking culture. The variable is not collectivism against individualism but the relative absorbency of communal sanction against venture hazard — a structural quantity, in principle measurable, and one that does not require attributing dispositions to populations.

It is testable in a form that isolates the framework: hold the sum at stake constant and vary ruin exposure and community closure. Ordinary transaction-cost reasoning predicts verification intensity tracking value at risk; this predicts it tracking the hazard-to-sanction ratio. Notarial series and court records carry both quantities.

The cost is that a conditional prediction is a weaker falsifier than an unconditional one, and it is heavier to state. It belongs as a work-package research question rather than as the headline falsifiable claim in a five-page synopsis.
