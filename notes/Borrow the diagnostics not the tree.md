# Borrow the diagnostics not the tree

Every concept the project has taken from phylogenetic systematics — clade, synapomorphy, symplesiomorphy, polarity, homoplasy, congruence — is defined *relative to a tree*. None of them means anything on its own. And the project cannot build a tree: the four preconditions fail, and they fail for documented reasons rather than for want of effort (no outgroup, characters that are not independent, reticulate descent, and the textual resurrection of dead forms — set out in [[Homoplasy is the finding not the noise]] and [[Deep-roots variables are symplesiomorphies]]).

The position this forces is not a retreat but a boundary, and it has to be stated once, explicitly, in the methodological section: **cladistics enters this project diagnostically and never constructively.** It supplies a vocabulary for naming failure modes and a discipline for coding. It does not supply a reconstruction method, and no cladogram will be produced.

## What the boundary licenses

The diagnostic use is the whole of the value already extracted, and it survives intact without a tree.

It names failure modes in other people's inference: shared ancestral characters that cannot discriminate within a sample, absent polarity, reversal counterfeiting the ancestral state. It disciplines the project's own coding, by separating convergence from parallelism, by insisting that arbitrary particulars carry the evidentiary weight that functional agreement cannot ([[Divergent arbitrary particulars are the independence diagnostic]]), and by requiring that independence be earned rather than assumed ([[Galton's problem]]). And it pre-empts objections from referees who know the machinery, which is worth more than it sounds, because those are the referees most likely to notice the borrowing in the first place.

Note in passing what the boundary makes harmless: **the project's comparative categories are polyphyletic by design.** "Risk-pooling institutions," "*commenda*-type instruments," "entity-shielded forms" group by function, not by ancestry. Under a tree-building programme that would be a defect to be corrected. Under a diagnostic programme it is the hypothesis under test — indeed if any of these categories turned out to be a clade, Objective 1 would have failed.

## What the boundary forbids

No cladogram, no parsimony search, no consistency or retention index reported as a finding, no phylogeny of the *commenda*, and above all no tree figure. A tree figure would be the single most damaging illustration the book could carry, because it asserts recovered descent — the exact claim the project spends its evidentiary effort refusing to assume.

The prohibition also governs corpus design, and this is the practical consequence. A corpus built to make a tree recoverable is coded differently from one built to make the diagnostics runnable: the first maximises characters with clean states and low homoplasy, the second preserves the messy free parameters and the non-checkmark states where the discriminating evidence actually lives. The Zwicky box specified in [[The morphological matrix is dictated by the shape of the feature space]] is the second kind, and it should stay that way. See also [[The coding commons must record stated rationale not only component presence]].

## Where the descent hypotheses come from, which is the crux

The distinction that makes this coherent is between a tree as **output** and a tree as **input**.

The project never recovers a descent structure from character analysis. It takes its descent hypotheses from outside — from philology and the documentary record. The western family rests on the Sasanian substrate argument ([[The qirad's antecedents are Sasanian not Sogdian]], [[The western family needs only plausible common descent not proven genealogy]]); the one documented transmission route is the *ortoq* ([[The ortoq is the discriminating instrument]]). Those are historical claims, evaluated by historians' methods, and they arrive at the character analysis already made.

What the cladistic apparatus then does is ask what follows *given* them: which agreements the hypothesised descent explains, which it does not, and therefore which residue is doing evidentiary work. That is a legitimate and modest operation, and it is emphatically not phylogenetic reconstruction. Stating the division of labour this way also answers the obvious question — *where is your tree?* — in one sentence: there is none, because the descent claims are philological rather than computational, and the apparatus is applied downstream of them.

## A modest gain against the Clark exposure

[[Never let the units slide from instruments to populations]] concludes that running a character matrix forecloses the strong objection to Clark, leaving only the argument that his execution is faulty. The boundary narrows that concession slightly and it is worth recording the difference.

What is conceded is that descent *questions* are legitimate in economic history. What is not conceded is that descent *structures* are recoverable from the kind of evidence either programme has. The project says so of its own material and declines to build the tree; Clark does not decline. That is not the domain attack and it should not be dressed as one — Clark can reply that he estimates transmission rather than reconstructing a tree — but it does mean the project is not committed to the recoverability claim, and the difference should be visible in print rather than left for a referee to assume away.

## Why the boundary is needed now

Three notes in this vocabulary have been written in a single session. That is precisely how a borrowed frame metastasises: each use makes the next feel natural, the vocabulary accumulates faster than the justification, and a reader eventually infers a method that was never claimed. Borrowing a vocabulary creates an implicit promise of the machinery behind it. This note is the discharge of that promise, and it should be written into the methodological section before any further cladistic language reaches a draft.
