# British archives online — the access layers

<https://discovery.nationalarchives.gov.uk/>

**Britain is where this project's digital-asymmetry argument was first made**, and the survey has now been round the rest of Europe and come back to it. The argument holds, and the comparative material makes it sharper than it was.

## The structure

**Discovery** is both TNA's own catalogue and a national aggregator, describing the holdings of thousands of other British archives — a single point of description over a distributed system. In that respect Britain resembles France more than Germany: **one national finding aid, many custodians.**

⚠️ Discovery's own scale figures were not captured in this pass and should be. It also exposes an API, listed on `api.gov.uk`, which is a capability none of the Spanish, Italian or Belgian portals advertises.

**Scotland, Ireland and Wales run separately** — see [[National Records of Scotland]], [[National Archives of Ireland PRONI and the Four Courts fire]], [[National Library of Wales]] — and the Scottish case is not merely administrative: it is a different legal system, which is the point of [[Scots partnership has legal personality and English partnership does not]].

## What is and is not imaged

**The description layer is excellent and the image layer is thin where this project needs it.** TNA states that **none of the company records it holds are viewable online** — the finding from the survey's first pass, and still the sharpest single fact about British access. See [[BT 41 BT 31 and the company files]].

Digitisation has gone instead where there is a commercial partner or a research funder. That produces two conspicuous exceptions, and neither was paid for by British public money:

- [[The Prize Papers - HCA 32 and the German money]] — a nineteen-year open-access programme funded principally from Germany
- The India Office material, opened by a commercial publisher and by a Gulf sovereign foundation — [[British Library - the India Office Records]], and the existing argument at [[The QDL corpus is conditioned on its funder's geography]]

**Three of Britain's most significant early-modern commercial corpora are being opened by foreign or commercial money, and the map of what is open is a map of who paid.** That is the funding-map argument in its purest form, and Britain is where it is least deniable.
