# The waqf and the nación flamenca diverge only at legal personality

The Kuran–Harris axis compares the *waqf* to the *business* corporation and reads the divergence as a **scope** claim: immortality granted to the collective, refused to the private accumulation vehicle (see [[The waqf partition converts absence into refusal]]). Hold that axis fixed and pick a different comparandum — the *nación flamenca*, a charitable-collective corporate body, not a profit enterprise. Both institutions now sit on the *same* side of the scope cut: the sacred/collective domain where both traditions freely grant perpetuity. The divergence cannot be scope. It is the internal form of the collective vehicle itself.

That isolates the variable. Match the two on everything the comparison holds constant — a perpetual, pious, asset-shielded corpus, detached from the founder's or members' private patrimony, governed by quasi-sacralized founding rules (*shurūṭ al-wāqif* ≈ the cofradía's *constituciones*) — and one difference remains: **legal personality**. The *nación* is a *universitas*, a corporate person constituted *by* its members, self-governing, with standing and perpetual succession through a collective body. The *waqf* is dedicated *property* with a purpose and a trustee (*mutawallī*) — no members, no deliberation, no personhood. In the Western taxonomy the *waqf* is the analogue of the charitable **trust/use**, not the corporation.

So the two occupy opposite poles of the trust/corporation cut in the asset-partitioning literature while controlling for purpose: property-centered partition (*waqf*) versus entity-centered partition (*nación*). This is the comparison's whole yield — it does what the *naties* and the *kabu-nakama* cannot, supplying the entity-shielding-*without*-personhood pole and throwing the corporate personhood of the *nación* into relief.

Two guards. Tie the comparison to the trust/corporation axis, not to shared "charitable origins," or the loose-functionalism charge bites. And the thesis that the *waqf* is the ancestor of the English trust (Gaudiosi et al.) is **contested, not settled** [verify] — flag it or a legal historian pounces.
