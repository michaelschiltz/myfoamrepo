# Toda's citation allegations must be checked before we cite Peters

The sharpest content in [[Toda 2023 on ergodicity economics as pseudoscience]] is not the demarcation argument but three specific, verifiable allegations of citation error. They are the only part of the paper that could damage us directly, because a citation error in a source propagates into anything that cites it approvingly.

**Allegation one — Merton and Samuelson cited against their sense.** Peters (2011, *Quantitative Finance* 11: 1593) is said to cite Merton and Samuelson (1974) in a sentence describing the *advantage* of maximised time-average growth, when that paper is hostile to growth-rate optimisation. Toda quotes it at p. 69 on the hope of replacing an arbitrary utility function with $\log W_T$. **The vault is already on the right side of this**: Merton and Samuelson is in the library as `TXBPDNH2` and is cited at [[The growth-rate criterion is asymptotic and the horizon comparison cuts both ways]] as the counter-tradition, which is what it is. Verify the Peters passage, and never cite it as support.

**Allegation two — a claim attributed to a review that does not contain it.** Peters (2011) is said to state that "in economics, a mistaken belief in ergodicity has produced widespread conceptual inconsistency," citing Yakovenko and Rosser, *Reviews of Modern Physics* 81 (2009): 1703, with Toda asserting that nowhere in that article can such a statement be found. If true this is the most serious of the three. Checkable directly — the review is open and the search is mechanical.

**Allegation three — thin experimental base.** That the only experimental evidence Peters (2019) cites is Meder et al., itself criticised by Doctor, Wakker and Wang. Partly a matter of judgement rather than fact, but note the bibliographic point recorded in [[Doctor Wakker and Wang 2020 on the ergodicity problem]]: Toda cites the **published** Meder et al. (*PLOS Computational Biology* 17: e1009217, 2021) while DWW criticised the 2019 working paper. Whether the published version answers their Appendix is the comparison to run.

## Why this is a stub and what closes it

None of the three has been checked here. Closing this note requires reading Peters (2011) and Yakovenko and Rosser (2009), neither of which is in the library, and comparing the two versions of Meder et al., which is.

**The rule in the meantime.** Cite Peters for the framing and the reframing, not for the intellectual history, and never repeat one of his attributions without going to the source. This is the vault's standing discipline on unverified references — the same rule that governs [[Typology and character coding literature]] and produced the flags there — applied to a source we are otherwise sympathetic to, which is exactly where it is hardest to apply and most necessary.

The general form of the risk: **a critic who is wrong about the framework may still be right about the footnotes, and the footnotes are what a referee can check in ten minutes.**
