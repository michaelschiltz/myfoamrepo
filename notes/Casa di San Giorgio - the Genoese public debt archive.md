# Casa di San Giorgio — the Genoese public debt archive

Held at [[Archivio di Stato di Genova]]. SIAS description: `Casa delle compere e dei banchi di San Giorgio`.

**This is the most important single fondo in Europe for the project's corporate-form argument, and it is the best inventoried.** The Casa di San Giorgio held the Genoese public debt as transferable shares — *luoghi* in *compere* — from the fourteenth century, with a general assembly of shareholders, an administration, and a secondary market in the shares. Whatever the joint-stock company is supposed to be an innovation in, San Giorgio was doing a version of it two centuries earlier.

## Extent and structure

**1239–1863, approximately 40,250 units** — registers, *filze*, boxes and volumes. Documentation before 1407 comes from the predecessor bodies; after 1805 it is thin and concerns the liquidation of the debt.

Seven series areas:

| Series | Dates |
|---|---|
| Affari generali | 1313–1830 |
| Banchi e tesoreria | 1408–1815 |
| **Debito pubblico** | **1346–1839** |
| Imposte e tasse | 1239–1805 |
| Beni immobili | 1341–1805 |
| Gestioni speciali | 1366–1811 |
| Altro | 1358–1863 |

`Debito pubblico`, running from 1346, is the series that holds the *luoghi* — the transferable claims themselves.

## The inventory is the asset

Giuseppe Felloni's inventory ran to **sixteen published volumes (1989–2002)**, with a further twelve left unpublished; in **2008 both the published and the unpublished parts were converted into a database and put online as a complete inventory**. For a fondo of forty thousand units that is an extraordinary finding aid, and it inverts the usual constraint: here the description is the strong layer and the images are the weak one. ⚠️ Locate the current URL of the Felloni database — it is the single most valuable link in the Italian set and the archive's own pages did not surface it in this pass.

## What it is evidence for, and the trap

The temptation is to read San Giorgio as an early joint-stock company and therefore as a precursor. **Resist it.** [[The Roman universitas makes the JSC case parallelism not convergence]] already establishes that a shared ancestral condition turns an apparent convergence into parallelism, and San Giorgio sits inside the same Italian legal world as the later Dutch and English forms are argued to descend from. Its evidential value is not "the JSC was invented in Genoa"; it is that **transferable claims on a perpetual body, with capital lock-in and member exit, were in routine operation before any of the forms the literature treats as the innovation** — which is [[Transferable claims reconcile lock-in with member exit]] with a fourteenth-century witness.

Handled carelessly this becomes a Whiggish precursor story of exactly the kind [[Whiggish history of finance names the ascent narrative]] warns against. Handled correctly it is a datum about the distribution of the form, not a point on a trajectory.
