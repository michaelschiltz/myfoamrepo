# The time-average growth rate from cross-sections is a licensed space-for-time step

Adamou, Berman and Peters compute the time-average growth rate of income — the rate along one trajectory — from two cross-sections, as the change in mean log income across the population (eqs. 2.19–2.20). The step is valid because, under their null, log-income increments are i.i.d. and stationary, so the growth rate is ergodic and its population average equals its time average. **That is a space-for-time substitution, licensed by an assumption about the dynamics.**

It is licensed. It is also exactly the move Objective 3 examines in the persistence literature, and a referee who has read Part 2 will see it the moment the project cites the paper for the Great Divergence corollary. The answer is to say it first: the project does not object to substituting space for time; it objects to doing so without stating and checking the condition — [[Licensing the ensemble is a dynamical question not a metaphysical one]]. Here the condition is stated, and it can fail: serial correlation in individual growth, regime breaks, or absorption (households that leave the income distribution) all break eq. 2.19.

## When applying it historically

- **State the null each time.** A social table for an agrarian polity with periodic famine has absorption in it; the computed rate is then a rate for survivors — [[Conditioning on survival is an ensemble operation]].
- **Keep the corollary fenced.** Part 2 now calls the misreading of laggards "measurable as the gap between ensemble- and time-average growth" and still declines to promise the result.
- **The method critiques the ascent narrative quantitatively.** A polity whose GDP per capita grows faster than its geometric-mean income is getting richer as an aggregate and poorer as a typical trajectory — [[The ascent narrative is the Jensen error as historiography]].
