# South Asian archives — the colonial state kept the record and the merchants kept their own

**The structural fact: South Asia's institutional archive is a colonial administrative archive, enormous and increasingly online, and it is not the record of the institutions the project names. Those are in three other places — stone, private account books, and the colonial state's own enquiries into them.**

## The three deposits

**1. The administrative record, in two countries.** The East India Company's and the India Office's papers are in London — [[British Library - the India Office Records]] — and the government of India's are in Delhi and the state capitals: [[National Archives of India - Abhilekh Patal]], [[Maharashtra State Archives - the Peshwa Daftar]], [[Tamil Nadu Archives - the Madras records and the Dutch series]], [[Rajasthan State Archives - Bikaner and the princely bahis]]. **The Company's archive is in the metropole and the successor state's archive is in the successor state**, and the two halves describe the same transactions from opposite ends. **Pattern 2 in its imperial form.**

**2. The indigenous commercial record, in private hands.** [[Indian merchant account books - bahi khata and kadai kanakku]]. **No South Asian counterpart to the Huizhou corpus or the Japanese *monjokan* network was found in this pass.** Firms' books stayed with families, and reached institutions by litigation, by enquiry or by the market.

**3. The institutional record before paper: inscriptions.** [[Indian inscriptions - the endowment recorded in stone]]. **The earliest and clearest South Asian evidence for a funded institution is epigraphic**, and it is better than the Chinese stele evidence because it records the terms.

## And the layer that substitutes for all three

[[The colonial enquiry as South Asia's layer 3]]. **The institutions the census would want — rotating funds, caste banking, moneylending, joint families — were surveyed by the colonial state**, in banking enquiry committees, district manuals, glossaries and customary-law compilations, **and those surveys are printed, free and searchable.** The census's single South Asian source is one of them, a glossary of 1855.

## What this does to the evidence

**1. The funding map is a state map.** Digitisation follows government records: 189 million pages at the national archive, a Dutch-funded project for the Dutch series at Chennai and Colombo. **No programme images merchants' books, because no institution holds them.**

**2. The script wall is real and is being lowered by machines.** [[Modi script and the forty million documents]]. Marathi administration is in Modi; Mughal and princely administration in Persian *shikasta*; merchants' books in *mahajani*, *moḍī* and Tamil commercial hands. **Every one of these is a specialist hand and the first machine transliteration of Modi shipped in 2025.**

**3. The census is emptiest here, and honestly so.** Its only South Asian row, `huf`, is uncoded and recommended for removal — see [[The joint Hindu family is a law-report institution]]. **This pass did not find a repository that would fill it**; it found two institutions elsewhere that could carry rows, and printed evidence for both.
