# Het Scheepvaartmuseum — Amsterdam

<https://www.hetscheepvaartmuseum.nl/>

A museum collection rather than an archive, and the note exists to say what that changes.

## What a museum collection is, for a project like this

**Objects, printed works, charts, atlases and ships' equipment, selected for display value.** There is a library and a collection of printed maritime works, including the legal and commercial literature — the *zeerechten*, the insurance handbooks, the printed ordinances — which is a genuine resource, and it belongs to the acquisitions layer rather than the archival one: this is where a printed edition is *held*, not where a manuscript series survives.

⚠️ Collection scope and access unverified.

## The reason to be careful

A museum's selection principle is **exhibition**, and it is the strongest filter in this whole survey. What survives into a maritime museum is what looks like maritime history: fine charts, ship models, instruments, the visually impressive. The mundane contractual paper that this project reads is exactly what such a collection does not collect.

That is not a criticism of the museum; it is a warning against the inference. [[Refusals are observations of the filter not inferences from survivors]] applies with unusual force, because the filter here is aesthetic and therefore invisible to a researcher who is not looking for it.

**The honest placement: consult it for printed works and iconography; do not treat its holdings as evidence about what maritime commerce consisted of.**
