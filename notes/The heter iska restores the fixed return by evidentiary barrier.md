# The heter iska restores the fixed return by evidentiary barrier

The early modern *heter ʿisqa* keeps the *ʿisqa*'s risk-sharing form intact on the page and destroys it at the **evidentiary layer**. The contract remains formally multiplicative and is functionally additive.

R. Mendel Avigdors of Cracow (d. 1599) standardised the instrument as *Shetar Heter ʿIsqa ke-Tikkun MaHaRaM*, converting any loan into a nominal *ʿisqa*. Three devices do the work:

- a stipulated "profit" the manager may pay **in lieu of** rendering account;
- a nominal wage discharging *sekharo ke-poʿel batel*;
- proof of loss requiring the manager's oath **and** two kosher witnesses.

The third is load-bearing. The bar is set where it cannot be met. Loss therefore cannot be established, the stipulated sum is always due, and what is nominally a residual claim on a venture is in payoff terms a fixed coupon.

## Why this belongs beside the gharar note

[[Gharar excludes designed-in unverifiability]] holds that Islamic doctrine voids contracts whose unverifiability is engineered in. The *heter ʿisqa* **is** designed-in unverifiability — and it was ratified rather than voided, by the same kind of jurisprudence, against the same underlying prohibition. That is not a minor divergence in *ḥiyal* practice; it is opposite treatment of the identical defect, and it is the sharpest available contrast between the two traditions' handling of verification.

Note also the direction of travel. [[Decoupling substitutes proxy legibility for outcome legibility]] describes forms that abandon outcome-coupling and substitute a proxy. The *heter ʿisqa* does something stranger: it **retains outcome-coupling as the operative legal term** and disables the machinery that would ever read the outcome. The coupling survives as text and dies as practice. Do not code it as decoupled on the face of the instrument — code it as coupled-and-unverifiable, which is a distinct cell.

## Where this is soft

**Verify Avigdors' dates and the received formula** — he is given as d. 1599 while the printed *nusaḥ* circulates later, and the oath/witness clause varies between recensions. The comparison above needs the specific clause from a specific text, not the modern bank form. Also: the framing "evasion" versus "accommodation" is contested — Gamoran's *Jewish Law in Transition* reads the whole ribbit trajectory as economic pressure overcoming doctrine, which is a strong thesis a referee may want resisted rather than assumed.
