# Ragusan maritime insurance

⚠️ **A candidate, recorded on its reputation rather than on a finding aid.**

Ragusa regulated marine insurance by statute in the sixteenth century, and the Ragusan ordinance is routinely named in the literature among the earliest systematic insurance legislation in Europe. ⚠️ **The date and the title were not confirmed in this pass and must not be repeated until they are.**

## Why it belongs in the register

Because it would complete a set the survey has been assembling without planning to. The census's risk-pricing argument — [[Pricing risk does not remove it]], [[MOC - Risk-sharing vs risk-pricing]] — currently rests on Italian, Catalan and Low Countries evidence. Ragusa adds **a small republic that legislated for insurance while also running a shipping fleet of its own**, which is a different political position from Genoa's or Antwerp's.

**And the archive is complete.** Where a Genoese or Florentine insurance claim has to be pursued through notarial protocols, Ragusan practice sits inside the registered series described at [[The Ragusan chancery and notarial series]] — so the statute and the instruments drawn under it are in the same building, in series, for the same period. **That configuration is rare enough to be worth a trip on its own.**

⚠️ Nothing here is verified. The specific first task is to establish the ordinance's date and text and whether an insurance series exists as such, rather than insurance acts scattered through *Diversa*.
