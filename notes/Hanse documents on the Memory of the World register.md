# Hanse documents on the Memory of the World register

**A cross-repository note**, because the inscription is itself a description of where the Hanseatic record is.

Inscribed **2023**: *Dokumente zur Geschichte der Hanse* — **21 documents, in 17 document groups across six categories, held by 11 archives in six countries**, covering the twelfth to seventeenth centuries.

## The holding archives

| Country | Archives |
|---|---|
| Germany | Lübeck, Braunschweig, Hamburg, **Cologne**, Stralsund |
| Belgium | Bruges city archive |
| Denmark | Royal Library, Copenhagen |
| Latvia | Latvian State Historical Archive, Riga |
| Estonia | Tallinn city archive |
| Poland | Toruń state archive |

## Why this is worth its own note rather than a line in each repository's

**Because the distribution is the finding.** A league of some two hundred cities left its record in the archives of its members, and the members are now in six states, four of which did not exist as such when the documents were written. **There is no Hanseatic archive**, because there was no Hanseatic state — the institutional form is legible only as a distribution across the archives of its participants.

That has a direct methodological consequence. Every other institution in this survey can be worked from one or two repositories; **this one cannot be worked from fewer than six countries' archive systems, each with its own language, catalogue and access regime.** A form whose evidence is structurally dispersed will be under-described relative to forms whose evidence is concentrated — and that is a fact about custody, not about the institution.

It is the [[The VOC-EIC digital asymmetry is an access regime not a survival difference]] argument in an unfamiliar shape: **not funding this time, but polity.** The VOC left one archive because it had one state; the Hanse left eleven because it had none.

**One of the eleven is [[Historisches Archiv der Stadt Koln and the 2009 collapse]]**, which makes the dispersal a risk as well as an inconvenience.
