# Insolvente Boedelskamer — Antwerp

Held at [[FelixArchief - Antwerp city archive]]. UNESCO **Memory of the World**, ref. 2008-40.

**The best-suited single fonds encountered anywhere in this survey**, and it is worth being precise about why, because its fame rests on something else.

## What it is

Antwerp's municipal council required the papers of **insolvent** merchants and firms to be impounded to protect creditors. The result: **approximately 204 linear metres, over 3,000 fonds (`IB 1`–`IB 3,038`), from 1518 onwards** — correspondence, account books, registers, parchments, maps, drawings and personal effects, covering **more than 150 trading enterprises**, among them the Della Faille, Affaitadi, Forchondt, van Colen, de Pret, de Wael and Dormer firms, in textiles, diamonds, spices, art and colonial goods.

The nomination describes it as a **crucial complement to the VOC archives**, and stresses what is genuinely rare: **complete firm archives, preserved whole**.

## Why it is the right archive for this project specifically

**First, the creditor limb.** [[The creditor limb of entity shielding has no witness]] records that the census asks whether members' *creditors* can reach a firm's assets, as distinct from whether members can force partition, and that no coded source answers it — `deed_of_settlement_company AP2` fell from `P` to `.NR` on exactly that reading. **An insolvency institution cannot avoid the creditor limb**: impounding a failed firm's papers *in order to protect creditors* is that question made into an administrative procedure, and it ran for nearly three centuries.

**Second, and this is the stronger claim: it is an archive of absorption.** The project's dynamics are organised around an absorbing barrier, and the standing methodological complaint — [[Persistence samples are conditioned on non-absorption]] — is that the evidence available about institutions is evidence about the ones that did not hit it. **Here the selection runs the other way.** These firms' papers survive *because* they failed. That is a serial, inventoried, three-century record of non-survivors from the population every persistence argument silently conditions on, and it is the only one of its kind found in this survey.

**Handle the inversion carefully.** A bankruptcy archive is not an unbiased sample either; it is conditioned on failure as tightly as the persistence literature is conditioned on survival, and reading it as *the* population would repeat the error with the sign flipped. Its value is as the **complement** — the missing half of a sample whose other half is everywhere — and that is a different and more defensible use than treating it as representative.

**Third, it repairs a single-witness row.** The Antwerp entity rows rest on one scholar's reading; the firms' own papers are a different kind of witness entirely. See [[FelixArchief - Antwerp city archive]].

## What has to be established before any of this is real

⚠️ Whether the records state the **reasoning** behind a distribution or only its outcome. [[The coding commons must record stated rationale not only component presence]] is the rule that makes the difference decisive: an outcome-only series records who was paid and in what order, and not the ground on which the ranking was made. This is the same question left open at [[Desolate Boedelkamer - Amsterdam]], and here it is worth answering first because the answer determines a great deal.
