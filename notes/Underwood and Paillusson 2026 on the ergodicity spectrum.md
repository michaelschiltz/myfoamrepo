# Underwood and Paillusson 2026 on the ergodicity spectrum

Source note. A data-only method for detecting, counting and weighting the ergodic components of a system. It turns WP3's diagnosis of the mita boundary — two components, not two points on one trajectory — into something that can in principle be tested.

**Bibliographic facts.** Underwood, Nicolas G., and Fabien Paillusson. 2026. "Spectroscopic Classification of Non-Ergodic Populations." *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society A* 384 (2334): 20260059. DOI `10.1098/rsta.2026.0059`. Zotero `QNK3QIV7`; citekey `underwoodSpectroscopicClassificationNonergodic2026`. Newcastle (Underwood), Lincoln (Paillusson). Received 24 February 2026, accepted 14 July 2026. Supplementary material `10.6084/m9.figshare.c.8741586`. Pp. 1–7 of 19 read from page images; appendices unread.

## What it does

- **Separates three notions (§§2–5).** Metric indecomposability, equality of averages and equality of distributions, with the implication MI ⇒ equality of distributions for any observable ⇒ equality of averages for the same observable. The contrapositive lets either equality serve as a null hypothesis against MI, and distributional tests are typically the more powerful. Molenaar's distributional notion is standard in medicine and psychology; economics has adopted Peters's equality-of-averages notion.
- **Defines the ergodicity spectrum (eqs. 7.1–7.2).** For each initial state, the distance between the trajectory's distribution of an observable and the ensemble's. Histogrammed over initial states, an ergodic system gives one line at zero; a decomposable system gives one line per ergodic component, located by that component's deviation and weighted by its share of the population.
- **Reconstructs it with the Kolmogorov–Smirnov distance (§8),** the only distance for which a non-parametric, finite-sample bound on resolution exists (the Dvoretzky–Kiefer–Wolfowitz–Massart inequality, extended in a companion paper).
- **Demonstrates it on the Kob–Andersen glass model (§9).** Additional lines appear as density rises, each matched to a class of frozen configurations.

## What it does for the project

- **The Dell diagnosis becomes countable.** WP3 says Dell's hacienda mechanism makes the mita boundary a separation between basins; on this method, two basins are two spectral lines, and their weights are the shares of the population in each. Cited at Part 2 Objective 3, "testable from data". See [[The mita boundary separates two components not two points on a trajectory]] and [[Attractor selection bounds what a persistence design can resolve]].
- **It gives the pre-committed survivor case a test.** A design that the critique should leave standing ought to show a single line for its observable at its horizon. See [[The survivor case should discriminate inside a single design]].

## Limits that bind

- **It needs trajectories.** The method takes many trajectory samples of length $m$ from initial conditions drawn from the stationary density. Historical persistence data are usually one cross-section, or two; a spectrum cannot be built from them. Usable on long panels — prices, rents, harvests, a merchant house's accounts — not on deep-roots regressions as published.
- **It presupposes a stationary ensemble.** Appendix B is said to offer an alternative prescription where none can be found [verify, unread]. Without it, [[Stationarity is a precondition of ergodicity not a corollary]] applies and the method has nothing to compare against.
- **Continuous state spaces may give continuous spectra (§10)**, which indicate non-ergodicity without counting components.
