# A shared compilation never licenses an inference to shared practice

Edda Frankot's case is that medieval northern Europe had no supra-territorial law of the
sea, and that the appearance of one is an artefact of reading compilations instead of
courts. The *Rôles d'Oléron* travelled from Brittany to Damme to the Baltic and into the
Wisby collection; Lübeck, Reval, Danzig, Kampen and Aberdeen all had access to written
maritime law. **They decided differently anyway.** Her reason is institutional rather than
cultural: a law requires either an authority able to impose it or a community sworn to it,
and the Hanse was neither — every statute had to be confirmed town by town, and the larger
towns guarded their own. Kampen's own law tells its burghers to submit abroad to *the law
that is decent and customary there*, expecting it to differ.

The methodological consequence for a comparative typology is a prohibition, and it is
sharper than it looks. **Textual diffusion is not evidence of institutional identity.** A
form named in a compilation that circulated widely may still be a different object in every
port that received it, and coding one row for the compilation's reach silently asserts the
opposite.

Two cautions on using her, both learned by trying.

**She is not evidence about credit.** Neither the 2007 chapter nor the 2010 Aberdeen article
mentions bodemerij, bottomry or the sea loan once. Her material is jettison, damage,
freight and crew — the adjudicative law of the sea, not its instruments. Citing her *for*
the pluralism of northern maritime credit would be exactly the move she is arguing against,
made in her name.

**The prohibition is symmetrical.** It forbids inferring one form from shared texts, and it
equally forbids inferring several forms from separate jurisdictions. Diversity has to be
shown in practice too. In the Amsterdam bodemerij corpus the sharpest disagreement — three
incompatible rules for what happens when a master deviates — sits inside a single city,
inside twenty years, in the registers of two notaries. **The unit that varies is the
drafting practice, not the jurisdiction**, which is where this project's rule that units are
instruments rather than populations does real work.
