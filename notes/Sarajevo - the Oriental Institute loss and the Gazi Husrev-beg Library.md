# Sarajevo — the Oriental Institute loss and the Gazi Husrev-beg Library

**Two Ottoman collections in one city, and a war that destroyed one and not the other.**

## The Oriental Institute, May 1992

The **Orijentalni institut u Sarajevu** was shelled with incendiary munitions in **May 1992** — sources give the 17th and the 18th. Its manuscript and document collection burned: **5,263 bound manuscripts** in Arabic, Persian, Ottoman Turkish, Hebrew and *arebica*, and **tens of thousands of Ottoman documents**; about **1 per cent** of the holdings is reported saved. ⚠️ Figures from the Wikipedia article and the sources it cites (Riedlmayer 1995–2007, Karić 2014, Ljevaković 2017), not checked against them. ⚠️ Whether the Institute held Ottoman cadastral registers or court registers of Bosnia, and whether any microfilm survives, not established.

**This loss has a shape the five European ones lacked.** Naples 1943 or Cologne 2009 destroyed an administration's record; **Sarajevo destroyed a research institute's collection** — a set of documents already selected, gathered and catalogued for study. What was lost is known better than what survived elsewhere, and what was lost was a selection. **Pattern 3 applies with a twist: the survival rule is a collecting rule.**

## The Gazi Husrev-beg Library

Founded under the **1537 endowment** of Gazi Husrev-beg's *medrese*. **More than 10,500 manuscript codices**; manuscripts moved eight times during the siege, the 500 most valuable held in bank vaults; reopened in a new building in **January 2014** on a Qatari grant. The manuscript collection is on UNESCO's Memory of the World register.

**A waqf library that outlived the war because its custodians moved it** — successor custody by the endowment itself, and pattern 2 again. ⚠️ That the library is still administered as the founder's *vakuf* is stated from general knowledge, not from a source read here.

## Court registers

The *TDV İslâm Ansiklopedisi* counts **158 registers from the Sarajevo area, divided among three institutions.** ⚠️ Which three, and the Gazi Husrev-beg Library's share, not established.

## The honest assessment

**For the census, marginal.** No coded row draws on Bosnian material. Bosnian *vakufnamas* bear on `waqf_khayri` in the same way the Anatolian deeds do, and the Bosnian frontier courts are the other side of [[Kadijski spisi - the kadi documents at Dubrovnik]] — the courts of Novi and Ljubinje sat in Ottoman Herzegovina. **That second point is the one that could make Sarajevo a destination**, and only if a Ragusan–Ottoman debt case needs its court's own register rather than the copy Ragusa kept.
