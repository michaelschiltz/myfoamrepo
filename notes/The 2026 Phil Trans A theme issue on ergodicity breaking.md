# The 2026 Phil Trans A theme issue on ergodicity breaking

Hub source note. "Ergodicity and ergodicity breaking in the sciences," *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society A* 384, issue 2334 (19 November 2026), sixteen contributions, guest-edited from the London Mathematical Laboratory. All sixteen are in Zotero (added 2026-11-20). **None of the PDFs carries a text layer after page 1**: full-text search in Zotero finds nothing past the abstract, and every reading below was done from page images.

**Why the issue matters to the project.** Three of the four PIs' home institution put the programme's current state in one place, and the issue is what a panel member will read first. It is also the first time the programme's founders concede, in print, the formal identity between the time-average criterion and expected-utility theory that the published critics alleged — see [[Every utility function is growth-optimal under some dynamic]] — while the same issue carries the paper that shows where that identity breaks — [[The utility mapping fails exactly where absorption begins]]. The two papers sit side by side and do not cite each other.

## Read, and filed separately

- [[Peters and Adamou 2026 on the time interpretation of expected utility theory]] — the Bernoulli question, answered by generalisation. Read in full.
- [[Vanhoyweghen Verbeken and Ginis 2026 on absorbing boundaries]] — the time-average criterion under ruin. Read in full.
- [[Connaughton Lamb and Paillusson 2026 on what ergodicity is]] — the issue introduction; the co-PI's definitional hierarchy. Read in full.
- [[Soysal 2026 on the ergodicity assumption in climate damages]] — Objective 3's argument, made for climate econometrics, from inside LML. Pp. 1–8 of 11 read.
- [[Underwood and Paillusson 2026 on the ergodicity spectrum]] — a data-only test for ergodic decomposition. Pp. 1–7 of 19 read.
- [[Adamou Berman and Peters 2026 on the two growth rates]] — GDP per capita as an ensemble average. Pp. 1–6 of 12 read.
- [[Raposo and Pulido 2026 on additive ruin with borrowing]] — the stipend debtor's dynamic. Pp. 1–5 of 12 read.

## Marginal, held here

- **Britto, Anthony. 2026. "Likelihood-Based Estimation of Ergodicity Transformations for Time Series." 384: 20250158. DOI `10.1098/rsta.2025.0158`. Zotero `DQL2PPSX`.** Estimates which transformation renders a series' increments stationary, by likelihood, inside ARMA, GARCH and Gaussian-process models; recovers known transforms on simulated GBM and ABM and is tried on FRED-QD. The only paper in the issue that addresses the which-dynamic objection at [[Jensen supplies the gap but only the dynamic privileges the logarithm]] with an estimator rather than an assertion. Candidate instrument for WP4's regime markers on long historical series (price lists, the Capital Markets of the World archive, harvest series); useless on the short, gapped series most cases supply. Abstract only [verify].
- **Fleming, Hugo, Lasse Christiansen, Simon R. Steinkamp, and Oliver Hulme. 2026. "Ergodicity and the Speed–Accuracy Trade-Off: Why Optimal Response Times Depend on Reward Dynamics." 384: 20250169. DOI `10.1098/rsta.2025.0169`. Zotero `UI6SJPE4`.** Time-optimal duration maximises expected log-increment per unit time under multiplicative dynamics, and the optimum lengthens with volatility. The formal precedent for recording stated voyage duration and stated return as fields in the deed-level instance files. A historical duration test bites only where the dynamic fixes the discount with no free parameter, so it belongs in the corpus and not in the proposal. Abstract only.
- **Fisher, Aaron J. 2026. "Beyond Parametric Ergodicity: A Framework for Structural Generalizability." 384: 20250159. DOI `10.1098/rsta.2025.0159`. Zotero `YJ65XUF2`.** Molenaar's psychological sense of ergodicity. A single changed covariance among predictors moves every coefficient (fig. 1, p. 5), so coefficient-level equivalence between group and individual is an unrealistically high bar; structural descriptors (partitions, topology, logical relations) can generalise where coefficients cannot. Supports the categorical design of the coding ontology. Pp. 2–6 read; not worth proposal space.
- **Peters, Ole, and Alexander Adamou. 2026. "Leverage Efficiency." 384: 20250162. DOI `10.1098/rsta.2025.0162`. Zotero `3EAUDP6S`.** Objectively optimal leverage, hypothesised to be attracted to one by stability, corroborated on several thousand price series; applications include a rate-setting protocol. A possible lens on sea-loan premia and usury ceilings, speculative. Abstract only.
