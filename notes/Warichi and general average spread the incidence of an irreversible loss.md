# Warichi and general average spread the incidence of an irreversible loss

Two institutions with no plausible common ancestor perform the same office. Jettison destroys one merchant's cargo; the cargo is gone, the ship's aggregate loss is fixed, and general average redistributes only *who bears it*, pro rata among those with a stake. A river avulsion destroys one household's parcel; the land is gone, the village's aggregate loss is fixed, and *warichi* redistributes only who bears it, by re-drawing the lots. In both cases the loss is real, total for the asset, and untouched by the institution. What the institution allocates is **incidence**.

That is a sharper description than either "insurance" or "reset". Insurance implies a transfer that restores the sufferer; general average and *warichi* restore nobody — the pool is permanently smaller afterwards. And "resetting" overstates it: *warichi* does not return a household to a prior level of wealth, it re-randomises position within a pool that may itself have shrunk. The formal object is the distribution of an absorbing event's incidence across members, not the reversal of the event.

**Why incidence is the thing that matters here.** An irreversible asset loss threatens the *household's* absorbing barrier only if it lands concentrated on one household ([[Warichi re-randomises after absorption rather than diversifying]]). Spread thinly across all members it is a proportional reduction that no one's trajectory need terminate on. So the institution converts an idiosyncratic ruin risk into a shared level effect — which is precisely what raises a time-average growth rate under multiplicative dynamics, and precisely what a levels-based test of equality will fail to detect ([[Brown tests equality where a risk reading predicts dispersion]]).

**The mechanisms differ and the difference is legible in the matrix.** General average assesses contributions *ex post* and compensates the sufferer in money — `CN1=ex-post-assessment`, `PY0=individual-indemnity`. *Warichi* holds no fund, levies no contribution, and reallocates the surviving asset itself at fixed intervals. One indemnifies; the other re-deals. Functionally equivalent, structurally unalike — which is the profile worth having, since a resemblance in function with divergent particulars is the diagnostic that carries evidential weight ([[Divergent arbitrary particulars are the independence diagnostic]]).

**Not yet a claim about independent arrival.** The pairing is asserted here at the level of function only. Whether it survives as an independence case requires the *Leitfehler* treatment — arbitrary particulars diverging where a shared ancestor would predict agreement — and neither form has been examined for that ([[Transmission versus independent recurrence is the load-bearing test]]). The word to avoid meanwhile is the cladistic one.

**The pairing needs one qualification, and it is doctrinal.** General average requires a *deliberate* sacrifice for the common safety; damage that merely befalls the venture is *particular* average and is not shared at all. So the two forms agree that incidence should be spread and disagree about which losses qualify — see [[General average shares decisions where warichi shares hazards]], which is the sharper statement of what this pairing establishes.

**One open question that would discriminate.** General average is triggered by an event: a jettison happens, an assessment follows. *Warichi* runs on a calendar: redistribution occurs at five- or ten-year intervals whether or not a parcel has been destroyed. If incidence-spreading were the whole story one would expect event-triggered reallocation, and Brown's interval data show scheduled reallocation instead. Either the schedule is a cheap approximation to event-triggering under costly verification, or the institution is doing something besides incidence-spreading. That is the question his interval series could actually answer.
