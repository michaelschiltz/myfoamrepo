# The *archives départementales* system

About a hundred services, one per department, each with its own portal — and **one classification frame shared by all of them.**

## The frame

Series letters are uniform across the country. The ones this project needs:

| Series | Content |
|---|---|
| **`B`** | Ancien-régime courts and jurisdictions — including the **amirautés**, the *sénéchaussées*, the consular courts |
| **`C`** | Provincial administration, intendances, chambers of commerce in some departments |
| **`E`** | Feudal and family papers, **and the notarial minutes** in most departments |
| **`H`** | Regular and secular clergy, hospitals |
| **`J`** | **Private archives** — company fonds, family papers, anything given or deposited |

**`B` and `E` are where this project lives**, and `J` is where a company's own archive lands if it survived into private hands.

## What this makes possible

A uniform frame turns a question into a sweep. **"What do the admiralty courts hold on average and insurance" is answerable across every coastal department at once**, because the material is in série `B` in all of them — see [[The amirautes - a series across the coastal departments]].

That is a genuine methodological advantage over every country surveyed so far, and it is the one thing France offers that the Low Countries do not.

## What it does not solve

⚠️ **Digitisation is decided department by department**, and the decisions have been overwhelmingly genealogical: *état civil*, parish registers, censuses, conscription. The series this project needs are the least likely to have been imaged, and coverage varies by an order of magnitude between neighbouring departments for no reason connected to the records.

**A departmental coverage map is therefore a map of local council budgets.** The funding-map argument, one administrative tier further down than it has been stated so far.
