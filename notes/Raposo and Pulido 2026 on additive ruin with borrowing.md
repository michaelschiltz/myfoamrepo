# Raposo and Pulido 2026 on additive ruin with borrowing

Source note. A household on a fixed income that borrows at interest, and the ergodicity transformation of its wealth dynamic. Written for behavioural economics; read here as a model of the Edo stipend debtor.

**Bibliographic facts.** Raposo, Carlos Rodriguez, and Pablo Coello Pulido. 2026. "Ergodicity Transformation for Additive-Ruin Wealth Dynamic with Money Borrowing." *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society A* 384 (2334): 20250170. DOI `10.1098/rsta.2025.0170`. Zotero `2RGAAA3P`; citekey `raposoErgodicityTransformationAdditiveruin2026`. BeWay Consulting, A Coruña. Received 2 October 2025, accepted 12 January 2026. Supplementary material `10.6084/m9.figshare.c.8741092`. Pp. 1–5 of 12 read from page images; the solutions in §4 and the discussion unread.

## What it does

- **A modified additive dynamic (§2).** Income $p$ per period while wealth is positive. In debt the agent pays interest $r$ on it, so drift falls linearly. At the *perpetuity point* $-p/r$ the whole income goes to interest and the principal can never be reduced; beyond it lies the region the authors call ruin. Ruin is a region the agent cannot leave, not a fixed absorbing threshold.
- **Two treatments of the ruin region.** *Dynamic I*: the creditor takes the whole income and freezes the principal, which the authors read as a bankruptcy settlement. *Dynamic II*: unpaid interest is added to the principal, so the debt grows without bound.
- **Transformations (§4).** Dynamic I gives a linear-exponential (linex) transform and one-switch preferences: a lottery refused below a wealth threshold is accepted above it. Dynamic II gives a logarithm with a vertical asymptote at the perpetuity point. Both imply risk aversion where pure additive dynamics imply neutrality.
- **The smoothing is a modelling choice (p. 4).** Logistic step functions are used to make the dynamic differentiable; other smoothings may give other transforms.

## What it does for the project

- **It is a model of the stipend debtor.** Fixed rice stipend, borrowing from the *fudasashi* against future stipends, a debt level at which interest absorbs the stipend, and shogunal settlements that froze or restructured principal. Filed as a hypothesis at [[Stipend debtors live at the perpetuity point]].
- **It shows risk aversion arising from an additive dynamic with a debt boundary**, without any preference assumption. The same move the project makes for institutions, made for a single household.

## Limits

- Consultancy authors; a short derivation paper, not an empirical one.
- Results depend on the smoothing; the authors say so.
- Debt cancellation — a jump in wealth — is not in the model. The historically important settlements were often cancellations.
