# A long-horizon persistence coefficient bounds no relaxation time

[[A significant persistence coefficient is evidence against its own licensing condition]] turns the squeeze into an inequality: a non-zero coefficient over *n* centuries is a lower bound on the relaxation time. **In general that is false.** In the stable linear case, as the horizon grows every λₖᴸ goes to zero and the coefficient tends to β_µ, the alignment of the initial configuration with the differences in steady states ([[The persistence coefficient fails even where the dynamics are ergodic]]). β_µ is non-zero whenever steady states differ and the historical configuration happens to line up with them. Numerically, with *c* and *A* fixed, the long-run coefficient ranges from −0.70 to +0.71 across initial configurations, after every mode has relaxed. A coefficient that survives six centuries is compatible with complete relaxation.

## Where the inequality survives

Only under homogeneous steady states and regular coupling — Kelly's clean case. There the weights are non-negative and the coefficient lies below ρ(*A*)ᴸ, so a non-zero coefficient does bound the slowest contrast mode's relaxation from below. The squeeze note's inequality must carry that condition wherever it is used, in the vault or in print.

## The third reading the fork lacks

The squeeze note forks between a temporal reading (the finding contradicts its licence) and a causal-inference reading (the temporal vocabulary is decoration). A third reading has the same two-date signature: **fixed heterogeneity.** Places differ in characteristics *c*ᵢ that set their steady states, and the historical event fell, for reasons of its own, where those characteristics lay. The event then marks the steady state rather than causing anything that propagated. That is the reading the rival accounts of Voigtländer and Voth already supply — political fragmentation (Finley and Koyama), the post-1517 division of labour (Becker and Pascali) — in which the treatment proxies a structural variable.

The same applies to [[Attractor selection bounds what a persistence design can resolve]]. Proportional transmission and attractor selection share a cross-sectional signature, and so does heterogeneity of steady states with full relaxation: three mechanisms, one two-date result.

## What separates them

Intermediate dates. Under relaxation the sequence of coefficients decays; under steady-state heterogeneity it falls to a plateau and stays there; under attractor selection it holds at the partition. A design with three or more dates can tell them apart, which is one more reason the pre-committed survivor case should be sought where intermediate observations exist. Kelly's clean case also gives a stateable selection criterion for that case — homogeneous steady states and regular coupling — to be written down before the case is chosen.
