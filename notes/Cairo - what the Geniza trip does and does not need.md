# Cairo — what the Geniza trip does and does not need

**Decision_log A7 budgets "Cairo / Cambridge (Cairo Geniza materials)".** On what this pass found, **Cairo holds essentially none of the Geniza.** The deposit was emptied by dealers before 1896, by Schechter in 1896–97 and by d'Hulst's excavation in 1897–98; what the Ben Ezra synagogue is now is a restored monument. ⚠️ Whether any fragments remain in Egyptian custody — at the synagogue, with the Jewish community, or with the antiquities authorities — was not established.

## So the Geniza half of the line is Cambridge, and possibly not even that

- **Every major collection is imaged and machine-read.** See [[Friedberg Genizah Project and Ktiv - the image union layer]] and [[MiDRASH - the machine transcription of the Geniza]].
- **A visit answers physical questions only** — a verso, a join, an Arabic text under a Hebrew one. See [[Cambridge University Library - the Taylor-Schechter Genizah Collection]].

## What Cairo is for, if it is for anything

**A different question, carried over from the Ottoman pass.** The Egyptian National Archives (*Dār al-Wathāʾiq al-Qawmiyya*) hold Cairo's Ottoman court registers — **1,851 registers, 1530–1876** on the *TDV İslâm Ansiklopedisi*'s count — and the Ministry of Awqaf holds waqf deeds. Those bear on `waqf_khayri` and on the `cash_waqf` question, **not on the Geniza**. See [[MOC - Ottoman archives]].

**Access there is the binding constraint.** A 2020 survey of Middle East archives reports that access for foreigners at the national archive "hinged largely on connections from influential figures"; the Bibliotheca Alexandrina was reported digitising 90 million national-archive documents with no public access plan. ⚠️ Both from one secondary account; nothing checked with the archive.

## The recommendation, stated for the maintainer

**Split A7's line.** "Cambridge (Geniza)" — short, optional, for physical examination. "Cairo (Ottoman and Mamluk court and waqf records)" — a separate trip with a separate purpose and an access problem to solve first. **Keeping them together budgets a Cairo visit for material that is in England and online.**
