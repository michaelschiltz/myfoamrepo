# The Virtual Record Treasury and reconstruction as method

<https://virtualtreasury.ie/> — the Beyond 2022 programme, led from Trinity College Dublin

**The most methodologically interesting thing in the British and Irish pass**, and it belongs in this hub as a *method* rather than as a repository.

## What it does

It reconstructs the archive destroyed in 1922 by:

- taking **Herbert Wood's 1919 *Guide*** — the pre-fire finding aid — as the key to the lost structure
- cross-referencing surviving pre-1922 office records and indexes
- conserving and digitising the salvaged fragments
- linking material held elsewhere into a single digital platform

State-funded 2017–2022, beginning with the National Archives of Ireland, The National Archives (UK) and PRONI, and joined by **nearly seventy** other archives, libraries and learned societies in Ireland, Britain and the United States.

## The result that should be quoted

For the **1766 religious census**, only **59 original items — about 7% of the series — survived the fire.** Combined with transcriptions from multiple other sources, **over 50% of the complete census has been reconstructed.**

## Why this matters to the project rather than to Irish history

**It is a worked demonstration that an archive's destruction and an archive's absence are different things**, and that the gap between them is a function of how much the archive had been copied before it burned.

That cuts directly against a habit this project has to guard. [[Refusals are observations of the filter not inferences from survivors]] treats absence as evidence about the filter. **Ireland shows the filter can be partly inverted** — and it also shows what the reconstruction costs and what it selects: *what had been transcribed*, which is what earlier scholars and administrators found worth copying.

**So a reconstructed series is doubly conditioned**: once by the fire and once by nineteenth-century copying interests. The same structure as the Paris *état civil reconstitué* at [[Archives de Paris and the fire of 1871]] — but here **the method is documented, the base is published, and the proportion recovered is stated per series.**

**Where the reconstruction rule is published, the reconstruction can be corrected for.** That is the same conclusion reached at [[Historisches Archiv der Stadt Koln and the 2009 collapse]], arrived at from the other direction, and two independent instances make it a principle worth stating in the methods section: **ask not only what survived, but what the survival rule was and whether anyone wrote it down.**
