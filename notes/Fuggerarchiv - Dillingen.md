# Fuggerarchiv — Dillingen

<https://www.fugger.de/archiv/bestaende> · the *Fürstlich und Gräflich Fuggersches Familien- und Stiftungsarchiv*

**A private family and foundation archive**, held by the family's own institutions, with published *Bestände* and a stated procedure for visits and use.

## The third instance of a pattern, which is now worth calling a pattern

This survey has found the same structure three times:

- The **Bazacle**'s modern archive, plausibly with EDF after the 1946 nationalisation — [[The Bazacle records - a custody problem]]
- The Antwerp ***naties***, possibly with their trading successors — [[Natie archives - where the Antwerp nations records are]]
- The **Fuggers**, whose archive is held by the family and its foundations to this day

**Institutions that survive keep their own papers.** That is not a curiosity; it is a systematic bias in the evidence available to a project about institutional durability. **The archives of the durable are disproportionately in private custody, and private custody means access by permission rather than by right.**

The consequence is precise, and it sharpens [[Persistence samples are conditioned on non-absorption]]: the persistence literature's samples are conditioned on survival, and **the archival record of the survivors is additionally conditioned on the survivor's willingness to be read.** Two filters stacked in the same direction.

## What is here

The business and family papers of the Augsburg house — the Fugger being the sixteenth century's dominant European financial enterprise and the principal creditor of the Habsburgs. ⚠️ Bestände, date coverage and what is open are to be read from the archive's own listing; access is by arrangement and terms are unestablished.

The **Fuggerei**, the family's almshouse foundation of 1521 and still operating, is a separate matter and a genuine candidate form: an endowed, perpetual, purpose-bound charitable body of the kind the entity census reads through the *waqf* rows. ⚠️ Noted, not proposed.
