# Zigong salt contracts — shareholding in the wells

**Zigong Municipal Archives**, Sichuan. Selection in print: **《自贡盐业契约档案选辑（1732–1949）》**, edited by the Chinese Salt History Research Office (1985).

**The share contracts of the Zigong salt yard** — the deep brine and gas wells whose drilling took years and whose financing was divided into shares, including **day-shares (日份) and pan-shares (锅份)**, with partnership drilling agreements. A local account gives more than 200 salt works and more than 3,000 contracts. ⚠️ That figure and the archive's holdings are unverified.

## Why it has a note

**Because Zelin built her account of Chinese shareholding on it first.** *The Merchants of Zigong* (2005) is the monograph behind the view that Qing shares were abstract, tradable income claims, and the 2019 article that the census codes from generalises it. **The census's Chinese share rows therefore rest, one step removed, on one industry's contracts in one municipal archive and a 1985 printed selection.**

**And it is the Chinese case most like the census's European share cases** — the Bazacle and the *Kux*: a capital-hungry extractive enterprise with long gestation, divisible shares and, on the Kux-talha pair's evidence, **the call-and-forfeiture question** — what happened to a shareholder who would not pay for further drilling. ⚠️ Not read; a question to put to the selection before a row is proposed.
