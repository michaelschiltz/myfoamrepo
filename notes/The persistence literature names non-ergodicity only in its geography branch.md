# The persistence literature names non-ergodicity only in its geography branch

Part 2 states that the persistence literature has assembled every component of the critique "without naming the property that unifies them". A sweep of 14 November 2026 (three Undermind deep searches, abstract-level) shows that this holds for the deep-roots regressions and their methodological critics, and fails for three neighbouring branches. A referee from any of the three will notice.

## Where the word is already in use

**Path-dependence theory.** David defines a path-dependent process as non-ergodic (2005; 2007; 2019). Heckman (1991) names nonergodicity as a fashionable feature of current models and asks whether data can separate state dependence from heterogeneity — the econometric ancestor of the question WP3 poses. Stachurski (2001; 2003) shows that in nonlinear stochastic growth models persistence of the historical state and formal ergodicity can coincide, which is the published form of [[Distinguish strong from weak ergodicity breaking]].

**Convergence empirics.** Quah (1993; 1996) and the distribution-dynamics literature compute "ergodic distributions" of cross-country income; Landon-Lane and Quinn (2000) test whether the world has reached its ergodic state; Bernard and Durlauf (1996) show that cross-section and time-series tests of convergence encode different notions of convergence and different assumptions about the limiting distribution — the closest published statement of the space-for-time problem in growth economics. The usage is the applied-probability sense of [[The word ergodic carries four incompatible senses]]: an irreducible chain is presupposed, and "convergence clubs" are the shape of its invariant measure, not a decomposition of it. It assumes what WP3 tests.

**Historical economic geography.** Wolf (2025), surveying the field since 1991, files the shock and multiple-equilibria studies — Davis and Weinstein and their successors — explicitly as an examination of the non-ergodicity implied by new economic geography.

## Where it is absent

The deep-roots critiques — Kelly (2020), Conley and Kelly, Casey and Klemp, Voth (2021), Arroyo Abad and Maurer (2021), Cioni, Federico and Vasta (2022), Hoffman and Guinnane (2025) — do not use the word. That is the gap the application may claim, and the only one.

## The operational precedent

Maseland (2021) re-runs 43 deep determinants at successive dates and classifies their trajectories as persistence, multiple equilibria or regime transition. The effects neither hold constant nor wane slowly; they rise or peak around 2000, which he reads as a hybrid of multiple equilibria and regime transitions. This is the closest thing in print to WP3's re-analysis, done without the vocabulary, and Part 2 must cite it. Maseland (2017), on the declining weight of colonial legacies in Africa, is its companion.

## Consequence for the text

Narrow the claim: the property has been named in path-dependence theory, in convergence empirics and in economic geography, but never as a condition on reading a deep-roots coefficient as within-society propagation. Cite Wolf 2025, Bernard and Durlauf 1996, Heckman 1991, Stachurski 2003 and Maseland 2021. The guardrail is [[Do not claim history-dependence as the novelty]].
