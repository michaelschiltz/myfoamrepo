# General average shares decisions where warichi shares hazards

The village looks like a hull, and for one purpose it is one. The hull makes "those with a stake" enumerable: the average is struck only among cargo owners, shipowner and passengers on this voyage, and no outsider bears anything. *Warichi* closes its boundary the same way, on the village. Both constitute a risk community by an inescapable shared exposure — you cannot step off a ship mid-voyage, and you cannot move a paddy off the floodplain.

But the hull's doctrine does not travel, and the failure is the finding. **General average requires a deliberate sacrifice for the common safety.** The master orders the jettison; the loss is chosen. Its corollary is strict: damage that merely befalls the venture is *particular* average and is **not** shared — it lies where it falls. No one chooses to sacrifice a parcel to save a village, and a river avulsion is nobody's decision.

So in maritime terms *warichi*'s triggering event is particular average — precisely the class the hull refuses to socialise. The metaphor imports a body of law that would exclude the case it is being used to illuminate.

**The sharper claim is therefore about the line, not the likeness.** If the general/particular distinction turns on deliberateness — you share what was chosen for the common good, you bear alone what merely befell you — then *warichi* crosses it. **General average socialises decisions; *warichi* socialises hazards.** In this project's reserved vocabulary that is exact: a hazard is a peril conceived as an event that may befall, carrying no distribution and no price, and it is hazards that the village shares.

That places the two forms on a common axis with a genuine disagreement between them: **does the cause of a loss determine who bears it?** The hull says yes — deliberate sacrifice is shared, accident is not. The village says no. Compare [[The amana-daman structure refuses to infer fault from loss]], where the same question is asked of an agent's conduct rather than of nature's, and answered the same way the village answers it. Three traditions, one axis, and the axis is causal attribution rather than pooling.

**Two disanalogies to keep, because the matrix already holds them.** The hull settles on arrival — `general_average DR1=single-event` — while the village never arrives and runs `fixed-cycle`. And general average identifies a sufferer and compensates him (`PY0=individual-indemnity`, `CN1=ex-post-assessment`), where *warichi* compensates nobody, holds no fund (`CN1=.NA`), and re-deals every position at once.

**The open question stays open.** General average is event-triggered and *warichi* is calendar-triggered. Either the schedule is a cheap proxy for event-triggering where verifying which parcel was destroyed is costly, or the institution is doing something besides sharing incidence ([[Warichi and general average spread the incidence of an irreversible loss]]). Brown's interval series is the evidence that would decide it.
