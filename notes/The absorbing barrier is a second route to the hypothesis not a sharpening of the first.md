# The absorbing barrier is a second route to the hypothesis not a sharpening of the first

Until 2026-11-21 the application stated its mechanism as multiplicative dynamics first, with the barrier as a refinement: Part 2 §0 said that where the failure mode is irreversible "the same logic sharpens into the suppression of variance and the avoidance of ruin". That is formally inaccurate. Pooling under a barrier pays by a different logic — it lowers each member's probability of ruin, with no change in expected gain and no Jensen gap ([[Under absorption pooling raises the probability of ruin to the power of the pool]]) — and that logic holds where gains merely add. **The hypothesis has two independent sufficient conditions, compounding and an absorbing loss, and the second does not depend on the first.**

## Why this matters more than any single citation

- **It extends the hypothesis to the material the application says the canon neglects.** Much of it is not plausibly multiplicative: *warichi* land and harvests, the *kō* and *mujin*, stipend households, peasant subsistence, the labour side of the *commenda*. If the argument rested on compounding, a referee could confine it to capital-intensive commerce and set the East Asian agrarian forms aside. With the barrier as an independent route, they are inside it.
- **It answers the relabelling charge with predictions no fixed utility mimics** — [[The utility mapping fails exactly where absorption begins]].
- **It is the thread through all three objectives.** Objective 1's noise-against-ruin prediction and the regime prediction ([[Pooling pays only where ventures grow on average]]); Objective 2's continuity against death, partition and ruin ([[Perpetual succession redefines the unit of survival from a line to a union of lines]]); Objective 3's absorbing states and survivorship ([[Persistence samples are conditioned on non-absorption]]). The published critiques engage none of it — [[The published critique never engages absorption]].

## What entered the text, 2026-11-21

- **Part 2 §0:** "and where the failure mode is irreversible (an *absorbing barrier*) pooling pays even where gains merely add, by lowering each member's chance of ruin (Vanhoyweghen, Verbeken, and Ginis 2026)."
- **Part 1 §1, after the cooperation sentence:** "Where losses are final, pooling pays even when gains merely add: it leaves expected wealth unchanged and lowers each member's chance of ruin (Vanhoyweghen, Verbeken, and Ginis 2026)."
- **Part 1 §1, the hypothesis:** "If pooling raises the time-average growth of its participants under multiplicative dynamics, and their survival where losses are final, then …"

## Cautions that bind

- **Not on one paper.** The route rests equally on classical ruin theory and on Galton–Watson, which no referee disputes; Vanhoyweghen et al. 2026 is cited for the decision ordering and boundary-dependence, not as the foundation. See [[Peters's growth criterion is the boundary-free case of the first-passage ordering]].
- **The ruin-theory objection now applies directly** — [[Absorption without ergodicity economics is just ruin theory]]. Its answer is the conjunction stated there: ruin supplies the mechanism of elimination, the time-average criterion what survivors were selected on, shielding the technology that moves the barrier.
- **Peters must own it.** His own 2026 paper concedes the log identity; this route is the escape. The application presents it as the programme's position, which only Peters can confirm [raise with Peters before submission].
- **"Single observation" is now two.** Part 1 §1 still opens the hypothesis paragraph "From this single observation"; with two routes the phrase should change [flagged to MS].
