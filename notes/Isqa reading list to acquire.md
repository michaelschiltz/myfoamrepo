# Isqa reading list to acquire

The Zotero library is well stocked on the *qirāḍ*/commenda side — Udovitch (1962, 1965, 1967, 1970), Pryor (1977), Astuti (1970), Lopez & Raymond, Khalilieh (2020), Aslanian (2007), Harris (2007 conference paper), Francesca (2026), Enkhbold (2019).

## Correction — the gap was overstated

An earlier version of this note claimed the library held **nothing** on the Jewish-law side. That was wrong, and the error is instructive. Semantic search was uninstalled during the session, so retrieval fell back to substring matching on title, creator and year. The queries used were `iska`, `isqa` and `usury Jewish` — strings that appear in none of the relevant titles. **Absence of a search hit was read as absence from the library.**

Already held, and missed:

- **Ackerman-Lieberman**, 'Contractual Partnerships in the Geniza…', *JESHO* 54 (2011) — key `ZJ24ZN53`. The very item flagged below as the top acquisition priority.
- **Ackerman-Lieberman**, 'Commercial Forms and Legal Norms in the Jewish Community of Medieval Egypt' (2012) — key `SB7JBG7S`. Not previously identified at all, and on the face of the title central to the argument.
- **Ackerman-Lieberman**, 'Legal Pluralism among the Court Records of Medieval Egypt' (2015) — key `HWEFEB6U`.
- **Hansmann, Kraakman & Squire**, 'Law and the Rise of the Firm' (2006) — held twice, as preprint `P5DA9TQ3` and article `L9WWJJDS`. Also 'Incomplete Organizations: Legal Entities and Asset Partitioning in Roman Commerce' `MC7D966U`, 'The New Business Entities in Evolutionary Perspective' `YKW8XKGB`, and Hansmann & Kraakman, 'The Essential Role of Organizational Law' `C7HH6ZEB`.
- **Goldberg**, 'The use and abuse of commercial letters from the Cairo Geniza' (2012) `JPJNAFHD`; 'The Courts, the Qadi, and the "People"' (2020) `3VCANJZN`.

**Method rule this yields:** a substring-only search returning nothing is evidence about the query string, not about the library. Before recording an absence, search by author surname alone, and restore semantic search first. The same reasoning error the project names in [[The deficit reading of absence is the scalar ranking in evidentiary form]] — absence read as deficit rather than as a property of the instrument.

## Priority — the live controversy

- ✅ **Ackerman-Lieberman, Phillip I.** *The Business of Identity: Jews, Muslims, and Economic Life in Medieval Egypt.* Stanford UP, 2014. ISBN 9780804785471; DOI `10.11126/stanford/9780804785471.001.0001`. Zotero key `TAU5WETW`. Argues, against Goitein and Udovitch, that Geniza Jews transacted in distinctively **Talmudic** rather than Islamic partnership forms. This is the evidentiary crux for [[The isqa refuses the agent's shield]] and the terminological caution in [[Isqa qirad and commenda are a liability-variable triad]].
- ✅ **Ackerman-Lieberman.** 'Contractual Partnerships in the Geniza…', *JESHO* 54 (2011), 646–676. DOI `10.1163/156852011x614000`, resolved via CrossRef. Held as **`ZJ24ZN53`**. My duplicate `9JC2D6DP` was merged into it on 2026-08-03, carrying the DOI and five tags across. The collision had gone undetected because `if_exists: file` matches on DOI and the original record carried none — a reminder that idempotency keys only work on the field they key on.

## Ribbit and the isqa

- ✅ **Gamoran, Hillel.** *Jewish Law in Transition: How Economic Forces Overcame the Prohibition against Lending on Interest.* HUC Press, 2008. Added via the Pittsburgh imprint ISBN 9780822963721; JSTOR `j.ctt16rpqsn`. Zotero key `ZTXHWGKN`. **Metadata came from Open Library — check publisher and date against the HUC imprint before citing.** Strong thesis — economic pressure overcoming doctrine — that [[The heter iska restores the fixed return by evidentiary barrier]] flags as contestable rather than assumable.
- **Elon, Menachem.** *Jewish Law: History, Sources, Principles.* JPS, 1994 — on legal fictions and the *heter ʿisqa*.
- **Soloveitchik, Haym.** 'Pawnbroking: A Study in Ribbit and of the Halakhah in Exile.' *PAAJR*, c. 1970–71. **Volume and year unverified.**
- **Stein, Siegfried.** On the development of Jewish interest law, *Historia Judaica* 17 (1955). **Citation unverified — I am working from memory here; confirm or drop.**
- **Bleich, J. David.** 'Hetter Iska', in *Contemporary Halakhic Problems*. Halakhic rather than historical, but sets out the modern mechanism cleanly.
- **Resnicoff, Steven H.** On the *heter ʿisqa* in American courts — a Cardozo faculty-articles piece exists; useful for the partnership-versus-disguised-loan question, which is the modern replay of the evidentiary-barrier argument.
