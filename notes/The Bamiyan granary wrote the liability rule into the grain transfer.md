# The Bamiyan granary wrote the liability rule into the grain transfer

**The distinction between the hand of trust and the hand of guaranty is not only a *fiqh* category in the Bamiyan Papers; it is a clause in the instrument.** A grain note of 1219 states that the recipient takes the grain *bi ḥukm-i ḍamānī*, "accepting full liability", so that its loss by flood or other force majeure stays with him. The same archive makes tax officials personally liable for arrears, and an eleventh-century lease puts any loss to the grain owed on the tenant. **Liability is allocated before the event, in writing, by the party who controls the asset — which is the doctrine of [[The amana-daman structure refuses to infer fault from loss]] run as administrative practice.**

## The documents

- **Ms. Heb. 8333.37 verso** (Rajab 616/September–October 1219; Azad 2025, Doc. 25): "I, ʿUthmān b. ʿUmar, have received by the hand of Jamāl al-Dīn Muḥammad b. Maḥmūd, *bi ḥukm-i ḍamānī*, [what has been] provided [to me] by … Jamāl al-Dīn: 100 *mann* of grain, [half of which is] 50 *mann*. Whenever he tells me, I shall pass it on to whomever he wants." Azad's gloss: *bi ḥukm-i ḍamānī* "makes the recipient liable for any loss of or damage to the goods changing hands … The opposite scenario, *bi ḥukm-i amānī*, relieves the recipient of any liability. So if grain transferred under *ḥukm-i ḍamānī* were to be ruined by a flood or other force majeure, the recipient would still need to pay back an equivalent amount" (Azad 2025, 280–281). The intermediary is a *shiḥna* officer who collects grain tax (*muḥaṣṣil-i ghalla*). ⚠️ Azad offers an alternative translation, "by the order of"; and this is the only occurrence of the root *ḍ-m-n* in the corpus (105–106 n. 44).
- **Ms. Heb. 8333.100** (early Shaʿbān 614/November 1217; Doc. 23): two *shiḥna* officers acknowledge they should have delivered ninety donkey-loads of grain and "have accepted the total amount outstanding" — 34 loads 80 *mann* of wheat and 40 loads 95 *mann* of barley — to be handed "without any excuse" to whoever presents the note within twenty days. **The collectors are in debt to the warehouse for what they have not yet delivered** (Azad 2025, 103–104).
- **Ms. Heb. 8333.36 verso** (614/1218; Doc. 18): a postscript has the *shiḥna* pay for the commander's donkey; Azad suggests he was "deemed liable for the extra expense given his original failure to recover the full tax payment" (259).
- **Ms. Heb. 8333.15** (430/1039): the tenant of three parcels declares, "Whatever happens to the provisions, whether it be a small or large (amount), and (to) the sulṭān's straw, I, Ḥusayn b. Ḥasan, shall pay (it) off" (Haim 2019, 433–434) — [[The Afghan corpora offer loss allocation in tenancy not commenda]].
- **No tax-farming (*ḍamān*) contracts** survive in the corpus, unlike the Egyptian papyri (Azad 2025, 105–106).

## What it adds

- **The documents do not leave liability to be inferred from the outcome.** Every one of these instruments fixes, before the loss, who will bear it. That is the state-verification side of [[Institutions substitute state-verification for outcome-inference as ruin exposure rises]]: the conduct question is replaced by a clause.
- **The *amānī* / *ḍamānī* pair gives the coding a native term.** Where a form's documents name the liability regime, the code can take the tradition's own word rather than the analyst's — the Haspelmath distinction recorded in `tags.md` under `comparative-concept`.
- ⚠️ **One attestation is not a regime.** The claim is that the vocabulary was available and used in a Bamiyan grain note, not that every transfer was so qualified. Most receipts state no liability clause at all.
