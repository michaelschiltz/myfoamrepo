# Bottomry separates from the sea loan on recourse not on collateral

The received gloss makes bottomry a sea loan secured on the ship. That is a description of the
English name, not of the institution. Schuster states the differentia twice over, and neither
statement is about which object was pledged.

> Der Unterschied zwischen dem antiken Seedarlehen und der Bodmerei liegt in der **Bedeutung des
> Pfandes**: Während beim antiken Seedarlehen die Vereinbarung eines Pfandes im **Ermessen der
> Parteien** stand, ist die Verpfändung (Verbodmung) von **Schiff, Fracht oder Ladung** für die
> Bodmerei charakteristisch. Die eigentliche Besonderheit der Bodmerei besteht darin, dass der
> Vertrag … lediglich eine **auf das Pfand beschränkte, dingliche Haftung** des Schuldners
> begründet; an das persönliche Vermögen des Reeders kann sich der Gläubiger grundsätzlich nicht
> halten.

Two variables, and the object of the pledge is a free parameter within both:

- **The pledge moves from optional to constitutive.** In the sea loan it is at the parties'
  discretion; in bottomry there is no contract without it.
- **Liability moves from personal to *in rem*.** *Reine Sachhaftung* — the creditor cannot reach
  the owner's estate.

The second is the load-bearing one, and it is a shielding claim rather than a security claim. The
borrower under a bottomry bond is protected by a barrier at the value of the pledge; the borrower
under a sea loan is not, once the peril window closes and the lender becomes an ordinary creditor.

## The attestation is early and it is not German

The dating matters because the institution is routinely treated as an early-modern or modern
narrowing. The **Statutes of Marseille of 1253/55**, III.5, already carry a pledge with the clause
*quod dictum pignus eat in viagio ad fortunam creditoris*: loss of the pledge discharges the debtor,
and on safe arrival his liability is confined to the pledge, personal liability arising only by
special agreement. A Hanserezess of **1418** legislates on it; the Dutch sea laws descending from the
*Rôles d'Oléron* carry it through the fifteenth century; the **Amsterdam Statute of 1527** supplies
the name *bomerie*; Hamburg 1603 gives the first comprehensive regulation.

So the institution precedes its northern name by two and a half centuries, and the name precedes the
nineteenth-century systematisation by three.

## Where this lands in the matrix

The discriminating characteristic is **`LS3`** — liability beyond contribution, the participant-side
barrier — and not `RB3`. On the sources the sea loan is `P`: shielded within the *dies praestitutus*
and on total loss, exposed once the ship arrives safely and the lender becomes a secured creditor
with recourse. Bottomry is `0` outright.

That is a pair of forms separating on a substantive value of a characteristic neither was coded
against, which is discriminant validity in the sense the project already uses — see
[[Count degrees of freedom not cells]]. It is also a reminder that a boundary asserted on one
characteristic can be carried by another entirely.

A structure neither cell holds: in the late medieval material the debtor could free himself of all
further liability by **abandoning the pledge** — *Abtretung des Pfandes* — a unilateral election
surviving into the Prussian ALR (II 8 §2427). That is neither automatic discharge on loss nor a
standing exposure, and it is the medieval ancestor of shipowner's limitation by abandonment.
