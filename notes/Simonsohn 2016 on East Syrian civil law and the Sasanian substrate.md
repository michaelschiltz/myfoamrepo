# Simonsohn 2016 on East Syrian civil law and the Sasanian substrate

Uriel Simonsohn, "The Introduction and Formalization of Civil Law in the East Syrian Church in the Late Sasanian–Early Islamic Periods," *History Compass* 14/5 (2016): 231–243. DOI 10.1111/hic3.12311. Sent by the author in reply to a project enquiry, July 2026.

## What it is, and what it is not

The article reconstructs how the East Syrian Church came to possess a body of civil law: first under late Sasanian rule, in an institutionally permeable setting with dense Christian–Zoroastrian ties, then under Islamic rule, where the eighth- and ninth-century codification of civil regulation answered the institutional and theological competition posed by Islam. Its subject is jurisdiction and communal boundary-making — the growing purview of the ecclesiastical judiciary — not commercial instruments.

The word **partnership does not occur in it. Nor does commercial, nor contract.** Simonsohn said as much in his covering letter, and the text bears him out. Anyone using this article to support a commercial-law genealogy is using it for something it does not do.

## What it does supply

- **Ishoʿbokht of Rewardashīr**, East Syrian metropolitan of Fars (d. c. end of the eighth century), wrote the first comprehensive East Syrian ecclesiastical civil law book — **in Middle Persian**, later translated into Syriac at the behest of the patriarch Timothy I. Edition: Sachau, *Syrische Rechtsbücher*, vol. 3, 1–201.
- **Kaufhold, 'Sources'** (305–306 and following) is the map of the East Syriac legal corpus — Bar Baḥrīz, Gabriel of Basra, Ibn al-Ṭayyib, Bar Brīkha — and the route to knowing which texts exist in which editions.
- **Weitz, 'Syriac Christians'** (74–75) notes the *Iranian orientation* of Ishoʿbokht's treatise, in both its sources, formal and customary, and its intended audience.
- **Macuch, 'Ein mittelpersischer terminus technicus im syrischen Rechtskodex des Išōʿboḫt und im sasanidischen Rechtsbuch,'** in *Studia Semitica necnon Iranica* (Wiesbaden: Harrassowitz, 1989), 149–160 — a Middle Persian technical term present in *both* Ishoʿbokht's Syriac code and the Sasanian law book. Simonsohn cites it for the proposition that Ishoʿbokht was well immersed in Zoroastrian legal principles.
- Abdisho bar Brīkha, *Ordo iudiciorum ecclesiasticorum* (1290), as the point by which an ecclesiastical civil order needed no apology.

## Why the Macuch citation matters most

It is the *Leitfehler* diagnostic already executed. A Middle Persian *terminus technicus* surviving inside a Syriac code, and traceable to the Sasanian law book, is a shared arbitrary particular — the strongest available evidence of textual dependence, and precisely the instrument the project's own transmission argument invokes. It has been done for one term. Whether it can be done for a partnership term is the question on which the whole Sasanian joint turns, and it is a defined, answerable research task rather than a gesture at secondary literature. See [[The qirad's antecedents are Sasanian not Sogdian]].

## Simonsohn's own caution, verbatim in substance

He judges the general proposition plausible — that East Syrian law could have served as a conduit for Sasanian legal concepts into the early Islamic legal environment — and separates it explicitly from the specific case: "Whether the specific genealogy of the *qirāḍ* can be demonstrated is, I suspect, a separate question, and one that will depend on the strength of the textual evidence." He declines to opine on partnership contracts or the Sogdian material, and points instead to Macuch, Kaufhold and Weitz. He offers to comment on the relevant pages of the draft.

Treat this as a productive negative of the de la Vaissière kind: it names the right people, names the right corpus, and withholds endorsement from the load-bearing claim. See [[The western family needs only plausible common descent not proven genealogy]].
