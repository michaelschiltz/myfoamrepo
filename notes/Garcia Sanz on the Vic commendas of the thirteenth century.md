# Garcia Sanz on the Vic commendas of the thirteenth century

Source note for three articles by Arcadi Garcia Sanz in *Ausa* (the Vic journal), all with appended Latin acts from the Curia Fumada and the Arxiu Capitular de Vic. All three PDFs are image-only and were OCR'd in this session (Spanish and Catalan models); numbers and Latin endings read from the OCR are to be checked against the page image before they enter a cell.

## The three pieces

- **"Un contrato de 'commenda' de 1221"**, *Ausa* 3, no. 27 (1959), 166–172 (Zotero LBK7RJDU). The commenda-deposit of money between kinsmen in 1221, with a profit clause. The Roman equation *commendare autem est deponere* returns with the twelfth- and thirteenth-century reception; the *Furs de València* (1240) and the *Costums de Tortosa* (1272) are the first codes to group deposit rules under the comanda (168). The commenda *ad usum maris* appears at Vic only from 1231 and is regulated by no Catalan code except the custom of the sea (168–169).
- **"Contractes comercials vigatans de principis del segle XIII"**, *Ausa* 43 (1963), 321–329 (VRZXUQGD). An edition of early Vic contracts. Doc. 12 licenses trade "in mari vel in terra sive in terra serracenorum" (cited by Fynn-Paul 2017, 91 n. 20). The 1240 act quoted by Martínez Gijón 1974, 266 (Pere Clocari for Berenguer de Villa, goods to Flanders or France, "ad tuum resecum et fortunam") is at 329.
- **"Tipos ausetanos de la 'commenda' en el siglo XIII"**, *Ausa* 3, no. 30 (1959), 284–293 (H9VVVKUT; volume and year from Martínez Gijón 1966, 380 n. 5, consistent with the running heads; RACO page not read). A typology from one Curia Fumada manual of 1271–1274, kept to one notary on purpose so that the types cannot be differences of hand.

## The typology (Tipos ausetanos)

Commenda of persons (apprenticeship, 1271, doc. 1); of immovables (castle tenure, doc. 2; sharecropping of land *a medias* for seven years, doc. 3); of movables, in three kinds (288):

1. **commenda-deposit**, returnable at the depositor's will or at a fixed term (doc. 4, 1273: "in pura comanda sive deposito", returnable at Easter);
2. **commercial commenda**, with deep variants: *ad usum maris* the capitalist takes three quarters of the profit and bears the whole capital risk; in local trade or craft the profit is commonly halved **and so is the loss** (doc. 5, 1273: two shoemakers take 100 s. "in pura comanda ad societatem" for wine and their craft, profit halved after the capital is deducted, and "si diminutio in dicto capitali venerit mitamus nos et tu talem partem qualem accepimus in lucro", for one year, *quisque in solidum*);
3. **livestock commenda** *ad parceriam* (doc. 6, 1274), structurally the same as the commercial one.

The common element is fidelity *intuitu personae* and nothing else (289–290): notaries used *commendare* for any trust relation the incipient technique had no name for.
