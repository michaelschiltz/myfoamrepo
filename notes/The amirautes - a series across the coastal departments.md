# The *amirautés* — a series across the coastal departments

A **series note, not a repository note.** The local admiralty courts are in *série B* of the *archives départementales* in every coastal department, and the central Amirauté de France is `Z/1d/1`–`Z/1d/141` at [[Archives nationales - Paris and Pierrefitte]].

## What an *amirauté* held

Maritime jurisdiction: **average and its distribution, bottomry, insurance disputes, wrecks, prizes, ship registration, crew engagements, and the *rapports de mer***, the masters' sworn declarations after a voyage.

That list is the loss census's subject matter written out as a court's docket. The *rapport de mer* in particular is the French counterpart of the Amsterdam *scheepsverklaring* — and the average adjudications at [[Averijgrossen - the general average adjudications]] show what a run of such material can support.

## Why this is the single most promising French lead

**Because the uniform classification makes it a sweep rather than a search.** Calvados, Finistère, Pas-de-Calais, the Côtes-d'Armor and others all describe their *amirauté* fonds in série `B`, with the same letter and comparable structure. **The same question can be put to a dozen jurisdictions at once**, which is the configuration the comparative method most wants and almost never gets: one legal tradition, one procedural frame, many independent local practices.

[[Divergent arbitrary particulars are the independence diagnostic]] is the rule that makes it valuable. Where a dozen *amirautés* under one ordinance diverge in their incidental practice, the divergence measures local drafting habit — which is the baseline against which a **cross-national** divergence has to be judged. **Without that baseline the project has no way to tell a tradition apart from a habit**, and France is where the baseline can be measured.

## What has to be established

⚠️ How complete the series are department by department; whether any are imaged (assume not); and whether the ordinance of 1681 — the *Ordonnance de la marine*, which codified average and insurance for the whole kingdom — makes the local practices too uniform to be informative. **That last is the risk**: a single royal ordinance may have flattened exactly the variation this design needs. Test it on two departments before designing anything around it.
