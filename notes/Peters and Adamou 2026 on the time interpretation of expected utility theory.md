# Peters and Adamou 2026 on the time interpretation of expected utility theory

Source note. The programme's own answer to the charge that it relabels Bernoulli, and the answer is to concede the identity and generalise it.

**Bibliographic facts.** Peters, Ole, and Alexander Adamou. 2026. "The Time Interpretation of Expected Utility Theory." *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society A* 384 (2334): 20250166. DOI `10.1098/rsta.2025.0166`. Zotero `QQBGLQSL`; citekey `petersTimeInterpretationExpected2026`. Received 18 November 2025, accepted 2 March 2026. Supplementary code `10.6084/m9.figshare.c.8735002`. Read in full from page images; no text layer after p. 1.

## What it does

- **§3, the central result (p. 7).** Growth optimality and expected-utility maximisation coincide when a time-average growth rate exists, it is ergodic, and the utility function is the ergodicity transformation, $u = v$. Utility is required to follow Brownian motion with drift (eq. 3.16), reached by a chain of sufficient conditions (stationary independent increments, hence Lévy, hence with continuous paths Brownian), not necessary ones. Offered as an axiomatisation of Laplacian expected-utility theory in dynamical terms, an alternative to von Neumann and Morgenstern.
- **§4, Theorem 4.1.** Any invertible utility with twice-differentiable inverse is growth-optimal under the class of dynamics given by eq. 4.2. Linear utility gives additive Brownian motion, Bernoulli's logarithm geometric Brownian motion, Cramer's square root a CIR-like process, and the isoelastic family eq. 4.10.
- **§5, the inverse problem.** For $dx = a\,dt + b\,dW$ a utility exists only if $a(x) = b(x)\,(\alpha/\beta + b'(x)/2)$ (eq. 5.7). Worked example: a convex exponential utility that conventional theory calls risk-seeking is growth-optimal under its matching dynamic.
- **§6.** Wealth distributions under bounded utility, handled by keeping only the trajectories that never reach the bound.
- **§7.** Expected-utility theory as "an eighteenth-century patch to a seventeenth-century model"; the testable hypothesis that wealth dynamics predict risk preferences, with Meder et al. 2021 and Vanhoyweghen et al. 2022 as the evidence.

## What it does for the project

The paper supplies the sentence Part 1 §1 needed: under purely multiplicative dynamics the time-average criterion ranks gambles exactly as logarithmic utility does, and the difference lies in where the transformation comes from. It is cited there with Bernoulli. Its testable hypothesis is the individual-level form of the project's societal one — that a change of dynamic within one tradition should change how risk is handled without any change in what members want.

## What it does not do, filed as claims

- **It concedes more than it resists.** [[Every utility function is growth-optimal under some dynamic]] — the theorem leaves the programme's distinct content in the observability of dynamics.
- **Its correspondence holds for a narrow class.** Capital-plus-labour wealth fails eq. 5.7; see [[The commenda splits a dynamic that has no utility representation]].
- **Its central comparison is within one dynamic.** Criterion 3.7 ranks alternatives under a single transform, which is the limitation already filed at [[EE cannot rank across dynamics and the comparative project must not need it to]]; the paper does not state it.
- **Its isoelastic cases have boundaries the paper does not handle.** Footnote 1, p. 11 ("ranges must be adjusted appropriately") leaves Brownian motion in a half-line utility unresolved; see [[Among isoelastic transforms only the logarithm has no boundary]].
- **§6's survival conditioning is an ensemble operation**, and its claim (p. 11) that bounded cases leave genuine Brownian realisations is false; see [[Conditioning on survival is an ensemble operation]].
- **The correspondence ends where absorption begins**: [[The utility mapping fails exactly where absorption begins]].

## The intellectual history is thin

§1b repeats Peters 2011 on Bernoulli substituting subjective utility for wealth. It does not engage the *Specimen*'s own statement of the rule as a probability-weighted geometric mean of outcomes, the textual ground of the "EE is Bernoulli" objection, nor the fact that Bernoulli's problem is a single gamble where an argument from repetition has no purchase. Menger 1934, Latané 1959 and Breiman 1961 (cited only as a probability textbook) are unengaged; Samuelson 1979, "Why We Should Not Make Mean Log of Wealth Big though Years to Act Are Long," the standard objection to the identification the paper makes, is absent. The supplied genealogy runs Cardano, Whitworth 1870, Kelly 1956, Lopes 1981, Boltzmann. The vault's standing instruction holds: cite Peters for the framing, never for the intellectual history — [[Toda's citation allegations must be checked before we cite Peters]].
