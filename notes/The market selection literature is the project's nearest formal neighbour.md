# The market selection literature is the project's nearest formal neighbour

The vault holds nothing on market selection, and it is the literature a referee will reach for on hearing the selective claim. Not the ergodicity critics — [[Time-average optimization is a survivorship property not an intention]] is not a decision theory, and the objections catalogued at [[The published critique never engages absorption]] are aimed at one. The relevant neighbours are the economists who asked whether selection favours accurate beliefs, and they asked it in *Econometrica*.

## The literature

**Blume, Lawrence, and David Easley. 2006. "If You're So Smart, Why Aren't You Rich? Belief Selection in Complete and Incomplete Markets." *Econometrica* 74 (4): 929–966.** The central item. Asymptotic properties of consumption allocations in a stochastic general-equilibrium model with heterogeneous consumers, testing the **market selection hypothesis**: that markets favour traders with more accurate beliefs.

Two results matter here.

**In Pareto-optimal allocations, survival is determined entirely by discount factors and beliefs.** Not by risk preferences, and not by anything the trader chooses. The market selects for Bayesians with the truth in the support of their prior, and among Bayesians by the size of the parameter space.

**With incomplete markets, the market selection hypothesis fails.** Payoff functions matter for long-run survival, and accuracy of belief no longer guarantees it.

Adjacent and unread: **"Market selection and survival of investment strategies," *Journal of Mathematical Economics* (c. 2004)**; **"Survival in speculative markets," *Journal of Economic Theory* 181 (2019): 1–43**; and the evolutionary-finance programme of **Evstigneev, Hens and Schenk-Hoppé**. **All bibliographic details in this note are from search results rather than from the papers; verify every one before it reaches a footnote** [verify].

## Why it is an ally

The incomplete-markets failure is the useful result, and it cuts in the project's favour.

Premodern markets are radically incomplete — that is nearly a definition of the material. If the selection hypothesis fails under incompleteness in the modern formal treatment, then **"the market selected the efficient form" is unavailable as a mechanism for the periods the project covers**, and unavailable on the discipline's own terms rather than on ours. That is a formal reinforcement of [[Whiggish history of finance names the ascent narrative]] and of [[The corporation is not the omega - the naties are an undisplaced niche]]: the ascent narrative needs selection-for-efficiency, and the theory says selection-for-efficiency requires completeness the period did not have.

The Kelly connection is also direct. Betting one's beliefs, in this literature, reduces to maximising the expected logarithm of an appropriately defined relative growth rate — the same object the project uses, arrived at independently by economists working on a different question [verify].

## Why it is also a standard we must meet

Two disciplines follow, and both bind.

**Blume and Easley derive survival from primitives.** Discount factors and beliefs are stated, and survival is a theorem. The project asserts survival-tracking-the-time-average and does not derive it. That asymmetry will be noticed, and the honest reply is the one at [[Absorption without ergodicity economics is just ruin theory]]: the derivation exists — Kelly, Breiman — for repeated multiplicative exposure, and the project's work is to show that institutional trajectories are of that shape, which is [[Restate the cost puzzle as a question about a trajectory]] and its requirement that the multiplicative variable be named in each case.

**The mechanisms are not the same, and conflating them would be a serious error.** Market selection operates by **relative impoverishment**: a trader's wealth share goes to zero, the trader vanishes from the allocation, and nothing is absorbed — the state space is not decomposed. The project's mechanism is **elimination**: the entity crosses a barrier and ceases, per [[An absorbing barrier breaks ergodicity by construction]] and [[Galton-Watson is the extinction branch Peters named and did not develop]].

That is the same distinction, for the third time in this cluster, between asymptotic impoverishment and absorption. Its recurrence across the ergodicity critics, the market-selection literature and the branching case is either strong evidence that the distinction is the project's real contribution, or a sign that we keep finding it because we keep looking for it. The test is whether the two mechanisms ever make different predictions on the same material — and they do: a form that is out-competed survives in reduced circumstances and remains codable, while a form that is absorbed leaves only [[Refusals are observations of the filter not inferences from survivors]]. **The *naties* are the first case to run this on** [verify].
