# Sort the persistence objections by what they cost the referee

The vault now holds four statements of one defect: **a cross-section stands in for a history only under conditions nobody checks.** The number is not the point and should never be the headline. What matters is what each of them asks the reader to concede before it works, because on that criterion they do not sort evenly.

## Three cost nothing

Each of these is conventional methodology in some field the referee already accepts, and none of them requires a line of ergodicity economics.

- **The inert character.** [[Deep-roots variables are symplesiomorphies]]. A shared ancestral character cannot group, so the regressor may be real, precisely estimated and evidentially empty. Cladistics is unremarkable in its home field.
- **The collider.** [[Persistence samples are conditioned on non-absorption]]. Where the treatment has an absorbing outcome the estimation sample is conditioned on a variable downstream of the treatment, and the bias is unsigned without a model of absorption. This is ordinary causal inference.
- **The resolution bound.** [[Attractor selection bounds what a persistence design can resolve]]. Under increasing returns the terminal state records the partition and nothing finer, so a continuous deep-roots regressor is over-resolved. It runs on the path-dependence literature the persistence papers cite approvingly on their own account.

## One costs the framework

**The squeeze.** [[A significant persistence coefficient is evidence against its own licensing condition]]. A persistence result asserts that a partition has gone uncrossed over the design's horizon, which is the space-for-time licence's failure condition; stated as weak breaking, it is an inequality between relaxation time and horizon.

This is the strongest of the four and the least portable, because it is built on the ergodicity criterion itself and falls with it. **It should be presented last, not led with.** Leading with it hands the falsifiability charge against the framework a second target, and forfeits the property that makes the other three worth having.

## Sort by dependency, not by provenance

The distinction the ordering turns on, and the one most easily muddied. The absorption objection was *found* by thinking in this framework's terms — its own note calls it native to the framework rather than borrowed — and it does not *rest* on them. Where an argument came from is not what a referee is being asked to accept.

Provenance is worth recording, because novelty is a claim about priority and this project takes priority seriously. But it belongs in the note's `Source`, not in the ordering of the argument.

## Why four is a structure rather than a pile

A referee who meets four arguments for one conclusion will reasonably suspect that if the first worked the other three would be unnecessary. The answer is that they attack four different objects, and any audit of a regression would visit all four in turn:

- the **regressor** — inert character
- the **sample** — conditioning on non-absorption
- the **resolution of the estimand** — attractor selection
- the **licence** — the squeeze

Objective 3 is therefore a four-position audit of a design class, not four attempts at a single argument. Written that way it reads as systematic; written as a list it reads as insistence.

One precision keeps the independence honest. The resolution bound is the dynamical form of the *polarity* sub-claim in [[Deep-roots variables are symplesiomorphies]] — that reversal counterfeits the ancestral state, two histories sharing one terminal state — and not of that note's inert-character claim, which remains independent of it. See [[Retrodiction fails on non-invertibility not on induction]].
