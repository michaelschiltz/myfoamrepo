# *Muhallefât* and *müsadere* — confiscation as an archive

**The Ottoman state confiscated the property of office-holders and tax contractors — *müsadere* — and the confiscation produced an inventory, the *muhallefât*.** The practice ran to **1839, when the Gülhane rescript renounced it**; the standard study is **Yasin Arslantaş, "Confiscation by the ruler: a study of the Ottoman practice of müsadere, 1700s–1839" (PhD, London School of Economics, 2017)**, which finds confiscation aimed at office-holders and tax farmers and selected by timing, location, the holder's bargaining power and the nature of the assets. Individual *muhallefât* registers of named pashas are published as articles.

## Why it belongs in this project's account of failure

**It is generator two of [[The record of non-survivors survives where failure was administered]]: the state as claimant.** The record exists because someone with power had a claim on an estate at the moment it ended — and the estate ended *because* of the claim.

**The collective case is already in the vault.** [[Orta sandigi records - the guild and Janissary chests]]: after the abolition of 1826 the chests' assets went to the Treasury, **and the liquidation created the surviving series** — inventories, auction registers, and a loan book surviving largely as the debts outstanding in June 1826. **A corporate body's whole financial record survives as the confiscator's paperwork.**

## The caution, which is sharper here than for a bankruptcy chamber

**A confiscation inventory is a valuation made by the party taking the assets**, at the moment of taking, for the purpose of taking. **It is not the institution's account of itself, and it is not neutral**: Arslantaş's finding that confiscation was *selective* means the surviving inventories are a sample chosen by the confiscator's calculation, not by anything resembling chance. **Anything coded from such a series is a cell about confiscation** — the standing check recorded for the Janissary chest.

⚠️ **The archival series were not established in this pass**: the *muhallefât* registers sit in the Ottoman State Archives' finance sections, and the precise series names and extents were not verified. That is the first thing to fix if this is ever used.
