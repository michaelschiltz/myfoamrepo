# Prefectural and municipal archives — the *monjokan* network

**Public archives in Japan are a recent and uneven layer.** The **Public Archives Act of 1987** (公文書館法) made preservation of public records as historical material a public duty; prefectures had begun founding archives from the 1950s and 1960s. Municipal archives are fewer still — the Japanese Wikipedia list gives some forty, among them Kawasaki (1984), Osaka (1988), Sapporo (2013) and Sendai (2023). ⚠️ The number of prefectures with an archive was not established in this pass.

## What they hold for the census

**Two kinds of material under one roof.** The prefecture's or city's own modern records; and **early-modern private documents donated or deposited by local families** — the *jikata* and *shōka* monjo — plus materials collected for the prefectural history. Hiroshima's prefectural archive, for example, lists several hundred document collections by family name. **The second kind is the census's material, and each archive catalogues it its own way.**

## The honest assessment

**No single note can cover these, and at seed depth none should try.** The rule is the one [[Rijksarchief in Belgie - the state archives network]] and the French *départementales* already taught: **find the fonds through the census row's source, then find the archive**, never the reverse. The survey found one negative case worth recording: **no Mitarai collection appears in the Hiroshima Prefectural Archives' list**, so the documents behind the three Mitarai *kō* rows are somewhere else. ⚠️ Where is not established. *[Corrected 2026-10-21: placed. Katō read them at the 広島県豊田郡豊町教育委員会 (Katō 1998, 132–133: 本稿において利用した史料は、ことわりのないかぎり、広島県豊田郡豊町教育委員会所蔵のもの). Toyo was absorbed into Kure on 20 March 2005, so the custodian is presumably now Kure's board of education ⚠️. The negative case keeps its lesson in sharper form: a town board of education, not the prefectural archive, held a port town's merchant papers, and only the row's source could have said so.]* *[Same day: the prefectural archive's 呉市 古文書 page lists no 豊町 or board-of-education collection, so the papers did not pass to it at the merger. 『豊町史』資料編 (豊町教育委員会, 1993; 977 pp.; NDL GC223-H75) is print only. Katō cites it for a 文政元年 kō dispute (414–415), so it prints at least some kō material; whether it prints the thirteen associations' rules is not established. 『豊町史』本文編 (2000) is digitised at the NDL, readable on its premises only.
