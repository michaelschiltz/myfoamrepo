# Prefectural and municipal archives — the *monjokan* network

**Public archives in Japan are a recent and uneven layer.** The **Public Archives Act of 1987** (公文書館法) made preservation of public records as historical material a public duty; prefectures had begun founding archives from the 1950s and 1960s. Municipal archives are fewer still — the Japanese Wikipedia list gives some forty, among them Kawasaki (1984), Osaka (1988), Sapporo (2013) and Sendai (2023). ⚠️ The number of prefectures with an archive was not established in this pass.

## What they hold for the census

**Two kinds of material under one roof.** The prefecture's or city's own modern records; and **early-modern private documents donated or deposited by local families** — the *jikata* and *shōka* monjo — plus materials collected for the prefectural history. Hiroshima's prefectural archive, for example, lists several hundred document collections by family name. **The second kind is the census's material, and each archive catalogues it its own way.**

## The honest assessment

**No single note can cover these, and at seed depth none should try.** The rule is the one [[Rijksarchief in Belgie - the state archives network]] and the French *départementales* already taught: **find the fonds through the census row's source, then find the archive**, never the reverse. The survey found one negative case worth recording: **no Mitarai collection appears in the Hiroshima Prefectural Archives' list**, so the documents behind the three Mitarai *kō* rows are somewhere else. ⚠️ Where is not established.
