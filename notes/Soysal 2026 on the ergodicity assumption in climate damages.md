# Soysal 2026 on the ergodicity assumption in climate damages

Source note. Objective 3's argument, made for climate econometrics, by a researcher at the host institution. The closest published analogue of the WP3 critique, and therefore both an ally and a priority risk.

**Bibliographic facts.** Soysal, Emilie Rosenlund. 2026. "The Ergodicity Assumption in Statistical Approaches to Climate Damages." *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society A* 384 (2334): 20250165. DOI `10.1098/rsta.2025.0165`. Zotero `2HB5FUZW`; citekey `soysalErgodicityAssumptionStatistical2026`. Opinion piece, LML. Received 18 January 2026, accepted 13 March 2026. Keywords include space-for-time substitution and generalisability. Supplementary material `10.6084/m9.figshare.c.8834929`. Pp. 1–8 of 11 read from page images; conclusion and references unread.

## What it does

Regression-based damage projections assume that a relationship estimated across space, or over short horizons, describes how one location's economy responds as its climate changes over time. Soysal calls that assumption ergodicity (mean ergodicity, Box 1) and shows where it enters by three routes.

- **Space-for-time substitution (§2a).** A cross-sectional estimator stands in for the time-series estimator only if the relevant second moments are ergodic; figure 1 shows the two fits diverging. The failure is related to Simpson's paradox but is not omitted conditioning on time: averaging across places and along one place are different operations.
- **Generalisability (§2b).** Panel models avoid the direct substitution but still average location-specific temporal responses into an ensemble predictor, which describes no single location unless the responses are homogeneous.
- **Unexplored state space (§2c).** Projection beyond the historically observed range is extrapolation, and trade linkages mean regions never changed climate simultaneously in the sample. Abrams et al.: goods "can be shipped across space, but not through time".

## What it does for the project

- **The absorbing-state argument is WP3's mita argument in another field.** A region that floods becomes uninhabitable, and misclassifying whether an absorbing state has been reached is sufficient on its own to invalidate the substitution (p. 5). Compare [[The mita boundary separates two components not two points on a trajectory]].
- **Its concession is the one Part 2 makes for Dell.** Where the object is the ensemble-average level at a future date, ergodicity is not needed, only time-invariance of the cross-sectional relation (p. 5). That is the logic of the sentence in Part 2 that Dell's estimand, a level difference, survives — see [[The mita critiques establish five nulls and none is the estimand]].
- **It cites the ecological space-for-time debate**, as Part 2 does through Pickett and Johnson and Miyanishi. The two texts arrived at the same precedent independently.
