# Transferable claims reconcile lock-in with member exit

Capital lock-in traps the member. Transferability untraps him **without releasing the capital**. That is the device which resolves the tension in [[Entity shielding and owner shielding protect different trajectories]], and it is why transferable claims belong in the corporate-form bundle rather than being an incidental convenience of developed markets.

The mechanism is exact. Lock-in forbids *withdrawal* — the member cannot pull his contribution out, which is what keeps the fund whole through a bad draw. Transferability permits *sale* — he exits at market value while the capital stays where it is. The entity's trajectory is protected and the member's liquidity is restored, by the same clause.

Without transferability, lock-in is confiscation deferred.

## The coded contrast

- ***waqf khayri*** — `CI2=1` (inalienable), `CI3=0`, `CI4=0`. Maximal entity continuity purchased at the total extinction of the member's exit. There is no claim to sell because there are no members, only beneficiaries.
- ***Bazacle*** — `TS1=1`, `CI2=P`, **`CI3=1`**. Entity continuity *with* alienation, and alienation **without the consent of the other *pariers***, documented from the Castel enfeoffment of 1182, which already schedules transaction tax on halves, thirds and quarters. Both barriers addressed at once, in the twelfth century.
- ***asiento de avería*** — `CI2=1`, `CI3=P`. Lock-in firm; transferability present but its extent unresolved pending Sayous (1902), which is precisely the question of whether the *partícipe* could actually exit.

## Two consequences

**It reframes the Harris objection.** Harris rejects the Toulouse mills partly because "their shares were not traded in impersonal stock exchanges." But the exchange is a *venue*; what does the reconciling work is the *alienability of the claim*, which he concedes. On this reading the mills solved the problem the joint-stock company is usually credited with solving, four centuries earlier and without a bourse. See [[The Roman universitas makes the JSC case parallelism not convergence]].

**It weakens the strong form of the trade-off.** If transferability jointly satisfies both barriers, then entity protection and member protection are not straightforwardly in tension — they are in tension *only where claims are not alienable*. The trade-off is a property of a subset of forms, not a general law, and the general claim should be stated in that weaker form.

## Where this is soft

Alienability is necessary but plainly not sufficient: a claim that can legally be sold but has no buyer leaves the member as trapped as one that cannot. `CI3` records the legal permission, not the existence of a market — and the matrix has no characteristic for market depth. The *asiento*'s "chronically hard to assemble" capital is the warning case. Whether to add such a characteristic, or to treat it as outside the form and belonging to the environment, is unresolved and bears on [[Count degrees of freedom not cells]].
