# The Ragusan chancery and notarial series

At [[Drzavni arhiv u Dubrovniku]]. ⚠️ Reference codes and date ranges to be transcribed from the archive's published fonds list.

## The series

The Ragusan chancery kept its business in named, continuous registers, and the names are the finding aid:

- ***Diversa Cancellariae*** — miscellaneous acts before the chancery
- ***Diversa Notariae*** — miscellaneous notarial acts
- ***Debita Notariae*** — **registered debts**, which is where credit instruments sit
- ***Testamenta Notariae*** — wills
- ***Lamenta*** — complaints and criminal process
- ***Acta Consilii Rogatorum*** — the Senate, the Republic's governing council, with a published inventory

## Why this series structure is worth naming

**Because it is a registration system, not a notary's private protocol.** A Ragusan obligation was entered in a public register kept by the state, under a heading that says what kind of act it is.

That places Ragusa with the systems this survey has flagged as unusually findable — the Scottish registers of deeds at [[National Records of Scotland]], the Lübeck *Niederstadtbuch*, the Leuven and Antwerp *schepenregisters* — and **against** the notarial-protocol systems of Genoa, Barcelona and Amsterdam, where an instrument is findable only through the notary who drew it.

**The practical consequence is large.** *Debita Notariae* is a series of registered obligations *classified as such*. A question like "how were maritime obligations drafted in Ragusa" has a series address here, and in Genoa it has a research programme.

⚠️ **The coding consequence is the one already stated at [[Stockholms stadsarkiv and the Swedish town records]]** and it applies again: a registered act and a notarial minute are not the same kind of record, and a difference between a Ragusan row and a Genoese one may be a difference in what the register records. **This is now the fifth system of its kind in the survey and the rule should be treated as general.**
