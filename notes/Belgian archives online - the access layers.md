# Belgian archives online — the access layers

**The expectation going in was that Belgium would compare badly with the Netherlands. On infrastructure it does, and by a wide margin. On holdings it does not, and the exception is large enough to reverse the judgement for this project's purposes.** Both halves are set out here because the contrast is the finding.

## Where Belgium is worse, and it is not close

**The licence and access posture.** The Rijksarchief offers free online access to millions of digitised documents through its AGATHA platform, prioritising *ruggengraatreeksen* — backbone series of high research value — but **registration is required to view the images**, and no CC0 dedication, OAI-PMH endpoint or bulk-download route is advertised. Against the Dutch position in [[Dutch archives online - the access layers]] — CC0 inventories *and* scans, OAI-PMH harvesting, 300 dpi downloads, free scanning on demand — this is a reading room with a login, not a reusable corpus.

**The full-text layer is an order of magnitude thinner.** BelgicaPress holds **over 1.2 million** historical newspaper pages; Delpher holds **over 18 million**. See [[BelgicaPress and KBR - the thin full-text layer]].

**Custody is split more ways than in any country surveyed so far.** Federal state archives ([[Rijksarchief in Belgie - the state archives network]]), municipal archives that are often richer than the state ones for this period, a university documentation centre holding the religious archives ([[KADOC - religious archives at Leuven]]), and private-archive registers (Archiefbank Vlaanderen, Archiefpunt) that exist precisely because so much is in private hands. Spain's devolution problem and Italy's state/non-state problem are both present at once.

**And one archive is simply gone.** [[Archief Ieper - one charter and five accounts]] records a loss with no parallel in the Dutch, Spanish or Italian sets.

## Where Belgium is better, and it is the part that matters

**[[Insolvente Boedelskamer - Antwerp]].** 204 linear metres, over 3,000 fonds, from 1518 — the complete archives of more than 150 merchant firms, preserved because the firms went bankrupt and the city impounded their papers. Its own UNESCO nomination calls it a *crucial complement* to the VOC archives. For this project's questions about the creditor limb and about absorption it is the **best-suited single fonds found anywhere in the European survey**, and the Dutch equivalent at [[Desolate Boedelkamer - Amsterdam]] is at present only a hypothesis beside it.

**[[Itinera Nova - the Leuven aldermen's registers]]** is a genuine layer-3 corpus — volunteer-transcribed, full-text searchable, free. Spain has none. Italy has none.

## The rule this pass produces

**Digital infrastructure and archival holdings are independent variables, and this is the country that proves it.** The temptation after the Dutch pass is to read ease of access as a proxy for richness; Belgium is worse served and holds better material. [[The VOC-EIC digital asymmetry is an access regime not a survival difference]] states the principle; until now every country surveyed happened to confirm it in the same direction, which made it easy to mistake for a description rather than a warning.
