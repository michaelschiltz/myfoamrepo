# The British Newspaper Archive and the commercial full-text layer

<https://www.britishnewspaperarchive.co.uk/> — a partnership between the British Library and **Findmypast**, a commercial genealogy company.

**Over fifty million** digitised newspaper pages, full-text searchable — **by subscription**, with a small free tranche.

## The five-country table, now complete

| Corpus | Scale | Terms |
|---|---|---|
| Delpher (NL) | 18M+ | Free; 1618–1879 downloadable as open data |
| **British Newspaper Archive (UK)** | **50M+** | **Subscription, commercial partner** |
| Deutsches Zeitungsportal (DE) | 1671–1950 | Free |
| BelgicaPress (BE) | 1.2M+ | Free |
| RetroNews (FR) | large | Subscription |

**Britain has the largest corpus in Europe and the most commercial terms.** Three of five national libraries give the press away; the two that charge are the French and the British, and the British charge through a genealogy firm.

## The consequence for the project, stated once and for all

**No frequency claim may cross these corpora**, which has been the rule since [[The VOC-EIC digital asymmetry is an access regime not a survival difference]]. What the completed table adds is that **the variation is now measured**: five neighbouring states, five arrangements, a forty-fold range in scale and two different pricing models, and none of it is about what was printed.

**Anyone assembling a European press corpus is assembling a sample of five library policies.** That sentence should appear in the methods section rather than in a footnote.

⚠️ The British corpus's *composition* — which titles, which regions, which decades — is set by what Findmypast judged commercially worth scanning. That is a selection rule of exactly the kind [[The QDL corpus is conditioned on its funder's geography]] describes, and it is less openly stated than Qatar's.
