# Van Dam's *Beschryvinge* — the VOC described by its own *advocaat*

Pieter van Dam (1621–1706), *advocaat* to the VOC for some four decades, was commissioned by the Heren XVII in 1693 to compile a guide and reference work tracing the Company from its foundation to the present; he finished in 1701. It was written for the directors and not for publication. That is the whole of its evidentiary interest: it is the Company's own legal officer stating what the Company's constitution *is*, to the men who governed under it, with no external audience to be managed.

## Bibliographic identity

Pieter van Dam, *Beschryvinge van de Oostindische Compagnie*, ed. F. W. Stapel and C. W. Th. baron van Boetzelaer van Asperen en Dubbeldam, 7 vols (The Hague: Martinus Nijhoff, 1927–1954). Published in the *Rijks Geschiedkundige Publicatiën*, Grote Serie, nos **63, 68, 74, 76, 83, 87, 96** — Deel 1.1, 1.2, 2.1, 2.2, 2.3, 3, 4 respectively. Nijhoff reprint 1977.

**Orthographic trap.** The Huygens landing page and site title render it *Beschrijvinge*; the RGP edition and the standard citation form use *Beschryvinge*. Both must be searched in catalogues and in Zotero. Standardise on *Beschryvinge*, record *Beschrijvinge* as an alias.

## Contents

Chronological and thematic: the founding; governance and the sale of Asian goods in Europe; intra-Asian trade and the conquest and administration of territory; the governing bodies, administration of justice, army and navy in Asia; the growth of the settlements and the abuses attending them; ecclesiastical affairs. The **fifth book, on the disputes with the English, is lost** — the gap falls precisely on the Anglo-Dutch comparative material one would most want.

## What it supplies that the charter does not

The standard route to the VOC as corporate form runs through the 1602 charter, the Le Maire episode and the 1623 renewal. Van Dam is a different kind of evidence: a **near-contemporary internal articulation of the settlement the charter had become after ninety years of operation**. Against the component decomposition — lock-in, transferable claims, entity shielding, legal personality, perpetual succession — the questions are:

- The transition from the 1602 subscription to permanent capital, and what the directors took the lock-in to *license*. Compare [[Lock-in and outcome-coupling are distinct bindings]]: van Dam should show whether the two bindings were understood as one thing or two.
- The mechanics of transfer and registration, and whether depersonalisation of the claim is described as a design feature or merely as practice.
- Recourse against Company obligations, and the standing of the chambers relative to the whole — is the Company one entity or six in his account? This is where the secondary literature leans hardest on inference and where [[Entity-shielding]]'s asset-partitioning facet either does or does not get an explicit contemporary statement.
- The charter renewals as the Company's own account of continuity, bearing on [[Perpetuity splits into adaptation and petrification]].

## The sharpest use: stated rationale

[[The coding commons must record stated rationale not only component presence]] demands a mandatory field carrying the actors' own account of what a component was *for*, coded from the source's vocabulary. On the Iberian side that field is richly populated — [[The depersonalised share was produced by honour not by pooling logic]] rests entirely on the *asiento*'s own documents saying why the share was made impersonal. **On the VOC side the field is thin, and van Dam is the obvious place to fill it.** If the Company's counsel gives a reason for permanent capital, for the transferability of *actiën*, for the partition of liability, that reason enters the grid as data of the same evidentiary rank as Hierro Anibarro's *asiento* material — and the two pathways to anonymity become directly comparable rather than assumed identical. This is the single highest-value read in the seven volumes.

## Guards

1. It is a **1690s retrospective compilation**, not a documentary series. Van Dam works from the archive but selects, and a reference work has a purpose. Do not read the settlement of 1693 back into 1602 — [[Guard against reverse-teleology]] applies with full force, and the temptation here is exactly the one [[The ascent narrative is the Jensen error as historiography]] names.
2. Written *for* the Heren XVII, it is a document of **corporate self-understanding**. Strong evidence for how the Company conceived its own legal form; weak evidence for practice, especially in Asia. Keep the two claims separate in drafting or a referee will collapse them for me.
3. **Stapel's apparatus** (1927–54) carries its own period assumptions, heaviest in the colonial-administrative volumes. Cite the text; treat the introductions as historiography.

## The evidentiary asymmetry, stated rather than concealed

Van Dam gives the VOC something no East Asian case in the corpus has: internal doctrinal articulation of the entity by its own legal officer. There is no *Beschryvinge* of the *ie* or of a *kabu-nakama*. The asymmetry is **evidentiary, not functional**, and it will be read as functional unless it is named. This is [[The deficit reading of absence is the scalar ranking in evidentiary form]] in its most seductive form, because here the abundance is real and on one side only. See also [[Refusals are observations of the filter not inferences from survivors]] on the general shape of the error.
