# Handoff — the non-European archive survey

**The European survey is complete: nine countries, one hub, nine country MOCs, and around 150 repository notes.** The non-European passes begin with the Ottoman archives and continue to the Geniza, Japan, China and South Asia. This note carries what the European work established so the next pass does not rediscover it.

## The method, in one paragraph

For each country: establish **the structural fact** — what the country's archival administration does to the evidence — then work repository by repository, one note each, at seed depth, with **explicit ⚠️ flags wherever a placement rests on secondary literature rather than a finding aid**. Country notes hang off a country MOC; the country MOC hangs off [[MOC - European archives and digital collections]], which holds only what is common. **Never inflate a secondary site: [[Archivio di Stato di Lucca]] and [[Het Scheepvaartmuseum - Amsterdam]] are the models for saying so plainly.**

## The three-layer frame, which is the hub's core

**Description**, **image**, **text**. The failure mode is treating layer 2 as layer 3. **Carry this into the Ottoman pass unchanged** — and expect the script problem to make the layer-3 question sharper, not softer, since Ottoman *dīwānī* is to Latin HTR roughly what *kuzushiji* is, and [[MOC - Digitisation and text recognition]] already records that the Japanese and European stacks do not overlap.

## Seven patterns the European survey produced

**1. The funding map is not a survival map — and it is not a holdings map either.** Belgium has poor infrastructure and first-rank holdings; the Nordics the reverse. **Ease of access is not a proxy for richness**, and the hub's second section now says so.

**2. Successor custody.** Five instances — the Fuggers, the Bazacle to EDF, the Antwerp *naties*, the London livery companies, the Bank of England. **Institutions that survive keep their own papers**, and private custody means access by permission. Two filters stacked on the persistence literature's own. **Expect this to recur in the Ottoman case with the *waqf*** — an endowed perpetual body is exactly the kind of institution that outlives its records' custodians.

**3. Catastrophic loss, and the value of a published survival rule.** Five: Naples 1943, Ypres 1914, Paris 1871, Cologne 2009, the Four Courts 1922. **A fire has no shape, so a gap licenses no inference** — but Cologne and Ireland both publish their reconstruction rules, and where the rule is published it can be corrected for. **Ask not only what survived, but what the survival rule was and whether anyone wrote it down.**

**4. The record type is a variable.** Notarial minute, *schepenakte*, *Niederstadtbuch*, *tänkebok*, Ragusan register, Scottish register of deeds. **A difference between two coded rows may be a difference in what the record type records.** This is now a standing check, first flagged for `bodmerei` at [[Bodmerei - where the German bottomry records are]].

**5. National arrangements, chosen for unrelated reasons.** Six business-archive systems, five press corpora with a forty-fold range in scale and two pricing models. **A comparative corpus assembled from what is open is a sample of national administrative traditions.**

**6. The merchant corporation has six instances and no row.** Marseille 1599, Bilbao, Burgos, Lucca, Handelskammer Hamburg, the London livery and regulated companies. **This should go to a selection brief.**

**7. Failure is where the census is emptiest.** [[Insolvente Boedelskamer - Antwerp]] on the loss side, the Brandenburg African Company on the entity side. Every coded entity form is a survivor.

## What is already committed for the Ottoman pass

- The grant's travel line names **Istanbul and Ankara** (Yaycıoğlu and postdocs) and **Cairo / Cambridge** for the Geniza.
- **[[The Ottoman documents at Dubrovnik]] is the bridge and should be read first.** Fifteen thousand Ottoman documents sit in a European archive alongside the Ragusan chancery's registration of what the merchants then did — **the same bilateral relationship in two apparatuses, in one building**, which is a cleaner test site than either capital.
- The census already codes `waqf_khayri`, `waqf_ahli`, `avariz_vakfi`, `avariz_vakfi_kirkcesme`, `cash_waqf` (parked), `qirad` and `mudaraba` (a determination). **The Ottoman pass is not opening a new subject; it is finding the archives behind rows that already exist.**
- **Language and script are the binding constraint**, not custody — the reverse of most of Europe. The grant's Decision_log A6 already budgets for transcription of *dīwānī* and certified translation.

## What to do differently

**Ask the layer question about script, not only about imaging.** In Europe an imaged series is usable by a reader of the language; an imaged Ottoman series may not be usable by anyone on the team without paid transcription. **That makes "imaged" a weaker result than it was in Europe, and the register's *language and script* column becomes load-bearing rather than descriptive.**

## Progress

- **Ottoman — done, 11 September 2026.** [[MOC - Ottoman archives]]. The Dubrovnik start held; the script prediction was right and understated — the census's Ottoman rows are accounts, and accounts are in siyakat; successor custody recurred with the waqf in a stronger form than predicted.
- **Geniza — done, 11 September 2026.** [[MOC - Geniza archives]]. Better served digitally than expected — images of the whole deposit online and a machine transcription released in 2025 — so the binding constraints moved from access and script to the denominator and the deposit's positionality. The `isqa_alloc` dispute turned out to be a record-type difference.
- **Japan — done, 11 September 2026.** [[MOC - Japanese archives]]. Successor custody is the default rather than the exception; books are imaged and the census's documents are not; the Japanese rows rest on printed transcriptions; and `clearing_records` has no archive yet.
- **China — done, 11 September 2026.** [[MOC - Chinese archives]]. The Qing central record is split between Beijing and Taipei by a 1921 waste-paper sale and the 1949 evacuation, under opposite access regimes; it is not where the census's evidence is. Every coded Chinese cell passes through Zelin 2019 and the earliest through one 1995 compilation; the associations' operating record survives abroad (Hong Kong, Leiden); A7 has no Chinese destination.
- **South Asia — done, 12 September 2026.** [[MOC - South Asian archives]]. Run without waiting on Decision_log D4 and without deciding it. The colonial state's record is vast and online; the merchants' books are gone; layer 3 is the colonial enquiry; the best evidence is epigraphic. **Two candidates parked — the *śreṇi* endowment and the South Indian chit fund — and the census's one South Asian row is confirmed for removal.**
- **Silk Road / Inner Asia — done, 12 September 2026.** [[MOC - Silk Road archives]]. A cross-cutting pass at MS's request, including the Japanese holdings. Expedition dispersal and digital reunification; **the record is documentary while `ortoq_equity` and `ortoq_loan` are coded from chronicles**; the Dunhuang *she* association parked as the survey's best-shaped candidate.
- **The non-European survey is complete.** South-East Asia was not surveyed as a region; what the Chinese and Dutch passes found on that side is recorded in the South Asian hub under D4.
