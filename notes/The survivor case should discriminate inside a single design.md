# The survivor case should discriminate inside a single design

Part 1 promises to name in advance one canonical result the critique will leave standing; Part 2 says the case will be chosen jointly and recorded before the re-analysis is run. "Name in advance" and "record before re-analysis" are different commitments, and the gap is visible. Naming a case closes it, but only a case meeting four conditions makes the pre-commitment informative.

1. **A genuine space-for-time step.** The design must read a cross-section as a claim about time. Where trajectories are observed directly there is nothing to license, and survival is trivial.
2. **An observable that plausibly mixes on the horizon at issue** — modest effects, fast-relaxing observables, as the retrodiction work of August 2026 already required.
3. **Intermediate observations.** Two-date designs cannot separate proportional transmission from attractor selection; anything observed in the middle can ([[Attractor selection bounds what a persistence design can resolve]]).
4. **Both kinds of outcome within one design**, so that the criterion is seen to discriminate rather than merely to sort cases chosen because they differ.

## Recommended

**Grosfeld and Zhuravskaya (2015), with Backhaus (2019).** The partition of Poland, read by spatial regression discontinuity. Differences in income, industrial production, education, corruption and trust in state institutions disappear; differences in religious practice and democratic values persist. Backhaus supplies the middle: an enrolment gap of up to 80 percentage points between the partitions closes within two decades of independence, and literacy converges. All four conditions hold.

## Weaker candidates

- **Davis and Weinstein (2002; 2008).** Meets the first three conditions, but the return may be equilibrium selection — [[Recovery to the old configuration is not evidence of a single attractor]]. Keep as the bridge to the economic-geography branch, which already speaks of non-ergodicity ([[The persistence literature names non-ergodicity only in its geography branch]]).
- **Miguel and Roland (2011).** A pooled null at one date — [[A pooled null can hide trajectories that never return]]. Drop or footnote.
- **Ager, Boustan and Eriksson (2021)** on slaveholding households after emancipation, and **Clark and Cummins (2026)** on wealth shocks. The cleanest relaxation results in the sweep, but linked panels: condition 1 fails.
- **Iyer and Weir (2025)**, re-measuring Iyer (2010) and Banerjee and Iyer (2005) two decades on. The gaps close or narrow, but the authors credit targeted policy, so regime homogeneity fails.
