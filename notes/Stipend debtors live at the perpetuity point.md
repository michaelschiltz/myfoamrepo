# Stipend debtors live at the perpetuity point

A hypothesis for the Edo case, prompted by Raposo and Pulido 2026.

**The model.** A household on fixed income $p$ that borrows at rate $r$ reaches, at debt $p/r$, the *perpetuity point*: the whole income goes in interest and the principal can never be reduced. Beyond it the household is trapped. Raposo and Pulido give two treatments: the creditor takes all income and freezes the principal (Dynamic I), or unpaid interest is added to the principal (Dynamic II).

**The fit.** The shogun's retainers drew a fixed rice stipend and borrowed from the *fudasashi* against future stipends. A retainer whose debt reached stipend divided by the brokers' rate had crossed into the trapped region; one whose arrears were capitalised was on Dynamic II. The shogunal debt settlements are interventions in that dynamic, and they differ in kind [verify terms of each before writing]:

- **Restructuring that freezes principal and suspends interest** is Raposo and Pulido's Dynamic I imposed from outside. The Tenpō-era order converting retainers' debts to interest-free instalments is the candidate.
- **Cancellation** (the Kansei *kien-rei* of 1789 for older debts) is a jump in wealth the model does not contain: it moves the household back across the perpetuity point at the creditor's expense.
- **The pool's defence against cancellation.** The Hōryūji fifty-*kan* *tanomoshi* wrote a clause keeping its contributions untouched by any realm-wide *tokusei* — a pooling institution shielding itself from the political reset of individual debts. The clause and its correct attribution (Miura, p. 790; the Hōryūji fifty-*kan* association alone, not the Meiō 1498 rulebook) are in the project report `claude/seven-ko-coding-2026-08-20.md`; the vault has no note on it yet.

## Why it belongs to the project and not only to Edo

The household's risk aversion here comes from an additive dynamic with a debt boundary, with no preference assumption — the same move the project makes for institutions. And the creditor's side is a pooling problem: the *fudasashi* collectively held the retainers' debt, and the *kabu nakama* that licensed them is the institution that bore the cancellations. Where the model treats the creditor as a passive taker of income, the historical creditor was a licensed group — [[Natie and kabu-nakama are a convergence pair]] for the group, Schiltz 2026 ("Stipends, Brokers, and Borrowed Time") for the brokers.
