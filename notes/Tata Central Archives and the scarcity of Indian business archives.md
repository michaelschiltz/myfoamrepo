# Tata Central Archives and the scarcity of Indian business archives

Pune, on the grounds of the Tata Management Training Centre. **Established in 2001**; holdings include tens of thousands of documents assembled by the group's Department of Economics and Statistics in the early 1950s, and **the complete correspondence of J. R. D. Tata**. Digitised and arranged chronologically and by company.

**Access, as a historian who used it describes it**: arranged in advance, "with minimal bureaucratic hassle", but with "a fairly strong 'gatekeeper' effect" — **documents are delivered as PDFs rather than handled as originals.** **A company archive serves the company's memory first**, which is the Mitsui and Sumitomo finding in another economy.

## The general fact

**India has few dedicated business archives.** The Nehru Memorial (now Prime Ministers' Museum and Library) has been the default holder of commercial and association papers; **Godrej, Bajaj and Cipla have followed Tata in founding their own since the 2000s.** ⚠️ This is one researcher's account plus a newspaper feature; no survey of Indian business archives was found.

## For the census

**Out of period and out of scope, and worth a note for what it demonstrates.** These archives begin in the late nineteenth century and are corporate. **The census's South Asian gap is earlier, and the reason for it is visible here: there was no institution collecting commercial papers when the papers were current.** **Where Japan built a *monjokan* network and China a contract-collecting academy, South Asia built neither** — see [[Indian merchant account books - bahi khata and kadai kanakku]].
