# The Anatolian court registers — museum to National Library to State Archives

**The court registers of the Ottoman provinces now in Turkey, outside Istanbul — about 7,000 to 9,000 volumes, depending on the count.** Not a repository but a custody chain, and the chain is the point.

## The chain

**Local custody until 1991.** When the Republic abolished the *şer'iyye* courts in 1924, provincial registers stayed where they were or drifted into museums, libraries and mosques. **Bursa's is the documented case** (Pay 2001): the Şeyh Süleyman Efendi school, 1834–1854; then the Çendik *medrese*, the Evkaf office and the Ulu Cami; **the Bursa Archaeological Museum, 1945–1991**.

**1991: consolidated at the Milli Kütüphane in Ankara**, on a Ministry of Culture instruction, and microfilmed there. See [[Milli Kutuphane - Ankara]].

**10 December 2005: transferred to the State Archives** in Ankara. The *TDV İslâm Ansiklopedisi* gives the transferred originals as 8,934 registers.

**So a register cited by Çizakça in 1995 as held at Bursa, or by a thesis in 2003 as held at the Milli Kütüphane, is now in neither place.** Three custodians in sixty years. **Record custody at the date of citation, and key the register on court and number** — which is how İSAM holds it; see [[ISAM Library Archive - the court register image collection]].

## Scale

The Akgündüz union catalogue counts **about 17,000 registers inside Turkey: 9,883 Istanbul and 6,960 others**; the National Library's figure for the provincial set is 8,931–8,934. **Bursa: 1,062 registers, 860/1455 to 1342/1923** — the oldest Ottoman court registers anywhere. ⚠️ The difference between 6,960 and 8,934 is unexplained.

## Access, layer and script

- **Originals at the State Archives, Ankara.** ⚠️ Whether they are catalogued in the State Archives' online system and consultable in the Ankara reading room was not established.
- **Microfilm at the Milli Kütüphane; digital copies at İSAM.** In practice İSAM is the working route.
- **Layer 2.** No layer-3 edition equivalent to Istanbul's, beyond individual theses transcribing single registers — a large and unindexed secondary literature of its own.
- Arabic in the early registers, Ottoman Turkish later; siyakat in accounts.
