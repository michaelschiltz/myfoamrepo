# The Bank of England Archive

<https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/archive> · catalogue on CalmView · digital archive on Preservica at <https://boe.access.preservica.com/>

The archive of a **chartered joint-stock corporation of 1694** that is also a coded form's nearest English relative, and it is held by the institution itself.

## The holding that matters

**The stock collection** — the Bank's own ledgers and **transfer books**. For a project about transferable claims this is the thing itself: **a serial, dated register of who held what and of every transfer**, kept by the issuer, from the seventeenth century.

**That is what the Bazacle series is** — and [[Le Bris Goetzmann Pouget 2023 on convergent evolution toward the joint-stock company]] is built on exactly that kind of record. The Bank's registers are the English equivalent and are far better preserved. ⚠️ Whether they have been worked into a price or holdings series, and whether the digital archive exposes them, is unestablished and is the first question.

## Why the custody is worth noting

**The Bank keeps its own archive.** Fifth instance of successor custody, after the Fuggers, the Bazacle, the Antwerp *naties* and the livery companies — and the most respectable. The Bank also runs a **museum and a public digital archive**, so access is better than most private custody.

But the general point from [[Fuggerarchiv - Dillingen]] stands and is not a slur: **the archives of institutions that survived are disproportionately held by those institutions**, and the terms on which they are read are set by the subject of the study. Record who holds a repository in the register's own column.
