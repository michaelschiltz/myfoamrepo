# Hanafi and Maliki law part on who may force the liquidation of a mudaraba

**Both schools bar the investor's recall once the agent has bought goods. They part on what follows.** The Ḥanafī investor can force the liquidation at once; the Mālikī investor cannot, and the venture runs on to the market.

## The common rule

Before the capital is committed, either party may end the contract. Al-Sarakhsī: *li-kull wāḥid minhumā an yanfarida bi-faskhihi* (*al-Mabsūṭ* 22, 19). The *muḍāraba* is not binding, and the agent may refuse the work and dissolve the contract *matā shāʾa* (91).

Once the capital is in goods, the investor's prohibition fails in both schools:
- **Ḥanafī:** *fa-nahyuhu bāṭil*, because the agent's right to profit has attached (22, 53).
- **Mālikī:** the investor may not recall the capital once the agent has bought, or merely equipped himself for a journey, not even on the road (Saḥnūn, *al-Mudawwana* 3, 662; Mālik, *al-Muwaṭṭaʾ* 2027).

**The vault's earlier formulation, a Ḥanafī right of revocation against a Mālikī restriction after commencement, was wrong in its framing.** Both schools restrict.

## The divergence

- **Ḥanafī.** If the agent wants to hold the goods "until he finds a large profit" and the investor wants them sold, the agent is compelled to sell (22, 72). Alternatively, he hands over the investor's capital and profit share in kind and keeps his own share. The ground: delay is *ḥaylūla bayna rabb al-māl wa-bayna mālihi*, which the investor did not accept when he contracted. The investor's liquidity governs.
- **Mālikī.** The investor "cannot compel the sale". The ruler holds the goods to their market season, "like grain bought at harvest and kept for its season" (*Mudawwana* 3, 662). The investor taking the goods at valuation is the agent's choice: "he and any stranger are alike" (663). In a dispute over selling, "neither's word is looked to", and men of expertise decide whether to sell or wait (*Muwaṭṭaʾ* 2052). The agent, for his part, may not hand back goods unsold (2027), and must collect debts (*Mudawwana* 3, 663). The venture's completion governs.

- In `perpetual-succession` both rows now hold signatures no other form holds.

The Mālikī rule puts the *qirāḍ* beside the Latin commenda on lock-in. The Ḥanafī rule puts the *muḍāraba* beside the *ʿisqa* and the *fraterna*. Neither placement is a transmission claim.

## Why it matters for the argument

[[The mudaraba is revocable not locked in]] was written against the claim that Islamic doctrine binds partner to duration, and it stands for the Ḥanafī school. For the Mālikī school it needs the qualification above: the capital is locked in until the goods meet their market, by the ruler's judgement and against the investor's wish. What doctrine binds is still payoff to outcome; in Mālikī law it also binds capital to the venture's natural term. The tradition that set the *qirāḍ*'s term by the market is the one whose agent is shielded from the investor's impatience.

## Where this is soft

- **One text per school.** The Ḥanafī side is al-Sarakhsī alone. The *Muwaṭṭaʾ* and the *Mudawwana* are one Mālikī witness.
- **Within-school dissent is not surveyed.**
- **The Ḥanafī `TS3 = 1` is coded `medium`,** because 53's void-prohibition clause reads the other way on its face; 72 answers it.
- **Law, not practice.** No contract or Geniza document was read for how liquidation was actually timed.
