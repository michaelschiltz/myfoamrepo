# The laboratory tests the choice and the archive tests the pool

The experimental evidence for time-average reasoning and the historical evidence the project assembles are evidence at **different scales**, and the difference is not one of degree. The laboratory observes an individual choosing; the archive observes rules that allocate loss within a pool and pools that survived or did not. The standing objection to the experiments — that what they show is expected log utility conditioned on the dynamic — is an objection about individual choice, and **it does not reach the pool**: who bears a member's loss, and whether the pool outlives its members' ruin, is a property of a population's trajectory that no single agent's utility function encodes. That is where the archive adds something the laboratory cannot, and where the proposal should place its weight.

## Two experiments, not one

The two are routinely run together, including by their admirers. They differ on every axis that matters.

## Where the laboratory is weak and the archive is not

1. **Ruin.** The Brussels group has put ruin into an additive design (Vanhoyweghen and Ginis 2023), so the laboratory is not blind to the barrier. But laboratory ruin is in-game. No ethics board permits a real absorbing barrier, and the archive's are real: bankruptcy, debt bondage, the extinguished line.
2. **Horizon.** The experiments run for hours. The growth-rate criterion is asymptotic ([[The growth-rate criterion is asymptotic and the horizon comparison cuts both ways]]), and the archive is the only place its horizon is actually traversed — decades, generations, the life of a *waqf*.
3. **Unit.** Every design in the set, the 2025 redistribution experiment included, observes an individual's choice; the 2025 study brings *sharing* into the laboratory as a stated preference, but no experiment observes a pool persist. Loss-allocation doctrine, netting rules and exit penalties are evidence about the collective dynamic directly, which is the level at which the framework's content is distinctive rather than a re-description of Kelly.

## What the archive cannot supply

Three limits, each already a guardrail in the vault, and all three must be stated in the same paragraph as the claim above.

- **Convergence, not cognition.** The Brussels result is about intuition; the archive is not. A jurist's rule may come from intuition, learning, design or the differential survival of the groups that adopted it. History shows the outcome, never the mental route, and no sentence may imply that premodern actors reasoned in time averages: [[Time-average optimization is a survivorship property not an intention]]. The independent-origin design separates some routes, not all.
- **Doctrine, not practice.** A rule of validity is not a record of conduct: [[Doctrine, deed and conduct are three layers and the time-average test needs all three]].
- **The dynamic must be coded from the terms.** The archive escapes the log-utility objection only if the dynamic is read off the instrument and not off its fate: [[Ford and Kay concede the condition under which their objection lapses]].

## Use in the proposal

The analogy with the Gigerenzer–Taleb pairing in Part 1 is legitimate only as an analogy of function: the codified rule stands to the community as the intuition under time pressure stands to the subject. Never as a claim about what drafters felt. A formulation for Part 1, in the historian-facing register:

> Time-average reasoning has now been observed at two scales: in the laboratory, as individual risk preferences that shift with the dynamics subjects face, and in the historical record, as rules for the allocation of loss that communities codified, enforced and retained over horizons no experiment can reproduce. The second is not reducible to the first, for a rule governing who bears a loss within a pool makes a claim about the pool's trajectory that no individual's preferences encode.
