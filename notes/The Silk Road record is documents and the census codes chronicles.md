# The Silk Road record is documents and the census codes chronicles

**The finding that most concerns the census: Inner Asia's surviving record is unusually documentary — contracts, loans, leases, receipts, association bylaws, private letters — and the census's two Inner Asian rows are coded from court histories.**

## The two sides

**What the census codes.** `ortoq_equity` and `ortoq_loan` rest on **Enkhbold 2019, reading Juvaynī, Rashīd al-Dīn and Vaṣṣāf** — narrative chronicles written at or for Mongol courts. `ortoq_alloc` cross-references them. `caravan_partnership` is uncoded **on readability**, and its named sources are modern studies of the early-modern Armenian and Saharan diasporas.

**What survives.** In the same centuries and partly the same region:

- **Old Uyghur contracts** — loans, sales, leases, pledges, adoptions, in a typed corpus edited in Japan: [[Old Uyghur contracts - the documentary layer under the ortoq]].
- **Chinese contracts from Dunhuang and Turfan** — Tang and Five Dynasties loan, hire, sale and lease contracts, and the association documents at [[Dunhuang she associations - bylaws for a funeral fund]].
- **Khara-Khoto documents** — Tangut and Chinese, Xixia through Yuan, including social documents and contracts: [[Institute of Oriental Manuscripts - St Petersburg]].
- **Sogdian letters and legal documents** — [[Sogdian commercial documents - thin but not empty]].
- **Bactrian legal and economic documents** — [[The Khalili Bactrian documents - a private collection as the archive]].
- **Kharoṣṭhī wooden tablets from Niya and Loulan**, third century, largely legal, taken by Stein and edited by Boyer, Rapson and Senart in the 1920s. ⚠️ Counts and contents not established in this pass.

## Why this matters and what it does not license

**The vault's [[The ortoq is the discriminating instrument]] makes the ortoq the calibrated case of documented transmission, and the whole East-Asia-versus-West comparison is read against it.** **A calibration resting on chronicles is weaker than it looks**: a chronicler reports what a financing arrangement did to a prince's treasury, not what its instrument said. **Where an institution's own instruments survive, the census prefers them** — that is the *kō* rule and the Chinese contract finding.

**This is not a claim that the Uyghur contracts document the ortoq.** ⚠️ Whether any surviving contract is an ortoq contract was **not established in this pass** and is exactly the question to put to a specialist. **Matsui Dai's work on Uyghur documents of the Mongol period is the obvious place to start.**

**Recorded, not applied**: the ortoq rows' `key_source` fields say what they rest on and do not claim documentary support. **Nothing here changes a cell.**
