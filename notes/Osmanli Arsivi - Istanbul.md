# Osmanlı Arşivi — Istanbul

**Cumhurbaşkanlığı Devlet Arşivleri Başkanlığı, Osmanlı Arşivi** — the Presidency's State Archives, Ottoman department; formerly the Başbakanlık Osmanlı Arşivi (BOA), the abbreviation the literature still uses. Kağıthane, Istanbul, since 2013. Catalogue: <https://katalog.devletarsivleri.gov.tr/>.

## The scale, and the caution about it

**More than 100 million documents**, and about three-quarters classified by 2008 (Encyclopaedia Iranica). ⚠️ Holdings figures vary by source and date and none was taken from the archive itself. **It is the only major surviving archive of a pre-modern Muslim state**, which is why it is named in the grant's travel line.

## What the archive does to the evidence

**Two arrangements lie over each other.** Named classification collections made by early Republican directors arranged loose documents by reign or by subject; provenance fonds arrange registers and later documents by the bureau that produced them. See [[The tasnif collections - subject classification over provenance]]. **A question about waqfs has no single series address here**; it has a subject class, a reign class, and several bureau series.

## Access

- Open to Turkish and foreign researchers under the *Arşivlerden Yararlanma Esasları*, *Resmî Gazete*, 2 October 2021.
- Registration in person at the Istanbul or Ankara reading rooms, or through e-Devlet.
- The online system (BETSİS) allows catalogue search and **free preview**; images are **bought at 0.50 TL each**, by card, online or in the reading room.
- ⚠️ A 2024–2025 researcher's guide reports that remote access in practice runs through e-Devlet, which is a Turkish-ID route, and that **foreign researchers consult the digitised material on reading-room computers**. If that holds, the archive is at **layer 2 on site and layer 2 remotely only for a team member with Turkish ID** — a staffing fact, not an archival one.

## Layer and script

- **Layer 1:** the catalogue, with item-level summaries in modern Turkish for many fonds. ⚠️ Extent of summarisation by fonds not measured.
- **Layer 2:** images, bought per image.
- **Layer 3:** none as a corpus; only the transcriptions the archive publishes, volume by volume.
- **Script:** *dīwānī* for chancery acts; **siyakat for every accounting series** — the Treasury's and the Evkaf's. See the script note below.

**Accounts are the hard case.** Siyakat is a chancery accounting hand with its own numeral system, dotless letters and conventional abbreviations, used in tax, accounting and waqf registers from the fifteenth century into the nineteenth (*TDV İslâm Ansiklopedisi*, "Siyâkat"). The census's Ottoman rows are **accounting rows**. An imaged waqf account in siyakat is usable by fewer readers than any other document type in this pass.

## The honest assessment

**For the census, this is where the empire's waqfs are seen from the Treasury's side.** The great imperial foundations' accounts are here because a central office audited them. **The neighbourhood *avarız* funds and the ordinary cash waqfs are not primarily here**; they live in the court registers, at [[Istanbul Muftulugu Seriyye Sicilleri Arsivi]] and in [[The Anatolian court registers - museum to National Library to State Archives]]. Every Ottoman row the census has coded so far rests on literature working court registers or a waqf's own account book, not this archive. **Istanbul's Ottoman Archive is the grant's named destination and is not, on present evidence, the census's primary one.**
