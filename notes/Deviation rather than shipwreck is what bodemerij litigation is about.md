# Deviation rather than shipwreck is what bodemerij litigation is about

In forty-six Amsterdam notarial acts on bodemerij, 1601–1621, **not one concerns a ship
that sank**. All ten protests concern non-payment, non-acceptance, or a master who altered
his route.

The discharge clause is the celebrated part of the instrument: the money runs *op 't recht
aventuyr van der zee*, and if the pledged hull is lost the lender forfeits advance and
opgeld together. Winkelman calls that *ongekend in het credietwezen*. But the clause is
never litigated in this corpus, and the corpus is what survives of Amsterdam's formal
enforcement. **The contested margin is not whether the peril happened. It is whether the
peril was still the lender's to bear.**

The lenders speak with one formula. At La Rochelle in 1602 the master ran on from Danzig to
Riga, and the creditor declares he *geen voirder resicque oft pericule begeert te loopen;
alsoe naer recht ende coustume sijne penningen verschenen sijn* — the risk-bearing ends and
the principal falls due, on custom. Two years later, the same words for a master who took
freight for Portugal: *gemerct hij door 't veranderen van de voorsz. reyse niet gehouden is
eenige voirdere risicque te loopen*.

This is the instrument's real hazard, and it is not a peril of the sea. It is the master's
discretion. The contracts know it — the standing formula *wel heynder maer niet verder te
seylen* exists to fence the risk to a stated geography, and one act of 1609 makes the
consequence brutal: on deviation the borrower owes the principal with *de volle bodemerije
in 't geheel*, the entire risk price, for a risk the lender has stopped running.

**The consequence for reading the discharge clause is uncomfortable.** A generous rule
about total loss that is never tested in twenty years of surviving Amsterdam practice, next
to an acceleration rule that is tested constantly, suggests the instrument was priced and
policed for the agency problem rather than for the storm. Where a source is silent this
must be coded `.NR` and not read as evidence that discharge worked smoothly; the silence is
about the sample, not about the sea.
