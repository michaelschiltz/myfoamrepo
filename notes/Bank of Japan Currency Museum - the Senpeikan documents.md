# Bank of Japan Currency Museum — the Senpeikan documents

日本銀行金融研究所 貨幣博物館, Nihonbashi, Tokyo. <https://www.imes.boj.or.jp/cm/>

**The Senpeikan collection** of Tanaka Keibun (1884–1956), the largest private collection of Japanese monetary material, **about 120,000 items, most of it moved into the Bank of Japan in December 1944.** Among them about **2,400 documents** catalogued in ***Nihon Ginkō shozō Senpeikan komonjo mokuroku*** (July 2000), in six classes — monetary system, mining, gold/silver/copper, paper money and *hansatsu*, **social and economic materials**, manuscript copies. **The social and economic class includes *ryōgae* and banking: materials on the money-changers of Edo, Osaka and Kyoto from the Edo period to early Meiji, and on the national banks.**

## Why it matters

**A collector's selection, held by the central bank, catalogued to item level and published.** Unlike the house archives, **it is not one firm's record** — it is what a numismatist thought worth buying about money. **That makes it the widest and the least serial of the three money-market sources** — see [[The clearing records dataset has no archive yet]].

**A survival rule with a date**: the move into the Bank in December 1944, before the air raids on Tokyo. ⚠️ What stayed with the collector and was lost not established.

⚠️ Access terms, imaging, and the separate IMES Archives' digital archive (the Bank's own modern records) not captured.
