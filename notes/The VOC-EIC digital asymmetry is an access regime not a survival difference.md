# The VOC–EIC digital asymmetry is an access regime, not a survival difference

Both companies' central archives survive in bulk. Neither was destroyed, neither is fragmentary at the level that would license an argument from absence. The VOC's ≈25 million pages and the India Office Records are of the same order. **Everything that differs, differs downstream of survival.**

## The regimes

The Dutch corpus was opened by a **public research infrastructure**: NWO money, KNAW institutes, the national archive as data supplier, a UNESCO badge as political cover, open-source HTR, and an open-data mandate. The output is text, in bulk, free, with an ontology attached.

The English corpus was opened by **two private settlements**. A commercial publisher bought the right to image the constitutional series and sells access by institutional subscription. A sovereign wealth foundation paid to image the portion of the record that concerns its own region. Between them they produce excellent images, no bulk text, and a coverage map drawn by two balance sheets rather than by any archival logic.

The asymmetry is therefore **a fact about Dutch science policy and British library funding after 2010**, not a fact about the seventeenth century. Say so, or a reader will silently take searchability for evidentiary richness.

## Why this is the project's problem specifically

Digitisation policy is now a **selection filter on the comparative corpus**, and it selects in the direction the argument is most vulnerable. If the VOC is searchable at corpus scale and the EIC is not, the VOC becomes the default European referent — not because it is the better comparandum but because it answers queries. The Japanese material is worse placed still: *kuzushiji* transcription is only now tractable and nothing exists at OBP scale.

The resulting corpus is conditioned on funding, and conditioned corpora are exactly what [[Persistence samples are conditioned on non-absorption]] and [[Refusals are observations of the filter not inferences from survivors]] warn against — here the filter is contemporary rather than historical, which makes it easier to miss, not harder.

## The referee objection this pre-empts

A specialist referee will notice that the VOC evidence in the book is thicker than the EIC evidence and will ask whether the argument was built to fit the available material. The answer must be stated **before** the comparison, not defended afterwards: the density gradient runs Dutch → English → Japanese and tracks digital infrastructure investment, not institutional complexity. This is [[The deficit reading of absence is the scalar ranking in evidentiary form]] arriving through a twenty-first-century door.

The concrete discipline: **do not let a claim rest on a frequency that could only have been computed on one side.** Any statement of the form "the VOC records mention X far more often than the EIC records" is, as things stand, uninterpretable — one side was counted by machine over five million pages and the other by hand over whatever a subscription permitted.

## The one place the asymmetry is real and not artefactual

Corporate self-description. Van Dam has no English counterpart of equal internal authority, and that gap is genuine rather than an artefact of imaging. See [[Van Dam's Beschryvinge - the VOC described by its own advocaat]] — but note that IOR/B, the Courts of Directors and Proprietors minutes 1599–1858, is a *serial* constitutional record the VOC's counsel's retrospective compilation is not. The two are differently shaped evidence of the same object, and the English one happens to be the one behind the paywall.
