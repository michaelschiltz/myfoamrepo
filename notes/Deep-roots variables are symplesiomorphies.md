# Deep-roots variables are symplesiomorphies

The persistence literature's characteristic variable — an institution, a shock, a technology inherited from a deep common substrate and regressed on a modern outcome — is a *shared ancestral character*. Hennig's verdict on such characters is not that they are weak evidence. It is that they cannot group at all. This is a distinct objection from the one the literature already absorbs under Galton's problem, it is stronger, and it is not answerable by any refinement of the standard errors.

## What Galton's problem already covers, and why this is not that

Stated as *the units are not independent draws, so the effective* n *is smaller than the count*, the objection has been formalised in econometrics far more precisely than phylogenetics could manage: Conley and Kelly show that once residual spatial autocorrelation is taken seriously, randomly generated spatial noise reproduces the headline results of a large share of the literature. That is Galton's problem with a variance estimator attached, and it is a **precision** critique — the estimate is real, the uncertainty around it was understated, and the remedy is a better standard error. Repeating it in cladistic vocabulary adds nothing and invites the reading that the vocabulary is ornamental. See [[Galton's problem]].

## The inert-character claim

Hennig's cut runs elsewhere. **Shared derived** characters (synapomorphies) group; **shared ancestral** characters (symplesiomorphies) do not — and a symplesiomorphy is not noise. It is a real feature, causally genuine, correctly measured, and evidentially inert for the question asked, because a character inherited from a substrate the entire sample shares cannot discriminate *within* that sample.

Transposed: where the deep-roots regressor is ancestral to the whole sample, the finding is not that T caused Y but that the sample is one lineage. And the consequence is worse than imprecision. **A persistence result can be simultaneously true, precisely estimated, and evidentially empty.** Clustering does not touch this, because the defect is not in the variance; spatial corrections do not touch it, because the character would remain inert on an orthogonal grid. Adding observations tightens the interval around a quantity that answers a different question than the one posed, so the literature's own accumulation is the mechanism by which it becomes more confident without becoming more informative.

This is the version of the critique that cannot be met with a robustness table, which is precisely why it is worth having. It also stands independently of [[Non-ergodicity collapses the likelihood ratio on outcomes]] — a point taken up below.

## Polarity, and reversal as a coding error with a name

Cladistics establishes which state is ancestral by comparison with a taxon outside the study group. A cross-section of contemporary units has no outgroup and therefore no polarity, and without polarity the symplesiomorphy cannot be identified as one — the diagnosis above is unavailable to the very literature that needs it.

Worse, **reversal counterfeits the ancestral state.** A region that held an institution and abandoned it is indistinguishable, in a cross-section, from one that never held it. The persistence literature codes absence as ancestral by default, and that default is a substantive assumption doing unacknowledged work.

The defect is one this vault has already named in a different register. [[The waqf partition converts absence into refusal]] and [[The deficit reading of absence is the scalar ranking in evidentiary form]] make the same argument against historiographical practice: absence has at least two histories, and treating it as a deficit rather than a possible refusal decides the question in the coding. That the same error appears in a quantitative literature under a different disguise is the finding worth reporting, because it establishes that the objection is about the structure of the inference rather than about the register in which it is made. See also [[Refusals are observations of the filter not inferences from survivors]].

Credit where due, and it strengthens the critique to give it: Alesina, Giuliano and Nunn's plough paper does construct something functionally like a polarity, through ancestral crop suitability rather than through the observed cross-section. The objection is therefore not that the literature is incapable of the move but that the move is rare and unnamed, so nothing forces an author to make it or to justify its absence.

## Where the analogy does not reach

The persistence literature's core methodological problem is identification — confounding, collinearity among deep-roots regressors, and the multiplicity of researcher degrees of freedom. Cladistics has nothing whatever to say about omitted-variable bias, and any attempt to stretch it that far will be correctly read as borrowed authority. The phylogenetic apparatus earns its place on the inert-character claim and the polarity claim, and nowhere else. Overreach here would cost more than the argument gains.

## The reflexive cost, which is the price of making this argument

Objective 1 needs independence to hold; Objective 3 attacks the assumption that it does. A referee will set those side by side and observe that the project demands of the persistence literature a standard it cannot itself meet.

The reply exists and it is not a dodge. Persistence papers claim *causal transmission along a lineage*, and a claim of that form owes an account of the lineage. This project claims functional recurrence and treats transmission as the rival hypothesis to be eliminated, which is why the burden is discharged by the arbitrary-particulars diagnostic rather than by asserting separateness ([[Divergent arbitrary particulars are the independence diagnostic]], [[The theory predicts recurrence not independent origins]]). Different claims, different burdens. But this has to be written out rather than assumed, and it should appear in Part 1 alongside the existing reflexive paragraph, because importing cladistics sharpens the exposure exactly as much as it sharpens the attack.

One consistency constraint follows and is not negotiable. Parsimony analysis assumes character independence. The project cannot indict deep-roots collinearity in others while running a character matrix whose columns are the functionally coupled components of the corporate bundle. One standard, applied in both directions — see the correlated-evolution requirement in [[Homoplasy is the finding not the noise]].

## Why this de-risks Objective 3

Ergodicity and phylogenetic reconstruction are two independent statements of a single defect: **a cross-section stands in for a history only under conditions nobody checks.** The ergodicity criterion names the condition under which space-for-time substitution is valid. Cladistics names the conditions under which terminal states can be read as a history at all — polarity, character independence, absence of reticulation. Neither derives from the other.

The strategic value is that Objective 3 then does not depend on the reader accepting ergodicity economics. That matters directly given the falsifiability charge raised against the framework, since the phylogenetic version of the critique is unremarkable, conventional methodology in its home field and arrives at the same conclusion by a route no referee can call heterodox. Two foundations, one conclusion; the weaker-looking one is the one that travels.

## The literature gap, and its sharpest form

Cross-cultural anthropology formalised Galton's problem four decades ago and built phylogenetic comparative methods to handle it (Mace and Pagel 1994; Mace and Holden 2005). The economics persistence literature has largely not engaged that work. This is a checkable claim about a citation gap, and it is worth checking properly rather than asserting.

The sharper version concerns Spolaore and Wacziarg, whose genetic-distance work is the closest thing the literature has to an explicit phylogeny. **They place relatedness on the right-hand side as a regressor while retaining an error structure that assumes the units are independent.** Phylogenetic comparative methods do the opposite: relatedness belongs in the covariance of the errors, because that is where non-independence lives. Treating ancestry as an explanatory variable while assuming it away in the inference is having it both ways, and if the published specifications bear that reading it is the cleanest available hit on the programme's own terms.
