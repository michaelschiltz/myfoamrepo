# The sustainability criterion assumes the ergodicity it should be testing

Drelichman and Voth test whether Philip II's debts were serviceable using the IMF's standard criterion in the Aizenman–Pinto form, and arrive at *d\* = ps/(r − g)*: sustainable debt is the discounted stream of primary surpluses, discounted at the excess of the interest rate over the growth rate of revenue (2014: 121). The derivation requires, in their own words, "assuming that *r* and *g* do not change over time."

That is the ergodic hypothesis, entered as a technical convenience. Once *r* and *g* are constants the ordering of years carries no information that the period mean does not already carry, and the criterion cannot distinguish a reign whose debt ratio sat near its mean from one that oscillated violently around the same mean. Applied to public debt, which is the most temporal object in fiscal history, this is a strong step and it is not defended.

Their own Table 6 shows what it costs. Over 1565–96 the actual primary surplus is 31.5 per cent of revenue against the 34.8 required, and actual debt stands at 5.48 times revenue against a sustainable 5.23: a near miss on the wrong side, and the basis for the finding that "sustainability was not compromised" (122). By decade the picture inverts. Actual debt exceeds the sustainable level by 2.22 revenue-years in 1565–74 and by 1.98 in 1584–96; only 1574–84, following the 1577 *medio general* and the tax increase conceded by the Cortes, sits below it, by 2.75. Two decades of three are above the line, and the reign passes because the middle decade averages the other two out.

The annual series is sharper still. Debt to revenue peaks above seven in 1574 and above eight in 1594 (figures 8 and 9). The two payment stops falling inside the reconstructed accounts are 1575 and 1596. The maxima of the trajectory are the defaults; the period mean never sees them.

The calculation is not in error. The point is that a criterion built on period means answers a question about an ensemble of Castiles and is offered as an answer about the one Castile that existed. Whether the debts were serviceable *along the realised path* is a different question with a different answer, and it is the question the lenders were pricing.

## Consequence

Any fiscal-sustainability test of this family is disqualified as evidence for or against ergodicity, because it presupposes what would have to be shown. Where the vault needs a sustainability statement it should be an inequality on the trajectory, not on its mean.
