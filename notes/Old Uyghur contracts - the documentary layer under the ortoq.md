# Old Uyghur contracts — the documentary layer under the *ortoq*

**A corpus of private legal documents in Old Uyghur, mostly from Turfan, running into the Mongol period** — the nearest surviving documentary neighbours of the institution the census codes from chronicles.

## The corpus and its editions

- **Radloff and Malov, *Uigurische Sprachdenkmäler* (Leningrad, 1928)** — the first corpus, cited as **USp**.
- **山田信夫 Yamada Nobuo, 《ウイグル文契約文書集成》 / *Sammlung uigurischer Kontrakte*, 3 vols (Suita: 大阪大学出版会 Osaka University Press, January 1993), ISBN 978-4-87259-001-2**, cited as **SUK** — **the standard edition, made in Japan**, arranged by contract type with sigla: **Sa** sale, **Lo** loan, **Pl** pledge, **Ad** adoption, and others. **Volume 2 is the corpus: 121 documents in transcription with Japanese and German translation, notes, tables, bibliography and glossary (330 pp.)**; volume 1 collects Yamada's 18 revised papers (602 pp.); volume 3 is facsimiles (160 pp.). Yamada died in 1987 and the edition was completed by **小田壽典 Oda Juten, 森安孝夫 Moriyasu Takao, Peter Zieme and 梅村坦 Umemura Hiroshi**. Publisher's record verified 12 September 2026.
- **Continuing work**: Dai Matsui (Osaka) and colleagues publish new documents, including from the **Turfan Museum**, and re-edit old ones — his re-examination of **U 5330 (USp 77)** on Uyghur peasants and Buddhist monasteries **in the Mongol period** is the kind of item that bears on the ortoq question.

**Where the originals are**: Berlin (the U series), St Petersburg (the Serindia and Petrovsky collections), Japan (the Ōtani material), and museums in Xinjiang. **One corpus, four custodians, three editorial traditions** — [[The Silk Road layer 3 is a national editorial programme]].

## What it would test

**The census's `ortoq_equity` and `ortoq_loan` split rests on Enkhbold's reading of Persian chronicles.** A contract corpus from the Mongol period in the eastern half of the empire **could show what such arrangements said** — who bore loss, what the guarantor promised, what happened on default — **which is exactly the split the two rows encode.** ⚠️ **Nothing here establishes that any Uyghur contract is an ortoq contract**; that is a question for a specialist, and Matsui is the obvious one.

**It also bears on the caravan row.** `caravan_partnership` is uncoded because its modern sources are unreadable; **this is a body of readable, edited, typed contracts from the trade's own region**, though from settled oasis society rather than from caravans. ⚠️ Whether any SUK type is a trading partnership was not established.
