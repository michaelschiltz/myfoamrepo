# Entity shielding and owner shielding protect different trajectories

The corporate-form components and the loss-management components are **one mechanism applied to two units**. That is what makes WP1 and WP2 a single project rather than two, and it is not a framing convenience — it follows from what an absorbing barrier is.

**Owner shielding** truncates the member's downside. It removes the barrier from the **individual's** trajectory: a bad draw cannot carry him below zero, so repeated exposure to multiplicative venture risk remains rational for him.

**Entity shielding, capital lock-in and perpetual succession** do the same work for the **entity's** trajectory. Liquidation protection stops the fund being dismembered at the moment of maximum stress. Perpetual succession means a death does not trigger wind-up. Entity shielding stops one member's personal ruin dragging the pool down. Each is barrier-avoidance whose beneficiary is the vehicle.

Both are ergodicity-restoring. They differ only in **whose time-average is being protected**, and that is the whole of the distinction.

## Which predicts a trade-off

If the WP2 devices protect the entity's trajectory, they do so partly at the member's expense. Liquidation protection means he cannot realise capital when he personally needs it; lock-in traps him. **The devices that pull the fund away from its barrier can push him toward his.**

The coded forms line up on exactly that axis:

- ***waqf khayri*** — maximal entity continuity (`AP2=1`, `CI2=1`, `TS1=1`, irrevocable) with beneficiaries holding no realisable claim (`CI3=0`). The entity's barrier is removed entirely; the individual has no trajectory left to protect.
- ***ʿisqa*** — no entity at all (`LP1=0`, `TS1=0`, `CI2=0`), everything at the individual's level, and the manager fully exposed (`AP3=0`, `LR1=unlimited-several`, `LR6=symmetric`).
- ***Bazacle*** — entity continuity *and* member exit together. See [[Transferable claims reconcile lock-in with member exit]].

## Why this matters beyond bookkeeping

It explains the omission that first looked like an error. Owner shielding is absent from the five components because those define **what makes a corporation**, following Hansmann, Kraakman & Squire, and limited liability was late and plural — [[Harris 2020 on the late plurality of limited liability]]. That is right for the corporation question and wrong for the ergodicity question, where owner shielding is the most direct barrier-removal device there is.

Two component sets, one per unit, resolves it without disturbing the corporate-form five.

It also converts the falsification protocol into something that can fail. Not "traditions yield different functional combinations" — which enough characteristics guarantee — but **the framework predicts which unit each tradition protects**. Islamic doctrine solves the individual's barrier (the *qirāḍ* agent holds a call) and is blocked on the entity's by [[Kuran's brake is a refusal of immortal entities|the refusal of immortal entities]]. Latin Christendom solves the entity's early through the *universitas* and the individual's late. Jewish law solves neither, which is exactly why the *ʿisqa* manager is a debtor.

## Where this is soft

The trade-off is asserted from four coded forms, three of which I coded. It is a hypothesis with an illustration, not a result. It also assumes the member's and the entity's barriers are genuinely in tension rather than jointly satisfiable by some device not yet in the census — and [[Transferable claims reconcile lock-in with member exit]] is precisely a candidate for such a device, which weakens the trade-off claim in its strong form.
