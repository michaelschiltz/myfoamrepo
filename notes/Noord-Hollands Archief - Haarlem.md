# Noord-Hollands Archief — Haarlem

<https://noord-hollandsarchief.nl/>

**A second Dutch notarial archive at layer 3, and the first pass missed it.**

## What happened

The archive announced that the **entire Haarlem notarial archive is word-searchable** — *gehele notarieel archief Haarlem doorzoekbaar* — following successive expansions of the scanned notarial series. Toegang **`1617`**, *Notariële protocollen en akten van notarissen te Haarlem*. ⚠️ Date range, page count, and whether the transcription is HTR or index-derived are unconfirmed: the archive's own announcement page refused fetching and the claim here rests on its headline and a second report.

**Establish this properly before it is relied on**, because the difference between a full-text HTR corpus and a rich word-index is the difference between [[Stadsarchief Amsterdam - the notarial archive]] and an ordinary finding aid.

## Why a second searchable notariate is worth more than its size

Because it converts a corpus into a **comparison**. One searchable notarial archive answers questions about Amsterdam; two answer questions about whether an Amsterdam answer is about Amsterdam. Haarlem is inland, textile rather than maritime, and closely tied to the Amsterdam market — near enough to share drafting practice, different enough that divergence means something.

The census's standing worry that [[A migrated institution collects local names not local forms]] has a domestic version: **a form attested in one city's notariate may be that notariate's habit.** Two searchable corpora in one province is the cheapest test of it available anywhere in Europe.
