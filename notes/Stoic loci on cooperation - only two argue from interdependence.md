# Stoic loci on cooperation - only two argue from interdependence

The proposal's epigraph is *Meditations* 6.54, the swarm-and-bee passage (Decision_log H2). The wider Stoic corpus offers a dozen further candidates, and they divide along a seam that decides what an epigraph can be asked to carry.

Most argue from **membership**. The human being is a part — of the cosmos, the city, the body — and to act against the whole is to act against one's own nature. That is a claim about obligation, and it is orthogonal to the proposition the proposal advances: that pooling raises the time-average growth rate of each participant *severally*, so the case for cooperation never passes through altruism. A membership epigraph borrowed for a non-ergodicity argument decorates it; it does not license it.

Two loci argue otherwise — from **interdependence as a load condition** — and only those are structurally cognate with the pooling result.

## The load-bearing loci

- **Seneca, *Ep.* 95.53.** *Societas nostra lapidum fornicationi simillima est, quae casura nisi in vicem obstarent, hoc ipso sustinetur* [verify] — our fellowship is like an arch of stones, which would fall were the stones not to press against one another, and is held up by that very fact. The structure stands *because* of the mutual pressure: an argument from mechanism, not from duty. *Membra sumus corporis magni* sits two sentences earlier (95.52).
- **Cicero, *De officiis* 3.21-22** (Panaetius/Chrysippus material). If each limb supposed it could flourish by drawing its neighbour's strength into itself, the whole body would weaken and die; hence taking from another to increase one's own advantage is more contrary to nature than death, poverty or pain. An explicitly anti-zero-sum passage, and one the Latin scholastic and guild-confraternal sources under Objective 1 demonstrably knew.

Closest in sense to these, though weaker as an epigraph: **Seneca, *Ep.* 48.2**, *alteri vivas oportet, si vis tibi vivere* [verify] — live for the other if you would live for yourself. It states the pooling result's structure (the other's gain as the condition of one's own) in the vocabulary of moral counsel.

## The membership loci

- Marcus, *Meditations* 2.1: γεγόναμεν γὰρ πρὸς συνεργίαν, "we came into being for cooperation," with the hands/feet/eyelids/teeth series [verify].
- Marcus, *Meditations* 11.8: the branch severed from its neighbour is severed from the whole tree; the human being, uniquely, may rejoin. The one Stoic passage about exit from and re-entry into a body.
- Epictetus, *Diss.* 2.10.4-5 (the citizen deliberating as a hand or foot would if it had reason) and 2.5.24-26.
- Cicero, *De officiis* 1.22: *homines hominum causa esse generatos, ut ipsi inter se aliis alii prodesse possent* [verify]; and *De finibus* 3.62-63 on *oikeiōsis*.
- Seneca, *De ira* 2.31.7: *in adiutorium mutuum geniti sumus* [verify] — short enough to serve as an epigraph, but a claim about what we are made for, not about what interdependence does.
- Seneca, *De beneficiis* 4.18.1 (*tolle societatem...* [verify]); the treatise entire is a theory of reciprocal exchange, and is nearer the project's object than anything in Marcus.
- Hierocles, the concentric circles (Stobaeus 4.671-673): the only Stoic text concerned with the *scale* of the cooperating group, which is the variable the pooling result turns on.
- Plutarch, *De Alexandri fortuna* 329a-b, reporting Zeno's *Republic*.

## The Marcus passage that fits the hypothesis rather than the result

*Meditations* 6.42: πάντες εἰς ἓν ἀποτέλεσμα συνεργοῦμεν, οἱ μὲν εἰδότως..., οἱ δὲ ἀνεπιστάτως [verify] — all cooperate toward one outcome, some knowingly, others without awareness. A close antique restatement of the survivorship claim: the institutions recur not because anyone derived the mathematics, but because the trajectories that pooled persisted. See [[Time-average optimization is a survivorship property not an intention]].

6.54 remains the better epigraph for the *result* — what is not good for the swarm is not good for the bee states the alignment of individual and collective growth directly. 6.42 is the better epigraph for the *hypothesis*. Retaining 6.54 and citing 6.42 in the Objective 1 prose costs nothing and pays twice.

## The objection to anticipate

A reviewer with classical training will notice if the epigraph is asked to carry more than it can. The Stoic cooperation topos is about moral membership in a cosmic community; it is not a theory of material risk-sharing, and reading it as one is precisely the anachronism Objective 1 disclaims when it insists each tradition be read in its own categories. The arch and *De off.* 3.21-22 survive that objection because both argue from interdependence as a structural condition. The rest should be quoted, if at all, as evidence of the *idiom* in which cooperation was articulated — which is the project's own stated interest — and never as an antique anticipation of the mathematics.
