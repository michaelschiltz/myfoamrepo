# Archives départementales de l'Hérault — Montpellier

Montpellier was a Mediterranean trading city with a notarial tradition from the thirteenth century and, for a period, a possession of the Crown of Aragon.

## Why it is in the set

**It is the French place where the Catalan commenda and the French practice meet.** The Aragonese connection is not incidental: Montpellier belonged to the kings of Majorca and then of Aragon before passing definitively to France in 1349, so its instruments were drawn inside one legal world and continued to be drawn under another.

That is the configuration [[Archivio di Stato di Palermo]] offers for the Genoese-Catalan meeting and [[Archives departementales des Bouches-du-Rhone - Marseille]] for the Italian-French one. **Three such sites is a pattern that could be worked**, and Montpellier is the one with a documented change of sovereign over a stable notariate — which means the same notaries, in the same town, before and after.

**If a form's clauses change when the sovereign changes and the merchants do not, that is evidence about law. If they do not change, that is evidence about custom.** Either way it is an answer, which is rare.

⚠️ Wholly unverified: series, extent, survival and imaging. The argument above is the reason to look.
