# Pooling raises the rate and individuation draws the boundary

**The ergodicity result dissolves the cooperation dilemma without producing a new individual. Peters and Adamou model existing entities that pool and share; the pooled total exceeds the sum of the loners' because of the growth comparison, not because a unit with its own boundary has formed, and the transition to multicellularity appears in the paper as motivation, not result. The hive marks the further step: a store that is pooled, and also a germline separated from a soma, policing of worker reproduction, nestmate recognition and collective defence. The project has two levels to keep apart — pooling, in which the members remain the bearers of interest, and individuation, in which the collective becomes one.**

## The two levels

- **Pooling.** Members re-share each period and each grows at $\mu - \sigma^2/2N$ ([[The expectation value describes a pool that re-shares not a population]]). Ergodicity explains why this is the expected case, and Fant et al. 2023 make the horizon an empirical variable: below a critical horizon $T^*$ defection is favoured, above it sharing is stable. *Qirāḍ*, *commenda* and *kō* sit here, re-forming on a short cycle.
- **Individuation.** A new unit acquires a boundary, continuity, and the means to police and defend both. Ergodicity supplies the reason the members' interests align on the long horizon. It does not by itself explain the boundary, the policing or the perpetuity.

## How this maps on the existing strands

[[Pooling approaches the infinite ensemble and perpetual succession the infinite horizon]] already pairs the two infinite limits with the two strands. This note adds the biological reading of the second: perpetuity is one property of an individuated unit, and it cannot be had without a boundary that separates the unit's trajectory from its members' — which is what [[Entity shielding and owner shielding protect different trajectories]] calls entity shielding. In the transition literature the analogue is the germline–soma separation that Cremer makes the precondition of colony immunity ([[Social immunity is a consequence of individuation not evidence for it]]).

## The reply to inclusive fitness

West et al. 2015 and Gardner and Grafen 2009 treat the group as a maximising agent once conflict is negligible. The alignment that permits this is, under multiplicative dynamics, a consequence of the dynamics: on the time-average horizon the members' and the pool's growth rates coincide. That is a derivation of the alignment condition, not an appeal to relatedness, and it is the project's distinctive contribution to the debate — provided it is not stretched into a derivation of the boundary, which it is not.

## Revision 2026-11-13 — the boundary comes first

Clejan, Congleton and Lerch 2022, read in full, reverse the order this note implies. A well-defined group fitness emerges only under two conditions, and the second is a boundary: resources produced inside the group "are not shared" with resident groups. When the invaders share with residents, each part's fitness is still maximised separately; when they do not, part fitnesses become undefined and the group's growth rate — the dominant eigenvalue — is the only fitness there is ("the distinctness condition implies some boundary for the invading group"). Read against Peters and Adamou, the bridge is exact: a pool that re-shares with everyone raises each member's time-average rate and creates no unit; the same pool closed to outsiders is the minimal case of a new individual. **Closure is not a later step after pooling. It is what turns a pool into a unit.** For the archive this is a coding question about every pooling form: was the shared fund open to outsiders' claims or closed against them? Closure against members' creditors is entity shielding; closure against non-members' participation is membership. Both, not pooling, are the markers of individuation.

Boomsma and Gawne 2018 add a second constraint: in biology, aggregation of unrelated units has never produced a superorganism. See [[The hive is the wrong model for pools of unrelated partners]].
