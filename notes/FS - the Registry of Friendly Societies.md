# `FS` — the Registry of Friendly Societies

At [[The National Archives - Kew]]. Departmental records of the Registry of Friendly Societies and of the Friendly Societies Commission.

## Why it matters to the census

The `mutual_pole` and friendly-society rows are coded from British material, and this is the administrative record behind them: **the state's register of mutual benefit societies**, with the rules societies were required to deposit.

**Deposited rules are the best kind of evidence this project gets.** A society that must lodge its rules with a registrar produces a standardised, dated, serial statement of its own constitution — contribution, benefit, membership, governance — which is exactly what the census codes and rarely finds stated. It is the British counterpart of the codified merchant usage recorded at [[Archivo Foral de Bizkaia - the Consulado de Bilbao]], and there are thousands of instances rather than one.

⚠️ Subseries, extents, dates and survival are unestablished; `FS 1` and the registered-rules series should be identified specifically. ⚠️ Nothing is imaged.

## The opportunity, stated carefully

**A registry series turns the mutual society from a case into a population.** Where the census currently codes one or two societies from monographs, a registry could in principle supply a denominator — the configuration [[The Sound Toll Registers]] supplies for Baltic shipping and nothing else in the survey supplies for anything.

⚠️ Whether the deposited rules survive in quantity, and whether they are catalogued to society level, decides this entirely. **Check before hoping.**
