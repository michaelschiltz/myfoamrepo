# The stationarity presumption has four sources and only one is an adage

The preference for stationary, mean-reverting models in economics and economic history is usually put down to the maxim that things return to equilibrium. The maxim is the visible top of a stack of four commitments, which differ in age and in kind and must be answered differently.

## 1. Inherited before it could be noticed

Peters' Gresham lecture puts it historically: economics adopted probabilistic description in the 1650s and retained its original concepts of randomness, while the revolution that separated time from ensemble came with Boltzmann in the 1870s. The expectation operator was in place two centuries before the concept existed that would show what it assumes. This is tacit in the strict sense: the assumption is older than the vocabulary needed to state it. [[Economics had its ensemble moment in 1738 and psychologized it]] is the same story one step on.

## 2. Paid as the price of method

- **Haavelmo (1944).** The data are one realisation of a stochastic process; with one history only, a law of large numbers over time requires stationarity and ergodicity. Without them there is no estimand.
- **Samuelson's correspondence principle (*Foundations*, 1947).** Dynamic stability is assumed so that comparative statics acquire signs. Stability is postulated, not found.
- **The promotion to epistemology.** Samuelson's later "ergodic hypothesis" as the condition of economics as a science, and Lucas (1977) confining economic reasoning to recurrent situations — risk, not uncertainty — turn the price into a criterion of scientificity.
- **The unit-root episode is the test case.** When Nelson and Plosser (1982) found macroeconomic series non-stationary, the response was differencing and cointegration (Engle and Granger 1987): stationarity restored by transformation. Non-stationarity was a nuisance to remove, not a fact to explain.

This layer can be pure tractability and needs no ideology — the point of [[Separate the sociological and the methodological claims about economics]].

## 3. A theory of what counts as an explanation

If outcomes relax to a state fixed by fundamentals, the explanation is timeless and history supplies transients: economics explains, history narrates. Non-ergodicity returns explanatory authority to sequence and contingency, which is the historian's ground. The QWERTY dispute made the stake explicit: David (1985) argued lock-in; Liebowitz and Margolis (1990; 1995) answered with a prior that competition undoes inefficient lock-in, not with mathematics. Davis and Weinstein frame their bombing paper as fundamentals against increasing returns.

This is a **sociological** claim and falls under the discipline of [[Separate the sociological and the methodological claims about economics]]: it needs a mechanism, not a correlation. The candidate mechanism is jurisdictional — a non-ergodic world relocates explanation from law to sequence, and so from the economist to the historian. It is a hypothesis, not a finding.

## 4. Built into the statistical tool

[[Regression was invented as a stationarity argument]].

## The challenges, and why they did not land

The Post-Keynesians — Davidson against Samuelson; Robinson, *History versus Equilibrium* (1974); Hicks, "Some Questions of Time in Economics" (1976); Shackle; Georgescu-Roegen (1971) on mechanical, reversible time — lost the argument, partly because Davidson's "ergodic" is the loose fourth sense of [[The word ergodic carries four incompatible senses]]. The far-from-equilibrium and complexity programmes (Prigogine; Anderson, Arrow and Pines 1988; Arthur) stayed on the side of theory. Neither reached the persistence literature, for the reason given in [[Causal inference removes time as an interval where equilibrium removed it as iteration]].

## Verdict

An incapability built into the inferential grammar (layers 1, 2 and 4), protected by an unwillingness (layer 3) whose mechanism remains to be shown. The application can press layers 1, 2 and 4 as hygiene; layer 3 belongs to the thinking pieces.
