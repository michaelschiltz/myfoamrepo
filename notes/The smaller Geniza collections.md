# The smaller Geniza collections

**One note for some fifty holders, because at the census's grain they do not need more.** The Friedberg project counts about sixty institutions holding Geniza material. Beyond Cambridge, New York, Oxford, St Petersburg and Manchester, the ones named in the survey literature:

- **British Library**, London
- **Alliance Israélite Universelle**, Paris
- **Library of the Hungarian Academy of Sciences** — the Kaufmann collection — Budapest
- **University of Pennsylvania**, Katz Center, Philadelphia
- **Westminster College**, Cambridge — its Lewis-Gibson fragments now jointly owned by Cambridge and the Bodleian
- the **Younes and Soraya Nazarian Library**, Haifa, and private collections

⚠️ Sizes, catalogues and imaging not checked for any of them.

## Why they get no separate notes

**Because the union layers already reach them.** [[Friedberg Genizah Project and Ktiv - the image union layer]] was built to gather images from every holder, and [[MiDRASH - the machine transcription of the Geniza]] reads what Ktiv holds. **A fragment in Budapest is, for a desk researcher, as near as one in Cambridge** — which is the reverse of Europe, where a small archive's material is typically the least visible.

**The one reason to open a separate note later**: a partnership instrument or a pious-foundation account the census needs turns out to sit in one of them and not to be imaged. Record it then.
