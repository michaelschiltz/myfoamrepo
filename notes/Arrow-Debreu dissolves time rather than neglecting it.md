# Arrow-Debreu dissolves time rather than neglecting it

The standard complaint that general equilibrium "ignores time" is weak and easily rebutted. The accurate charge is stronger: the framework **removes time by construction**, and does so through a device it states openly.

Commodities in Debreu are indexed by date as well as by physical kind, location and state of nature. A "dynamic" economy is thereby rendered formally identical to a static one over a larger commodity space, and all trade clears in a single market at the outset. Time enters as a coordinate, exactly like location — and a coordinate is a label, not an order. Nothing in the formalism distinguishes the sequence $t_1, t_2, t_3$ from any relabelling of it, because no operation is ever iterated: the equilibrium is a fixed point satisfying simultaneous conditions, established by topological argument rather than by following anything forward.

This is why the framework never confronts the question the ergodicity programme forces. **Choosing between a time average and an ensemble average presupposes an iteration to average over.** Where the whole future is settled in one clearing operation there is no sequence of realised states, no trajectory, and therefore no second candidate for the averaging operator. The expectation is not preferred over the time average; the time average is not definable.

Stating it this way has two advantages over the usual complaint. It is textually accurate rather than polemical — the dated-commodity device is not a hidden assumption but the framework's advertised method of handling time. And it locates the disagreement precisely, which the loose version does not: the objection is not that equilibrium theory lacks dynamics but that its representation of time is order-insensitive by construction, so that an absorbing barrier has nowhere in the formalism to sit. An agent who is ruined at $t_2$ still trades at $t_3$ in the dated-commodity market, because the market cleared before $t_2$ occurred.

The complementary error is to suppose that adding iteration is sufficient. It is not; see [[The representative agent is the ergodicity assumption in costume]], where the equivalence is restored by a different device in models that do iterate.
