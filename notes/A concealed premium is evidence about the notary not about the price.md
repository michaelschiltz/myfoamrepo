# A concealed premium is evidence about the notary not about the price

Where a peril was priced but the price was written into some other number, an instrument that asks
whether the cost of bearing the peril was set in advance will read zero. The Mediterranean notarial
record is systematically of this kind, and two editors working sixty years and two corpora apart say
so independently.

Zeno, on the Palermo charter-parties in which the shipmaster lends to the merchant:

> Il premio di questa forma speciale di assicurazione poteva essere stato cumulato nel prezzo del
> nolo … ma poteva essere stato incluso nel prestito e cioè in quella maggior somma della quale il
> noleggiatore si riconosceva debitore mentre con tutta probabilità ne aveva ricevuto una minore.

Either into the freight rate, or into the gap between the sum acknowledged and the sum received. He
gives the reason: the decretal *Naviganti*, and the fact that the notaries, *non conoscendo ancora il
meccanismo giuridico dell'assicurazione, erano proclivi ad inquadrare l'istituto nelle forme del
mutuo*.

De Roover, on the Genoese and Marseillese *cambium maritimum*, states the same thing as one of his
two objects:

> to illustrate how **the lender's profit, since it was not stated openly, was cleverly concealed in
> the exchange rate or rates**, so that the usurious character of the contract was not apparent at
> the first glance.

Two constraints, both external to the conception of risk: an ecclesiastical prohibition, and the
absence of a juridical form into which insurance could be drafted. Neither is a fact about whether
anyone could price a peril.

## The control case

Schuster prints the northern material, and it does not conceal. The *Bodmereiprämie* is stated
openly as *Entgelt für die Gefahrübernahme*, calibrated to the individual voyage, running c. 20–30%:
a Lübeck *Bodmereibrief* of **1431** at a third of capital for six months; 16% for the few days
Hamburg–Stettin as late as 1887.

**Same instrument, same century, priced openly in Lübeck and buried in Palermo.** The variable that
separates them is the reach of the prohibition, not a difference in how the peril was conceived. That
is as close to a controlled comparison as this material offers, and the northern half of it has never
been in the census.

## Consequences

For coding: a premium recovered by inference from a freight rate or an exchange rate is
**`analyst-imposed`**, never `articulated`. Coding it `articulated` would launder a notarial evasion
into evidence about the conception of risk — the same move as coding a source's silence as an
observed absence.

For the argument: the ecclesiastical prohibition is a **filter on the record**, and a filter must be
modelled before the record is read as a signal. See
[[Refusals are observations of the filter not inferences from survivors]], and
[[Naviganti delayed the priced premium it is credited with dating]] for the doctrinal chronology that
makes the filter datable.
