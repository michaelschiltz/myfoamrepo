# BelgicaPress and KBR — the thin full-text layer

<https://www.belgicapress.be/> · KBR, the Royal Library of Belgium

**Over 1.2 million** digitised historical newspaper pages, full-text searchable, free, and recently extended to the German-speaking community's press.

## The comparison, which is the point of this note

[[Delpher - the full-text layer]] holds **over 18 million** pages. The ratio is roughly **fifteen to one**, between two countries of comparable size whose commercial histories are entangled and which for this project's period were often one economic region.

**A comparative claim drawn across the two corpora would therefore be measuring library budgets.** This is the same prohibition already standing for the VOC and the EIC in [[The VOC-EIC digital asymmetry is an access regime not a survival difference]], and it applies here with less excuse, because the two press corpora are superficially the same kind of object and invite exactly the comparison they cannot support.

## What it is still good for

Everything that does not require a denominator. Tracing a named firm, a named natie, a bankruptcy, an insurance notice; establishing that a practice existed and roughly when. **Existence claims survive a thin corpus; frequency claims do not.**

⚠️ The chronological coverage is weighted to the nineteenth and twentieth centuries — the Belgian press begins late relative to the Dutch — so for the project's early modern spine this is a supplementary source rather than a primary one. Establish the earliest holdings before relying on it for anything before 1800.
