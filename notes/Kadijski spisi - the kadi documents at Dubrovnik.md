# *Kadijski spisi* — the kadi documents at Dubrovnik

Subseries `HR-DADU-7.2.4` of [[Acta Turcarum - the Ottoman series at Dubrovnik]]. **About 2,000 documents in 1,244 units, 15 boxes, 2.5 linear metres, 1487–1805.** Analytical inventory by Vesna Miović, 2019, published as a PDF on the archive's website.

## What it is

The acts of Ottoman courts bearing on Ragusa and Ragusans, issued along the frontier the Republic shared with the empire. **The *kadılıks* of Ljubinje and Novi dominate**; the rest come from courts in present-day Bosnia-Herzegovina, Montenegro, Serbia, Kosovo, Croatia, Albania, Greece, Bulgaria, Hungary, Turkey and Egypt.

The inventory's own summary of the matter: robberies, killings, armed clashes, **debts**, and usurpation of fields. **Debt is on the list, and that is the reason for this note.** This is the Ottoman judicial record of what went wrong between Ragusan and Ottoman parties — the loss side of the relationship, which the census has found emptiest everywhere else. See [[Insolvente Boedelskamer - Antwerp]] for why that matters.

## What the inventory does and does not give

**Item-level, by court and date, and no content.** Each entry gives the *kadılık*, the Gregorian and Hijri date, the *kadi's* name, the old signature with the document's form — original, translation, transcript or summary — and the new box and number. **It does not say what the document is about.**

So `7.2.4` is at **layer 1 at item grain with no subject access**: a researcher can find every act of the Novi court in 1634 and cannot find every debt case without opening all of them. ⚠️ No imaging advertised.

**But many originals carry an eighteenth-century Italian regesta by the Republic's dragomans**, recorded in the old signatures (for example `4400a` original, `4400b` *regesta, talijanski*). That is a contemporary summary layer in Latin script, and it is the cheapest route into the subseries — with the caveat set out at [[The Dubrovnik Ottoman documents in three scripts]].

## Why it is worth a trip

**The frontier courts are where a Ragusan debt contracted under the notarial system at home would have been pursued inside the empire.** If *Debita Notariae* — see [[The Ragusan chancery and notarial series]] — records the obligation and a *kadi's hüccet* at Novi records its enforcement, the same transaction is in two legal systems in one building. ⚠️ That match has not been made for any single transaction; it is a research design, not a finding.
