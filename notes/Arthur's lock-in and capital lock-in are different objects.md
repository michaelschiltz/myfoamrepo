# Arthur's lock-in and capital lock-in are different objects

The vault now carries two unrelated senses of *lock-in*, and letting them touch would wreck an argument that depends on both.

**Attractor lock-in** (Arthur 1989) is a property of a *stochastic process*. Under increasing returns the allocation dynamic has multiple stable fixed points; the realized path selects one and cannot subsequently leave it. Nothing is held; a basin is entered. See [[Reinforcement selects an attractor the ensemble average never occupies]].

**Capital lock-in** (Blair, Harris) is a property of a *legal form*. Contributed capital is non-withdrawable at the member's will, which is one half of the temporal binding treated in [[Lock-in and outcome-coupling are distinct bindings]]. Nothing is selected; a claim is disabled.

The two are independent along both axes. A form can be capital-locked without being attractor-locked — a chartered company whose capital cannot be withdrawn but whose organizational type never achieved market dominance. A form can be attractor-locked without capital lock-in: the *commenda* propagated across the Mediterranean by increasing returns to a shared contractual template while remaining revocable and unlocked in the legal sense.

The distinction is worth policing rather than merely noting, because the interesting historical claim lives in the *interaction*: capital lock-in plausibly raises the increasing-returns coefficient on a form, since a form whose capital cannot be withdrawn accumulates the scale that makes adoption self-reinforcing. That is a claim connecting a legal property to a dynamic one, and it is only statable if the words stay apart.

**Convention.** Write *attractor lock-in* or *capital lock-in* in full where either could be meant. Reserve the bare tag `capital-lock-in` for the legal sense already in `tags.md`; the dynamic sense takes `attractor-selection`.
