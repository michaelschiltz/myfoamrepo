# Chit funds and *kuri* — a living rotating association with no historical archive

**The South Asian rotating savings and credit association**: *chit*, *chitty*, *kuri* in Malayalam, *chit fund* in the colonial and modern record. Members subscribe periodically; each period one member takes the pot, by lot or by auction discount; the cycle runs until all have drawn.

## What is documented, and where

- **1887**: William Logan, Collector of Malabar, describes *kuri* among groups of friends in the *Malabar Manual*.
- **1894**: described as raising money for weddings and similar occasions in South India.
- **1930s**: "between 1,000 and 10,000 formal funds" a year in what is now Kerala, and **166 banks conducting chits**; the provincial banking enquiries of 1929–30 are the systematic source — [[The colonial enquiry as South Asia's layer 3]].
- **1969**: Kerala State Financial Enterprises founded as a public alternative to private organisers; **77 per cent of the state's chit capital by 2000**.
- **1982**: the Chit Funds Act; registrars of chits in each state hold the registers.

⚠️ All of the above is from a Wikipedia article read through a summary and from search results; **no manual, no enquiry volume and no register was read.**

## The archival finding

**There is no historical archive of chit funds, and there are state registers of modern ones.** A *chit* is constituted by an agreement among members and, since 1982, by a registered *chitty variola* filed with a registrar. **The custodian of the nineteenth- and early twentieth-century record would be the organiser's own papers**, and no collection of them was found. **The colonial enquiry is the whole of the accessible historical record.**

## Why the census should care

**The census codes about twenty Japanese *kō* and *mujin* rows and nothing else of this shape.** The vault already holds [[Kye is not a ROSCA and the equation is an artefact of comparison with mujin]]: **a two-way comparison between Japan and Korea cannot tell a general property of rotating associations from a property of the pair.** A South Indian *kuri* described in 1887 and 1930 by observers with no interest in Japan **is the third case that makes the comparison testable** — and it is the place to test the claim recorded in logbook 6 that a *kō* organised for the relief of a named distressed person is not reported outside Japan.
