# The Afghan corpora offer loss allocation in tenancy not commenda

**What the Bamiyan and Firuzkuh papers can give HistorEE is the incidence of loss between landlord, agent and cultivator — a transport loss, a tax shortfall, a reduced allocation — not a partnership instrument.** That maps onto the loss components of the coding ontology and onto the mutual pole, not onto the five corporate components. Why the instruments are missing is in [[The Afghan corpora show partnership as traces because their archives are a landlord's and a granary's]].

## The cases

- **Ms. Heb. 8333.18** (IEDCID101; Group I, undated, c. 1000–1040): the community of Khustgān and Angār writes to its landlord, probably Yehuda b. Daniel. His agent promised camels for a consignment of grapes, and the grapes rotted unsold; a hundred pomegranates went for three dirhams; the agent took "his share" in the villagers' absence. They threaten to stop working "the master's places" and ask for a different agent, not for compensation. Azad infers a crop-sharing tenancy (Ludden and Azad 2024, PDF pp. 8–10; ed. Haim, *ʿAtiqot* 112, 2023, 201–24).
- **Ms. Heb. 8333.36** (614/1218): the peasants of Zīrdamān refuse 20 of 90 *kharwār* of assessed grain and straw tax — "we will not give more than 70". The diwan sends an *amīr* to support the *shiḥna* in recovering it (PDF pp. 12–14).
- **Firuzkuh 100** (c. 1170s–1180s): a woman cultivator protests that her landlord, an *amīr* and *isfahsālār*, is halving her plot. Nomani's distinction between *muzāraʿa* tenants on one- or two-year contracts and tenants at will places her in the second group (PDF pp. 16–17).
- **Joint obligors**: several debtors in one *iqrār* (Ms. Heb. 8333.216, 8333.217) and levy entries "with partner" (Firuzkuh 60). ⚠️ Joint or several liability not established.

## What the grape letter shows

**A loss-allocation dispute in which no rule is cited.** The loss is the agent's doing, the complaint is the tenants', and the remedy sought is the agent's removal. Under [[The amana-daman structure refuses to infer fault from loss]] an agent holding on *amāna* bears a loss only on proof of negligence or contravention of instructions; the letter is a negligence claim in everything but name. **It is the doctrine's conduct test, run by peasants without a *qāḍī*.** ⚠️ That reading is mine, not Azad's or Haim's.

## Why it belongs in the comparison

**A crop-share is outcome-coupled risk-sharing**: the landlord's claim moves with the harvest. Scott's subsistence ethic is the reading in which share rents are tolerated where fixed claims are refused ([[Scott 1976 on the subsistence ethic as safety-first]]). The Zīrdamān refusal is the fixed claim meeting that resistance.

**Islamic jurists argued the *muzāraʿa* by analogy with the *muḍāraba*** — land and labour in place of capital and labour, the yield divided in agreed shares. ⚠️ Verify the analogy and its standing (Abū Ḥanīfa's rejection, his disciples' acceptance) against the *fiqh* literature before using it. If it holds, **the corpora are useless to the corporate-form leg and directly useful to the loss-allocation one.**

## Parallels already in hand

- **Tang Turfan loan contracts** carry a standard clause putting repayment on wife, children and guarantors if the borrower dies or absconds — a written default rule. ⚠️ Uyghur parallels to be checked in SUK.
- **Old Uyghur leases**: Zieme 1980 edits thirteen Turfan leases of the fourteenth century, written either as a fixed rent (*yaqa*) or as a 1:1 crop share (*anuq*), with tax incidence in a separate clause — [[Uyghur leases were written either as a fixed rent or as a crop share]].
- **The mutual pole**: [[Dunhuang she associations - bylaws for a funeral fund]].
