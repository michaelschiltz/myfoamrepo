# The Afghan corpora offer loss allocation in tenancy not commenda

**What the Bamiyan and Firuzkuh papers can give HistorEE is the incidence of loss between landlord, agent and cultivator — loss to the grain owed, a transport loss, a tax shortfall, a reduced allocation — not a partnership instrument. One archive writes two opposite rules: the grain owed under the lease is at the tenant's risk, "little or much"; the carriage of the crop to market is at the landlord's.** That maps onto the loss components of the coding ontology and onto the mutual pole, not onto the five corporate components. Why the instruments are missing is in [[The Afghan corpora show partnership as traces because their archives are a landlord's and a granary's]].

## The cases

- **Ms. Heb. 8333.15** (Rajab 430/1039), the written lease: Ḥusayn b. Ḥasan of Rām K.ray acknowledges taking from Abū Naṣr b. Dāniyāl three parcels of his land in the village — Pūlar, Sūyrak and Bar Bahārak — *ba-ghalla ba-muddat-i sih sāl*, "for grain, for three years", at **42 bowls (*ṭās*), two parts barley to one part wheat, "over three years 126 bowls"**, and **1½ donkey-loads (*kharwār*) of straw, "over three years 4½"**; he took it "to sow it, keep it prosperous and fulfil the *ḥaqq-i warzī*". Then (ll. 9–10): *wa harch maʾūnat ōftad, ammā andak wa bisyār, wa kāh-i sulṭānī, man Ḥusayn b. Ḥasan bidiham*. Haim glosses: "whatever loss, little or much, befalls the grain (the provisions), I, Ḥusayn b. Ḥasan, will give from my sultanic straw" (Haim 2019, Persian trans., 100). The verso docket in Hebrew script records "Ḥusayn's deed, land of Rām K.ray, Sūyrak, Bahārak and Pūlar, 42 *ṭās*" and "grain of the year 1350 [Sel.]".
  - **Rent or loan?** Haim calls it the *kirāya* (hire) of three parcels in 2019 (Persian trans., 83) and in 2023 a lease with **a loan of 42 bowls repaid threefold** (211–212). The text multiplies both quantities by the three-year term, which reads most naturally as a **fixed annual rent in kind**. The English original confirms Haim's reading: "a lease of three parcels of land … along with a loan of forty-two bowls of grain and one and a half *kharwār*s of straw … In exchange, the *muqarr lahu* will apparently receive a tripled amount of grain and straw after three years" (Haim 2019, 440; translation 433–434: "(in exchange) for grain for a period of three years"). ⚠️ "Apparently" is Haim's; the fixed-rent reading is mine and remains open.
  - **Either way the claim is fixed and the tenant carries the loss.** No share of the harvest is stated anywhere in the edited text. Haim 2023's description of the lease as crop-sharing (211–212) is ⚠️ not supported by this document; it rests on Lambton and on the income report of 1026 (8333.220 fol. 8v) where the parcel names recur.
  - ⚠️ *maʾūnat* also means "charge, expense" (Dihkhudā), which would make ll. 9–10 a clause putting all charges and the sultan's straw levy on the tenant. Haim, the editor, reads loss, and his English translation has "Whatever happens to the provisions" (433–434); follow him.
- **Ms. Heb. 8333.18** (IEDCID101; Phase I, undated, c. 1000–1040), "a commercial letter": the community of Khustgān and Angār, some 80 km north-east of Bamiyan, writes to "the master", probably Yehuda b. Daniel or his son. ʿAlī's man (*piyāda*) told them to send out the grapes — "the master has given me gold for hiring camels" — so they sent theirs first in the region, "but all our fruit was ruined and dumped to the ground", two months' fruit left behind; he sold a hundred sweet pomegranates for three dirhams and "took his cut … in our absence". If it recurs "we will not be able (to cultivate) the master's lands"; "the master has many servants there; whomever he sends there, he should tell (him that)" (Haim 2023, 218–219, translation). **Haim reads it as a complaint about irregularities in the division of the yield under a contractual agreement, with transport paid by the landowner and his representative in charge of its safe arrival** (212–213). Azad also infers crop-sharing (Ludden and Azad 2024, PDF pp. 8–10).
- **Ms. Heb. 8333.29** (Ghazna to Bamiyan): "You had said that 200 dirhams were lost by the arrival of ʿAlī and Fārisak" — Fārisak Ushtur being Yehuda's debt-collector and carrier (Haim 2023, 212; Haim 2012 [2016], 110–111, l. 34). ⚠️ The context in the letter is obscure — the writer, Yaʾir b. Ēmēd, a Ghazna shopkeeper who also buys oil on commission for the family, answers it in a sentence; it is not a loss-allocation document.
- **Ms. Heb. 8333.36** (614/1218): the peasants of Zīrdamān refuse 20 of 90 *kharwār* of assessed grain and straw tax — "we will not give more than 70". The diwan sends an *amīr* to support the *shiḥna* in recovering it (PDF pp. 12–14).
- **Firuzkuh 100** (c. 1170s–1180s): a woman cultivator protests that her landlord, an *amīr* and *isfahsālār*, is halving her plot. Nomani's distinction between *muzāraʿa* tenants on one- or two-year contracts and tenants at will places her in the second group (PDF pp. 16–17).
- **Advance sales and state loans**: two Phase II debt acknowledgments, Ms. Heb. 8333.62 (617/1220) and 8333.67 verso (618/1221), are read by Haim as advance sales of crops (*salam*), and he suggests some Phase I *iqrār*s and debt entries were prearranged sales; the royal granary of Phase II lent to cultivators (Haim 2023, 207, 216). ⚠️ The *salam* reading of Phase I is Haim's hypothesis; the term does not occur there. A forward sale is risk-pricing, not risk-sharing.
- **Sharecropping in the granary's vocabulary**: Azad reads the recurring *muʿāmala* of the Phase II receipts ("the *muʿāmala* of year 1") through al-Samarqandī's *Nawāzil*, where it denotes a sharecropping arrangement close to *muzāraʿa*, a contract for "the outlay required to ensure a healthy crop", customarily for one harvest cycle (Azad 2025, 115–116, after Tsafrir 2021). She reads the Zīrdamān peasants of 8333.36 as sharecropping tenants on the *iqṭāʿ* of the commander Sayf al-Dīn (146). ⚠️ Interpretation; in Ludden and Azad 2024 *muʿāmala* was a land-tax classification, and Azad's note to Doc. 18 allows "tax assessment or payment … also … a rental transaction" (258).
- **Seed advances and grain loans**: the warehouse issued seed to commanders and probable *iqṭāʿ* holders and could recall it if not sown; documents in both corpora speak of *qarḍ-i lāzim* (Azad 2025, 110–112). Taxes "were due regardless of the success of the harvest" (105–106 n. 44).
- **The liability rule stated in advance**: a grain note of 1219 taken *bi ḥukm-i ḍamānī*, and tax officials made personally liable for arrears — [[The Bamiyan granary wrote the liability rule into the grain transfer]].
- **Joint obligors**: several debtors in one *iqrār* (Ms. Heb. 8333.216, 8333.217) and levy entries "with partner" (Firuzkuh 60). ⚠️ Joint or several liability not established.

## What the two documents show together

**The lease puts any loss to the grain owed, "little or much", on the tenant without regard to fault — *ḍamān*. The letter treats the carriage of the crop as the landlord's charge and the loss in carriage as his agent's failure.** Two allocation rules in one landlord's papers, keyed to who controls the asset at the moment of loss.

**The letter is a loss-allocation dispute in which no rule is cited.** The loss is the agent's doing, the complaint is the tenants', and what they ask is that the next man sent be told the terms. Under [[The amana-daman structure refuses to infer fault from loss]] an agent holding on *amāna* bears a loss only on proof of negligence or contravention of instructions; the letter is a negligence claim in everything but name. **It is the doctrine's conduct test, run by peasants without a *qāḍī*.** ⚠️ The *amāna* mapping is mine; Haim's reading supports the allocation (transport at the landlord's charge) but does not invoke the doctrine.

## Why it belongs in the comparison

**A crop-share is outcome-coupled risk-sharing**: the landlord's claim moves with the harvest. Scott's subsistence ethic is the reading in which share rents are tolerated where fixed claims are refused ([[Scott 1976 on the subsistence ethic as safety-first]]). The Zīrdamān refusal is the fixed claim meeting that resistance.

**Islamic jurists argued the *muzāraʿa* by analogy with the *muḍāraba*** — land and labour in place of capital and labour, the yield divided in agreed shares. ⚠️ Verify the analogy and its standing (Abū Ḥanīfa's rejection, his disciples' acceptance) against the *fiqh* literature before using it. If it holds, **the corpora are useless to the corporate-form leg and directly useful to the loss-allocation one.**

## Parallels already in hand

- **Tang Turfan loan contracts** carry a standard clause putting repayment on wife, children and guarantors if the borrower dies or absconds — a written default rule. ⚠️ Uyghur parallels to be checked in SUK.
- **Old Uyghur leases**: Zieme 1980 edits thirteen Turfan leases of the fourteenth century, written either as a fixed rent (*yaqa*) or as a 1:1 crop share (*anuq*), with tax incidence in a separate clause — [[Uyghur leases were written either as a fixed rent or as a crop share]].
