# Peters's growth criterion is the boundary-free case of the first-passage ordering

How far Vanhoyweghen, Verbeken and Ginis 2026 depart from Peters, stated so that neither is overstated. **The principle is shared; the object is new.** Peters ranks options by an asymptotic growth rate. Vanhoyweghen and co-authors rank them by first-passage quantities — whether a boundary is hit, when, and with what probability within a horizon. Where the probability of absorption is zero their ordering reduces to Peters's criterion. It extends his criterion; it does not rival it.

## What is shared

- **The normative stance.** Judge a decision by the trajectory one lives, not by the average across parallel trajectories.
- **The extinction case.** Peters's history of the field already names Galton–Watson as "a different kind of ergodicity breaking" — [[Galton-Watson is the extinction branch Peters named and did not develop]].
- **Ruin under multiplicative dynamics.** Peters's criterion already refuses ruin there, bluntly: a gamble that can return zero has expected log return of minus infinity. The paper calls ruin "a limiting edge case" of the multiplicative setting for this reason.

## What is new

Three cases the published Peters–Adamou apparatus does not order, because its correspondence requires the transformed process to live on the whole line ([[Among isoelastic transforms only the logarithm has no boundary]]).

- **Additive dynamics with a floor.** Without a boundary Peters's criterion under additive dynamics is the expectation, and pooling gains nothing. With one, the first-passage ordering favours distance from ruin — the ground on which [[Under absorption pooling raises the probability of ruin to the power of the pool]] stands.
- **Certain absorption and finite horizons.** Where ruin will come, options are ranked by time to it or by its probability within the horizon; the asymptotic growth rate is undefined or uninformative.
- **Two boundaries.** Ruin below and a favourable target above: a dowry sum, the price of a guild share, a voyage completed.

## What it does not add

- **No pooling.** The paper treats a single agent; the cooperation result under a barrier is derived from its quantities, not stated in it.
- **No new stochastic calculus.** First-passage probabilities and times are classical; what is new is the decision ordering placed on them.
- **An ensemble step in its headline formula.** $g_\theta = (1-p_\theta)g$ is an average across paths — [[Conditioning on survival is an ensemble operation]].
