# The Prize Papers — `HCA 32` and the German money

<https://www.prizepapers.de/> · portal: <https://portal.prizepapers.de/>

**An open-access digitisation of roughly 500,000 records from the English High Court of Admiralty, 1652–1815, in more than nineteen languages, including some 160,000 undelivered letters** — documents and objects taken from ships captured by the British in wartime: trading papers, mariners' and passengers' personal archives, intercepted mail, and the court proceedings around them.

Run from the **Carl von Ossietzky University of Oldenburg** with The National Archives, funded by the **Göttingen Academy of Sciences and Humanities**, TNA and the William Nelson Cromwell Foundation, **2018–2037**.

## Why this is the most important British fact in the survey

**Because of who is paying.** The single richest early-modern commercial corpus in British custody is being opened, over nineteen years, principally with **German** academic money.

Set that beside the rest of the British picture. The company records are description-only because nobody funded them. The India Office material was opened by a commercial publisher and a Gulf sovereign foundation — [[The QDL corpus is conditioned on its funder's geography]]. **Three corpora, three funders, none of them the British state**, and the resulting map of what is readable from a desk is a map of those three decisions.

[[The VOC-EIC digital asymmetry is an access regime not a survival difference]] argued that the Dutch–English difference is a fact about science policy after 2010. **Britain now shows the general form: it is not that a state under-funds its own record, it is that the record gets opened by whoever has a reason to open it**, and their reasons have shapes.

## What the corpus is good for, and the shape of its filter

Seized ships' papers are **commercial documents captured in transit** — bills of lading, insurance policies, accounts, correspondence between principals and factors. For this project that is instrument evidence of the best kind.

⚠️ **But the selection rule is capture in wartime by one navy.** The corpus over-represents the enemies of Britain, wartime voyages, and routes the Royal Navy could reach — which is a filter with a very definite shape, and unusually, a knowable one. [[Refusals are observations of the filter not inferences from survivors]] applies, and here the filter can be modelled rather than merely conceded.
