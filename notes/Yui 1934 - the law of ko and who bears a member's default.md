# Yui 1934 — the law of kō, and who bears a member's default

由井健之助『頼母子講の法律関係』, 『司法研究報告書集』第18輯1 (司法省調査課, March 1934), 175 frames. NDL Digital pid 1445784, **open online by 裁定** (2019), so the IIIF image service is closed and the pages were read in the viewer, 21 October 2026. Not downloaded. The 1935 岩波書店 edition, 『頼母子講と其の法律関係』 (pid 1280052), is the same work. It is a report of the ministry's 司法研究 programme, a doctrinal survey of 大審院 and lower-court decisions under the Civil Code of 1898.

## What it says

**Two types, by where the relations run** (pp. 108–113):

- **集團的頼母子.** All members form one body and the relations are multilateral, between members. Yui reads it as an unnamed contract resembling a 民法組合, so the partnership articles apply by analogy unless the rules or custom say otherwise. He says most kō are of this type, non-business kō almost all.
- **個別的頼母子.** A 講元 or 會主 recruits members as his own business, and the relations run between him and each member, none between members. The licensed 營業無盡 is of this type, but **many kō of this type ran outside the 1915 無盡業法** because their 講元 did not trade as a business (p. 114 n.).

The courts treated the type as a question of fact, and Yui argues the 組合 type should be presumed and the other side made to prove the exception (p. 113).

**In the 組合 type the fund is the members' and they are each other's creditors** (pp. 186–187). The contributions are co-owned (民法 668); a member keeps a share in the fund through the rounds; and the 大審院 upheld a member's right to sue each winner directly for his repayment, so that unpaid members can sue a defaulting winner themselves (p. 187 n. 3). An old 大審院 holding that each meeting closed its own accounts, leaving no claim afterwards, Yui rejects because it would make the kō a wager.

**The 世話人 does not, by default, answer for a member's default** (pp. 164–167):

- The officers of a 組合 kō stand to the members as mandataries (民法 671, applying the 委任 articles). They owe a good manager's care in collecting and paying over.
- They are liable for loss caused by negligent collection, and for failing to hold the meetings until the kō's term ran out.
- They are **not** bound to pay a winner out of their own funds when a member has not paid. The 東京控訴院 held that a 世話人 owes no such duty "講則ノ規定其他特別ノ證據アルニ非サレハ", and that a custom to the contrary must be proved (p. 166).
- **But rules often impose it, especially in kō of the individual type.** Article 34 of the 無盡業法 imposes it on every licensed operator by statute. Yui puts the principle as a scale: the weaker the members' trust controls over the officer, the heavier his liability (p. 167).
- Where several officers carry such a guarantee, the 大審院 held the resulting damages divisible among them, not joint (p. 167).

**The officers hold the security as trustees** (pp. 168–175). They take the winner's 借用證書 and the 抵當權 or guarantor in their own names, and consuming what they hold is embezzlement. On an officer's death the claim passes to his heirs for collection, by a 1931 大審院 decision that departed from an earlier one.

**The sanctions for default are the rules', and the law fills in only where they are silent** (pp. 202–204). Yui's examples from rules in force: delay interest on a winner's late repayment; loss of the 割戻配當 after two missed payments; **exclusion from the kō after two missed payments**; and acceleration of the whole remaining debt on a winner's default. An unpaid member who defaults may lose his claim to his own contributions. A winner's repayment is a debt like a loan's, due on its dates whether or not a meeting is held (pp. 193, 207).

**In the individual type the fund is the 講元's** (p. 205). Members have no share, only a claim against him for what they have paid in, and they have no vote.

## Why it matters

- **It turns the 1928 study's test into case law, and qualifies it.** [[Shiho kenkyu 1928 - who answers for a ko and how it was defrauded]] says a mutual kō puts a member's default on the other members. Yui shows how: the fund is co-owned, and the members hold the claims. He also shows the default rule is only a default. The rules often move the loss onto the 世話人, and the statute moves it onto the operator.
- **So who bears a co-member's default is a clause, and it can be coded from the clause.** It is not a latent property of "mutual" kō, and the strain recorded in logbook 6 (2026-10-21 (ii)) is a question a rulebook answers. Yui's scale is the 1934 jurist's version of the entity-shielding question. Where the members hold the claims, the fund is theirs. Where the officer guarantees the payout, he is the debtor the members look to, which is how an operator's fund becomes his own.
- **The phase caution is strict.** This is Civil Code doctrine, built by courts after 1898 on an analogy with the partnership articles. Where a Tokugawa rulebook is silent, Yui's default rule says nothing about it. The coding must come from the instrument's own clause, or be `.NR`.
- **Exclusion after missed payments is a rule-level sanction the census can see.** It is one more source on how a kō disciplined its members, alongside `ko_meio_1498`'s 座敷衆中 and `ko_mitarai_gin_1805`'s 請合人.
