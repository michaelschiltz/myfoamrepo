# Chinese genealogies — Shanghai Library and FamilySearch

家谱 / 族谱. **The lineage's own printed record** — descent lines, biographies, and very often **the lineage's rules, its ancestral hall and its trust land**: the regulations of a charitable estate (义庄规条), lists of sacrificial and charitable fields (祭田, 义田), and the terms on which their income was distributed. **Most surviving genealogies were printed or hand-copied in the Qing and Republic, and a lineage reprinted its rules in each new edition.**

## Where they are

- **Shanghai Library** — the largest institutional holding. **About 40,000 titles in 380,000 volumes, covering 567 surnames**, on a December 2025 report; a dedicated genealogy library opened on **29 December 2025** at 746 Changle Road. An older count gives **11,700 pre-1949 titles in about 100,000 volumes**. ⚠️ The two counts differ in date and scope and were not reconciled. The library compiled **《中国家谱总目》, a union catalogue of 52,401 Chinese genealogies held worldwide** — the layer-1 finding aid for the whole genre.
- **National Library of China** — the second holding, **3,006 titles as of 1990** on the same older survey. ⚠️ Current figure not found.
- **FamilySearch** — **China Collection of Genealogies, 1239–2014**, images from **institutions and private holdings in China, North America and South-East Asia**. The collection page gives **27,406,879 personas**; the collection's wiki page says it **has no searchable index, only images**. ⚠️ The contradiction was not resolved; the persona count may be a partial or machine index. Image access "may vary" by contract and was not tested.

## Layers

- **Layer 1**: the union catalogue; library OPACs.
- **Layer 2**: FamilySearch images at scale; Shanghai Library's digitised genealogies, released in batches (third batch in 2017; more than 8,000 titles by 2018). ⚠️ Current online total and access terms not checked.
- **Layer 3 for the rules**: **the printed genealogy is itself a printed edition of the lineage's rules.** Reprints and anthologies exist; the rules are not transcribed as a corpus. ⚠️
- **Script**: printed or neatly copied classical Chinese. **The easiest script in the Chinese survey.**

## What it bears on

**The grant's "lineage trusts", and the candidate parked at logbook 2, 2026-09-11 (viii).** **A lineage charitable estate's rules — who receives rice, on what event, at what rate — are printed in the lineage's genealogy.** That is the rulebook layer the census requires before a *kō* moves a cell, in a genre that is catalogued worldwide and imaged by the hundred thousand. **What the genealogy does not carry is the estate's accounts**, which survive, where they survive, as private documents — see [[Huizhou documents - the largest private contract corpus]]. **Rules at layer 3, accounts at layer 1 at best: the record type sets the resolution, again.**

**Two warnings.** **A genealogy is the lineage's self-presentation**: rules are what the compilers wished to print, not proof of practice. **And FamilySearch's selection is a filming programme's selection**, shaped by what libraries and families agreed to film.
