# The eight thousand sacks — the Grand Secretariat archives sold as waste paper

八千麻袋事件. **The Chinese instance of pattern 3, and it is almost exactly the Ottoman one ten years earlier.**

## What happened

- **About 1921** the Historical Museum under the Beiyang government, short of money for salaries, **sold the Grand Secretariat's archive in sacks to paper dealers for pulping** — some eight thousand sacks, the second batch weighed at more than 148,000 *jin*.
- **Early 1922** Luo Zhenyu and Jin Liang bought back some 120,000–130,000 *jin*; part had already been dispersed.
- **End of 1924** Luo sold them to Li Shengduo for 18,000 *yuan*.
- **1928–29** the **Institute of History and Philology of Academia Sinica** bought them for 20,000 *yuan*, Chen Yinke handling the purchase — about 120,000 *jin*, "seven thousand sacks" in Fu Sinian's estimate — delivered in May 1929.
- **The first batch, running from the Yongle to the Wanli reign, was never recovered.**

Ma Zimu, *The Paper* (澎湃新闻), 24 June 2015.

## The survival rule, and its shape

**A sale selects by store.** What was sold was the Grand Secretariat's warehouse of routine documents — what the Republic judged worthless because it was routine. **So the surviving Grand Secretariat record is what scholars bought back from dealers, from what dealers had not yet pulped or resold**, and it is now in Taipei — see [[Academia Sinica IHP - the Grand Secretariat archives in Taipei]].

**Compare [[National Library Sofia - the Oriental Department and the 1931 sale]]**: an Istanbul finance office selling Treasury papers as waste to a Bulgarian paper factory in 1931. **Two successor states, ten years apart, disposed of an imperial archive by weight**, and in both cases the survivors are in another polity. **The European survey's losses were fires; the non-European ones include sales.**
