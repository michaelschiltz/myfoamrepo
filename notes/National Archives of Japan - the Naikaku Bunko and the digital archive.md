# National Archives of Japan — the Naikaku Bunko and the digital archive

国立公文書館, Kitanomaru, Tokyo. Digital archive: <https://www.digital.archives.go.jp/>.

## The Naikaku Bunko

**The shogunate's libraries, carried into the Meiji state.** A library section under the Dajōkan from 1873, the Dajōkan Bunko from 1884, the **Naikaku Bunko** from 1885, merged into the National Archives in 1971. Its core is the shogunal **Momijiyama Bunko**, with the Shōheizaka academy, the Wagaku Kōdansho and the Igakukan, **and documents the government collected — including manuscripts of Tōdaiji and of the Daijōin**. About **490,000 volumes** (280,000 Japanese, 180,000 Chinese, and Western). Catalogue of about 430,000 volumes searchable online since April 2005.

## What it holds for the census

**The earliest *kō* row's source is imaged here.** `ko_furuichi_sengan` — the Nara *senkan tanomoshi* convened by the Furuichi — is coded from Miura 1959's reading of the ***Daijōin jisha zōjiki***, the Kōfukuji Daijōin abbots' diary. **Volumes of the diary, including its *shihai monjo* (documents on the versos), are in the digital archive and in Japan Search.** ⚠️ Whether every volume Miura cites is imaged not checked. **The census's medieval row can be checked against the image from a desk**, which is more than can be said for its nineteenth-century rows.

**And a burnt remnant of the Treasury.** The Ministry of Finance's records burned in 1923; a small group of fire-damaged survivors, including records of the former shogunate, is held here. See [[Shiryo Net and the disaster survival rule]]. ⚠️ Contents and count not established.

## Layer

- Catalogue online; **images for selected manuscripts** — the Daijōin diary and important cultural properties such as the Tenpō provincial maps. ⚠️ Image counts not captured.
- **The Meiji state's records** (公文録, 太政類典) are the archive's larger subject and outside this pass, except where a Meiji row needs them: `ko_mochiyori_yutsu_1876` is a Meiji association. ⚠️ Not searched.
