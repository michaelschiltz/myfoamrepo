# Antiquarian booksellers — the top tier and where economic history sits

**Prestige and reliability are separate properties and the trade routinely conflates them.** Prestige is a function of the objects a house has handled — Gutenberg Bibles, the Codex Sinaiticus, Althorp's Caxtons. Reliability is a function of description practice: whether the catalogue tells you what is wrong with the copy. The firms below score on both, but the reason to prefer them is the second, and the second is largely institutional rather than personal.

## The institutional guarantee is the actual product

ILAB — founded Copenhagen 1948, now 22 national associations, businesses in 38 countries, 1,600+ affiliates — is not a directory. Admission runs through sponsorship, investigation and a vote of peers, and members bind themselves to the **Code of Usages and Customs**, which imposes four obligations that are the entire reason to buy through the trade rather than at auction or on a marketplace:

- **Disclosure of all defects, restorations and sophistications.** Withholding by technically-true statement is a violation. This is the clause that matters: a sophisticated copy is one assembled from parts, and a marketplace listing has no obligation to tell you.
- **Full cash refund including return shipping** on misrepresented material; buyer signals intent to return within ten days absent other agreement.
- **Guarantee of authenticity**, with the obligation to substantiate it. Questionable material is returnable.
- **Clear title**, and an obligation to contact the original owner and cooperate with law enforcement on stolen property.

The national bodies underneath ILAB — **ABA** (UK, 1906), **ABAA** (US), **NVvA** (Netherlands), **ABAJ** (Japan, founded November 1964 by ten Tokyo/Osaka/Kyoto houses, ILAB member from 1965, now ~30 members) — apply the same standard locally.

**Operative rule: ILAB/ABA/ABAA/ABAJ affiliation is the floor, not the ceiling.** It converts a purchase from *caveat emptor* to a warranted transaction. Everything below is about who is good above that floor.

## The great generalist houses

- **Bernard Quaritch Ltd** — London, founded 1847 by a German émigré with almost no capital; by 1880 the catalogues ran 28,000 items over 2,166 pages, and Quaritch's own 1884 purchases of a Gutenberg Bible (£3,900) and the 1459 Mainz Psalter (£4,950) were successive world records. Incunabula, manuscripts, Bibles and liturgies, early English literature, cartography, Americana, bindings. Bedford Row since 2019; owned by the collector John Koh. Founding member of the ABA. The default first call for anything early and important.
- **Maggs Bros. Ltd** — London, 1853, sixth generation of the family, Royal Warrant, 48 Bedford Square with a Mayfair room at 46 Curzon Street. Bought a Gutenberg Bible from Soviet Russia in 1932 and sold it on by telegram to Bodmer; brokered the Codex Sinaiticus to the British Museum for £100,000; handled Napoleon's letters to Josephine. Broadest departmental spread in the trade.
- **Henry Sotheran Ltd** — founded York 1761, London from 1815; claims the oldest continuously trading bookshop in the UK. Sold Sterne's library (1768), Dickens's (1870), Althorp with the Caxtons (1892, £210,000), a vellum Gutenberg to J. P. Morgan (1896), and the Warwick Castle Shakespeare library to Folger (1897). Left Sackville Street in 2024 for Charing Cross Road and Cecil Court.
- **Peter Harrington** — London, 1969; Chelsea (100 Fulham Road) and Mayfair (43 Dover Street from 2014). The commercially dominant modern house: fifteenth to twentieth centuries, heavy in literature, modern firsts, and landmark works in science, philosophy and political economy. Deep pockets and aggressive at auction.
- **William Reese Company** — New Haven. The reference house for Americana and a serious literature department. Reese died in 2018; the firm continued under Dorothy Hurt and is now **jointly owned by James Cummins Bookseller (New York) and Peter Harrington (London)**, with Nick Aretakis running day-to-day operations from the New Haven premises.
- **Antiquariaat Forum / Asher Rare Books** — 't Goy-Houten, near Utrecht. Forum founded Utrecht 1970 by Sebastiaan Hesselink, moved to 't Goy 1994, run by Laurens Hesselink from 2008; **Asher Rare Books, founded Berlin 1830 by Adolph Asher, acquired 2010**. Travel and exploration, natural history, Middle East and Islamic world, medicine, science, emblem books, incunabula, manuscripts. The strongest Dutch house and therefore the first place to look for VOC-adjacent and Low Countries material.
- **Antiquariat Inlibris, Gilhofer Nfg.** — Vienna, Rathausstraße 19, trading since 1883. 50,000+ rare books, manuscripts and autographs under Hugo Wetscherek. Unusually strong on economics, Austriaca, Middle East, and archival material that other houses will not take on.

## Early printing and manuscripts

Where description quality separates dealers most sharply, because the claims are bibliographical and checkable.

- **Sokol Books** — London (239A Fulham Road since 2014), roughly forty years in business. English STC books printed before 1640, Continental printing to the mid-seventeenth century, and medieval/renaissance manuscripts. Sells on the accuracy and depth of the descriptions and on copies in contemporary bindings — which is the right thing to sell on.
- **Bruce McKittrick Rare Books** — Narberth, Pennsylvania. Continental European printed books, sixteenth to eighteenth century. Sixty-four numbered stock catalogues plus more than twenty *hors série*: the catalogue run is itself a scholarly resource.
- **Musinsky Rare Books** — New York. Early printing and book history, bibliographically exacting.
- **Jonathan A. Hill, Bookseller** — New York. Science, medicine, natural history, early printing; long-standing relationships with Japanese institutional buyers.
- **Libreria Antiquaria Alberto Govi** — Modena. Early Italian printing; Fabrizio Govi sits on the Breslauer Prize jury for bibliography, which is the trade's own signal of bibliographical seriousness.
- **PRPH Books** — New York, Upper East Side, opened 23 October 2013 as the joint venture of Umberto Pregliasco and Filippo Rotundo (whose own firm is *Philobiblon*). Manuscripts and early printed books at the top of the market; the *One Thousand Years of Bibliophily* catalogues are reference-grade bibliography rather than sales literature.
- **Libreria Antiquaria Pregliasco** — Turin, with Milan and Rome. Founded 1911 by Lorenzo Pregliasco; Umberto (b. 1960) is the third generation and joined his father Arturo in 1984. The senior Italian family house.
- **Les Enluminures** — founded Paris 1991 by Sandra Hindman (an academic art historian, which shows in the cataloguing); Paris (Palais-Royal), Chicago, New York from 2012. Medieval and Renaissance manuscripts, miniatures, and period rings. TEFAF and Winter Show.
- **Dr. Jörn Günther Rare Books** — founded 1990, Basel Old Town gallery since 2009. Medieval and Renaissance manuscripts and miniatures at museum level.
- **Sam Fogg** — London. Medieval manuscripts and medieval art more broadly. **Verify current structure before citing** — the business has shifted toward art dealing.

## Economic and institutional history — the working list

The headline houses will sell you Smith, Ricardo and Malthus in fine bindings at fine-binding prices. The specialists below are where the *unglamorous* material actually surfaces — company charters, insurance treatises, commercial arithmetic, guild and averia documentation — and their catalogues function as finding aids.

- **Antiquariaat Matthys de Jongh** — Zutphen (Groenmarkt 11), established 1975, NVvA/ILAB. "Old and rare books on economics and social and political sciences." The single closest fit to this project's material. **Caution: the firm's own site describes a significant reduction of inventory after roughly forty-five years of trading — confirm current activity and whether stock was dispersed before relying on it.**
- **Pickering & Chatto, Antiquarian Booksellers** — trading since 1820, now Croydon/London, ABA/ILAB/PBFA. Historically *the* house for economics, political economy, medicine and science. Still the name to write to for a want-list in the history of economic thought.
- **Athena Rare Books** — Fairfield, Connecticut, ABAA/ILAB. First editions in economics and philosophy. Narrow, scholarly, and the most likely US source for canonical economic texts.
- **Antiquariat Inlibris** (above) — for economics reaching into Habsburg administrative and Ottoman/Middle Eastern commercial material.
- **Forum / Asher** (above) — for Dutch commercial, colonial and legal printing.

## Regional houses worth the direct approach

- **Hordern House** — Sydney. Voyages, exploration, Pacific and Australiana. No serious competitor in its field.
- **The Isseido Booksellers** — Jimbōchō, Tokyo. ABAJ/ILAB; the established Tokyo house for Japanese and Western antiquarian material, and one of the few Jimbōchō firms operating fully to international trade standards.
- **Kitazawa Bookstore** — Jimbōchō, Tokyo. ABAJ/ILAB, Western-language books.
- **Maruzen-Yushodo Co.** — Tokyo. ABAJ/ILAB. The corporate end of the Japanese trade; historically the conduit by which Western rare books entered Japanese institutional collections, which makes it the party to ask about provenance when a Western item turns up in a Japanese library.

## How to use them

- **Buy the catalogue, not the book.** These firms' catalogues are bibliography. A McKittrick, Govi or PRPH description is often the fullest published account of the copy, and the run of catalogues is a research instrument independent of purchase.
- **Send a want-list.** The specialists work from them and will report material before it is catalogued. This is the only reliable way to see the unglamorous material, which never reaches a public listing.
- **The ten-day return clock is the leverage.** Collate on arrival — leaves, plates, cancels, binding — because the warranty is worth nothing unexercised.
- **Description language is diagnostic.** "Sophisticated", "made-up", "washed", "supplied", "remboîtage" are the words that carry the loss. A catalogue that never uses them is either selling perfect copies or not telling you.
- **Provenance is a claim about priority and independence, not a decoration.** The same distinction the project draws in [[Split provenance into priority and independence]] applies to copies: a chain of named owners establishes priority; it does not by itself establish that the copy is what it is described as.

## Deliberately excluded

Auction houses — Christie's, Sotheby's, Bonhams, Forum Auctions, Swann, Reiss & Sohn — are not dealers and carry no equivalent warranty. Auction catalogue descriptions are not subject to the ILAB code, condition reporting is buyer-initiated, and the "as is" clause defeats most recourse. Serious dealers bid at auction *on behalf of clients* precisely to interpose their own warranty. Similarly excluded: AbeBooks, Biblio and vialibri, which are aggregation layers over dealers of every quality and confer nothing themselves — though vialibri is the correct tool for a global want-list sweep.
