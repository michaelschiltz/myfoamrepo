# Averijgrossen — the general average adjudications

Index: <https://archief.amsterdam/uitleg/indexen/29-averijgrossen-1700-1810> · records in the *Archief van Schout en Schepenen*, series *Vonnissen ter zaken van Averij Grosse*, 1700–1810, at [[Stadsarchief Amsterdam - the notarial archive]].

**This is the closest thing in the whole European survey to a purpose-built source for a form the census already codes.** `general_average` is a coded row; this is a century of adjudications of it, indexed, with scans linked.

## What each ruling carries

Ship name, skipper, ports of departure and destination, date of departure, the damage, and the date of the ruling — and, in the fuller versions, an abridged ship's declaration, an itemised list of damage costs, **and how the liability was distributed between the skipper and the cargo owners**.

That last item is the census's question, in the source's own words, serially, for a hundred and ten years.

## Why this is different from every other placement in this hub

Everywhere else in Europe the survey has located an archive where a form's documents *ought* to be. Here the match is exact: the form is `general_average`, the characteristic is loss allocation among the venture's participants, and the series is a run of judgments doing precisely that allocation. **It is indexed and searchable by skipper, ship, date and port**, and it cross-refers to the notarial *scheepsverklaringen*, which are themselves in a transcribed corpus — so the ruling's compressed account can be read against the fuller narrative it was drawn from.

## What it could support, stated carefully

A serial run with a known extent offers something the census almost never has: **a denominator**. Frequency claims about how peril was allocated become possible *within this series*, which is a different and much stronger thing than a frequency across national corpora — the move [[The VOC-EIC digital asymmetry is an access regime not a survival difference]] prohibits.

⚠️ The record count is not published on the index page and must be established; without it there is no denominator and the paragraph above is aspiration. ⚠️ Whether the *fuller* rulings are a minority, and whether their survival correlates with the size of the loss, decides whether the itemised ones are a sample or a selection.

**A caution about what a court series is.** These are *disputed* averages. Averages settled between the parties without litigation never reach this archive, and there is no reason to think the disputed ones are typical of the settled ones — they are selected on disagreement, which is likely to correlate with the size and the novelty of the loss. [[Refusals are observations of the filter not inferences from survivors]] is the general rule; here the filter is litigation, and it is sharp.
