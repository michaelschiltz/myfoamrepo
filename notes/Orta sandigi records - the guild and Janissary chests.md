# *Orta sandığı* records — the guild and Janissary chests

**Where the records of two Ottoman corporate chests are, and why so few survive.** A candidate form surfaced while placing the census's waqf rows; the candidate is parked in `HistorEE_codebooks/logbook/2. data inclusion and exclusion.md`, 2026-09-11 (iv). **This note is only about the records.**

## The Janissary *orta* chests

Each of the Janissary corps' **196 *ortas*** kept a chest run as a cash waqf: a *mütevelli* drawn from the unit's senior officers, accounts audited and sealed by the unit's elders, loans at 12–15 per cent against pledges — above all Janissary pay certificates (*esâme*) — and guarantors. Sunar 2025, working the accounts of the 44th *Ağa Bölüğü*, 1796–1826.

**Where they are.** In [[Osmanli Arsivi - Istanbul]], **scattered across four collections**: four registers in `D.YNÇ.d`, one in `MAD.d`, two in `D.BŞM.d`, one in `D.PYM.d`; supporting documents in `C.AS` and the *Mühimme* registers. The scatter is the worked case at [[The tasnif collections - subject classification over provenance]].

**The survival rule, and it is published.**

- **15 June 1826**, the abolition of the corps: **170 of the 196 *ortas* were barracked in the Yeni Odalar, which was shelled and burned.** Their chest registers are presumed lost with it.
- Sunar infers from the record that the state **burned some Janissary papers in the Ayasofya bath-house** afterwards. ⚠️ The author's inference, not a documented order.
- **Only units of the Eski Odalar survive**, and those were partly looted.
- **The assets went to the Treasury, not the Evkaf** — to fund the new army — and **the liquidation created the surviving series**: *muhallefat* and auction registers, mostly in `MAD.d`, drawn up to collect what civilians and artisans owed the dissolved chests.

**So the loan book survives principally as bad debt at the moment of confiscation.** Anyone counting borrowers from these registers is counting the debtors outstanding in June 1826, recorded by a creditor with every reason to find them.

## The guild chests

The guilds' *orta sandığı* or *esnaf sandığı*. **The evidence reached in this pass is thin and secondary.** A 2024 game-theoretic article (Yardımcı, Gümüş & Cengiz) reports capital from member premiums, entry and promotion fees, fines, donations, property rents and guild-founded waqfs; aid in illness, old age, disability, death and marriage; loans to members; and **management transferred to the municipalities in 1869**. ⚠️ Every one of those statements rests on that article's own secondary sources.

**Where the records are, on that article's citations:**

- **Davutpaşa court and *Evkaf Muhasebesi* records** — nineteenth-century accounts of the halva-makers', tanners', saddlers' and booksellers' chests, as worked by Gürsoy 2017 and 2018. See [[Istanbul Muftulugu Seriyye Sicilleri Arsivi]].
- **The *İstanbul Ahkâm Defterleri***, published as *İstanbul Esnaf Tarihi* I–II (Kal'a et al., 1997–98) — for example a 1762 order on the Beyazıt coppersmiths' *orta akçesi*.
- ⚠️ **After 1869, municipal custody.** If the transfer moved the chests' papers, the later guild record is a municipal archive's — successor custody again. Not checked.
