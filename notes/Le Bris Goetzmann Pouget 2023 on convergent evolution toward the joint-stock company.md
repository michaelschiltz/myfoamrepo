# Le Bris Goetzmann Pouget 2023 on convergent evolution toward the joint-stock company

David Le Bris, William N. Goetzmann and Sébastien Pouget, 'Convergent Evolution Toward the Joint-Stock Company', NBER Working Paper 31821, November 2023, 47 pp. JEL G30, K22, N20, N43, O43. Read in full, 3 August 2026, from the working-paper text. Zotero article versions at `LQYRKZX6` (2025) and `FQWLWX4S` (2026).

## Structure

§I introduction; §II historical background (A famous emergences for the Asian trade; B cases in medieval southern Europe); §III convergent evolution hypothesis; §IV distinct legal genealogies (A guild and commenda paths; B the pariage path); §V on the migration of the joint-stock company (A evaluating the migration hypothesis; B factors limiting migration); §VI (A a common Roman background; B socio-economic and political background); §VII conclusion.

## The claim

The joint-stock company emerged **several times** rather than once-and-then-spread. Cases: the Toulouse milling companies (Bazacle and Castel Narbonens, formally constituted 1372 and 1373), the Casa di San Giorgio (1407), the EIC (1600) and the VOC (1602). The northern paths run from guilds (EIC) and aggregated *commenda* (VOC); the southern from *pariage*. Harris (2020) is the named opponent, holding migration.

**They use a declared component grid**, taken from Hansmann, Kraakman & Squire (2006): legal personality, permanent capital, limited liability, transferable shares, entity shielding, delegated management. This distinguishes them sharply from Harris, who argues from an undeclared prose list — see [[Count degrees of freedom not cells]].

## The decisive material, in §VI.A

Roman law in the form of the rediscovered *corpus iuris civilis* supplies the *universitas*, which they show already carried entity shielding (Ulpian, *Dig.* 3.4.7.1), delegated management or representation (*Dig.* 3.4.2), and legal personality — read through Avi-Yonah (2005) and Michaud-Quantin (1970). The *pariage* was **treated as** a *universitas* (Sicard 2015; Débax 2012), so:

> "since a universitas was endowed with the various characteristics that now define legal personality, this also automatically applied to pariages. Thus, a pariage enjoyed the benefits of legal personality without the need for any approbation by the state or any other authority."

Equality of *pariers* (*par* = equal footing; the king of France, a *parier* of the Castel mills, treated as any other) yields identical shares irrespective of holder — which they call "a preliminary for depersonalization", their hedge. Perpetuity follows from *universitas* status. The *consortium* of undivided heirs, via Gaius, is offered as the specific legal ancestor of *pariage*.

**This section is the counter-evidence to their own title.** See [[The Roman universitas makes the JSC case parallelism not convergence]].

## Facts worth having

- Bazacle enfeoffed 1177, again 1184 (first use of *parier*); Castel 1182. Perpetual merger of constituent *pariages* into the two companies in 1372–73. Bazacle ran to nationalisation in **1946**.
- The 1182 Castel enfeoffment schedules transaction tax on fractions of a share — half, third, quarter — so fractional alienation was routine and fiscally anticipated in the twelfth century.
- **Pariers could sell without the consent of the other pariers.** This is the cell that decides the Harris dispute on the structural question.
- Montpellier, 1192: *pariers* could seize the *pecol* (1/16 share) of those refusing to pay common expenditure. Capital by call, enforced by forfeiture. Compare the English mining cost-book system.
- Share names by locality: *pecol* (Montpellier), *meules* (Moissac), *rases* (Montauban).
- Early *pariers* were plainly capitalists rather than workers — the Daurade monastery; men who had been or became *Capitouls*.
- Governance included general assemblies and representation, and later a board of directors and a professional chief executive, before the EIC and VOC.
- Genoa: *comparia salis* from the 12th century, shares widely traded; several *comparias* merged into the Casa di San Giorgio in 1407; from 1420 shareholders received something that can be regarded as a dividend; shares nominative but freely negotiable, transfers entered by a notary in the firm's register. Closed by Napoleon in 1805.
- Toulouse mint, 1198: c. 20 owners of high standing, purely investing, shares inherited across generations, *dominium* rather than usage right.
- Montady, 1248: *portionarii* and *comportionarii* in *pariage* to drain 1,000 acres of wetland.
- They concede the *societates publicanorum* (Malmendier 2009) as a possible earlier emergence, and note it is contested (Dufour 2010).

## Two objections, kept separate

**The label.** Their own definition requires that the common ancestor *lacked* the trait. §VI.A shows it had them. The correct term is parallelism; the word does not appear in the paper, nor does *homoplasy*. They preserve "convergence" by filing the Roman background under **environmental** features conducive to the form, in the conclusion — but a received body of law from which forms inherit traits is ancestry, not a selective pressure.

**The units.** "An analogy can be made between organizations and organisms, the organizational traits being coded into legal rules as phenotypic traits are coded into genes." This is the slide [[Never let the units slide from instruments to populations]] exists to block. Object to it separately; bundling it with the label objection lets a referee defeat both by defeating the weaker.

## What they get right, and should be conceded

The `pariage` evidence is excellent and largely new to the anglophone literature. Their §V case that there is little evidence of migration from southern to northern forms is worth taking seriously on its own terms. And the observation that the joint-stock company appeared in southern Europe centuries before any economic take-off is a real problem for legal-origins accounts (La Porta et al. 1997) and is independent of the convergence-versus-parallelism dispute.

## Verify

- Read the **published** version before citing; §VI.A may have been revised between the 2023 working paper and the 2025/2026 articles.
- **Whether guilds are themselves *universitates*.** If so, the northern path shares the ancestral condition too and the parallelism argument becomes general rather than confined to the south. This is the load-bearing step and it is *not* established in the paper.
- Ulpian *Dig.* 3.4.7.1 and 3.4.2 are quoted through Avi-Yonah. Check against the Digest before quoting.
- Sicard (1953; Yale trans. 2015, ed. Goetzmann, intro by these three authors) is the primary base for the milling material and has not been read here.
- Débax (2012) on the family origin of *pariage*, and Heers (1963) and Sayous (1932, 1933b) on the Genoese *comparias*, are unconsulted.
