# The mujin's Buddhist name is not the rotating association's Buddhist descent

**無尽 is a Buddhist word, and it reached the Japanese rotating credit association by a Buddhist route. The association did not.** The name was attached to an institution that already existed, roughly two centuries after that institution is first attested. To read the name as the form's pedigree is the etymological fallacy: inferring the descent of an instrument from the descent of the word that eventually labels it.

The fallacy is worth a note because it arrives with credentials. A mock panel review of the ERC application (October 2026) proposed coding the *mujin* and *tanomoshi-kō* as transmitted from Indian monastic endowment-lending via the Tang "inexhaustible treasury", which would have moved the rotating association from the independent column of Objective 1 into the transmitted one. The chain was checked link by link before any of it was written into the proposal. It holds for the word and for temple lending. It breaks exactly where the rotating association enters.

## The chain, link by link

**India to China: monastic endowment-lending. Secure, but not through the famous case.**

- The Mūlasarvāstivāda-vinaya presents the saṅgha as a juristic person owning real and movable property and authorises permanent endowments, deposits, written wills, lending at interest and written loan contracts (Schopen 1994; Schopen, "Business Model").
- Gernet treats the vinaya as the doctrinal background of Chinese monastic lending in his chapter on the Indian heritage.
- 無盡 is the standard Chinese rendering of Sanskrit *akṣaya*, and 無盡藏 of *akṣayakara*. The vocabulary is a calque.
- **The Sanjiejiao treasury at 化度寺, the case everyone cites, does not carry the line.** Its doctrine has been traced to the Mahāyāna bodhisattva "inexhaustible stores", above all the store of giving (施藏), not to vinaya law; and 楊學勇 (2017) reads it as a charitable redistribution body rather than a lender, the lending label borrowed from other monasteries' practice. The India-to-China continuity runs through ordinary monastic lending.
- ⚠️ The Indian term is itself less settled than the textbooks: Martini (2022) argues that the standard translation of *akṣayanīvī* as "permanent endowment" misleads in both members of the compound.

**China to Japan: the word into temple finance. Plausible, and hedged by the standard reference.**

- 無尽銭 was originally pledge-backed lending; there were pawnbrokers called 無尽銭土倉. Heibonsha derives the term おそらく, "probably", from temple loans under the name 無尽財.

**Temple lending to the rotating association. Broken.**

- The rotating association is first attested as 憑支 in the 紀伊国猿川・真国・神野三荘荘官請文 of 建治元年 (1275) 12th month, in the 高野山文書.
- It appears to have grown out of temple religious confraternities (講), with temples acting as 頼母子親 to relieve their own finances; the 信貴頼母子, with 信貴山寺 as *oya*, is the outstanding case.
- 無尽 became synonymous with 頼母子 **only from the Muromachi period**.

The institution precedes the name. A Japanese survey of the problem states the methodological point directly: the literature confuses the origin of the word with the origin of the mechanism, and the two have to be kept apart (流通経済大学論叢 27(2)).

## Why the name is a dangerous character

A borrowed name is precisely the kind of arbitrary particular that [[Divergent arbitrary particulars are the independence diagnostic]] treats as the strongest evidence of descent: nothing about rotating credit requires it to be called "inexhaustible". That is what makes the fallacy persuasive, and it is why the diagnostic carries a condition the note on it should be read with. **A shared arbitrary particular attests descent only if it travelled with the form.** A label attached afterwards to an existing institution records reticulation of the vocabulary, not origination of the instrument ([[Homoplasy is the finding not the noise]]). So the name has to be dated before it is counted, and here the dating removes it from the evidence altogether.

The *kye* case is the same error at the scale of a national literature: a category travels and is read back as a description of what it lands on ([[Kye is not a ROSCA and the equation is an artefact of comparison with mujin]]).

## What follows for the coding

- **Rotating association** (*tanomoshi-kō*, *mujin*): a Japanese development within a Buddhist institutional matrix. It stays in the independent column, flagged for the confraternal matrix, not for transmission of form.
- **Monastic endowment-lending**: transmitted, India to China to Japanese temple finance. It belongs to the perpetual-endowment question of Objective 2, not to Objective 1.
- **Chinese *hui***: unresolved. The Dunhuang lay associations are an obvious candidate ancestor ([[Dunhuang she associations - bylaws for a funeral fund]]), but the link has not been verified and the row should carry an "uncertain" flag rather than a claim either way.

The schema should carry the channel explicitly, so that the distinction between a Buddhist matrix and Buddhist transmission is recorded in the genealogy field of the *kō*/*mujin* rows rather than asserted in prose. The point was deliberately kept out of the ERC text (decision of 16 October 2026): once the check came back as it did, the passage would have defended against an objection the main text no longer invites.
