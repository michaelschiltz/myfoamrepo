# Agent-borne capital loss clusters in local contracts

**Hypothesis, not finding.** In both the Genoese and the Catalan commenda material, the variants that expose the working party to the principal's capital loss cluster in local and land-based contracts, and the sea-going acts put the peril either wholly on the investor or pro rata on contributed capital. If that holds by count, the agent's shield against the principal's loss is tolerated away where a single venture's variance is low and kept where it is high.

**The observations.**

- **Catalonia.** The loss-sharing comandas are mostly craft and local trade: shoemakers and butchers at Vic and Barcelona (1261, 1273), a skinner and his wife at Manresa (1323). See [[The comanda ad societatem shares losses in the proportion of profits]].
- **Genoa.** In the pilot's stage-4 sample, fourteen of the fifteen *societas* acts that read `CF2 = 1` were land-based (shop, workshop, credit at a term), owing the capital at term under the double penalty. Stage 5 reread the formula as a duty to render what there is, not an assumption of the peril; the clustering of the formula in land acts stands either way.

**Why it would matter.** It is the within-tradition form of the differential prediction in [[The amana-daman structure refuses to infer fault from loss]], that the fault requirement should broaden as ruin exposure rises, so a maritime agent is held to a more forgiving standard than a local factor. Here the variable is allocation rather than the fault standard, but the shape is the same: protection of the working party from ruin scales with the venture's exposure to catastrophic loss. See [[Institutions substitute state-verification for outcome-inference as ruin exposure rises]].

**What counts against it.** The Vic loss-sharing *societates* of 1230, 1231 and 1239 went by sea. The Catalan sample is about six acts. The Genoese pattern was found in a sample framed by drafting term, which mixed land and sea, and has not been counted under a frame defined by the loss rule.

**Test.** Count loss clauses against terrain and venture type in Madurell & García Sanz 1973 and *Societats mercantils* 1986 for Catalonia, and in the full Genoese frame recoded on the loss rule. The prediction fails if agent-borne loss is as common at sea as on land once the frame is fixed.
