# Permanent capital pools across voyages as well as across partners

A voyage-by-voyage stock pools subscribers within one venture and dissolves at its end. One disastrous voyage then falls entirely on the people who subscribed to it, and for a subscriber with most of his capital in it, that voyage is the barrier. **A permanent fund pools the voyages themselves**: the losses of a bad voyage are netted against the profits of others in the same fund, before and after, and the fund's retained capital keeps it at a distance from ruin that no single voyage stock can have.

In the terms of [[Under absorption pooling raises the probability of ruin to the power of the pool]], the voyage stock sets $N$ to the number of subscribers and exposes them to the correlated peril of one fleet. The permanent fund raises $N$ to the number of voyages at risk over its life, and those voyages are far less correlated with one another than the ships of one fleet are. Antonio's third axis — "nor is my whole estate / Upon the fortune of this present year" — is what permanent capital institutionalises ([[Diversification runs across bottoms not across partners]]).

## The English and Dutch sequence

The English East India Company financed its first voyages separately, moved to terminable joint stocks running several voyages, and adopted a permanent joint stock only in 1657. The Dutch company's capital was subscribed in 1602 for a fixed term, and its locking-in as permanent capital was settled over the following two decades. [Verify both sequences and dates against Gelderblom, de Jong and Jonker 2013, Harris 2020 and Stern 2011 before citing.] Read through this note, each step enlarges the set of ventures over which a single loss is spread.

## What this note does not claim

- **Not the reason for adoption.** The standard explanations — fixed investment in forts and factories in Asia, the directors' interest in retaining capital, state demands — are about why permanence was chosen. This note is about what permanence does to ruin once chosen. The two can both be true, and the selective reading at [[Time-average optimization is a survivorship property not an intention]] governs.
- **Not that the corporation is the summit.** Pooling across time is available without a corporation: a family firm that retains earnings across generations does it, and the *ie* does it under household discipline. See [[Pooling approaches the infinite ensemble and perpetual succession the infinite horizon]], which this note adds to: perpetual succession lets the time-average rate be lived, and under a barrier it also spreads the ruinous voyage across all the others.
- **Not that time-pooling always works.** A fund can be ruined by a correlated sequence — several bad seasons together, or a war — and the Ottoman *avarız* case shows how easily a buffer reading can be imposed on an endowment that never drew down its principal ([[A perfectly correlated peril leaves only the time average]]).
