# Cooperation is an averaging puzzle and sovereign repayment is a barrier puzzle

The two puzzles most often produced as showcases for the ergodicity framing are dissolved by **different parts of the apparatus**, and running them together would waste the distinction the vault has spent two clusters establishing.

**Cooperation is an averaging problem, and nothing else.** Peters and Adamou's resolution is that pooling raises the time-average growth rate of both parties even where it leaves the expectation unchanged or lowers it for one. No barrier is required, no sequence is required, and the model is permutation-invariant throughout. What does the work is the non-linearity of the observable and the resulting [[Jensen gap]] — the first row of the two-by-two in [[Ergodicity breaking and order-dependence are independent axes]]. The vault's constraint on this case is not about mechanism but about sign: [[Pooling's sign is set by the correlation of its baskets]], and a pool of correlated baskets buys nothing.

**Sovereign repayment is a barrier problem.** Default approximates absorption — exclusion from the credit relationship, on the reading where reputation alone cannot sustain repayment. Repayment is then barrier-avoidance rather than expectation-maximisation, and this case *is* order-dependent in the sense of [[Terminal wealth is permutation-invariant but survival is not]]: a liquidity crisis arriving before reserves accumulate absorbs the borrower, while the identical shock later is survivable. Debt service is precisely the $b_i$ term of [[Flows make the dynamic affine and affine maps do not commute]], which is why sovereign fiscal history is order-sensitive as a matter of structure rather than as a matter of luck.

## Why the distinction is worth carrying

Three reasons, in ascending order of importance.

It is more accurate. Presenting both as instances of "sequence matters" misdescribes the cooperation result, which does not involve sequence at all.

It is more persuasive. An apparatus with independent moving parts that apply to different cases is stronger evidence of a framework than one big idea restated; a referee who sees the same move made twice suspects a single trick.

It is more useful. The two mechanisms make different demands on evidence. The averaging case requires showing that the relevant variable compounds and that baskets are imperfectly correlated. The barrier case requires identifying the absorbing state, arguing that exclusion approximates it, and — the harder part — establishing the *ordering* of shocks relative to reserve accumulation, which is an archival question rather than a modelling one.

## A caution on the Genoese case

[[The moratorium held because leaving the pool is not a one-period cost]] is filed under cooperation but is not a clean instance of it. Refusal to break ranks is a pooling-and-growth-rate argument, which is the averaging mechanism; exclusion from the relationship is a barrier argument. Both are present, and the note's strength is that it runs the first without needing the second. Do not retrofit the barrier onto it.
