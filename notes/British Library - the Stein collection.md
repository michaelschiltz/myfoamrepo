# British Library — the Stein collection

**Over 45,000 manuscripts and printed items** from Aurel Stein's Central Asian expeditions, within the Library's **almost 50,000** Eastern Silk Road items, **in more than twenty languages and scripts** — Chinese, Tibetan, Sanskrit, Tangut, Khotanese, Kuchean, Sogdian, Uyghur, Turkic, Mongolian and others — plus **over 10,000 photographic prints, negatives and lantern slides**, paintings, textiles and artefacts.

**It is the largest single share of the Dunhuang library cave**, and it is where the census-relevant items sit: the **Sogdian Ancient Letters** (Or. 8212/92 and Or. 8212/95) and *she* association documents such as **S. 527**.

## Custody and its consequences

**The finds were divided.** Stein's expeditions were funded jointly by the British and Indian governments, and antiquities went also to the British Museum and to India. ⚠️ **The division and its proportions were not established in this pass.** **A student of one site therefore works across three institutions in two countries before leaving the British share.**

**Access and layers**: catalogued and largely imaged through [[IDP - the digital reunification of a dispersed archive]], which the Library founded; the **transcriptions of the social-historical documents are being published in China** — [[The Silk Road layer 3 is a national editorial programme]]. ⚠️ Current reading-room and licence terms not checked; see [[British archives online - the access layers]] for the Library's post-2023 situation.
