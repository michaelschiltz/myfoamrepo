# Time-homogeneity not stationarity is what removes time

Three properties travel together under the name of stationarity, and the critique needs them apart.

- **Time-homogeneity**: the law of motion is the same at every date. Clark's per-generation persistence is the same in 1300 and in 2000.
- **A restoring force**: an invariant distribution exists — the persistence parameter is below one.
- **Indecomposability**: the invariant measure has a single component, so the cross-section describes every lineage — [[Ergodicity is indecomposability not randomness]].

The ergodicity programme is not against stationarity. Its method is to find the observable whose increments are stationary and to average that — the growth rate under geometric Brownian motion, for which [[Nothing in the ergodic theorem fails in geometric Brownian motion]]. Its objection is to stationarity presumed rather than shown, and attached to the wrong observable; and [[Stationarity is a precondition of ergodicity not a corollary]] forbids calling a non-stationary process non-ergodic in the first place.

Of the three, it is time-homogeneity that removes time. Where the law never changes, the passage of $n$ generations is the exponent in $b^n$: an index of iteration, not a history. Nothing that happens between generations — enclosure, revolution, the welfare state — can enter, because the operator applied at each step is fixed before the history begins. This is a third way of disposing of time, beside the coordinate of [[Arrow-Debreu dissolves time rather than neglecting it]] and the interval of [[Causal inference removes time as an interval where equilibrium removed it as iteration]].

The demand that follows is specific: test whether the transition law is homogeneous across regimes before testing whether it mixes. A law that changes with regimes is a historical object; a law that does not is either a deep constant or, as [[A persistence parameter stable across regime change indicts the measure]] argues, a property of the instrument.
