# MOC — Ottoman archives

Regional hub, the first of the non-European passes, linked from the country table of [[MOC - European archives and digital collections]] and following [[Handoff - the non-European archive survey]]. **Infrastructure layer, not argument layer.** The unit is the Ottoman court and chancery, not the modern state: Dubrovnik is in Croatia, the frontier courts in Bosnia, the capital's registers in Istanbul, the provinces' in Ankara, Sofia, Thessaloniki, Jerusalem and Cairo.

**This pass opened no new subject.** It looked for the archives behind rows the census already carries — `waqf_khayri`, `waqf_ahli`, `avariz_fund`, `avariz_vakfi`, `avariz_vakfi_kirkcesme`, `cash_waqf` (parked), `qirad` and `mudaraba`.

## The structural fact

**Three things the Ottoman archival administration does to the evidence, and a fourth that governs all three.**

**1. The court record was divided twice — by the Republic and by the borders.** When the *şer'iyye* courts were abolished in 1924, **Istanbul's registers stayed with the religious administration** at [[Istanbul Muftulugu Seriyye Sicilleri Arsivi]]; **the provinces' passed from local museums to the National Library in 1991 and to the State Archives in 2005** — [[The Anatolian court registers - museum to National Library to State Archives]]; and **about 8,000 registers of the former provinces are in successor states with no union catalogue**. **[[ISAM Library Archive - the court register image collection]] reunites 23,756 of them at layer 2, in one reading room.** That inverts Europe, where the union layer is description and online; here it is image and on site.

**2. The central archive was classified by subject before it was arranged by provenance.** [[The tasnif collections - subject classification over provenance]]. **A waqf question has no series address** at [[Osmanli Arsivi - Istanbul]]; it has a reign class, a subject class and several bureau series, and the Janissary chest accounts turned up in four of them. **Never compute a frequency across a *tasnif* collection.**

**3. The waqf's records are with the waqf's administrator.** [[Vakiflar Genel Mudurlugu Arsivi - Ankara]] holds 27,000 deeds because it administers the endowments. **The handoff predicted successor custody; the prediction was too weak.** In Europe the successor was a business keeping its papers; here it is a state administrator for which a deed is a live title.

**4. Script decides the layer, and the census's evidence is in the hardest script.** Chancery acts are in *dīwānī*, court registers in *ta'lik* and *rik'a* and — early — in Arabic, deeds often in Arabic, and **every account in siyakat**, a hand with its own numerals that few read. **The census's Ottoman rows are accounting rows.** "Imaged" is weakest precisely where the census needs it.

## Dubrovnik, first

- [[The Ottoman documents at Dubrovnik]] — the hinge, now with its ⚠️ answered
- [[Acta Turcarum - the Ottoman series at Dubrovnik]] — `HR-DADU-07.2`, 15,000 documents, four subseries and a large unarranged remainder; **the survival rule is published** (Vienna 1833, Zadar 1836)
- [[Kadijski spisi - the kadi documents at Dubrovnik]] — 2,000 frontier-court acts, 1487–1805, inventoried to item level in 2019; **debts among them**
- [[The Dubrovnik Ottoman documents in three scripts]] — Cyrillic and edited before 1525; Ottoman Turkish after; **an eighteenth-century Italian translation layer made by the Republic's dragomans**, which may locate a document and may not witness it
- [[Duvel-i Ecnebiye - the Istanbul side of the Ragusan relationship]] — the Porte's register copies, **already set against Dubrovnik once, and the two apparatuses disagree**

**What the Dubrovnik start established.** The site is as clean as the handoff said, and cleaner on script than expected: the fifteenth century is Slavonic and printed, the eighteenth has a contemporary Italian layer. **But the comparison the handoff proposed is already a small literature, and its one result is that the centre's register records the grant and the frontier record records the refusal.** That is the finding to build on, and it is about privilege, not contract. **Whether a Ragusan commercial obligation can be followed from *Debita Notariae* into a frontier *kadi's* court is untested.**

## Istanbul

- [[Osmanli Arsivi - Istanbul]] — the grant's named destination, and on present evidence **not the census's primary one**: it sees the waqf from the Treasury's side
- [[The tasnif collections - subject classification over provenance]]
- [[Istanbul Muftulugu Seriyye Sicilleri Arsivi]] — **the census's archive**: nearly every coded Ottoman row rests on literature working these registers; and a catalogue of **3,950 cash-waqf deeds** that makes `cash_waqf`'s purpose question countable for one city
- [[ISAM Library Archive - the court register image collection]] — the working route to every court register, and **a correction to Decision_log A5**

## The record of failure, added 12 September 2026

A follow-up pass, prompted by the general finding at [[The record of non-survivors survives where failure was administered]]: **the Ottoman empire administered endings, and the administration is serial.**

- [[Tereke defterleri - the Ottoman estate inventory series]] — post-mortem inventories in the court registers, **assets and liabilities**; **8,810 of them used for two north-west Anatolian provinces, 1460–1870**, in one recent study
- [[Gurema - the insolvent estate distributed among creditors]] — **the insolvent estate, distributed pro rata**: the census's creditor limb, in a serial source, with two 2024 case studies (Bursa 1703–1710; Kastamonu 1753–1889)
- [[Muhallefat and musadere - confiscation as an archive]] — **the state as claimant**, to 1839; the Janissary chests are the collective case

**What this does to the pass's seventh pattern.** The Ottoman pass reported failure as the emptiest region of the census. **It is not the emptiest region of the archive**: dying insolvent was an administered event with a written distribution, and it happened in the same registers the census already cites. ⚠️ **The failure *rate* is still unknown** — no study read gives insolvent estates as a share of inventories.

**`qirad` and `mudaraba` have no Ottoman archival home in this census, and that is correct.** Both are doctrinal rows, coded from Hanafi and Maliki texts. Ottoman court registers do record *mudaraba* disputes, but a row coded from them would be an Ottoman-practice row — a regional split of the kind logbook 2 refused for the Ragusan collegantia on 2026-08-18 — and Kaya & Akkaya found cash waqfs did not operate through *mudaraba* in the period they studied. **The practice evidence for `qirad` belongs to the Geniza pass.**

## What this pass adds to the seven patterns

**1. The funding map reaches a coded row.** The Istanbul half of `avariz_fund`'s evidence may be the sample a publication programme chose. See [[Istanbul Kadi Sicilleri - the published transliterations]].

**2. Successor custody, strongest form** — a live administrator holding title deeds.

**3. Four more survival rules, and all four are written down:** Dubrovnik's transfers to Vienna and Zadar (1833, 1836); the Treasury depot sold to Bulgaria (1931); the Yeni Odalar burned with the Janissary chests (1826); the Oriental Institute (1992). **Two of them have a shape** — a sale selects by store, a collection by collector.

**4. The record type is a variable, and a waqf has three.** The deed (*vakfiye*) at the administrator, the account (*muhasebe*) in siyakat at the Treasury or in the court register, and the court's record of the fund's loans. **A difference between two waqf rows may be a difference in which of the three a source used.** The census's `avariz_vakfi_kirkcesme` rests on an account book, `avariz_fund` on court registers.

**5. National arrangements, at their purest.** One empire's court registers are now held under the archival regimes of at least nine successor states, on borders drawn for reasons that have nothing to do with the record. **A comparison across Ottoman provinces assembled from what is open is a sample of successor-state archive policy.**

**6. The merchant corporation's Ottoman cousin.** The guild chest, reportedly funded by members' dues, and the Janissary chest, run as a cash waqf on money that was probably the Treasury's — corporate funds, parked as a candidate, not coded. See [[Orta sandigi records - the guild and Janissary chests]].

**7. The loss side is visible through enforcement.** Ragusan debts in the frontier courts at Dubrovnik; the Janissary chests' borrowers in the liquidation registers of 1826. **In both cases the record of failure was made by a creditor.**

## Script and the budget

**Decision_log A6 is `[OPEN]`, estimates €15–30k for all four of the project's languages, and names *dīwānī* as the Ottoman hand to budget for.** *Dīwānī* is the chancery hand — the Dubrovnik *fermans* and *ahdnames*. **The census's rows need readers of court hands and siyakat**, and siyakat is the scarcer skill. Two consequences for the budget line, stated for the maintainer and not applied: **name siyakat**, and **price it separately from chancery transcription**.
