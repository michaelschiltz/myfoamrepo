# MOC — Silk Road archives

Hub for the Inner Asian documentary material, linked from the country table of [[MOC - European archives and digital collections]] and following the five regional passes of [[Handoff - the non-European archive survey]]. **Infrastructure layer, not argument layer.**

**This is not a region with an archival administration; it is a set of deposits removed by expeditions and held elsewhere.** The pass was asked for **where the sparsely-held Silk Road sources are, including the Japanese holdings**, and it answers that; it also found **the best-shaped census candidate of the whole survey** and **one problem with an existing pair of rows.**

## The structural fact

**1. One site's archive is in twelve institutions.** [[Silk Road documents - one site's archive in twelve institutions]]. Stein for Britain and India, Pelliot for France, four German expeditions, Oldenburg and Kozlov for Russia, Ōtani for a Kyoto temple, and what stayed in China. **Provenance is an expedition, not a fonds; findspot survives in the expedition report, not the shelfmark.**

**2. It was digitally reunified thirty years ago.** [[IDP - the digital reunification of a dispersed archive]] — **154,919 catalogue entries and 114,332 items digitised as of January 2026**, from 40-plus institutions in 15 countries. **The Geniza's answer to dispersal, arrived at first.**

**3. The record is documentary, and the census's Inner Asian rows are coded from chronicles.** [[The Silk Road record is documents and the census codes chronicles]]. **This is the pass's main finding for the matrix.**

**4. Layer 3 is national.** [[The Silk Road layer 3 is a national editorial programme]]. Images are pooled internationally; **transcriptions are made in Beijing, Kyoto, Osaka, St Petersburg, Berlin and Oxford, each on the collection it can reach** — and **two of the largest programmes are in Chinese and one in Japanese.**

## The holdings

- [[British Library - the Stein collection]] — **over 45,000 manuscripts and printed items**, 20-plus scripts; the Ancient Letters and *she* bylaws
- [[Berlin - the Turfan collection]] — **about 40,000 fragments**, split across three Berlin institutions; wartime losses undetermined
- [[Institute of Oriental Manuscripts - St Petersburg]] — Dunhuang, **Khara-Khoto**, and the **Mount Mugh** Sogdian archive
- [[National Library of China and the Dunhuang Academy]] — **about 16,000 items**, plus the site and the editorial capacity
- **Bibliothèque nationale de France** — the Pelliot collection, **some 14,000 items** ⚠️ not given its own note; figure second-hand
- [[The Khalili Bactrian documents - a private collection as the archive]] — **150-plus contracts, privately held**

## The Japanese holdings, which is what was asked

- [[Ryukoku University and the Otani collection]] — **about 9,000 items in Kyoto**, an IDP founding partner, **8,465 manuscripts digitised** by its centre
- [[The Otani dispersal - Kyoto, Tokyo, Lushun, Seoul]] — **16,035 fragments at Lüshun, 621 scrolls moved to the National Library of China, 373 objects given to the Governor-General of Korea in 1916 and now 1,500-plus at the National Museum of Korea, the remainder bought by the Tokyo National Museum in 1964**
- [[Toyo Bunko and the Digital Silk Road - the expedition library not the finds]] — **yes, and it is a library**: 245 rare books, 72,591 pages, free, with NII; **the expedition reports, not the finds**
- **And the editorial layer**: [[Old Uyghur contracts - the documentary layer under the ortoq]] — **the standard edition of the Old Uyghur contracts was made at Osaka**, and the Tang contract corpus was put in order in Tokyo by Niida and Ikeda

**In one line: Japan holds a substantial share of the originals (Kyoto, Tokyo, and — as a consequence of empire — Dalian and Seoul), publishes the standard editions of two contract corpora, and gives away the expedition library for free.**

## The candidate

[[Dunhuang she associations - bylaws for a funeral fund]] — **logbook 2, 2026-09-12 (xi)**. Ninth- and tenth-century lay associations whose **own bylaws (社條) fix a contribution in kind at a member's death**, with penalties for default and named officers. **Membership twelve to twenty-six. Four women's bylaws survive, dated 658 or earlier to 968, in four different countries' collections; the corpus as a whole is 422 manuscripts, 345 of them collated in 1997.** **The *shihōsho* problem is solved before the work starts.**

## What this pass adds to the seven patterns

**1. The funding map is an expedition map.** What is digitised is what a national institution acquired; **the site keeps nothing but the site.**

**2. Custody: an expedition, then an empire.** [[The Otani dispersal - Kyoto, Tokyo, Lushun, Seoul]] is successor custody with no successor — **a private collection following money and borders until four states hold it.**

**3. Survival rule: sealed and dry.** The cave was walled up; the Turfan tombs and the desert sites kept paper because nothing rained on it. **What survives is what was thrown away or hidden in the right climate** — the Geniza's rule in a desert.

**4. The record type is unusually good.** Contracts, bylaws, receipts, private letters. **The census's usual complaint — that only the state's or the editor's version survives — does not hold here.**

**5. National arrangements: images pooled, texts national.**

**6. The merchant corporation with no row**: nothing here is a corporation; **the Sogdian network is a family-and-agency network and its record is letters** — [[Sogdian commercial documents - thin but not empty]].

**7. Failure is emptiest — inverted.** These deposits survive **because the communities that made them ended**: an abandoned oasis, a sealed cave, a fortress that fell. **The archive exists because the institution failed.**

## For the grant, stated for the maintainer and not applied

- **WP1's calibration should be strengthened where it is cheapest.** The vault makes the *ortoq* the documented transmission case that licenses the independent-origin reading elsewhere. **It rests on chronicles.** One consultation — a specialist in Mongol-period Uyghur documents — would establish whether the instruments survive. **That is an advisory-board approach, not a work package.**
- **A6, scripts.** Sogdian, Old Uyghur, Tangut, Kharoṣṭhī and Bactrian are **not** in the transcription line, and **need not be**: the corpora are edited. **What the project buys here is editions, not transcription time.**
- **A7, travel.** **This is a desk region** — after the Geniza, the best-imaged material in the survey. **The one exception is the Japanese material, which is in Kyoto**, where the project's East Asian PI already is.
- **A5.** The editions are the cost: SUK, the 释录 series, Sims-Williams's three volumes. **The Tōyō Bunko expedition library and IDP's images are free.**
- **Two live ERC comparators** for Part 2: [[Invisible East - an open corpus for the eastern Islamicate documents]] (ERC 851607) and [[DHARMA - an ERC Synergy project that produced a TEI corpus of inscriptions]] (ERC Synergy 809994).

## Open questions

- **Is any surviving Uyghur or Khara-Khoto contract an *ortoq* contract?** The question that would upgrade WP1's calibration.
- ~~How large is the *she* corpus?~~ **Answered 12 September 2026: 422 manuscripts from Dunhuang and Turfan; 345 in Ning and Hao's 1997 compilation, plus 77 added in 2019** (Teiser 2020). **What remains is its composition** — how many are bylaws rather than circulars.
- **Does the *she* bylaw fix the contribution by household, by share or per head** — `CN2` — and what happened to a member who could not pay?
- **What is IDP's licence**, and what survived the British Library's systems disruption?
- **Berlin's wartime losses and the Soviet removals** — before any completeness claim.
- **The Kharoṣṭhī tablets from Niya** — a third-century legal corpus, unexamined here.
