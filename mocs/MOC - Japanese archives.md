# MOC — Japanese archives

Regional hub, the third non-European pass, linked from the country table of [[MOC - European archives and digital collections]] and following [[Handoff - the non-European archive survey]], [[MOC - Ottoman archives]] and [[MOC - Geniza archives]]. **Infrastructure layer, not argument layer.**

**This pass opened no new subject.** It looked for the archives behind the census's Japanese rows — about twenty *kō* and *mujin* rows, `warichi_iwade`, `torinoke_mujin`, `nakai_fictive_household`, `kabu_local`, `kabu_edo_export`, and the uncoded umbrellas `ie`, `kabu_nakama`, `ton_ya` and `ko_mujin` — and behind the dataset that carries the book's title, `clearing_records`, **which turned out to have no archive at all.**

## The structural fact

**1. Custody follows the house.** [[Japanese archives - custody follows the house]]. The shogunate's record reached the Meiji state in pieces; **the village and merchant record — the census's record — was the household's own**, and still sits with successors, donees, purchasers or in ceilings. **Successor custody, five instances in Europe, is here the default.** And the census codes the *ie* as an entity: **the institution coded as perpetual is the institution that kept the archive.**

**2. Dispersal by policy.** [[Genchi hozon - keeping documents where they are]]. From the 1970s the national body filmed documents in place instead of collecting them. **Japan decided, recently and deliberately, not to centralise.**

**3. Books are served; documents are not.** [[Books are imaged and documents are not - the Japanese digital split]]. **The expectation that Japan is very widely served digitally is right for *kotenseki* and the state's records** — 32.8 million records in Japan Search, 300,000 classical books imaged, 75,000 OCR'd this month — **and wrong for the *komonjo* the census is coded from.** Digitisation followed literature, not economic history.

**4. Japan's documents are at layer 3 in print, paid for by towns.** [[Jichitaishi - the municipal histories as Japan's print layer 3]]. **Every Japanese row in the census was coded from a printed transcription or a historian's quotation of one**, so the census's Japanese evidence is a sample of Japanese local-history editing.

**5. The binding constraint is permission and residency, not script.** Script has tools and the PI reads it. **The walls are a residency requirement at the NDL, appointment-only house archives, and no-photography reading rooms.**

**Read the table for its shape.** Of the Japanese rows, **one is checkable against an image from a desk** (`ko_furuichi_sengan`), a handful rest on printed editions, and **the largest group — Mitarai, Kawachi, Yamagata, Settsu — cannot yet be placed in any repository.** The *kō* acquisition rule already recorded — no *kō* moves a cell without a *shihōsho* — **now has an archive-side corollary: no *shihōsho* can be looked for until the row is placed.**

## What this pass adds to the seven patterns

**1. The funding map is a genre map** — literature's books imaged, economic history's documents not — **and a municipal map** for the print layer.

**2. Successor custody is the default**, and it has taken a new shape: **out to the state and back** (Mitsui), **given because of a disaster** (Kōnoike).

**3. Survival rules are serial and some are accidents** — earthquakes on a schedule; a ceiling.

**4. The record type is a variable**: the ledger rows (`ko_daikokuya_1848`) and the rulebook rows (`ko_meio_1498`) already differ in what the census can code — **the record type sets the resolution**, as the 2026-08-20 kō batch found.

**6. The corporate chest's Japanese cousin** — the Edo town office fund — parked as a candidate.

**7. Failure** — the Edo magistrates compiled what they chose as precedent; the house archives are survivors by definition.

## For the grant, stated for the maintainer and not applied

- **Part 2's risk table** says core corpora including **NDL Digital Collections and ISAM** are "already digitised and catalogued with verified access". **On this survey that overstates both**: the NDL tier the census needs is resident-only, and İSAM is an on-site image archive. Worth a sentence's revision.
- **A7** names "Osaka / Tokyo / Kyoto … National Archives, NDL, prefectural collections". **Add Nakano (Mitsui), Hikone (Shiga University), Tachikawa (NIJL), Toyonaka, and the house archives** — and **treat NAJ and NDL as desk sources rather than destinations** — the Daijōin diary is imaged at NAJ and the *hikitsugisho* are digitised at the NDL, though their access tier (internet or in-library) was not checked ⚠️.
- **A1–A2**: **the Japanese posts should be resident in Japan**, or the reading cannot be delegated.
