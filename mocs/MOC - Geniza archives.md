# MOC — Geniza archives

Regional hub, the second non-European pass, linked from the country table of [[MOC - European archives and digital collections]] and following [[Handoff - the non-European archive survey]] and [[MOC - Ottoman archives]]. **Infrastructure layer, not argument layer.** The unit is the deposit and its documents, not a city: the Geniza was made in Fustat and is kept in Cambridge, New York, Oxford, St Petersburg, Manchester and some fifty other places.

**This pass opened no new subject.** It looked for the archives behind rows the census already carries — `isqa`, `isqa_alloc`, `qirad`, `qirad_alloc` — and above all behind the **dispute `isqa_alloc` holds open** over whether Geniza practice was *ʿisqa*-shaped or *qirāḍ*-shaped.

## The structural fact

**1. It is not an archive; it is a deposit with a survival rule.** [[Cairo Geniza - the deposit and its survival rule]]. Hebrew-script writing went in by norm; **Arabic-script documents survive mainly by accident of reuse**; one congregation; one dominant phase, c.1000–1250. **Everything read from it is conditioned on script, period and community.**

**2. It was dispersed by sale and expedition, and nobody who made it kept any of it.** About 400,000 fragments in some sixty collections. **Pattern 2 inverted: acquirer custody, not successor custody.** The unit left is the fragment, and **the join is the Geniza's series**.

**3. And it has been reunited online, at every layer.** Images of the whole deposit — [[Friedberg Genizah Project and Ktiv - the image union layer]]. A scholarly database of 35,855 documents with open data — [[Princeton Geniza Project - the documentary database]]. **A machine transcription of the entire imaged Geniza, released CC BY in November 2025** — [[MiDRASH - the machine transcription of the Geniza]]. **No other pre-modern corpus in the survey is this legible.** The expectation that the Geniza would be well served digitally is right, and understated.

**4. Legibility reproduces the selection.** The recognition model reads Hebrew script well and Arabic script less well — **the script the deposit kept by rule is the script the machine reads**. And with access and script largely solved, **the binding constraints are the ones that do not digitise: the denominator and the deposit's positionality.**

## What this pass adds to the seven patterns

**1. The funding map, legible from its sponsors.** Private philanthropy (Friedberg), a national library (Israel), a US research group (Princeton), UK research money (the Rylands imaging) and an ERC Synergy grant (MiDRASH) made this corpus legible — **all of them interested in Jewish texts**. The Arabic-script component is least served. **The map is again a map of legibility, not of evidence.**

**2. Custody inverted.** Not the institution keeping its papers, but the community keeping none. See [[MOC - Acquisitions and the antiquarian trade]].

**3. A published survival rule, and it is a script rule** — the sharpest instance yet of the handoff's instruction to ask the layer question about script.

**4. The record type is a variable — on a coded row.** The `isqa_alloc` dispute is letters against deeds.

**7. Failure is outside the deposit.** Goldberg 2020 finds **only five full-blown mercantile lawsuits before the Jewish court in 990–1150**; her chapter's title names where else disputes went — the *qadi* and "the people". ⚠️ Read from the chapter's opening only. **The loss side of Geniza commerce is recorded mainly where the Geniza's apparatus does not reach.**

## Candidate parked

**The Jewish pious foundation (*heqdesh*, *qodesh*) of Fustat** — logbook 2, 2026-09-11 (v). A perpetual endowment of houses administered by communal trustees, in a non-Muslim community under Islamic rule, **with its accounts edited** (Gil 1976). The comparandum is `waqf_khayri`. **Recorded as out, not parked: the *ṣuḥba*** — reciprocal agency without contract or fund.
