# MOC — Belgian archives

Country hub under [[MOC - European archives and digital collections]]. **Infrastructure layer, not argument layer.**

## The structural fact

**Belgium separates digital infrastructure from archival holdings, and shows they are independent variables.**

On infrastructure it is clearly worse than the Netherlands: images behind a registration wall with no open-data posture, a full-text layer fifteen times thinner, custody split four ways at once, and one great archive destroyed outright. On holdings it is not worse — and for this project's central question it is **better than anything else surveyed**.

Until this pass every country confirmed the funding-map argument in the same direction, which made it easy to mistake for a description rather than a warning. **Ease of access is not a proxy for richness.**

## Antwerp

- [[Insolvente Boedelskamer - Antwerp]] — 204 m, 3,000+ fonds, from 1518, 150+ complete firm archives, UNESCO Memory of the World. **An archive of absorption**: these papers survive because the firms failed
- [[FelixArchief - Antwerp city archive]] — the city archive; the one that could repair a single-witness row
- [[Antwerp schepenregisters and the regesten]] — cite the register, never the regest; the calendar is searchable and the series is not
- [[Rijksarchief te Antwerpen and Beveren - the notarial series]] — the custody trap: in Belgium the notarial protocols are with the **state**, not the city
- [[Natie archives - where the Antwerp nations records are]] — a coded form whose records have never been located

## The transcribed corpus

- [[Itinera Nova - the Leuven aldermen's registers]] — 14th–18th c., volunteer-transcribed, full-text, free, 100,000+ acts. Belgium has a layer-3 corpus; Spain and Italy have none. It is also the wrong material for this project's spine, which is the trap

## The state network and the other cities

- [[Rijksarchief in Belgie - the state archives network]] — AGATHA, backbone series, registration required
- [[Stadsarchief Brugge - the Hanse and the nations]] — the merchant nations before Antwerp; the other end of the Burgos-at-Bruges thread
- [[Stadsarchief Gent - the begijnhoven and the guilds]]
- [[Stadsarchief Mechelen]] — and the Great Council, the missing appellate tier for the southern Netherlands ⚠️ custody unverified
- [[Archief van de Stad Brussel]] — a court city's municipal archive; the central government's records are at the Rijksarchief in the same city
- [[Rijksarchief te Luik en Namen - the Walloon repositories]] — added on the second pass, because a survey that follows a language boundary has imported a nineteenth-century fact into a study of the fifteenth

## Religious custody and the beguinage problem

- [[Begijnhof archives - where the beguinages records are]] — a coded form dispersed across four custodians by a dissolution settlement that **sorted documents by type, not at random**
- [[KADOC - religious archives at Leuven]]

## The full-text layer, and the loss

- [[BelgicaPress and KBR - the thin full-text layer]] — 1.2M pages against Delpher's 18M
- [[Archief Ieper - one charter and five accounts]] — the belfry fire of 22 November 1914. **A fire has no shape**, so the gap licenses no inference in either direction

## Open questions

- Whether the Insolvente Boedelskamer records state the **reasoning** behind a distribution or only its outcome. This decides a great deal and should be answered first
- Where the `natie` records actually are, and whether they are in successor-company custody
- One beguinage worked through all four custodians, before any general claim
