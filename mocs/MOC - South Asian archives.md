# MOC — South Asian archives

Regional hub, the fifth non-European pass, linked from the country table of [[MOC - European archives and digital collections]] and following [[Handoff - the non-European archive survey]], [[MOC - Ottoman archives]], [[MOC - Geniza archives]], [[MOC - Japanese archives]] and [[MOC - Chinese archives]]. **Infrastructure layer, not argument layer.**

**This pass opened no new subject, and it had almost nothing to place.** The census's only South Asian row is `huf`, uncoded and recommended for removal. **So the question was not "where are the records behind the rows" but "is there an archival base for a South Asian row at all".**

**Scope, stated because Decision_log D4 is open and this pass does not decide it.** The survey read "South Asia" as the subcontinent and Ceylon, the referent of the *śreṇi* and *jajmani* the grant names. **South-East Asia was not surveyed**; what the passes have already found on that side is recorded under D4 below.

## The structural fact

**1. The colonial state kept the record and the merchants kept their own.** [[South Asian archives - the colonial state kept the record and the merchants kept their own]]. The administrative archive is split between London and the successor states, is enormous, and is increasingly online; **the commercial record stayed with families and mostly did not survive.**

**2. Layer 3 is the colonial enquiry.** [[The colonial enquiry as South Asia's layer 3]]. Banking enquiry committees of 1929–30, district manuals, glossaries, customary-law compilations — **printed, free, and shaped by the enquiry's purpose.** The same genre as *Taiwan Shihō*, and the census's one South Asian item is one of them.

**3. The oldest evidence is the best evidence.** [[Indian inscriptions - the endowment recorded in stone]]. **An endowment inscription states principal, trustee, rate, purpose and witness** — more than the Chinese guild stele, which states only founding and gifts.

**4. The script wall is being lowered by machines, just now.** [[Modi script and the forty million documents]]. Modi, Persian *shikasta*, *mahajani* and the Tamil commercial hands; **the first Modi transliteration model shipped in 2025 on a training set of 2,043 images.**

**5. There is no institution collecting commercial papers, and there was none when the papers were current.** [[Tata Central Archives and the scarcity of Indian business archives]]. **No *monjokan* network, no contract-collecting academy.**

## Repositories

- [[National Archives of India - Abhilekh Patal]] — **189.8 million digitised pages**; the administrative record from 1748
- [[Maharashtra State Archives - the Peshwa Daftar]] — **the most interesting unread archive in the survey**; thin, and no figure in it is sourced
- [[Tamil Nadu Archives - the Madras records and the Dutch series]] — Company records 1670–1857; **1,763 Dutch volumes, the unique two-thirds scanned**
- [[Rajasthan State Archives - Bikaner and the princely bahis]] — Mughal Persian documents and princely *bahiat*; thin
- [[Sri Lanka National Archives - the Dutch records and the thombos]] — **310 metres of VOC Ceylon records; the thombo index is searchable online**
- [[British Library - the India Office Records]] — surveyed in the British pass; the metropolitan half
- [[ASI epigraphy and the published corpora]] — *Epigraphia Indica*, *South Indian Inscriptions*, the *Annual Report*; **most inscriptions are unpublished**
- [[DHARMA - an ERC Synergy project that produced a TEI corpus of inscriptions]] — the machine-readable layer, and a finished ERC Synergy comparator
- **Marginal and not given a note**: the Historical Archives of Goa (Portuguese, 1510–1961; reported in a dilapidated building, with a digitisation plan under the Smart City Mission ⚠️ nothing established); the Prime Ministers' Museum and Library, Delhi, for business and association papers ⚠️ unexamined.

## What an evidence base would look like, institution by institution

⚠️ Every row rests on summaries read in this pass; **nothing in this table was read in an edition, a volume or an archive.**

| Institution | Record type | Custodian | Layer | Access |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| *Śreṇi* endowment (2c CE) | inscription stating principal, trustee, rate, purpose | in situ at Nasik; estampage with the ASI | **3 in print** (*Epigraphia Indica*), partly TEI | free |
| Temple and assembly endowments (medieval South India) | inscription | in situ; ASI | 3 in print (*South Indian Inscriptions*); **most unpublished** | free for what is printed |
| *Kuri* / chit fund | custom, then agreement, then a registered *chitty* deed | organisers; state registrars of chits | 3 in colonial enquiry and district manual | free in print; registers untested |
| Chettiar banking | *kadai kanakku* ledgers | **private, and much lost in Burma** | 3 in the 1930 enquiries | enquiries free; books not available |
| Marwari and Gujarati firms | *bahi khata* | private; a princely fraction at Bikaner; **stray sets in foreign libraries** | 1 at best | Lilly Library: **minimally processed, permission required** |
| Maratha state and its bankers | *daftar* in Modi | Maharashtra State Archives, Pune | 3 for the printed *Selections* only | on site; **script wall** |
| Joint Hindu family | law reports and digests | published | 3 | free |
| *Jajmani* | **ethnography** | fieldworkers' archives ⚠️ unlocated | — | — |

**Read the table for its shape.** **The two rows with a real, free, layer-3 evidence base are the oldest institution in the survey and the one that is still trading** — the endowment in stone and the chit fund. **Everything in between, the whole early-modern commercial middle, is missing.**

## What this pass adds to the seven patterns

**1. The funding map is a state map twice over** — two states hold the administrative record and both are digitising it; **no programme images a merchant's ledger, because no institution holds one.**

**2. Successor custody, the Japanese default, was not found.** The Indian joint family is coded by lawyers as perpetual and **its firms' books are gone.** ⚠️ This may be a finding about custody or a gap in a one-day survey; **the way to tell is to ask whether any surviving Indian firm holds its nineteenth-century books**, which no source read here addresses.

**3. Survival rules: publicity, litigation and print.** Stone survives because it was meant to be public; ledgers survive because a court or a commission called for them; **and the printed selection survives better than the archive it selects from.**

**4. The record type is a variable, and here it is the whole story.** Inscription states terms; enquiry states a category; law report states doctrine; ethnography states a system; **the instrument the census prefers — the arrangement's own paper — barely exists.**

**5. National arrangements**: mass digitisation of the state's record, no digitisation of the private record, and a script wall that machines reached in 2025.

**6. The merchant corporation with no row, in its oldest form**: the *śreṇi*, trustee of perpetual deposits at a stated rate — **and, unlike `huiguan` and `gongsi`, it left its terms in writing.**

**7. Failure is emptiest — and here the emptiness is the census's own.** One row, uncoded, recommended for removal. **That is the honest state and this pass does not disturb it.**

## For the grant, stated for the maintainer and not applied

- **Decision_log D4 is not decided here, and the pass supplies its archival input.** **On the South Asia side**: the *śreṇi* has a free, published, partly machine-readable evidence base; **the *jajmani* has none and its existence as a system is disputed** — see [[The jajmani system has no archive]]. **On the South-East Asia side**: the survey has already met the best-transcribed corpus it has seen — the VOC's five million machine-transcribed scans at [[VOC digital corpus - what is online and on what terms]] — and the Batavia council archive at [[Leiden University Library - the Kong Koan archive of Batavia]], **plus the `gongsi` thread the Chinese pass left open in Borneo and Singapore.** **Stated as costs and evidence, not as a recommendation.**
- **A7 names no South Asian destination.** Most of what this pass found is **desk-reachable**: the epigraphic corpora, the enquiry volumes, DHARMA's data. **The two things that need a trip are Pune (Modi) and Chennai or Colombo (Dutch and Company series)** — and neither is needed unless a Maratha or Coromandel row is wanted.
- **A6** names Ottoman *dīwānī* and cursive classical Chinese. **If the Maratha or princely record is ever used, add Modi and Persian *shikasta*** — or state in terms that the South Asian strand works from printed layers and needs neither.
- **A5**: the South Asian library line is **nearly free** — *Epigraphia Indica*, *South Indian Inscriptions*, the banking enquiries and the district manuals are all scanned and public.
- **A comparator worth naming in Part 2**: DHARMA, an ERC Synergy grant in South and South-East Asian epigraphy, 2019–2025, four PIs, whose deliverable was a TEI corpus on GitHub.

## Candidates parked

- **The *śreṇi* endowment** — logbook 2, 2026-09-12 (x). The perpetual deposit with a craft corporation at a stated rate, witnessed by a town assembly: **the fourth leg of the endowment family** beside the waqf, the *heqdesh* and the lineage estate, **and the only one whose instrument states a rate.**
- **The South Indian chit fund / *kuri*** — logbook 2, 2026-09-12 (ix). **The third rotating-association tradition**, which is what makes the Japanese rows comparable to anything.
- **Out, and not parked: Chettiar mutual credit**, on the *ṣuḥba* precedent of 2026-09-11 (v) — reciprocal credit on reputation, no fund, no contribution schedule. **Noted and not parked: the *nidhi***, the Madras mutual benefit fund, which is a credit institution and belongs with the blocked credit-cooperative pole rather than with hazard pools. ⚠️ Unread.

## Open questions

- **Ask whether any surviving Indian firm or family holds nineteenth-century books** — the test that would turn pattern 2 above from a gap into a finding.
- **Read one banking enquiry volume** — Madras 1930 — for chits, *nidhis* and Chettiar banking in the practitioners' own evidence.
- **Verify the Nasik endowment from Senart's edition** in *Epigraphia Indica* before any rate is quoted.
- **Check DHARMA's corpus** for endowment inscriptions; the data is on GitHub.
- **Establish the Peshwa Daftar's actual extent** from an official source, and what the *Selections* volumes cover.
- **Locate a chit agreement** — the *kuri* equivalent of a *shihōsho*.
