# MOC — British archives

Country hub under [[MOC - European archives and digital collections]], covering England, Scotland, Wales and Ireland. **Infrastructure layer, not argument layer.**

## The structural fact

**Britain is where this project's digital-asymmetry argument was first made, and the survey has now been round Europe and come back to it. It holds, and the comparative material makes it sharper.**

The description layer is excellent — Discovery is both TNA's catalogue and a national aggregator, with an API none of the southern portals advertises. **The image layer is thin exactly where this project needs it**, and TNA states outright that none of its company records are viewable online.

**Where digitisation has happened it was paid for by somebody else.** Three of Britain's most significant early-modern commercial corpora are being opened by foreign or commercial money:

- [[The Prize Papers - HCA 32 and the German money]] — c. 500,000 High Court of Admiralty records, 1652–1815, 19+ languages, funded principally by the **Göttingen Academy**, 2018–2037
- [[British Library - the India Office Records]] — a commercial publisher and a Gulf sovereign foundation
- [[The British Newspaper Archive and the commercial full-text layer]] — 50M+ pages, through a genealogy company, by subscription

**It is not that a state under-funds its own record. It is that the record gets opened by whoever has a reason, and their reasons have shapes.**

## London

- [[The London Archives]] · [[Guildhall Library and the City livery companies]] · [[Lloyd's and the marine insurance records]] · [[The Bank of England Archive]]

**Two things to carry out of London.** The livery and regulated companies are the **English member of the identity-wrapper family** the survey has assembled in six countries, and `organizational_forms` codes none of them — the row Harris's own regulated/joint-stock exclusion pointed at. And the Bank's **stock transfer books** are the English equivalent of the Bazacle series, better preserved, and ⚠️ possibly unworked.

## Ports and business

- [[Bristol Archives]] · [[Liverpool Record Office and the Maritime Archives]] — the cheapest test of whether the English rows describe England or describe London
- [[The Business Archives Council and the UK business archives]] — the sixth national arrangement, and the one that makes the six-row table an argument

## What the completed survey now supports

**Two tables that belong in the methods section rather than in footnotes.**

The **press corpora**: five states, a forty-fold range in scale, three free and two by subscription, and none of the variation about what was printed.

The **business archives**: six states, six arrangements — French centralism, German federalism, Swedish corporatism, British voluntarism — not one designed around what historians would need. With five instances of successor custody beside it, the conclusion is that **the archival record of durable private institutions is governed by national arrangements chosen for unrelated reasons.** That is a selection effect on the comparative corpus operating at the level of the state.

## Open questions

- Is the **instance** side of `HCA` — average, bottomry, insurance — part of the Prize Papers programme, or only the prize side? **The series the census needs and the series being digitised may not be the same**
- Are the Bank of England's stock transfer books worked into a series?
- Does a subject route into Chancery exist, or is the realistic route the secondary literature's footnotes?
- What is `BT 31`'s sampling rule?
- Does institutional access cover the subscription platforms?
