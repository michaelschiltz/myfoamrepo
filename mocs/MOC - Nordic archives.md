# MOC — Nordic archives

Country hub under [[MOC - European archives and digital collections]], covering Sweden, Denmark, Norway, Finland and Iceland. **Infrastructure layer, not argument layer.**

## The structural fact

**The Nordics are the exact mirror of Belgium: the best evidence infrastructure in Europe over the thinnest institutional variety.**

Belgium had poor infrastructure and first-rank holdings. Here it is reversed — national archives that are heavily imaged, free, and in Sweden's case **publishing their own open-source HTR models** — over a commercial world that produced no great notariate, no *consulado*-type merchant corporation, and no indigenous commenda family.

**Taken with Belgium, this retires any remaining temptation to read holdings off access.** The two cases fail in opposite directions.

**And the thinness is the one absence in the whole survey that is probably evidential.** Everywhere else an absence had a custodial or funding explanation available; here the archives are too good for that, which is the argument at [[The Nordic thinness - what is actually missing]].

## The two first-rank cases

- [[The Sound Toll Registers]] — **every ship and cargo through the Danish straits, 1497/1557–1857, transcribed.** UNESCO Memory of the World 2007. The only genuine *denominator* the survey has found, and the caveat that it is an interpretation rather than a copy
- [[Stora Kopparberg - the 1288 share]] — **the rival claimant to the Bazacle, uncoded.** Two constructions of a transferable share in a perpetual productive asset, in different legal families — worth more than the Italian quartet, where shared ancestry is guaranteed

## Three small-state chartered companies

- [[Svenska Ostindiska Companiet - the Gothenburg archive]] — 1731, with a dedicated digital archive
- [[Asiatisk Kompagni and the Danish trading companies]] — 1732, themed and digitised
- with [[Geheimes Staatsarchiv Preussischer Kulturbesitz - Berlin]] in Germany

**Three copies of the Dutch model, made independently by three minor powers, with different outcomes — wound up, persisted, failed.** Outcome variance across structurally similar cases is exactly what the entity census lacks, and all three archives are open.

## The Hanse from the receiving end

- [[Bergen and the Hanseatic Kontor]] — one of the four *Kontore*, and the fifth instance of the identity-wrapper form. **`organizational_forms` codes `natie` alone**
- [[Visby and the Gotland sea law]] — and the caution that a law named for a place is not evidence about that place

## Civic registers and the rest

- [[Stockholms stadsarkiv and the Swedish town records]] — the *tänkebok*, fourth name for one record type. **Where two census rows are sourced across the notarial/civic line, a difference between them may be a difference in what the record type records**
- [[Centrum for Naringslivshistoria - Stockholm]] — a business archive funded by the depositing companies: successor custody institutionalised
- [[Thjodskjalasafn Islands - Iceland]] — outside reach, and the note says why

## Open questions

- Has Riksarkivet applied its own HTR models to its own holdings, and do they transfer to other European hands?
- Does the Swedish East India Company's digital archive carry the **constitutional** record or only the voyage and sales material? That is the whole question
- Does the Nordic commercial record thicken after the Hanse's decline? **If it does not, the thinness hypothesis is wrong**
- Did the Finnish and Baltic coastal towns generate a *partenrederij*-comparable series?
