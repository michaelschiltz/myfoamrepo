# MOC — Chinese archives

Regional hub, the fourth non-European pass, linked from the country table of [[MOC - European archives and digital collections]] and following [[Handoff - the non-European archive survey]], [[MOC - Ottoman archives]], [[MOC - Geniza archives]] and [[MOC - Japanese archives]]. **Infrastructure layer, not argument layer.**

**This pass opened no new subject.** It looked for the archives behind the census's Chinese rows — `shenhui_gu` and `shenhui_gu_alloc`, the share classes `hegu_yingu` and `hegu_shengu`, the parked `hegu_gangu` and `gugu_death_share`, and the uncoded umbrellas `huiguan`, `gongsi` and `piaohao`.

**What it found, in one sentence: the Chinese state archive is vast, divided and not where the census's evidence is; the census's evidence is private contracts, read through one compilation by one article; and the operating record of Chinese associations survives mostly abroad.**

## The structural fact

**1. The Qing central record is divided by a sale and a strait.** [[Chinese archives - the Qing record divided by a sale and a strait]]. About 10 million documents in Beijing; the Grand Secretariat's archive in Taipei because it was **sold as waste paper around 1921** and bought back; the palace memorials in Taipei because they were **evacuated in 1948–49**. **No series is whole on either side.**

**2. The two sides run opposite access regimes.** [[PRC archival access - photography forbidden and catalogues curated]]. **Taipei catalogues and images; Beijing forbids photography and caps copies at twenty documents a trip.** And **a curated digital catalogue erases the evidence that closed files exist**, so **a null in a mainland online catalogue is weaker evidence of absence than any null elsewhere in the survey**.

**3. The census's record is private, and it moves by market.** [[Chinese contracts - the private document boom and the market that feeds it]]. Hundreds of thousands of household contracts, found in waves since the 1940s and bought by institutions and collectors. **The unit is the household box; the route is the market; layer 3 is the scholar's compilation.**

**4. The census's Chinese rows are one witness.** [[Zhang Chuanxi 1995 - the contract compilation behind the census's Chinese rows]]. **Every coded Chinese cell cites Zelin 2019, and the earliest instances pass through one 1995 compilation.** On [[Count degrees of freedom not cells]], agreement among the Chinese rows is not a finding.

**5. Associations left stone at home and paper abroad.** [[Guild stele inscriptions - the huiguan's surviving record]] — founding, donors and rules in stone, and no fund; [[Tung Wah Coffin Home Archives - Hong Kong]] — the payout event, in registers and letters. **The record type explains part of an uncoded row.**

## What this pass adds to the seven patterns

**1. The legibility map is a political map** — which side of the Strait a document was on in 1949. **And the grant's own map has a hole**: A5 and A6 budget for classical Chinese; **A7 names no Chinese destination**.

**2. Successor custody becomes acquirer custody and colonial custody** — the market in Huizhou, a Japanese imperial university in Taipei, a Dutch university for a Batavian council. **And institutional survival**: Tung Wah, and Taiwan's deity associations, still registered.

**3. Sale by weight is now two instances** — Beijing c. 1921, Istanbul 1931. **A flag, not yet a rule**: when a successor state is short of money, an imperial archive's routine series is paper.

**4. The record type is a variable, three ways**: steles record founding and gifts, not funds; genealogies carry rules, not accounts; contracts record constitutions, not practice.

**5. National arrangements**: two regimes for one record, and **digital curation as a closure that leaves no trace**.

**6. The merchant corporation with no row**: `piaohao`, with a house-rule series that would phase-split.

**7. Failure**: the Grand Secretariat's first sold batch was never recovered; the associations whose paper survives are those whose charity outlived them.

## For the grant, stated for the maintainer and not applied

- **A7 has no Chinese destination.** Either the Chinese strand is desk-based — **defensible**: the Taipei holdings, the Kong Koan archive, the grain price database and the compilations are all reachable without Beijing — **or a mainland trip should be costed as transcription time**, because photography is forbidden. **Leiden belongs on the Amsterdam–The Hague trip.**
- **A5**: the Chinese line is **books and one or two licences** — Zhang 1995, the Huizhou and Qingshui compilations, *Jinshang shiliao jicheng*, the National Palace Museum's paid image database, the grain price database's registration.
- **A6** names "cursive classical Chinese". **Right for contracts, and underspecified for accounts**, whose commercial numerals are the Chinese counterpart of siyakat. ⚠️ From general knowledge.

## Candidates parked

- **The Qing community and ever-normal granaries** — logbook 2, 2026-09-11 (vii). **The imperial-scale sibling of the Edo *shichibu tsumikin***, with a denominator in the grain price database and an existing finding that central relief crowded out local self-insurance.
- **The lineage charitable estate (*yizhuang*)** — logbook 2, 2026-09-11 (viii). An endowed kin fund paying on events, rules in print in genealogies. **Noted with it, not parked: the Huizhou credit association (钱会).**

## Open questions

- **Read Zelin 2019, 337–350, with its footnotes**, and place every Chinese row from the apparatus.
- **Is the Ruifeng ironworks instance from the Ba County archive?** If so, look for `hegu_gangu`'s second instance in Danxin or Huizhou first.
- **Acquire Zhang 1995**, and identify the source of the Shanyin deity-association contract.
- **Read *Taiwan Shihō* on the 神明會** as a second, independent instance of `shenhui_gu`; and **Tong Xu 2023** for Huizhou 神會 documents.
- **Kong Koan cemetery registers and accounts**: were Batavia's Chinese burial grounds funded by fees, donations or levies?
- **FamilySearch**: index or no index, and image access terms.
- **Out of scope and named**: the Second Historical Archives (Nanjing, Republican); the Hong Kong Public Records Office; the overseas associations' own archives in San Francisco, Vancouver, Melbourne and Singapore; the Japanese North China village surveys.
