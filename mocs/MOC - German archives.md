# MOC — German archives

Country hub under [[MOC - European archives and digital collections]]. **Infrastructure layer, not argument layer.**

## The structural fact

**Germany inverts France, and the inversion is the whole of it.**

France has one classification frame and a national archive. **Germany has neither** — the Bundesarchiv begins with the North German Confederation, so there is no national archive for the period at all — and competence sits with sixteen *Länder* and, decisively, with the cities.

**The cities are where the material is.** The Hanse was civic; south German merchant capital was civic. A merchant's papers did not go to a territorial prince. **So the German answer to "where is the commercial record" is: in about twenty city archives, none subordinate to any other**, with Archivportal-D the only thing that sees across them, and what it aggregates is description.

**A second, and specifically German, custody event:** the secularisation and mediatisation of 1803–06 moved enormous quantities of civic and ecclesiastical archives into territorial state custody, sorted by the new owner rather than by subject. For any German institution before 1800, ask what happened to its *territory* in 1803, not only where it was.

## The access layers

- [[German archives online - the federal access layers]] — Archivportal-D over ~20 tiered systems; better than Italy's competing catalogues, worse than France's single frame
- [[Deutsches Zeitungsportal - the German full-text layer]] — 1671–1950, free. With Delpher and BelgicaPress free and RetroNews charging, **three of four national libraries give the historical press away**

## The Hanseatic cities

- [[Archiv der Hansestadt Lubeck]] — the *Niederstadtbuch*, the civic register that does a notariate's work; and the Hanse as an **uncoded form with almost none of the census's characteristics that lasted five hundred years anyway**
- [[Staatsarchiv Hamburg]] — the `Admiralitätskollegium`; and a **printed law of insurance and bottomry**, which may make Hamburg codable from print before anyone enters the archive
- [[Staatsarchiv Bremen]] — and *Haus Seefahrt*, a sixteenth-century maritime mutual still operating
- [[Stadtarchiv Stralsund and the Wendish towns]] — one town law, several practices; and Swedish sovereignty after 1648
- [[Hanse documents on the Memory of the World register]] — **11 archives, six countries, inscribed 2023.** There is no Hanseatic archive because there was no Hanseatic state

## The coded form

- [[Bodmerei - where the German bottomry records are]] — the Dutch side is notarial and at layer 3; **the German side has no notariate of the same kind**, so the pair may differ in record type rather than in what the parties agreed

## South German merchant capital

- [[Fuggerarchiv - Dillingen]] — private family and foundation custody
- [[Stadtarchiv Augsburg and the Staatsarchiv]] — the city's record of the firms, created by a body with no stake in their reputation
- [[Stadtarchiv Nurnberg]] — the Banco Publico of 1621, and the unsettled distinction between subscribed-share banks and giro banks

## Fairs, Prussia, business

- [[Institut fur Stadtgeschichte Frankfurt]] · [[Stadtarchiv Leipzig and the fairs]] — a fair is a **periodic legal regime**, and the *Zahlungsmesse* is multilateral netting. Is that pooling? The census has no row either way
- [[Geheimes Staatsarchiv Preussischer Kulturbesitz - Berlin]] — the Brandenburg African Company of 1682: **a chartered company that failed**, and therefore variance in an outcome the census never records
- [[The German Wirtschaftsarchive - a distributed business archive]] · [[Stiftung Hanseatisches Wirtschaftsarchiv - Hamburg]]

## The territorial archives

- [[Bayerisches Hauptstaatsarchiv - Munchen]] — and the 1803 rule
- [[Landesarchiv Baden-Wurttemberg]] — a secondary region, and the note says so
- [[Landesarchiv NRW and the Rhineland]] — the unexamined eastern edge of the Low Countries question

## The loss

- [[Historisches Archiv der Stadt Koln and the 2009 collapse]] — 30 km of shelving, 1,255,724 recovery units, **6,000–6,500 person-years of restoration, thirty years to full usability**. The fourth catastrophic loss in the survey and the only one still in progress — and the only one whose **selection rule is knowable while it is being applied**

## Three patterns this pass sharpened

**Successor custody is now a pattern with three instances** — the Bazacle to EDF, the Antwerp *naties* to their trading successors, the Fuggers to their own foundations. **Institutions that survive keep their own papers, and private custody means access by permission.** Two filters stacked in the same direction on top of the persistence literature's own.

**The merchant corporation now has five instances and no row.** Marseille 1599, Bilbao, Burgos, Lucca, Hamburg — five countries, one institutional form, `organizational_forms` has nothing. This should go to a selection brief.

**Failure is where the census is emptiest.** The Insolvente Boedelskamer supplies it on the loss side; the Brandenburg company supplies it on the entity side.
