# MOC — French archives

Country hub under [[MOC - European archives and digital collections]]. **Infrastructure layer, not argument layer.** Twenty-three notes, the largest country set so far.

## The structural fact

**France's problem is neither Spain's nor Italy's. It is scale — and against that it sets one advantage nobody else has: a uniform classification.**

Every *archives départementales* in the country uses the same lettered frame. `B` is the ancien-régime jurisdictions everywhere, `E` the notaries, `J` the private archives. **A question can therefore be put to a series rather than to an archive, and asked of the whole country at once.** That is the single most useful structural fact in the European survey, and [[The amirautes - a series across the coastal departments]] is the move performed.

**What it does not solve:** digitisation is decided department by department and has been overwhelmingly genealogical. The series this project needs are the least likely to be imaged, and coverage varies by an order of magnitude between neighbouring departments. A French coverage map is a map of local council budgets.

**And one obstacle is specific to France.** The portals are JavaScript-driven and several sit behind anti-bot protection — the Haute-Garonne catalogue refused automated access outright during this survey. The data is not closed; the interfaces are built for humans with browsers. **French finding aids are the least remotely-workable in the survey**, and that belongs in the register as an access condition.

## The great series

- [[Minutier central des notaires de Paris]] — the largest notarial deposit in Europe after Barcelona's; excellent description, no Amsterdam-style query
- [[Parlement de Paris - serie X]] — judgments, **and charters registered with the court's own remonstrances attached**, which is stated rationale rather than component presence
- [[The amirautes - a series across the coastal departments]] — **the most promising French lead.** One ordinance, one procedural frame, a dozen independent local practices. The baseline against which cross-national divergence has to be judged
- [[Juridictions consulaires - the merchant courts]] — merchants judging merchants, a form four countries have now produced and the census has no row for

## The flagship case

- [[The Bazacle records - a custody problem]] — **not resolved, but no longer a blank.** The medieval material is at Toulouse under an unidentified cote; the modern company archive has a named and testable destination in **EDF's *Fonds Ex-Sociétés*** of companies nationalised in 1946
- [[Archives departementales de la Haute-Garonne]]

**The pattern worth noticing:** the Bazacle and the Antwerp *naties* are both long-lived institutions whose archives followed their corporate successor rather than their territory. **Institutions that survive keep their own papers — and institutions that survive are exactly the ones a persistence argument wants.**

## The ports

- [[Archives departementales des Bouches-du-Rhone - Marseille]] — the Mediterranean hinge; same sea as Genoa and Barcelona, different sovereign
- [[Chambre de commerce de Marseille - the 1599 archive]] — a merchant corporation levying on its members to fund consuls: `PY0=collective-good`, `MB3=compulsory`, under a second crown
- [[Archives departementales de la Gironde - Bordeaux]] · [[Archives departementales de la Loire-Atlantique - Nantes]] · [[Archives departementales de la Seine-Maritime - Rouen]] — three Atlantic ports, one crown, one ordinance
- [[Service historique de la Defense - Lorient and the Compagnie des Indes]] — **the known-transmission case**, and therefore the control the VOC/EIC comparison needs

## The borderlands

- [[Archives departementales du Nord - the Chambre des comptes de Lille]] — the Burgundian fiscal archive that ended up in France; its inventory is full-text on Gallica
- [[Archives departementales de l'Herault - Montpellier]] — the same notaries before and after a change of sovereign
- [[Archives municipales de Bayonne - the Basque coast]] — one people, one maritime economy, two crowns. A research design, not yet an archive

## Loss, business, diplomacy

- [[Archives de Paris and the fire of 1871]] — **and the reconstitution, which is the more dangerous object.** A hole announces itself; a reconstructed series does not
- [[Archives nationales du monde du travail - Roubaix]] — insurers, and the *sociétés de secours mutuels*
- [[Archives diplomatiques - La Courneuve and Nantes]] — the Levant consulates: the best-documented identity-wrapper in the survey

## Open questions

- **Did the *Ordonnance de la marine* of 1681 flatten the local variation the *amirauté* sweep depends on?** Test on two departments before designing around it
- Sicard's 1953 source list would settle the medieval half of the Bazacle in an afternoon. **Do that first**
- Whether institutional access covers **RetroNews**
- How the Compagnie des Indes records divide between Lorient, ANOM and the Archives nationales
- Whether the ANMT reaches back before the nineteenth century
